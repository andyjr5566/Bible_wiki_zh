Hushai Counters Ahithophel’s Advice

2 Samuel 17:1

Furthermore, Ahithophel said to Absalom,
Ahithophel had served David as a trusted counselor whose advice carried extraordinary authority in Israel (2 Samuel 16:23). His decision to support Absalom exposed the painful depth of the rebellion, since David’s former confidant now used his wisdom against him. Ahithophel may also have borne personal resentment toward David, for he was likely the grandfather of Bathsheba and therefore connected to the grievous wrong David committed against Uriah. David later recognized Ahithophel’s betrayal as part of the suffering described in Psalm 41:9.

Ahithophel proposed a substantial force, large enough to strike decisively yet mobile enough to move quickly. His strategy depended upon preventing David from gathering the loyal soldiers, priests, commanders, and tribes that would soon strengthen his position. The rebellion could succeed only if David were defeated before Israel had time to reconsider its allegiance.

Ahithophel urged Absalom to pursue David immediately, before the fugitives could rest, gather forces, or establish a defensible position. David’s flight from Jerusalem had left him physically exhausted and deeply grieved by his son’s rebellion. Amalek similarly attacked Israel’s weak and trailing members in the wilderness, revealing the cruelty of exploiting vulnerability (Deuteronomy 25:17–18). God’s people are called to vigilance because spiritual enemies also seek moments of weariness, fear, and isolation (1 Peter 5:8).

The Lord had often turned enemy armies into confusion, as when the Midianites fled before Gideon’s small force (Judges 7:19–22). Human schemes may use fear to weaken faith, but God gives His servants peace and steadiness when they depend on Him (Philippians 4:6–7).

David’s threatened life points forward in contrast to Christ, the greater Son of David, who willingly gave His life for His people and rose beyond the reach of every enemy (Isaiah 53:4–5).
