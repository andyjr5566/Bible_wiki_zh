The Valley of Vision

Isaiah 22:1

This is the burden against the Valley of Vision:
Jerusalem, the city entrusted with God’s temple, Davidic king, and prophetic witness, is called the Valley of Vision because the Lord had made His will known there. Yet spiritual privilege had not produced faithfulness. The oracle exposes a city that possessed divine revelation while resisting the God who gave it. Jerusalem lay among surrounding heights, but its greater tragedy was spiritual blindness in the place where God’s truth had been most clearly displayed.

Isaiah’s vision anticipates the fall of Jerusalem to Babylon, when King Zedekiah and his officials fled by night but were overtaken near Jericho (2 Kings 25:4–6; Jeremiah 39:4–5). Their flight exposed the bankruptcy of leadership that sought security in political strategy rather than in the Lord. Faithless shepherds leave the flock vulnerable, unlike the righteous King and Good Shepherd who lays down His life for His sheep (Ezekiel 34:23; John 10:11).

Eliakim son of Hilkiah received authority as steward over King Hezekiah’s household after the removal of Shebna (Isaiah 22:15–21). The key represented delegated royal authority over access, resources, and decisions within David’s kingdom. Carried on the shoulder, it also portrayed the burden of faithful service. Jesus Christ fulfills this royal imagery perfectly as the promised Son of David, bearing the government upon His shoulder and possessing supreme authority over God’s kingdom (Isaiah 9:6; Revelation 3:7).

Isaiah 22:23

I will drive him like a peg into a firm place,
The LORD would establish Eliakim securely in the office removed from Shebna. Like a tent peg fixed in solid ground, Eliakim would provide stability for the royal house through faithful service under Hezekiah. His appointment rested on God’s sovereign purpose rather than personal ambition. Yet no earthly servant can bear ultimate weight; the secure foundation toward which this image points is Christ, the chosen cornerstone upon whom God’s people are established.

His elevation pictured dependable administration under the Davidic king, yet the following verse shows that even a faithful human servant could not bear every burden indefinitely.
