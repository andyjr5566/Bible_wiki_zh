Everything Is Futile

Ecclesiastes 1:1

These are the words of the Teacher,
The Teacher (Qoheleth, “Preacher” or “Assembler”) speaks as one entrusted with wisdom for God’s people. Solomon gathered the results of a life marked by extraordinary knowledge, royal privilege, accomplishment, and painful spiritual failure. His reflections expose the limits of earthly pursuits and direct the reader toward the fear of God as the only lasting foundation for life.

Ecc 12:9-11 Not only was the Teacher wise, but he also taught…

1Ki 4:32 Solomon composed three thousand proverbs,…

Pro 1:1-2 These are the proverbs of Solomon son of David,…

the son of David,
Solomon inherited David’s throne and stood within the covenant line through which God promised an enduring kingdom. David’s greater Son, Jesus Christ, fulfills that promise perfectly as the eternal King. Solomon’s words therefore arise from Israel’s royal history while pointing beyond human kingship to the need for the righteous King God would provide.

1Ki 2:12 So Solomon sat on the throne of his father David,…

1Ch 29:23 So Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king…

2Sa 7:12-13 And when your days are fulfilled and you rest…

king in Jerusalem:
Jerusalem was the city David established as Israel’s capital and Solomon made the center of worship by building the temple. From this seat of power, wealth, learning, and religious privilege, Solomon tested the value of wisdom, pleasure, labor, possessions, and honor. His conclusions carry particular weight because he possessed opportunities beyond those available to most people, yet found that no earthly achievement can satisfy the soul apart from God.

1Ki 11:42-43 Thus the time that Solomon reigned in Jerusalem…

2Ch 9:30 Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years.

Ecclesiastes 1:2

“Futility of futilities,”
Solomon opens with the strongest possible verdict on life pursued apart from God. Human achievements, however impressive, prove as fleeting as breath when death brings every earthly gain to an end.

Ecc 12:8 “Futility of futilities,” says the Teacher.…

Rom 8:20-21 For the creation was subjected to futility,…

Psa 39:5-6 You, indeed, have made my days as handbreadths,…

says the Teacher,
Solomon speaks from unusual experience as David’s son and king over Israel. Given wisdom, wealth, peace, power, and every opportunity for pleasure and accomplishment, he could testify that no created gift can bear the weight of the human soul.

Ecc 12:9-11 Not only was the Teacher wise, but he also taught…

1Ki 4:29-34 And God gave Solomon wisdom, exceedingly deep insight…

Pro 1:1-7 These are the proverbs of Solomon son of David,…

“futility of futilities!
The repeated judgment reaches beyond isolated disappointments to the whole fallen order. Work, possessions, wisdom, and pleasure cannot secure lasting profit or answer the deepest questions of life.

Ecc 2:17 So I hated life, because the work that is done…

Ecc 11:8 So if a man lives many years, let him rejoice in them all.…

Ecc 2:11 Yet when I considered the works my hands had accomplished…

Everything is futile!”
Life “under the sun” is temporary and unstable because sin has subjected creation to decay. Ecclesiastes directs the reader beyond temporal pursuits to fear God, receive His gifts rightly, and seek the enduring hope fulfilled in Christ, whose resurrection overcomes death and grants eternal life.

Eph 4:17-18 So I tell you this, and insist on it in the Lord,…

1Co 15:17-19 And if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile…

Psa 127:1-2 A song of ascents. Of Solomon. Unless the LORD builds…

Ecclesiastes 1:3

What does a man gain from all his labor,
Solomon confronts the lasting value of human achievement. Wealth, reputation, learning, and accomplishment may provide temporary benefits, yet none can secure life, righteousness, or eternal inheritance. Jesus similarly warned that gaining the whole world is worthless if one forfeits his soul (Matthew 16:26).

Ecc 2:22 For what does a man get for all the toil and striving…

Mat 16:26 What will it profit a man if he gains the whole world…

Ecc 5:15 As a man came from his mother’s womb, so he will depart…

at which he toils
Human work bears the weight of life east of Eden, where Adam was sentenced to labor against the resistance of a cursed ground (Genesis 3:17–19). Work itself remains good and ordained by God, but sin has made it wearying, uncertain, and unable to satisfy the deepest needs of the heart. Christ offers rest to those burdened by futile striving and gives enduring purpose to labor done for the Lord (Matthew 11:28–30; 1 Corinthians 15:58).

Gen 3:19 By the sweat of your brow you will eat your bread,…

Psa 127:2 In vain you rise early and stay up late,…

2Th 3:8 nor did we eat anyone’s food without paying for it.…

under the sun?
Life viewed only within the boundaries of the present world ends in frustration because death eventually removes every earthly possession and achievement. Ecclesiastes exposes the poverty of an earthly perspective in order to direct the heart toward the Creator, judgment, and obedience. Believers are called to set their minds on things above, where Christ reigns and where faithful service has eternal significance (Colossians 3:1–4).

Ecc 2:11 Yet when I considered the works my hands had accomplished…

Ecc 2:17 So I hated life, because the work that is done…

Ecc 5:18 Here is what I have seen to be good and fitting:…

Ecclesiastes 1:4

Generations come and generations go,
Human life passes in an unbroken succession of birth, labor, death, and remembrance. The genealogies of Genesis record this movement from Adam through Noah and Abraham, while the wisdom books repeatedly confront the brevity of man’s days (Job 14:1–2; Psalm 90:3–6). Each generation inherits the world from those before it and leaves it to those after it, yet none can secure lasting meaning through earthly achievement alone.

Psa 90:5-6 You sweep them away in their sleep; they are like…

Job 14:1-2 “Man, who is born of woman, is short of days…”…

1Pe 1:24-25 All flesh is like grass, and all its glory…

but the earth remains forever.
God established the earth as the enduring stage of human history and the arena of His covenant purposes. Its continued order testifies to the faithfulness and sovereignty of its Creator (Psalm 104:5; Genesis 8:22). The earth outlasts individual lives, but it is not independent of God or exempt from His final purposes. Scripture promises not the abandonment of creation but its renewal in the new heaven and new earth, where God’s redeemed people will dwell with Him forever (Isaiah 65:17; Revelation 21:1–4).

Gen 8:22 As long as the earth endures, seedtime and harvest…

Psa 119:90 Your faithfulness continues through all generations;…

Psa 104:5 He set the earth on its foundations, never to be moved.

Ecclesiastes 1:5

The sun rises
God appointed the sun to govern the day at creation (Genesis 1:14–19). Its daily appearance testifies that the sun is not a deity, as many surrounding nations believed, but a creature obedient to its Creator. Each morning also recalls the steadfast mercies of God, which are new every day (Lamentations 3:22–23).

Mat 5:45 that you may be sons of your Father in heaven.…

Jas 1:11 For the sun rises with scorching heat…

Mal 4:2 But for you who fear My name, the sun of righteousness…

and the sun sets;
The close of each day marks the end of human labor and calls attention to the limits God has placed upon life under the sun. Evening brings the need for rest, reflecting the pattern established when God rested after His creative work (Genesis 2:2–3).

Psa 104:19 He made the moon to mark the seasons…

Luk 4:40 At sunset, all who were ill with various diseases…

Mrk 1:32 That evening, after sunset, people brought to Jesus…

it hurries back to where it rises.
The sun’s unceasing circuit illustrates the repetitive order of a fallen world. Generation after generation labors beneath the same heavens, yet no human effort can break the cycle of mortality and futility. Creation itself awaits the redemption God will finally accomplish, when He makes all things new (Romans 8:20–23; Revelation 21:1–5).

Psa 19:6 it rises at one end of the heavens and runs its circuit…

Psa 50:1 A Psalm of Asaph. The Mighty One, God the LORD…

Psa 113:3 From where the sun rises to where it sets…

Ecclesiastes 1:6

The wind blows southward,
The south wind brought heat to the land of Israel, as Job 37:17 observes. Its movement was visible evidence that creation operates under patterns established by God, yet remains beyond human control.

Job 37:17 You whose clothes get hot when the land lies hushed…

Act 27:13 When a gentle south wind began to blow…

Sng 4:16 Awake, O north wind, and come, O south wind.…

then turns northward;
The wind does not follow a path that people can direct or finally comprehend. Scripture often associates the north with invading powers and judgment, yet every direction remains under the Lord’s command (Jeremiah 1:14; Psalm 135:7).

Pro 25:23 As the north wind brings forth rain, so…

Jer 49:36 I will bring the four winds against Elam…

Zec 2:6 “Get up! Get up! Flee from the land of the north,”…

round and round it swirls,
The restless circulation of the wind reflects the recurring motions of the world described throughout Ecclesiastes. Human labor, generations, and natural processes continue without providing lasting gain apart from the fear of God.

Psa 107:25-29 For He spoke and raised a tempest that lifted…

Jon 1:4 Then the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea,…

Mrk 4:37-39 Soon a violent windstorm came up, and the waves…

ever returning on its course.
God preserves the order of His creation, even when its patterns appear wearisome and repetitive. The wind’s appointed course exposes the limits of human wisdom and directs the reader toward the One who gives purpose beyond life’s cycles.

Psa 19:6 it rises at one end of the heavens and runs its circuit…

Job 36:27-28 For He draws up drops of water which distill the rain…

Gen 8:22 As long as the earth endures, seedtime and harvest…

Ecclesiastes 1:7

All the rivers flow into the sea,
Rivers such as the Jordan, Tigris, and Euphrates sustained the lands and peoples of the ancient Near East. Their continual movement illustrates the ordered patterns God established in creation. Yet the ceaseless flow also supports Ecclesiastes’ larger observation that life under the sun often appears repetitive, with generations and human labors passing on without bringing lasting fulfillment.

Gen 1:9-10 And God said, “Let the waters under the sky be gathered…

Psa 104:8-9 the mountains rose and the valleys sank…

yet the sea is never full;
The sea receives the waters of countless rivers without reaching a permanent fullness. Evaporation returns its waters to the atmosphere, preserving the cycle God ordained. Human desire similarly remains unsatisfied when it seeks ultimate meaning in possessions, achievement, or pleasure; “the eye is not satisfied with seeing” (Ecclesiastes 1:8), and “the eye of man is never satisfied” (Proverbs 27:20).

Job 38:8-11 Who enclosed the sea behind doors when it burst forth…

Jer 5:22 Do you not fear Me?” declares the LORD……

Psa 33:7 He piles up the waters of the sea; He puts the depths…

to the place from which the streams come,
Water rises from the sea, gathers in clouds, falls as rain, and replenishes springs and rivers. The observable order of creation testifies to God’s sustaining providence. Psalm 104 celebrates the Lord as the One who sends springs into the valleys and provides water for His creatures.

Psa 104:10-13 He sends forth springs in the valleys; they flow…

Pro 8:24-29 When there were no watery depths, I was brought forth,…

Isa 41:18 I will open rivers on the barren heights,…

there again they flow.
The rivers continue their appointed course generation after generation. Creation faithfully obeys the boundaries and rhythms set by its Maker, while fallen humanity repeatedly pursues what cannot endure. The unchanging cycle points beyond earthly repetition to the need for the enduring work of God, whose word accomplishes His purpose as rain waters the earth (Isaiah 55:10–11).

Job 36:27-28 For He draws up drops of water which distill the rain…

Isa 55:10-11 For just as rain and snow fall from heaven…

Amo 5:8 He who made the Pleiades and Orion, who summons the waters……

Ecclesiastes 1:8

All things are wearisome, more than one can describe;
Life in a fallen world is marked by labor, frustration, and limits. Adam’s sin brought painful toil into human experience, and every achievement eventually reveals its inability to secure lasting rest. Human effort can fill the days without satisfying the soul. Christ alone gives the rest that labor, success, and pleasure cannot provide.

Ecc 12:12 And by these, my son, be further warned:…

Job 7:1-4 Is not man consigned to labor on earth?…

Psa 90:10 The length of our days is seventy years—or eighty…

the eye is not satisfied with seeing,
Created beauty and new experiences continually awaken desire, yet they cannot make the heart whole. Eve, meaning “life,” gazed upon the forbidden tree, showing how desire can be drawn beyond God’s command. The world offers endless sights, but lasting joy is found in the presence of God.

Pro 27:20 Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied; so the eyes of man…

Pro 30:15-16 The leech has two daughters: Give and Give.…

Ecc 6:7 All a man’s labor is for his mouth, yet…

nor the ear content with hearing.
Knowledge, stories, music, and human wisdom may increase, but they cannot answer the deepest needs of the heart. Solomon possessed unusual wisdom and access to every kind of learning, yet he recognized the limits of earthly understanding. God gives true wisdom, and Christ, the Word made flesh, reveals the truth that human inquiry cannot discover on its own.

Ecclesiastes 1:9

What has been will be again,
Human history repeatedly displays the same moral patterns: ambition, oppression, violence, idolatry, repentance, and rebellion. Kingdoms rise with confidence and fall through pride, as Babylon, Persia, Greece, and Rome each demonstrated. The Fall has left humanity subject to frustration, and apart from God, people continue to seek lasting satisfaction in what cannot endure.

Ecc 3:15 What exists has already been, and what will be…

2Pe 3:4 “Where is the promise of His coming?” they will ask.…

Luk 17:26 Just as it was in the days of Noah, so…

and what has been done will be done again;
New tools and cultures do not change the human heart. People still pursue power, wealth, pleasure, security, and reputation, often repeating the sins and failures of those before them. Labor remains marked by toil, as God declared after Adam’s sin, and human achievement cannot remove death or establish lasting righteousness.

Jdg 2:19 But when the judge died, the Israelites became corrupt…

Pro 26:11 As a dog returns to its vomit, so a fool repeats his folly.

2Ti 3:13 while evil men and imposters go from bad to worse…

there is nothing new under the sun.
Life viewed only within the boundaries of the fallen world offers no ultimate escape from sin, death, and repetition. Ecclesiastes exposes the limits of earthly wisdom and accomplishment so that people will fear God and receive His gifts rightly. God alone brings true newness through His saving work. In Christ, believers become new creations and await the coming kingdom, where the curse will be removed and the weary cycles of life under the sun will give way to eternal life in God’s presence.

Heb 13:8 Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.

Mal 3:6 Because I, the LORD, do not change, you descendants of Jacob…

Jas 1:17 Every good and perfect gift is from above…

Ecclesiastes 1:10

Is there a case where one can say, “Look, this is new”?
Solomon confronts humanity’s recurring confidence that novelty can satisfy the heart. Under the sun, inventions, ambitions, pleasures, and public achievements continually reappear in altered forms, yet they do not free mankind from sin, death, or the need for God. The Lord alone brings the decisive newness that human effort cannot produce, as seen in His promise of redemption and ultimately in the new creation through Christ.

Isa 43:19 Behold, I am about to do something new; even now it is coming.…

Isa 48:6-7 You have heard these things; look at them all.…

Rev 21:5 And the One seated on the throne said, “Behold,…

It has already existed in the ages before us.
The patterns of human nature remain remarkably constant across generations: people labor, seek recognition, pursue pleasure, build kingdoms, and face the same limits appointed by God. The regular cycles of seedtime and harvest testify to God’s enduring order in creation. Ecclesiastes humbles pride in human originality while directing attention beyond temporary achievements to the eternal God, whose purposes stand unchanged and whose grace makes believers new in Christ.

Deu 32:7 Remember the days of old; consider the years long past…

Job 8:8-10 Please inquire of past generations and consider…

Ecclesiastes 1:11

There is no remembrance of those who came before,
Human reputation and achievement rarely endure beyond a few generations. Even in Israel, where genealogies preserved covenant identity and inheritance, most individual lives passed from memory. Solomon’s observation exposes the limits of earthly legacy: people return to dust, as God declared after the Fall (Genesis 3:19), and their names often fade with them. Psalm 103:15–16 similarly compares human life to grass that flourishes briefly and then is gone.

Ecc 2:16 For there is no lasting remembrance of the wise,…

Job 18:17 The memory of him perishes from the earth,…

Psa 103:16 when the wind passes over, it vanishes…

and those yet to come will not be remembered by those who follow after.
Future generations will repeat the same pursuit of significance, yet they too will eventually be forgotten. The Preacher does not deny the value of faithful work, family, or wisdom; he denies that any of them can secure lasting remembrance apart from God. Life is like a mist that soon vanishes (James 4:14), but the fear of the Lord gives wisdom that reaches beyond death (Proverbs 9:10).

With Wisdom Comes Sorrow

Ecclesiastes 1:12

I, the Teacher,
The Teacher (often translated as Preacher or Qoheleth in Hebrew, meaning one who gathers or assembles) speaks as Israel’s wisest king, traditionally identified as Solomon, David’s son. God granted Solomon exceptional wisdom, understanding, and breadth of knowledge, drawing rulers and visitors from many lands to hear him. His searching reflections arise from a life that had tested wealth, pleasure, work, learning, power, and achievement.

Ecc 12:9 Not only was the Teacher wise, but he also taught…

1Ki 4:29-34 And God gave Solomon wisdom, exceedingly deep insight…

1Ki 10:23-24 So King Solomon surpassed all the kings of the earth…

was king over Israel
Solomon ruled the united tribes of Israel during a season of peace, prosperity, and international influence. His royal authority gave him access to every earthly advantage, yet Ecclesiastes records that no possession or accomplishment can supply the lasting meaning found only in fearing God and keeping His commands.

1Ki 4:1 So King Solomon ruled over Israel,

1Ki 6:1 In the four hundred and eightieth year after the Israelites…

1Ch 29:23 So Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king…

in Jerusalem.
Jerusalem stood as Israel’s political capital and spiritual center, where Solomon built the temple for the worship of the LORD. From the city associated with Solomon’s glory, the Teacher exposes the limits of human glory. Jesus Christ, greater than Solomon, later taught in Jerusalem and revealed the wisdom and kingdom of God in their fullness.

1Ki 11:42 Thus the time that Solomon reigned in Jerusalem…

2Ch 9:30 Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years.

Ecclesiastes 1:13

And I set my mind to seek and explore by wisdom
Solomon deliberately devoted his God-given understanding to investigate life’s deepest questions. His wisdom, granted by the Lord beyond that of other kings (1 Kings 3:12), did not lead him to passive observation but to disciplined inquiry. Wisdom literature repeatedly calls God’s people to pursue understanding, for wisdom begins with the fear of the Lord and directs the heart toward righteous living (Proverbs 4:7; Proverbs 9:10).

1Ki 3:9 Therefore give Your servant an understanding heart…

Pro 2:3-6 if you truly call out to insight…

Jas 1:5 Now if any of you lacks wisdom, he should ask God,…

all that is done under heaven.
Solomon’s investigation embraced the full range of human life: labor, pleasure, government, injustice, wealth, worship, and death. “Under heaven” presents the world as the realm of human activity beneath God’s sovereign rule. Nothing people accomplish escapes His sight, yet no earthly achievement can supply the lasting purpose found only in Him.

Heb 4:13 Nothing in all creation is hidden from God’s sight;…

What a miserable task
The search exposed the painful limits of human understanding. Sin has subjected the world to frustration, and even great wisdom cannot unravel every mystery of providence or remove the certainty of death. Job likewise recognized that true wisdom cannot be discovered by human effort alone but belongs to God (Job 28:12–28).

God has laid upon the sons of men to occupy them!
God has assigned humanity the burden of living and laboring in a fallen world while longing for what lies beyond it. He has placed eternity in the human heart, though people cannot fully comprehend His work from beginning to end (Ecclesiastes 3:11). The burden is meant to humble human pride and turn seekers from self-reliance to the wisdom God gives generously to those who ask Him (James 1:5).

Ecclesiastes 1:14

I have seen all the things that are done under the sun,
Solomon (whose name means “peaceful”) surveyed human life with unusual opportunity, wisdom, wealth, authority, and experience. He examined work, pleasure, knowledge, wealth, achievement, and reputation, not as a detached observer but as one who possessed them in abundance. Life under the sun describes humanity viewed within the boundaries of a fallen world, where death ends every earthly accomplishment and no human effort can secure lasting gain.

Ecc 8:9 All this I have seen, applying my mind to every deed…

Ecc 8:17 Despite his efforts to search it out, he cannot find…

Ecc 4:1 I looked, and I considered all the oppression under the sun.…

and have found them all to be futile,
Human endeavors cannot bear the weight of ultimate meaning when separated from the fear of God. Success may be real, labor may be necessary, and wisdom may be valuable, yet none can overcome sin, death, or the judgment to come. Psalm 39:5–6 likewise portrays human life as brief and restless, while James 4:14 compares it to a mist that quickly disappears.

Ecc 2:17 So I hated life, because the work that is done…

Ecc 2:26 To the man who is pleasing in His sight, He gives wisdom…

Rom 8:20 For the creation was subjected to futility,…

a pursuit of the wind.
Efforts to obtain permanent satisfaction from temporary things resemble trying to seize the wind: the pursuit consumes strength but yields no lasting possession. Solomon’s conclusion does not condemn faithful work or earthly blessings; it exposes the emptiness of making them the final purpose of life. Lasting purpose is found in receiving life as God’s gift, obeying Him, and living in light of eternity.

Ecclesiastes 1:15

What is crooked cannot be straightened,
Solomon recognizes that human wisdom cannot undo the brokenness introduced by sin. The fallen world is marked by injustice, decay, death, and disordered desires that no human effort can fully repair. Creation remains subject to frustration and bondage to corruption until God completes its redemption (Romans 8:20–22). Isaiah’s promise that crooked places will be made straight finds its ultimate fulfillment in the coming reign of the Lord through Christ (Isaiah 40:3–4).

Human understanding cannot measure every deficiency or supply what the soul lacks. Wealth, labor, learning, and pleasure cannot produce righteousness, lasting meaning, or freedom from death. God alone provides the wisdom and salvation humanity cannot attain for itself. In Christ are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge, and in Him believers receive the fullness that human striving cannot obtain (Colossians 2:3, 10).

Ecclesiastes 1:16

I said to myself,
The Teacher begins with private reflection, recounting a personal search for meaning through wisdom. His inward examination prepares for the sober conclusion that human insight, though valuable, cannot by itself answer life’s deepest problems.

Solomon received extraordinary wisdom from God after asking for discernment to govern Israel (1 Kings 3:9–12). His learning, judgment, writings, and understanding of creation became known throughout the surrounding nations (1 Kings 4:29–34). Yet even wisdom granted by God could not make fallen life free from sorrow, injustice, or death.

Solomon had tested wisdom through careful attention to human conduct, government, labor, pleasure, wealth, and the order of creation. His experience confirms that wisdom is better than folly, but it also exposes the limits of human understanding. Wisdom becomes saving only when it leads a person to fear God, and its fullest revelation is found in Christ, “the wisdom of God” (1 Corinthians 1:24).

Ecclesiastes 1:17

So I set my mind to know wisdom and madness and folly;
Solomon deliberately examined both wise living and the irrational, sinful patterns that oppose it. Having received exceptional wisdom from God, he could discern that folly does not offer an alternative path to meaning. His investigation exposed the limits of human understanding and the disorder produced when people live without the fear of the Lord.

Neither wisdom nor folly can give lasting mastery over life’s unanswered questions, death, or God’s sovereign purposes. Human wisdom remains valuable because it guides righteous living, yet it cannot save or satisfy apart from God. Ecclesiastes ultimately directs the reader to fear God and keep His commandments, while the New Testament reveals Christ as the wisdom of God and the One who gives eternal meaning to life.

Ecclesiastes 1:18

For with much wisdom comes much sorrow,
Solomon’s God-given wisdom enabled him to perceive the injustice, mortality, and frustration that mark life east of Eden. Knowledge exposes the vanity of trusting wealth, pleasure, achievement, or human power to give lasting meaning. Wisdom is therefore a gift, but it also brings the burden of seeing clearly a world damaged by sin.

and as knowledge grows, grief increases.
Greater understanding reveals the limits of human control and the certainty of death, producing grief over what cannot be repaired by human effort. Ecclesiastes does not condemn wisdom; it directs the reader beyond human wisdom to fear God and keep His commandments (Ecclesiastes 12:13). Creation still groans under sin’s curse, yet Christ, the wisdom of God, secures the redemption for which the world waits.
