Tried but Trusting

Psalm 86:1

A prayer of David.
David approaches the covenant God with the honesty of a servant who knows both the privileges and burdens of belonging to Him. Although David was Israel’s king, he repeatedly recognized that earthly position could not secure his life, forgive his sin, or preserve him from enemies. The prayer fits the pattern of a man after God’s own heart, not because David was without failure, but because he continually turned back to the Lord in faith and repentance.

Incline Your ear, O LORD,
David appeals to YHWH, the God who had chosen Israel, made covenant promises to Abraham, and established David’s house. He asks the Lord to bend near in mercy, confident that the God of heaven hears the cries of those who depend on Him. David had experienced such deliverance throughout his life, from his conflicts with Saul to the dangers that continued even after he became king.

God had set apart those who fear Him and seek to walk in His ways (Psalm 4:3). The godly may face danger, oppression, and weakness, yet they belong to the God who watches over His people.

David grounds his prayer in the revealed character of God. The Lord is good in all His ways and merciful toward repentant sinners. He forgave Israel repeatedly despite their rebellion and declared Himself “compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in loving devotion and faithfulness” (Exodus 34:6). God’s forgiveness does not ignore sin; it rests ultimately on the sacrifice He would provide through Jesus Christ, whose death bore the guilt of all who trust in Him.

Psalm 86:8

O Lord, there is none like You among the gods,
David worships the God of Israel as the only true and sovereign Lord, incomparable to every idol and every claimed deity of the nations. The gods of surrounding peoples could neither create, save, nor rule with righteousness. Israel’s deliverance from Egypt, the covenant at Sinai, and the Lord’s preservation of His people displayed His authority over all earthly powers and spiritual opposition. The confession accords with the command to have no other gods before Him and anticipates the day when every knee will bow before Jesus Christ.

nor any works like Yours.
The Lord alone made heaven and earth, governs history, judges evil, and redeems sinners. His mighty acts include the exodus, His faithful provision for Israel, and the resurrection of Jesus Christ, the decisive victory over sin and death. No human power, idol, or ruler can accomplish what God has done or fulfill what He has promised.

Psalm 86:9

All the nations You have made
God is the Creator and rightful ruler of every people, language, and kingdom. Israel’s covenant relationship with the Lord never meant that the surrounding nations lay outside His authority or care. Genesis 10 records the spreading of humanity into nations after the flood, and God’s purpose from the call of Abraham included blessing all the families of the earth through his offspring. The nations belong to God because He made them, and they remain accountable to Him.

David looks beyond Israel’s present conflicts to the day when the nations will acknowledge the Lord’s universal kingship. Prophets later described the same hope: peoples would go up to worship the God of Jacob and learn His ways. Bowing before the Lord signifies willing recognition of His supreme authority. The promise finds its center in Jesus Christ, before whom every knee will bow and every tongue confess that He is Lord.

Psalm 86:11

Teach me Your way, O LORD,
The psalmist approaches Yahweh as the covenant God who alone can direct His people in righteousness. God’s way is the path shaped by His character, commands, and purposes, not merely a route through difficult circumstances. Moses urged Israel to walk in all the way the Lord commanded so that they might live and prosper (Deuteronomy 5:33). Such instruction is needed because human understanding is limited and the heart is prone to wander.

Divine teaching is sought for obedient living. God’s truth is revealed in His word and displayed in His faithfulness; it establishes the standard by which His people are to order their lives. Walking in that truth means persevering in trust, integrity, worship, and submission to God. Jesus affirmed the same foundation when He prayed, “Your word is truth” (John 17:17).

Israel’s history repeatedly showed the danger of such divided allegiance, especially when its kings and people turned toward foreign gods. The Lord calls His people to love Him wholly, with all the heart, soul, and strength (Deuteronomy 6:5).
