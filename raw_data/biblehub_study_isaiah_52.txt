Deliverance for Jerusalem

Isaiah 52:1

Awake, awake, clothe yourself with strength, O Zion!
God summons His afflicted people to rise from the humiliation of exile and receive the strength He provides. Zion is called to live in keeping with the redemption God is about to accomplish. The repeated command carries urgency: Jerusalem must no longer remain as one defeated, for the Lord is coming to restore her. Believers likewise stand in God’s strength, clothed with His armor for spiritual conflict (Ephesians 6:10–17).

Put on your garments of splendor, O Jerusalem, holy city!
Mourning, captivity, and disgrace give way to honor, beauty, and restored holiness. Jerusalem belongs to the Lord and is to display the glory of the God who dwells among His people. Isaiah later describes this renewal as garments of salvation and a robe of righteousness (Isaiah 61:10). The restoration from Babylon would preview a greater fulfillment when the Lord establishes His holy city in righteousness.

Foreign armies had entered Jerusalem, defiled its sanctuary, and carried its people away. God promises a future in which His city will not be subject to such oppression or pollution. The immediate hope points beyond the return from exile, since Jerusalem later endured further invasion and destruction. Complete fulfillment awaits the holy city described in Revelation, where nothing unclean will enter and God’s redeemed people dwell securely in His presence (Revelation 21:27).

The Servant Exalted

Isaiah 52:13

Behold, My Servant will prosper;
Isaiah introduces the Servant whose mission fulfills the saving purpose of God. His prosperity is the certain success of His obedience, suffering, atoning death, and victory over sin and death. Jesus Christ fulfilled this mission perfectly, accomplishing redemption for all who believe in Him. Like Joseph and David, the Servant advances God’s purpose through adversity, yet His work surpasses theirs by bringing salvation to the nations.

Jesus was lifted up on the cross to bear the sins of His people, then raised from the dead and received into heaven. The language also recalls Isaiah’s vision of the Lord “high and lifted up” (Isaiah 6:1), revealing the Servant’s unique divine honor.

God’s public exaltation of the Servant declares His complete victory and approval. After His obedience unto death, Jesus was exalted to the Father’s right hand and given authority over all creation.
