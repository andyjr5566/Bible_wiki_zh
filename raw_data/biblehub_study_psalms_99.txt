The LORD Reigns!

Psalm 99:1

The LORD reigns;
Israel’s God rules with absolute authority over every people, king, power, and created thing. His kingship does not depend on Israel’s strength or on the rise and fall of earthly empires. Psalms 93 and 97 likewise proclaim His royal majesty, and the New Testament reveals the reign of God in Jesus Christ, the King of kings and Lord of lords (Revelation 19:16).

Psa 47:8 God reigns over the nations; God is seated on His throne…

let the nations tremble!
The peoples of the world are summoned to recognize the holiness and judgment of the true God. Israel lived among nations devoted to false gods and imperial rulers, yet the LORD alone governs history and will hold every nation accountable. Such trembling includes fearful awe before His justice and a rightful surrender to His rule (Isaiah 2:19–21).

He is enthroned above the cherubim;
The words recall the ark of the covenant in the Most Holy Place, where the LORD made His presence known above the mercy seat between the cherubim (Exodus 25:22). Though heaven cannot contain Him, He chose to dwell among His covenant people and receive atoning blood on the Day of Atonement. The cherubim testify to His unapproachable holiness, while the mercy seat points forward to reconciliation with God through Christ.

let the earth quake!
Creation itself stands subject to its Maker and responds to His appearing. Mount Sinai shook when the LORD descended in fire (Exodus 19:18), and the earth quaked at Christ’s death as the veil of the temple was torn (Matthew 27:51). God’s reign will ultimately bring every rebellious power down and establish His righteous kingdom over all the earth.

Psalm 99:2

Great is the LORD in Zion;
Zion, the city of David and the location of Solomon’s Temple, stood as the appointed center of Israel’s worship and the visible testimony of the LORD’s covenant presence among His people. From Zion the LORD revealed His holiness, received worship, and ruled over Israel. Psalm 48 likewise celebrates Mount Zion as the city of the great King. Zion also anticipates the future reign of Christ from Jerusalem and ultimately the heavenly Jerusalem, where God will dwell with His redeemed people.

Psa 48:1-2 A Psalm of the sons of Korah. Great is the LORD…

Psa 9:11 Sing praises to the LORD, who dwells in Zion;…

Isa 12:6 Cry out and sing, O citizen of Zion, for great among you…

He is exalted above all the peoples.
The God worshiped in Jerusalem is not a local deity confined to Israel’s land or history. He reigns over every nation, ruler, and people, and all the earth remains accountable to Him. Israel’s worship was meant to proclaim His glory among the nations, as the prophets foretold that peoples would come to Zion to learn His ways. Christ’s universal dominion will be openly acknowledged when every knee bows and every tongue confesses that Jesus Christ is Lord.

Psalm 99:4

The mighty King loves justice.
The Lord reigns with absolute power, yet His authority is never arbitrary or oppressive. He delights in what is right and judges every person with perfect knowledge and impartiality. Israel’s earthly kings were charged to defend the weak, punish evil, and preserve righteousness, but only the Lord fulfills that calling without failure. His holy kingship anticipates Christ, the righteous King whose kingdom will finally remove all injustice.

You have established equity;
God has fixed the moral order by His own righteous character. He does not bend judgment for wealth, status, strength, or human influence. His law revealed to Israel the standards of fairness, integrity, and mercy that reflect His rule. Though human courts and rulers may fail, God’s judgments remain upright, and His promised kingdom will be marked by enduring peace and righteousness.

You have exercised justice and righteousness in Jacob.
The Lord acted among the descendants of Jacob through covenant, law, discipline, deliverance, and judgment. He rescued Israel from Egypt, gave His commandments through Moses, raised judges and kings, and sent prophets to confront oppression and idolatry. Israel’s repeated failures did not cancel God’s righteousness; they displayed the seriousness of His holiness and the faithfulness of His covenant purposes. Jesus Christ fulfills God’s righteousness perfectly and secures mercy for His people through His atoning death, while remaining the Judge who will establish justice throughout the earth.

Israel is summoned to publicly honor the covenant God who reigns above every earthly ruler. His greatness is not merely acknowledged in private devotion but proclaimed among His people, who belong to Him by His gracious redemption and covenant faithfulness.

God’s heavenly throne cannot be contained by creation, yet He graciously made His presence known among Israel at the ark and in the temple. Approaching His footstool pictures humble submission before the King whose authority extends over heaven and earth. Worship requires reverence rather than self-exaltation.

The LORD is utterly pure, righteous, and set apart from all that is sinful or created. His holiness both exposes human uncleanness and provides the basis for true worship through the atonement He has appointed. In Christ, believers draw near to the holy God with confidence, not because holiness has been diminished, but because Christ has secured cleansing by His sacrifice.

Psalm 99:6

Moses and Aaron were among His priests;
Moses served as God’s appointed mediator in the establishment of the covenant and performed sacred duties during Israel’s early worship. Aaron, Moses’ brother, became Israel’s first high priest and the head of the priestly line through which sacrifices and intercession were offered. Their leadership demonstrated that access to the holy God required obedience, atonement, and reverence.

Samuel was among those who called on His name.
Samuel faithfully sought the LORD in prayer, guided Israel as judge and prophet, and interceded for the nation during times of crisis. He called Israel away from idolatry, led the people in repentance, and anointed Saul and David according to God’s command. His life shows the necessity of leaders who depend on the LORD rather than human strength.

Psalm 99:7

He spoke to them from the pillar of cloud;
The pillar of cloud visibly marked the Lord’s presence with Israel throughout the wilderness journey (Exodus 13:21–22). From it He directed, protected, and addressed Moses and the people. The cloud both revealed God’s nearness and veiled His consuming glory, reminding Israel that their covenant God was holy yet personally involved in their life and history.

they kept His decrees
Moses, Aaron, and Samuel are remembered as faithful servants who received and obeyed God’s commands. Israel’s covenant at Sinai called for willing obedience, and the Lord’s decrees governed worship, justice, and national life. Their obedience did not earn God’s redemption, but it demonstrated faithfulness to the God who had redeemed them from Egypt.

The law ultimately points to Christ, who fulfilled it perfectly and establishes His people in the new covenant through grace and truth.

Psalm 99:8

O LORD our God, You answered them.
Moses, Aaron, and Samuel called upon the LORD in Israel’s decisive moments, and He heard them from the pillar of cloud. God answered Moses’ intercession after Israel’s idolatry, confirmed Aaron’s priestly service, and responded to Samuel’s prayers for the nation. The covenant God was not distant from His servants; He made His will known and acted for His people.

You were a forgiving God to them,
The LORD pardoned the sins of His people when they turned to Him, even amid grave rebellion in the wilderness. His forgiveness preserved Israel despite the golden calf, repeated complaints, and unbelief at Kadesh. The sacrifices and atonement prescribed under the law testified that forgiveness requires the removal of sin’s guilt, ultimately fulfilled in the sacrifice of Jesus Christ.

yet an avenger of their misdeeds.
Forgiveness did not cancel God’s holy discipline. Moses was barred from entering the promised land after dishonoring the LORD at Meribah, and Aaron also died before Israel crossed the Jordan because of that rebellion. God’s mercy never compromises His righteousness; He pardons repentant sinners while dealing seriously with sin. At the cross, God’s justice against sin and His mercy toward His people meet fully in Christ.

Psalm 99:9

Exalt the LORD our God
Israel was called to acknowledge the LORD as the supreme King, worthy of public praise and wholehearted allegiance. “Our God” recalls His covenant commitment to the people He redeemed and set apart as His own.

Mount Zion, where the temple stood in Jerusalem, was God’s appointed earthly center of worship under the old covenant. Israel came there with sacrifices, prayers, and praise, not to honor the mountain itself, but to bow before the God who had placed His name there. Through Christ, believers now draw near to God in spirit and truth, yet the reverence demanded by God’s presence remains unchanged.
