Avoiding Immorality

Proverbs 5:1

My son, pay attention to my wisdom;
A father addresses his child with personal affection and moral urgency. Proverbs repeatedly presents wisdom as instruction passed within the covenant household, preparing the next generation to fear the Lord and walk uprightly. Wisdom includes more than knowledge; it directs the heart toward righteous choices, especially where temptation threatens life and faithfulness.

Pro 2:2 if you incline your ear to wisdom and direct your heart…

Pro 8:33 Listen to instruction and be wise; do not ignore it.

Pro 4:1 Listen, my sons, to a father’s instruction;…

incline your ear to my insight,
Receiving godly instruction requires humble, deliberate attention. Israel was called to hear the Lord’s words and teach them diligently to its children (Deuteronomy 6:4–9), and the same posture of listening governs the pursuit of wisdom. Insight enables a person to discern truth from deception and to apply God’s standards in daily decisions.

Proverbs 5:2

that you may maintain discretion
Solomon calls his son to receive wisdom as a safeguard against the seductive counsel that follows. Discretion is the disciplined ability to recognize moral danger, weigh consequences, and choose the path that honors God. It guards the heart from impulsive desires and preserves integrity in a world where adultery and deceit promise pleasure while bringing ruin. Such wisdom is received through reverence for the Lord and careful attention to His instruction.

Pro 2:11 Discretion will watch over you,…

Pro 3:21 My son, do not lose sight of this: Preserve sound judgment…

Pro 1:4 To impart prudence to the simple and knowledge…

and your lips may preserve knowledge.
Wisdom held in the heart should govern the words spoken by the mouth. Faithful speech preserves knowledge by confessing what is true, warning against sin, and passing God’s instruction to others. Parents, teachers, and leaders bear a particular responsibility to communicate truth clearly, as Israel was commanded to teach God’s words diligently to each generation (Deuteronomy 6:6–7). Jesus Christ perfectly revealed divine truth, and His followers are called to let their speech reflect the knowledge of God rather than the corrupting counsel of sin.

Proverbs 5:3

Though the lips of the forbidden woman drip honey
Honey was a valued delicacy in the ancient world, associated with sweetness, abundance, and delight. The forbidden woman is an adulteress, a woman who invites a man outside God’s covenant design for marriage. Her appeal is real at the outset; sexual sin often presents itself as pleasurable, private, and harmless. Proverbs warns that desire must be governed by the fear of the Lord rather than by immediate appetite. James likewise teaches that sinful desire, when entertained, gives birth to sin and ultimately leads to death (James 1:14–15).

Pro 7:21 With her great persuasion she entices him;…

Pro 2:16-17 It will rescue you from the forbidden woman…

and her speech is smoother than oil,
Oil conveyed richness, refreshment, and blessing, making its smoothness an apt picture of flattering and persuasive speech. The adulteress uses words to lower resistance, conceal consequences, and make rebellion appear desirable. Psalm 55:21 describes similar treachery: words smoother than butter can conceal conflict and violence. Wisdom guards the heart against such deception, delivering the righteous from evil paths and from those who speak perversely (Proverbs 2:10–12).

Psa 55:21 His speech is smooth as butter, but war is in his heart.…

Rom 16:18 For such people are not serving our Lord Christ…

Proverbs 5:4

in the end she is bitter as wormwood,
Sin often presents sweetness at the beginning, but its end brings sorrow, guilt, broken trust, and judgment. Wormwood, known for its intense bitterness, became a biblical image of affliction and calamity (Lamentations 3:15; Revelation 8:11). The forbidden woman’s promises conceal the bitter harvest awaiting those who depart from God’s wisdom.

Her path wounds deeply and leaves lasting damage in the lives of those who yield to temptation. A double-edged sword cuts with lethal force, portraying the ruin sin brings to conscience, family, reputation, and fellowship with God. What appears pleasurable for a moment can become an instrument of destruction unless sin is forsaken and mercy is sought from the Lord.

Proverbs 5:5

Her feet go down to death;
The adulterous woman’s path ends in death because sexual sin rejects God’s design for covenant faithfulness and leads the sinner away from wisdom, purity, and life. Her feet portray a chosen course of conduct, not an accidental fall. Sin often promises pleasure and freedom, yet it draws those who follow it toward ruin. Romans 6:23 confirms that the wages of sin is death, while God gives eternal life through Jesus Christ.

Sheol denotes the realm of the dead and conveys the grave’s darkness and finality. The warning is direct: yielding to seduction places a person on a road that ends in judgment and destruction. Proverbs repeatedly contrasts the way of wisdom, which leads to life, with the way of folly, which leads to death (Proverbs 14:12). God’s call to purity is therefore a call to turn from the path of death and walk in the fear of the Lord.

The adulteress described in Proverbs 5 is unconcerned with the way that leads to righteousness, covenant faithfulness, and the blessing of God. Her appeal conceals a settled rejection of God’s wisdom. Solomon contrasts her course with the path commended throughout Proverbs, where the fear of the Lord gives life, protection, and moral clarity.

Her life has no secure direction because sin cannot provide a lasting foundation. Sexual immorality promises pleasure while producing deception, broken trust, shame, and death. Jesus likewise warned that only the life built on His words will stand when judgment comes (Matthew 7:24–27).

Proverbs 5:8

Keep your path far from her;
Wisdom requires more than resisting sin at the moment of crisis; it directs a person to avoid the road that leads toward it. The adulterous woman represents sexual temptation and covenant unfaithfulness, both of which draw the heart away from the fear of the Lord. A person’s path is formed through repeated choices, companions, places, and habits. Guarding that path protects the heart before desire gains opportunity to grow. Joseph demonstrated such wisdom when he refused Potiphar’s wife and fled her presence rather than entertaining her invitation (Genesis 39:7–12). Believers are likewise commanded to flee sexual immorality, recognizing that sin promises pleasure while bringing spiritual ruin and grievous consequences (1 Corinthians 6:18).

Pro 4:14-15 Do not set foot on the path of the wicked…

do not go near the door of her house,
The warning reaches even to the boundary of temptation. Approaching her door would place the young man where secrecy, desire, and opportunity could weaken his resolve. In the ancient world, entering a private house implied access to intimate fellowship; therefore, remaining away from the door meant refusing the first steps toward adultery. Sin commonly develops through desire that is entertained and then acted upon (James 1:14–15). Wisdom establishes clear boundaries before temptation becomes urgent, preserving marital faithfulness, personal integrity, and fellowship with God.

Proverbs 5:9

Sexual sin drains strength, honor, resources, and usefulness that God intends for faithful service, marriage, and family. The forbidden woman of Proverbs 5 does not enrich a man’s life; her path leads to disgrace and the loss of what he was called to steward for God’s glory.

Sin eventually becomes a harsh master, consuming years through shame, broken relationships, financial loss, and spiritual ruin. Those who pursue forbidden paths may be exploited by others without mercy, but Christ calls His people into freedom, purity, and wholehearted devotion to God.

Proverbs 5:10

lest strangers feast on your wealth,
Sexual sin brings consequences that reach beyond private desire. In Israel’s agrarian society, wealth consisted of land, livestock, harvests, and the fruit of years of labor, all intended to sustain one’s household and preserve its inheritance. Adultery could lead to financial loss, public disgrace, retaliation, or the transfer of resources to those outside the family. What a man gathered for his wife, children, and future could be squandered through unfaithfulness. Deuteronomy 28:33 similarly warns that rebellion against God may result in others consuming the produce of one’s labor.

Ecc 6:2 God gives a man riches, wealth, and honor,…

and your labors enrich the house of a foreigner.
A life governed by illicit desire can place the reward of diligent work into another household rather than blessing one’s own. The adulterous relationship drains what should support covenant faithfulness, family stability, and godly service. The warning also recalls Israel’s history, when disobedience brought oppression and foreign powers enjoyed the wealth of God’s people. Wisdom calls God’s people to guard their hearts and steward their resources so that their labor honors the Lord and strengthens the household He has entrusted to them.

Proverbs 5:11

At the end of your life
The warning looks beyond the moment of temptation to the final outcome of a course marked by sexual sin. Hidden indulgence eventually produces public loss, broken relationships, diminished strength, and a conscience burdened by regret. Life’s end exposes the emptiness of pleasures pursued apart from the fear of the Lord.

Sorrow replaces the promised excitement when the sinner recognizes what has been surrendered. Israel groaned under bondage in Egypt; here groaning portrays the bondage created by foolish desire. Wisdom calls for repentance before regret becomes the defining sound of one’s later years.

God created marital intimacy as a good gift within covenant faithfulness, while adultery consumes the person who yields to it.

The adulterer’s ruin is intensified by the memory that he rejected repeated instruction. Parents, elders, and wise teachers were entrusted with forming Israel’s young people in the fear of the Lord and obedience to His commands. God’s people were commanded to teach His words diligently within the home and throughout daily life (Deut. 6:4–9). Like Israel in Jeremiah’s day, the sinner refused counsel and followed the stubbornness of his own heart (Jeremiah 7:24).

Moses prepared Joshua for leadership, Elijah trained Elisha, and Paul instructed Timothy, showing God’s pattern of passing truth and wisdom from faithful servants to those willing to learn. The warning in Proverbs 5 reveals that ignoring godly counsel leaves a person vulnerable to sin’s deception and its destructive consequences.

Proverbs 5:14

I am on the brink of utter ruin
This phrase captures a moment of deep personal crisis and impending disaster. In the context of Proverbs, it reflects the consequences of ignoring wisdom and engaging in immoral behavior, particularly adultery. The “brink of utter ruin” suggests a point of no return, emphasizing the severity of the situation. Biblically, this aligns with the warnings found throughout Proverbs about the dangers of folly and sin (Proverbs 1:32-33). The imagery of a “brink” suggests a precipice, a place where one can fall into destruction, reminiscent of the warnings in Deuteronomy 30:19 about choosing life over death.

Pro 6:32-33 He who commits adultery lacks judgment;…

Pro 7:22-23 He follows her on impulse, like an ox going to the slaughter…

Pro 2:18-19 For her house sinks down to death, and her tracks…

in the midst of the whole assembly.”
This phrase indicates public exposure and shame. In ancient Israelite culture, community and reputation were of utmost importance. Being disgraced “in the midst of the whole assembly” implies a loss of honor and standing within the community. This public aspect of sin’s consequences is echoed in other scriptures, such as Numbers 32:23, which warns that sin will find you out. The assembly, or congregation, was central to Israelite life, serving as a place for worship, judgment, and communal decision-making. The fear of public disgrace would have been a powerful deterrent against sin, highlighting the communal nature of sin and its repercussions.

Proverbs 5:15

Marriage is to be guarded as a covenant of exclusive love. In a land where stored water was precious, a cistern represented provision carefully preserved for one household. A husband and wife are called to cherish the intimacy, affection, and joy God has entrusted to them rather than seeking fulfillment outside the marriage covenant. Malachi condemns treachery against “the wife of your youth,” affirming that God Himself witnesses and upholds marital faithfulness (Malachi 2:14–16).

A well supplied fresh water for life and refreshment, portraying the vitality and satisfaction God intends within a faithful marriage. Rather than viewing marital love as restrictive, Proverbs presents it as a God-given source of continuing delight. The image also recalls the living water Christ gives, which alone satisfies the deepest need of the soul and leads to eternal life (John 4:14).

Marital intimacy is portrayed as a life-giving spring meant to be enjoyed within the covenant of one man and one woman. In a land where water was scarce and protected, allowing a private spring to spill into the streets pictured waste, vulnerability, and the loss of what should be guarded. Sexual unfaithfulness profanes a gift God designed for marriage and exposes the sinner to ruin.

Public squares belonged to commerce, dispute, and public scrutiny, not to the private faithfulness of a marriage. Proverbs 5:18

The fountain pictures the God-given source of life within marriage, including the fruitfulness and blessing of a faithful household. In a dry land, a dependable spring was a precious provision, sustaining life and bringing prosperity. So marital love and the children that may come from it are to be received as gifts under God’s favor, not treated as objects for selfish indulgence. The image stands against the forbidden waters of adultery described in the surrounding passage. God blesses the covenant union He established from the beginning and calls His people to honor it with purity.

Faithfulness requires more than avoiding adultery; it involves cherishing one’s spouse with gratitude, delight, and steadfast love. Malachi likewise condemns treachery against “the wife of your youth” and calls husbands to remain faithful before the Lord (Malachi 2:14–15). Such covenant love also points beyond itself to Christ’s faithful love for His Church (Ephesians 5:25–33).
