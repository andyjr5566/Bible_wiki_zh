Psalm 131:1

A song of ascents.
Psalm 131 belongs to Psalms 120–134, songs associated with Israel’s worship as pilgrims traveled up to Jerusalem for the appointed feasts. Jerusalem’s elevated setting made the journey an ascent in location, while worship called God’s people to draw near to Him in faith, humility, and dependence.

Psalm 131:2

Surely I have stilled and quieted my soul;
Having renounced proud ambitions and concerns beyond his calling, David deliberately rests his whole inner life before God. Peace does not arise from controlling circumstances but from trusting the Lord’s wisdom, goodness, and sovereign care. Prayerful dependence replaces restless self-assertion, as believers cast their anxieties on God and receive the guarding peace promised in Philippians 4:6–7.

like a weaned child with his mother,
A weaned child no longer cries merely for immediate nourishment but finds security in the mother’s presence. Weaning marked a meaningful transition in Israel’s household life, as seen in the celebration at Isaac’s weaning (Genesis 21:8). David’s image portrays faith that has learned contentment: God is sought not merely for His gifts, but for Himself.

like a weaned child is my soul within me.
David’s soul has become settled in humble trust rather than driven by craving, fear, or ambition. Such maturity does not lessen dependence on God; it deepens confidence in His faithful provision and timing. Jesus perfectly displayed this surrendered trust in the Father, especially in Gethsemane, where He submitted His will to the Father’s saving purpose.

Psalm 131:3

O Israel,
Israel stands before the Lord as the covenant people descended from Abraham, redeemed from Egypt, and bound to God through His promises and commands. David’s call gathers the nation into the humility modeled in the psalm: resting in God rather than pursuing self-exaltation or trusting earthly power.

put your hope in the LORD,
The LORD alone is Israel’s security, deliverer, and covenant-keeping God. Hope rests on His proven faithfulness in history and on His promises yet to be fulfilled, not on kings, armies, wealth, or human wisdom. Such hope leads God’s people to quiet dependence upon Him.
