Sanballat, the governor of Samaria, Tobiah the Ammonite official, and Geshem, an influential Arab leader, represented the surrounding powers threatened by Jerusalem’s renewal. Their hostility was not merely political; the restored city strengthened the covenant community and testified that God had not abandoned His people. Nehemiah rightly identified them as enemies because they consistently sought to hinder the work God had entrusted to Judah. God’s servants still face opposition when obedience, truth, and holy purposes expose rival loyalties and interests.

Completion of the Wall

Nehemiah 6:15

So the wall was completed
Nehemiah’s burden for Jerusalem’s broken defenses had become a finished work through prayerful leadership, careful planning, and the united labor of the people. The restored wall secured the city against hostile neighbors and visibly declared that God had not abandoned His covenant people. Opposition, intimidation, and internal hardship had not stopped the work because God strengthened those who served Him.

The speed of the rebuilding was extraordinary in light of the wall’s ruined condition and the continual threats from surrounding enemies. Families, priests, craftsmen, and civic leaders worked together at assigned sections, while guards remained watchful. The accomplishment demonstrated both the people’s resolve and the good hand of God upon Nehemiah’s mission, leading even Israel’s enemies to recognize that the work had been accomplished with divine help (Nehemiah 6:16).

The completed wall prepared Jerusalem for the sacred seventh month, when the people would gather to hear the Law, confess their sins, and renew their covenant commitment to the Lord (Nehemiah 8–10). Physical restoration became the setting for spiritual renewal, as a protected city again became a community ordered around the worship and Word of God.
