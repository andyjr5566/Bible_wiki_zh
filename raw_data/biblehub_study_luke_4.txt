The Rejection at Nazareth

Nazareth was an obscure Galilean village, yet it was the setting of Jesus’ hidden years and family life. He grew to maturity there under Joseph and Mary, sharing the ordinary labor, worship, and responsibilities of Jewish life. His return brought the One known locally as the carpenter’s son into the place that had watched Him grow up. Matthew connects His identification with Nazareth to the prophetic expectation that the Messiah would be despised and called a Nazarene (Matthew 2:23; Isaiah 53:3). Nathanael’s question, “Can anything good come out of Nazareth?” reflects the low regard in which the town was held (John 1:46), making Jesus’ ministry and glory all the more striking.

The synagogue served as the local center for prayer, Scripture reading, instruction, and community life. Though He was the Lord of the Sabbath, Jesus did not withdraw from the worshiping assembly. His faithfulness revealed perfect obedience to the Father and prepared the setting for His public declaration of His mission.

Jesus’ deliverance of the demonized man revealed power beyond anything the synagogue worshipers had witnessed. Exorcists were known in the ancient world, often employing formulas, objects, or prolonged rituals, but Jesus needed no such means. He rebuked the demon by His own command, and it obeyed. The event showed that the promised age of salvation had begun, fulfilling the hope of Isaiah 61:1 that the Lord’s Anointed would bring liberty to those held captive.

Jewish teachers commonly supported their instruction by appealing to earlier rabbis and traditions, but Jesus spoke as the One who possessed God-given authority in Himself. His words and works identified Him as the prophet like Moses promised in Deuteronomy 18:18–19, whose message demanded a response because it came from God.

Luke 4:40

At sunset,
The timing of this event is significant. The Jewish Sabbath ends at sunset, which means that people were now free to travel and carry burdens without breaking Sabbath laws. This context highlights the eagerness of the people to seek Jesus’ healing as soon as it was permissible. The setting sun also symbolizes the end of an era of waiting and the beginning of a new day of hope and healing through Christ.

This phrase underscores the widespread nature of sickness and suffering in the world, reflecting the fallen state of humanity. The mention of “various diseases” indicates that Jesus’ healing power was not limited to specific ailments but was comprehensive. This diversity of illnesses also points to the universal need for a Savior who can address all forms of human brokenness.
