Honest Scales

Ezekiel 45:10

You must use honest scales,
Israel’s rulers and people were required to conduct trade without fraud or favoritism. Accurate scales protected buyers, especially the poor and vulnerable, from exploitation by merchants who could manipulate weights for unjust gain. God’s holiness extends into ordinary economic life, requiring His people to reflect His righteousness in every transaction. Leviticus 19:36 and Proverbs 11:1 likewise condemn dishonest weights as an offense before the Lord.

a just ephah,
The ephah was the standard dry measure for grain and other goods, approximately twenty-two liters. A full and accurate ephah ensured that food and provisions were neither withheld nor overpriced through deceptive measurement. In the restored order described by Ezekiel, justice in worship, government, and commerce belonged together. Honest measures demonstrated that covenant life could not be separated from practical obedience.

The Lord who judges all injustice calls His people to deal truthfully, anticipating the perfect righteousness embodied by Jesus Christ.

Ezekiel 45:21

On the fourteenth day of the first month
Nisan 14, corresponding roughly to March or April, was the appointed day for Passover (Exodus 12:6; Leviticus 23:5). God established this date at the Exodus, making Israel’s deliverance from Egypt the beginning point of her religious calendar. In Ezekiel’s vision of restored worship, Passover remains a memorial of the Lord’s mighty redemption and faithfulness to His covenant.

The sacrifice pointed forward to Jesus Christ, “our Passover,” whose death delivers His people from sin and judgment (1 Corinthians 5:7). The observance in the restored temple order affirms that redemption by God’s provision remains central to true worship.
