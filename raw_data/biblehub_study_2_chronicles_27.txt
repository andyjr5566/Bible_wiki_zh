Jotham Reigns in Judah

Jotham had already exercised royal responsibility while Uzziah lived in isolation because of leprosy. As steward over the palace and governor of the people, he entered the throne with experience in Judah’s affairs and with the sober reminder that pride before the Lord brings judgment.

2 Chronicles 27:2

And he did what was right in the eyes of the LORD,
Jotham governed Judah in obedience to the Lord’s covenant and maintained the pattern of faithful rule seen in the better years of his father Uzziah. Divine approval measures a king not by political strength or building achievements, but by submission to God’s commands.

2 Chronicles 27:3

Jotham rebuilt the Upper Gate of the house of the LORD,
Jotham strengthened access to the temple, showing concern for the ordered worship of God in Judah. The Upper Gate was associated with the temple complex and its public ministry. Although Jotham did what was right before the LORD, he did not remove the high places (2 Kings 15:35), revealing that outward reforms could not by themselves turn the whole nation’s heart to God. The temple anticipated Christ, in whom believers receive true and permanent access to God.

and he worked extensively on the wall at the hill of Ophel.
Ophel lay on the southern slope of the temple mount, between the temple area and the City of David. By fortifying this vulnerable section of Jerusalem, Jotham protected both the city and its central place of worship. His work joined spiritual responsibility with faithful kingship: the people needed both a rightly maintained sanctuary and secure defenses. God remains the ultimate protector of His people, yet He calls leaders to act wisely and diligently in the responsibilities entrusted to them.

2 Chronicles 27:4

He also built cities in the hill country of Judah
Jotham strengthened Judah’s rugged central highlands, where fortified settlements could guard roads, protect agricultural communities, and resist invasion. His building program continued the policies of Uzziah, who fortified Jerusalem and developed rural strongholds (2 Chronicles 26:9–10). These cities increased Judah’s security and stability during a period when surrounding nations threatened its borders.

and fortresses and towers in the forests.
Jotham also secured wooded and less-settled regions with defensive posts and watchtowers. Such structures gave warning of enemy movement, protected valuable timber and grazing lands, and extended royal authority beyond the major cities. Judah’s strength under Jotham reflected orderly leadership under God, though lasting security depended not merely on walls and towers but on faithfulness to the Lord, who is the true refuge of His people (Psalm 18:2).

2 Chronicles 27:5

Jotham waged war against the king of the Ammonites and defeated them,
Jotham extended Judah’s strength eastward against Ammon, a nation descended from Lot through Ben-ammi (Genesis 19:38). Ammon had long opposed Israel, including during the days of Jephthah (Judges 11). Jotham’s victory secured Judah’s borders and demonstrated the Lord’s blessing upon a king who prepared his ways before Him (2 Chronicles 27:6).

2 Chronicles 27:6

So Jotham grew powerful
Jotham’s strength included military security, national stability, and the capacity to build and govern wisely. He continued much of the prosperity Judah had known under Uzziah, strengthening Jerusalem’s defenses, building cities in the hill country, and prevailing over the Ammonites. Unlike his father, whose pride brought divine judgment, Jotham did not allow success to draw him away from God.

because he ordered his ways
Jotham deliberately directed his life and reign according to God’s standards. His leadership was marked by purposeful obedience rather than self-reliance. Although the people of Judah continued in corrupt practices (2 Chronicles 27:2), the king himself maintained a faithful course before God, showing that personal integrity remains essential even amid widespread spiritual decline.

His reign illustrates that lasting strength is rooted in walking faithfully before the Lord.

Jotham’s death ended a reign marked by personal faithfulness and national strength. Rest with the fathers describes his death and his joining the generations of Judah’s covenant history.
