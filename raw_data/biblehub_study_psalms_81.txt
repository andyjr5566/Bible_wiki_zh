God’s people are summoned to joyful, audible praise. From Moses’ song after the Red Sea deliverance to the psalms associated with David and temple worship, singing proclaimed the Lord’s saving acts and instructed the congregation in His faithfulness. Believers continue this ministry of praise through psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, addressing one another with truth and giving thanks to God.

Psalm 81:4

God commanded Israel, the collective identity of the twelve tribes as God's chosen people, appointed worship through Moses, ordering the nation’s calendar around remembrance, thanksgiving, and covenant renewal. The trumpet call gathered the tribes before the Lord and marked sacred times such as the new moon and annual feasts. Israel’s worship was not a human custom but an act of obedience to the God who redeemed them from Egypt. These observances preserved the memory of God’s deliverance and taught each generation to depend upon Him.

The festivals pointed Israel beyond daily provision to the Lord’s grace and redemption, themes fulfilled supremely in Christ, whose sacrifice accomplishes the deliverance foreshadowed in Israel’s worship.

Deliverance came not because Israel deserved it, but because the Lord is merciful, faithful, and mighty to save. Their rescue became the foundation for their worship and obedience.

Psalm 81:9

There must be no strange god among you,
The God who delivered Israel from Egypt required exclusive covenant loyalty. Israel lived among nations devoted to Baal, Asherah, Molech, and other idols, and repeated compromise with such worship brought spiritual corruption and divine discipline. The command stands in harmony with the first commandment: “You shall have no other gods before Me” (Exodus 20:3). No created power, cultural custom, political alliance, or human desire may take the place belonging to the Lord alone.

nor shall you bow to a foreign god.
Bowing before an idol signified submission, trust, and worship. Such allegiance denied the uniqueness of the Lord, who alone is Creator, Redeemer, and King. Israel’s history showed the ruin produced by idolatry, yet God continued to call His people back to wholehearted devotion. Jesus affirmed the same demand when He declared that God must be loved with all the heart, soul, and mind (Matthew 22:37). True worship belongs to God alone and is fulfilled through Christ, who brings His people to worship the Father in spirit and truth (John 4:24).

Psalm 81:15

Those who hate the LORD
This phrase refers to individuals or nations that are in opposition to God and His commandments. In the biblical context, “hate” signifies a strong rejection or rebellion against God’s authority. Historically, this could include pagan nations surrounding Israel, such as the Philistines or Canaanites, who worshipped other gods and often opposed Israel. This enmity is not just a personal feeling but a spiritual stance against God’s sovereignty. In a broader sense, it can also apply to anyone who rejects God’s ways, as seen in Romans 8:7, where the mind governed by the flesh is hostile to God.

Feigning obedience implies a superficial or insincere compliance with God’s commands. This can be seen in the history of Israel, where certain kings or people outwardly followed religious practices but their hearts were far from God, as noted in Isaiah 29:13. This hypocrisy is condemned throughout Scripture, as God desires genuine devotion and obedience from the heart. The concept is also echoed in the New Testament, where Jesus criticizes the Pharisees for their outward religiosity but inward corruption (Matthew 23:27-28).
