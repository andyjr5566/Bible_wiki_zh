I Will Exalt You, My God and King

Psalm 145:1

A Psalm of praise.
Psalm 145 is the only psalm bearing this title, setting it apart as a sustained hymn of adoration. Its ordered, alphabetic structure reflects deliberate worship, calling God’s people to consider His greatness, mercy, faithfulness, and kingdom.

Of David.
David wrote as Israel’s king while confessing that he himself lived under a greater King. The shepherd who had faced Goliath, fled from Saul, and received God’s covenant promises knew both God’s protecting care and His sovereign rule.

I will exalt You, my God and King;
David’s worship is personal and submissive. God is “my God,” known and trusted by His servant, and “King,” possessing rightful authority over David, Israel, and all creation. True praise begins by honoring God above every earthly power and desire.

God’s name reveals His holy character, mighty works, and covenant faithfulness. David commits himself to continual praise because God’s worth does not diminish with changing circumstances. The worship begun by faith in this life anticipates the everlasting praise of God’s redeemed people.

Psalm 145:2

Every day I will bless You,
David pledges continual worship rather than occasional gratitude. Israel’s morning and evening offerings testified that devotion to the Lord belonged to the rhythm of each day (Exodus 29:38–42). Such blessing rises from trust in God’s daily faithfulness and joins the call to pray without ceasing (1 Thessalonians 5:17).

Psalm 145:3

Great is the LORD
David declares God’s supreme majesty, revealed in creation, in His righteous rule over the nations, and in His faithful care for His covenant people. The LORD who delivered Israel through the Red Sea, established David’s throne, and preserved His people is greater than every earthly king and every false god.

and greatly to be praised;
God’s greatness demands wholehearted worship. Israel gathered to bless His name with sacrifices, songs, and public thanksgiving, while every believer is called to honor Him in word and conduct. Praise rightly centers on God’s character and mighty works rather than human achievement.

His greatness is unsearchable.
No creature can measure the fullness of God’s power, wisdom, holiness, or mercy. Job recognized that no one can discover the limits of the Almighty, and Paul later worshiped the unsearchable depths of God’s wisdom and knowledge. God has truly made Himself known through His works, His Word, and ultimately His Son, yet His infinite majesty remains beyond complete human comprehension.

Psalm 145:4

One generation will commend Your works to the next,
Israel’s faith was preserved through deliberate instruction in the home and community. Parents were commanded to teach God’s words diligently to their children, speaking of them throughout daily life (Deuteronomy 6:6–7). The Exodus, the wilderness provision, the covenant at Sinai, and the entrance into Canaan were to be remembered as acts of the living God, not distant legends. David portrays praise as a sacred trust: those who have witnessed God’s faithfulness must make Him known to those who follow. Paul carries the same pattern into the church, charging Timothy to entrust apostolic teaching to faithful people who would teach others also (2 Timothy 2:2).

and will proclaim Your mighty acts—
God’s mighty acts reveal His sovereign rule over creation, nations, kings, and false gods. Creation, the flood, the deliverance from Egypt, the judgment of Pharaoh, and the conquest of Canaan displayed His power and faithfulness to His promises. Israel’s proclamation of these works distinguished Yahweh from the powerless idols worshiped among the nations. The resurrection of Jesus Christ stands as the climactic public declaration of God’s power, defeating death and confirming Christ as Lord and Messiah (Acts 2:24, 36). Each generation is called to testify that the God who acted in history remains worthy of trust and praise.

David directs attention beyond earthly royalty to the Lord’s incomparable kingship. God’s majesty is displayed in His sovereign rule over creation, His holiness, and His saving acts for His people. Isaiah saw the Lord enthroned in holiness, while John beheld heaven’s worship centered on the One seated upon the throne. No human glory can equal the radiance, authority, and purity belonging to God.

And I will meditate on Your wondrous works.
David deliberately remembers the works by which God reveals His character: creation, providence, judgment, deliverance, and covenant faithfulness. Israel’s history supplied abundant reasons for such meditation, including the exodus, the preservation of God’s people, and His continual provision. Believers likewise behold God’s greatest saving work in Jesus Christ, whose death and resurrection reveal divine power, mercy, and victory over sin and death.

Psalm 145:6

They will proclaim the power of Your awesome deeds,
God’s people are to speak publicly of His mighty acts, preserving the memory of His works from generation to generation. Israel proclaimed His deliverance at the Red Sea, His provision in the wilderness, and His power in bringing down Jericho. Such works revealed that the Lord alone rules over creation, nations, and history. The apostles likewise testified that Jesus’ miracles, death, and bodily resurrection displayed God’s saving power (Acts 2:22–24). God’s deeds call forth reverent fear and give His people a witness before the nations (Psalm 96:3).

and I will declare Your greatness.
David joins the corporate testimony with personal praise. No worshiper may leave the proclamation of God’s majesty to others alone. The Lord’s greatness includes His holiness, power, faithfulness, wisdom, and steadfast love, all perfectly revealed in His Son. Jesus Christ is the image of the invisible God, in whom all the fullness of deity dwells (Colossians 1:15, 19). Declaring God’s greatness honors Him and summons others to recognize the King whose kingdom endures forever.

Psalm 145:7

They will extol the fame of Your abundant goodness
God’s people publicly remember and celebrate His generous acts of mercy, provision, deliverance, and covenant faithfulness. Israel knew His goodness in the Exodus, the wilderness provision, the gift of the land, and His repeated patience toward a rebellious nation. God proclaimed Himself to Moses as merciful, gracious, and abounding in steadfast love and truth (Exodus 34:6). His goodness is not limited by human deserving but flows from His holy character.

God’s righteousness assures His people that He always acts rightly, judges evil faithfully, and keeps every promise. His justice is cause for joy because He defends the oppressed and establishes what is true. The righteousness revealed throughout Israel’s history reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, who perfectly fulfilled God’s law and became the righteousness of all who believe in Him (Romans 3:22).

Psalm 145:8

The LORD is gracious and compassionate,
David praises the covenant God who freely gives mercy to those who do not deserve it and who regards human suffering with tender concern. The words recall the LORD’s self-revelation to Moses after Israel’s idolatry with the golden calf (Exodus 34:6). Although Israel had broken covenant faithfulness, God preserved His people and continued His redemptive purposes. Jesus revealed this same divine character as He forgave sinners, healed the afflicted, and welcomed the helpless.

His patience never denies His holiness or removes the certainty of judgment for unrepentant evil. At the cross, God’s righteous wrath against sin was borne by Christ, opening the way for forgiveness and reconciliation.

Psalm 145:9

The LORD is good to all;
God’s goodness flows from His unchanging character and is displayed throughout His providential care of the world. He gives life, seasons, food, sun, and rain to all people, including those who do not acknowledge Him (Matthew 5:45; Acts 14:17). David’s praise rejects the pagan notion of distant or unpredictable deities; the LORD alone rules all things with steadfast goodness. His kindness toward all does not remove His righteous judgment, but it reveals His patience and calls sinners to repentance (Romans 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9).

His compassion rests on all He has made.
God watches over the creatures and people He formed, sustaining them according to His wise purposes. His mercy is especially evident in His covenant dealings with His people, yet His care extends over the whole created order. Jesus revealed this divine compassion in His ministry, healing the afflicted, feeding the hungry, and caring for those who were helpless and scattered (Matthew 9:36). God’s compassion finds its fullest saving expression in the sending of His Son, through whom mercy is offered to all who turn to Him in faith.

Psalm 19:1 declares that the heavens proclaim God’s glory, while Romans 8:19–22 anticipates creation’s deliverance from the curse. The coming kingdom will bring the full harmony for which creation groans, and every creature will join in honoring the One who made and redeemed all things (Revelation 5:13).

Jesus announced that kingdom’s arrival, revealed its character through His teaching and works, and taught His disciples to pray for its full coming. Psalm 145:17

The LORD is righteous in all His ways
God’s character is perfectly just, and every act of His providence, judgment, mercy, and rule accords with His holiness. Moses proclaimed that the Lord is faithful, without iniquity, just, and upright (Deuteronomy 32:4). Even when His ways are beyond human understanding, He never acts wrongly or breaks His covenant promises. His righteous rule provides the standard for His people, who are called to seek righteousness and walk in obedience. God’s justice is revealed fully in Jesus Christ, whose sinless life and atoning death uphold God’s righteousness while providing righteousness to all who believe (Romans 3:21–26).

let every creature bless His holy name
David’s worship expands from his own voice to the whole created order. Every living being belongs to the Lord who made and sustains all things, and all creation ultimately exists for His glory. Psalm 148 similarly summons the heavens, the earth, animals, rulers, and peoples to praise the Lord. God’s holy name reveals His perfect character and His separation from all sin and created limitation. The universal call anticipates the day when every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, and in the sea will honor the Father and the Lamb (Revelation 5:13).

forever and ever.
The praise of the Lord has no end because the Lord Himself is eternal and His kingdom endures through every generation. David’s closing confession reaches beyond present worship to the everlasting reign of God, fulfilled in Christ, whose dominion will never pass away. The redeemed will bless God’s name eternally in the new creation, where His servants will see His face and serve Him without interruption (Revelation 22:3–4).
