Joel 2:32

And everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will be saved;
God extends salvation to all who turn to Him in repentant faith, without distinction of nation, status, or past sin. Calling on the LORD involves trusting His mercy, seeking His help, and openly identifying with Him in worship and obedience. Peter applied these words to the outpouring of the Spirit at Pentecost and to salvation through Jesus Christ (Acts 2:21, 38–39). Paul likewise declared that all who call on the Lord Jesus will be saved (Romans 10:13).

Psa 116:4 Then I called on the name of the LORD: “O LORD, deliver…

for on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be deliverance,
Jerusalem, the place of the Temple and the earthly center of Israel’s worship, would be preserved by God amid the judgments associated with the day of the LORD. The promise reaches beyond temporary rescue, anticipating the Messiah’s saving work and the future restoration of Zion under God’s righteous reign. Those who flee to the LORD find refuge when human strength and earthly security fail.

as the LORD has promised,
Deliverance rests on God’s covenant faithfulness rather than human merit. The God who redeemed Israel from Egypt, preserved His people through exile, and fulfilled His promise of a Savior will accomplish all He has spoken. His promises remain certain even when judgment reveals the depth of human rebellion.

among the remnant called by the LORD.
God preserves a people for Himself through judgment and adversity. The remnant includes those whom He graciously calls to faith, beginning with faithful Israel and extending through Christ to believing Jews and Gentiles. Salvation is received by all who call on the Lord, yet every true believer stands secure because the LORD has first called them into His redemptive purpose.
