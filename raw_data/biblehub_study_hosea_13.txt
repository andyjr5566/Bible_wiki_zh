Israel’s national life began with the LORD’s mighty deliverance from Egyptian bondage. He redeemed them, led them through the wilderness, gave them His law, and established His covenant with them. Surrounded by nations devoted to Baal and other false gods, Israel was not permitted to divide its worship or trust among competing powers. God’s saving purpose reaches its fullness in Jesus Christ, through whom forgiveness and eternal life are given, for “there is no other name under heaven given to men by which we must be saved” (Acts 4:12).

Israel’s existence depended upon His faithful care, not their strength or faithfulness. Death and Resurrection

Hosea 13:9

You are destroyed, O Israel,
Israel’s coming ruin was the fruit of persistent rebellion against the Lord. The Northern Kingdom had embraced idolatry, rejected prophetic warnings, and trusted political alliances rather than God. Assyria destroyed Samaria in 722 BC, carrying many Israelites into exile. Their physical collapse revealed a deeper spiritual apostasy: the people had abandoned the covenant God who had redeemed them.

Sin was not merely a failure to keep religious commands; it was personal resistance against the God who had chosen, delivered, and sustained Israel. The nation violated its covenant obligation to worship the Lord alone, pursuing Baal and other gods while seeking security from foreign powers. Opposition to God brought the very judgment from which His covenant faithfulness had long protected them.

The Lord had been Israel’s helper from the Exodus onward, giving deliverance, provision, protection, and a land of inheritance. Israel’s tragedy was to reject the only One able to save while turning to powerless idols and unreliable nations. God remains the true help of His people, and His saving purpose reaches its fullness in Jesus Christ, who delivers sinners from judgment and restores those who return to Him.
