Ascribe Glory to the LORD

Psalm 29:1

A Psalm of David.
David, Israel’s shepherd-king, wrote worship that arose from both public leadership and personal dependence on God. His psalms reveal the Lord as sovereign over creation, faithful to His covenant people, and worthy of praise in every circumstance.

Psa 18:1 For the choirmaster. Of David the servant of the LORD…

Psa 3:1 A Psalm of David, when he fled from his son Absalom.…

Psa 63:1 A Psalm of David, when he was in the Wilderness…

Ascribe to the LORD,
David summons worshipers to acknowledge openly what belongs to God alone. Praise does not add glory to the LORD; it rightly declares the glory He eternally possesses. The divine name YHWH recalls God’s covenant faithfulness to Israel and His unchanging character.

1Ch 16:28 Ascribe to the LORD, O families of the nations,…

Psa 68:34 Ascribe the power to God, whose majesty is over Israel,…

Deu 32:3 For I will proclaim the name of the LORD.…

O heavenly beings,
The call reaches beyond earth to the angelic host that serves before God’s throne. Even the mighty inhabitants of heaven stand beneath the Creator and join in honoring Him. Heaven’s worship establishes the pattern for God’s people on earth.

Psa 89:6 For who in the skies can compare with the LORD?…

Job 38:7 while the morning stars sang together…

ascribe to the LORD glory and strength.
Glory belongs to the LORD because His presence reveals His holiness, majesty, and sovereign rule. Strength belongs to Him because all power is His, whether displayed in creation, judgment, deliverance, or redemption. Jesus Christ reveals the fullness of God’s glory and power, for in Him the fullness of deity dwells bodily.

Psalm 29:2

Ascribe to the LORD the glory due His name;
The LORD’s name reveals His character, covenant faithfulness, power, and holiness. Israel lived among nations that credited rain, fertility, victory, and kingship to false gods, yet Psalm 29 declares that all glory belongs to the Creator alone. The God who revealed Himself to Moses as “I AM” is eternally self-existent and wholly worthy of honor. David’s call accords with the worship later commanded in 1 Chronicles 16:29 and anticipates the full revelation of God’s glory in Jesus Christ, the eternal Son who made the Father known.

1Ch 16:29 Ascribe to the LORD the glory due His name;…

Psa 96:8 Ascribe to the LORD the glory due His name;…

Isa 42:8 I am the LORD; that is My name! I will not yield My glory…

worship the LORD in the splendor of His holiness.
God’s holiness displays His absolute purity and exalted majesty. Worship must therefore be marked by reverence, submission, and joyful awe rather than casual familiarity or divided loyalty. The holiness celebrated by the seraphim in Isaiah’s vision belongs to the LORD alone, and His people are summoned to honor Him as the One set apart from all sin and every rival. Through Christ, believers may draw near to God with confidence, yet they do so in spirit and truth, recognizing that the God who welcomes His people remains glorious and holy.

Psalm 29:3

The voice of the LORD is over the waters;
The psalmist portrays the LORD as reigning over the turbulent seas, which often represented chaos and untamable power in the ancient world. The God who brought order to the deep at creation rules every force that threatens human security. His sovereignty over the waters also recalls the Red Sea, where He made a path of deliverance for Israel and judged Egypt’s pursuing army.

Jer 10:13 When He thunders, the waters in the heavens roar;…

Rev 14:2 And I heard a sound from heaven like the roar of many waters…

Psa 77:16-19 The waters saw You, O God; the waters saw You…

the God of glory thunders;
Thunder reveals the fearful majesty of the God whose glory filled Sinai when He gave His covenant law to Israel. Creation itself bears witness to His power, and His voice cannot be dismissed as distant or uncertain. The LORD speaks with authority that both exposes human frailty and calls His people to reverent worship.

the LORD is heard over many waters.
No ocean, storm, nation, or distance limits the reach of the LORD’s rule. The roaring waters cannot overpower His voice; rather, they serve as the setting in which His greatness is displayed. Psalm 29 directs God’s people to rest in the certainty that the King over all creation remains present and mighty amid every overwhelming force.

Psalm 29:4

The voice of the LORD is powerful;
The God who spoke creation into being rules over all He has made. Psalm 29 portrays His voice as sovereign over the raging waters, forests, wilderness, and nations; no force of nature can resist His command. At Sinai, the Lord’s voice came with thunder and awe, revealing His holiness and authority to Israel. Jesus displayed that same divine authority when He rebuked the wind and sea, and creation obeyed Him.

Psa 68:33 to Him who rides upon the highest heavens of old;…

Psa 18:13 The LORD thundered from heaven; the voice of the Most High…

the voice of the LORD is majestic.
God’s voice reveals the splendor of the King who reigns above the flood and sits enthroned forever. The heavens and the earth bear witness to His glory, yet His majesty is especially known when He makes Himself known through His word. The heavenly worship described in Revelation anticipates the day when every creature will acknowledge the Lord’s reign and give Him glory.

Psalm 29:5

The voice of the LORD breaks the cedars;
God’s voice carries the authority that created the world and sustains it. Cedars represented the height of natural strength, durability, and majesty; yet they cannot withstand His command. The image also warns that human pride, security, and power are fragile before the God who rules all creation.

Exo 19:16-19 On the third day, when morning came, there was thunder…

Psa 18:13-15 The LORD thundered from heaven; the voice of the Most High…

the LORD shatters the cedars of Lebanon.
Lebanon’s celebrated forests supplied timber for palaces and for Solomon’s temple (1 Kings 5:6; 6:15–18). By naming these renowned cedars, David portrays the LORD as sovereign over the most impressive resources and powers of the nations. God humbles what exalts itself against Him, while His kingdom alone stands unshaken (Isaiah 2:12–13).

Psalm 29:6

He makes Lebanon skip like a calf,
God’s voice shatters Lebanon’s prized cedars and shakes its mighty mountain range as easily as a young calf leaps in play. Lebanon represented strength, grandeur, and the resources of kings; its cedars supplied Solomon’s Temple and royal buildings (1 Kings 5:6). Yet no natural power or human glory can stand unmoved before the Lord who rules creation.

Psa 114:4 the mountains skipped like rams, the hills like lambs.

Nam 1:5 The mountains quake before Him, and the hills melt away;…

and Sirion like a young wild ox.
Sirion was the Sidonian name for Mount Hermon (Deuteronomy 3:9), the lofty, snow-covered mountain marking Israel’s northern region. Its movement like a vigorous young wild ox portrays the irresistible force of God’s voice. The mountains, forests, waters, and wilderness all answer to their Creator, declaring that His dominion reaches over every land and every nation.

Psalm 29:8

The voice of the LORD shakes the wilderness;
God’s thunderous voice reaches even the barren and untamed places of the earth. The wilderness, associated with Israel’s years of testing and dependence upon God, is not beyond His rule. The Lord who spoke creation into being also commands the forces of nature, bringing fear to the rebellious and assurance to those who trust Him.

Exo 19:16-18 On the third day, when morning came, there was thunder…

Psa 68:8 the earth shook and the heavens poured down rain…

Jdg 5:4-5 O LORD, when You went out from Seir, when You marched…

the LORD shakes the Wilderness of Kadesh.
Kadesh-barnea stood near the southern boundary of Canaan and became a defining place in Israel’s history. There Israel refused to enter the land after the report of the spies, bringing judgment upon that generation (Numbers 13–14). The shaking of Kadesh recalls God’s holy presence amid human unbelief and rebellion. No place of failure, desolation, or uncertainty lies outside the reach of His authority.

Psalm 29:9

The voice of the LORD twists the oaks
The mighty trees of the forest, celebrated for their strength and endurance, yield before the Lord’s command. Psalm 29 portrays the LORD as ruling the storm over the waters, Lebanon, the wilderness, and every force that men regard as unshakable. His voice does not merely influence creation; it governs it. The God who spoke heaven and earth into existence still exercises absolute authority over all He has made.

Job 37:4-5 Then there comes a roaring sound; He thunders…

and strips the forests bare.
The storm driven by the LORD leaves even dense forests exposed and defenseless. Nothing in creation can conceal itself from His power or resist His judgment. Isaiah likewise pictures the LORD cutting down the lofty forest as He humbles human pride and removes false security (Isaiah 10:33–34). The devastation described in the psalm does not portray chaos beyond God’s control, but creation responding to its King.

And in His temple all cry, “Glory!”
God’s people answer the display of His majesty with worship. The temple was the appointed place where Israel gathered before the LORD, acknowledging His holiness, kingship, and covenant faithfulness. The cry of “Glory!” recognizes that all power belongs to Him alone. Heaven gives the same response before God’s throne, where the redeemed and angelic hosts continually honor the One who created and sustains all things (Revelation 4:8–11).

Psalm 29:10

The LORD sits enthroned over the flood;
The God who judged the ancient world through the flood of Noah remains sovereign over the waters He released and over the creation He preserved. The raging flood cannot unsettle His throne. He rules above every force that appears chaotic or destructive, and He faithfully remembers those who trust Him, as He remembered Noah in the ark. The waters that overwhelmed the wicked world also displayed God’s righteousness, power, and mercy.

Gen 7:17-20 For forty days the flood kept coming on the earth…

Psa 93:3-4 The floodwaters have risen, O LORD; the rivers…

Job 38:8-11 Who enclosed the sea behind doors when it burst forth…

the LORD is enthroned as King forever.
God’s kingship did not begin with any earthly kingdom and will not end when human rulers pass away. His reign extends over every generation, nation, and crisis. The permanence of His throne assures His people that judgment, redemption, and the final restoration of creation rest in the hands of the eternal King. Jesus Christ, the promised Son of David, will openly manifest that everlasting reign when every enemy is placed beneath His feet.

Psa 10:16 The LORD is King forever and ever; the nations perish…

Psalm 29:11

The LORD gives His people strength;
The God whose voice shakes the wilderness and rules over the flood supplies His covenant people with the strength needed to stand under His rule and carry out His purposes. Moses, Joshua, David, and the prophets served not by natural ability alone but by the power God provided. The weary receive renewed strength from the Lord, who does not grow faint or weary (Isaiah 40:29–31).

Psa 68:35 O God, You are awesome in Your sanctuary;…

Isa 40:29 He gives power to the faint and increases the strength…

Eph 3:16 I ask that out of the riches of His glory…

the LORD blesses His people with peace.
The Lord grants more than relief from conflict; He establishes wholeness, security, and fellowship with Himself. Israel’s peace depended upon God’s faithful presence and obedient trust in His covenant. Jesus Christ brings the fullest realization of that peace by reconciling sinners to God through His death and resurrection (Ephesians 2:14–18). His people may live with settled confidence because the King who governs the storm also keeps them in His care.
