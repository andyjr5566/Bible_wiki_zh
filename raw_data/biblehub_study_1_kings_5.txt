Their number reflects the careful order required for a work of such magnitude and holiness. God’s house was not built carelessly; its construction proceeded under accountable leadership and according to the plans God had given through David.

who supervised the workers.
These overseers maintained order, assigned labor, and ensured that the work met the demands of the temple project. Their responsibility illustrates the biblical pattern that great undertakings require faithful stewardship. As the temple was prepared as the place where God would manifest His name among Israel, those entrusted with its work bore a serious duty. The ordered service surrounding the temple also anticipates the care required among God’s people, where leaders are called to watch over those committed to their charge.

to quarry large, costly stones
The temple was built with materials worthy of the holy name of God. Great stones provided strength, while costly stones displayed the honor Israel owed to the Lord who had given them the land, kingdom, and peace necessary for the work. The expense and labor testified that worship was not treated as a casual matter.

to lay the foundation of the temple
A secure foundation established the place God chose for His name to dwell among His people. The work fulfilled the covenant promise that David’s son would build a house for the Lord’s name (2 Samuel 7:12–13). The temple’s foundation also points forward to Christ, the sure cornerstone upon whom God builds His spiritual house (Ephesians 2:20).

Skilled workers cut and prepared the stones so that they would fit together with precision and permanence. The care given to the hidden foundation showed that the temple’s beauty rested upon strength, order, and obedience. Believers likewise are described as living stones, being built together into a spiritual house through Christ (1 Peter 2:5).

Israel and Tyre joined their resources under the peaceful alliance between Solomon and Hiram. Hiram supplied skilled Phoenician craftsmen, long renowned for work in cedar, stone, and large-scale building projects, while Solomon organized Israel’s labor force for the work God had appointed.

The men of Gebal, or Byblos, came from a Phoenician city known for its craftsmen and maritime trade. Their participation brought specialized skill to the temple project and showed that the house built for the name of the Lord received the finest labor and materials available.

quarried the stone
Large, carefully selected stones were cut from the hills of the land for the temple foundation and structure. The durability of the stone suited a house intended to stand as the central place of Israel’s worship, though God Himself could not be contained within any earthly building.
