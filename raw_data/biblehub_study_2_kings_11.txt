2 Kings 11:1

Athaliah, daughter of Ahab and Jezebel, had entered Judah’s royal house through her marriage to Jehoram. Ahaziah’s death during Jehu’s judgment on Ahab’s house removed her son from the throne and exposed the kingdom to a violent struggle for power. Rather than mourning or seeking the Lord, Athaliah acted in keeping with the ruthless legacy of her parents and the idolatrous influence she had brought into Judah.

Jehoiada Restores the Worship of the LORD

2 Kings 11:17

Then Jehoiada made a covenant between the LORD and the king and the people
Jehoiada formally renewed Judah’s covenant allegiance to the Lord after Athaliah’s idolatrous rule and the preservation of Joash, the Davidic heir. The restored king, the priesthood, and the people were bound together under God’s authority and the obligations of His law. Like the covenant renewals under Moses and Joshua, this public act called the nation back to exclusive worship, obedience, and covenant faithfulness. Jehoiada’s mediating role anticipates Christ, the true Mediator who establishes the new covenant through His sacrifice.

that they would be the LORD’s people.
Israel’s identity rested upon God’s gracious choice and covenant claim, not merely upon national descent or royal succession. Judah could not faithfully belong to the Lord while tolerating Baal worship and the practices Athaliah had encouraged. The covenant demanded that the people reject idolatry and live as God’s holy possession, a calling later applied to believers as a people belonging to God.

Israel’s king was never autonomous; he ruled as the Lord’s servant for the good of God’s covenant people.

2 Kings 11:18

So all the people of the land went to the temple of Baal and tore it down.
The removal of Athaliah opened the way for Judah to reject the Baal worship promoted through the house of Ahab. The people acted together to destroy the center of a rival religion that had corrupted covenant life in Jerusalem. Their action upheld God’s command that Israel have no other gods and destroy places devoted to idolatry (Exodus 20:3–5; Deuteronomy 12:3). True renewal required more than crowning the rightful Davidic king; it required restoring exclusive loyalty to the LORD.

The thorough destruction of Baal’s altars and images showed repentance expressed in public action. Idolatry could not be accommodated alongside the worship of the LORD. Gideon had similarly torn down Baal’s altar when God began to deliver Israel from oppression (Judges 6:25–27). Judah’s cleansing anticipated later reforms under Hezekiah and Josiah, when faithful leaders removed the structures that continually tempted God’s people away from Him.

Jehoiada protected the temple after the nation’s covenant renewal, guarding its worship from desecration and preventing another attempt to seize or corrupt it. The Levites had long been entrusted with guarding the sanctuary because God’s dwelling place among His people was holy (Numbers 1:53). Jehoiada’s care for the temple joined spiritual reform with practical vigilance. Restored worship required both the removal of evil and the faithful preservation of what God had established.
