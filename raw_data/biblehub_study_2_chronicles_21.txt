Jehoram Reigns in Judah

2 Chronicles 21:1

And Jehoshaphat rested with his fathers
Jehoshaphat’s death ended a reign marked by devotion to the Lord, judicial reforms, instruction in God’s law, and relative peace and prosperity in Judah (2 Chronicles 17:3–9; 19:4–11). Resting with his fathers records his death and his place within the continuing line of Judah’s kings. Though Jehoshaphat made serious errors, especially in alliances with Ahab’s house, he sought the Lord in times of danger and called Judah to dependence upon God.

Jehoram’s brothers, the sons of Jehoshaphat,
Jehoram was the firstborn son of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah. Jehoshaphat was known for his piety and efforts to bring the people back to the worship of Yahweh. The mention of Jehoram’s brothers highlights the royal lineage and the potential for internal family conflict, which was common in royal families of the time. The brothers’ presence indicates a large royal family, which was typical for kings to ensure a strong dynasty.

The listing of Jehoram’s brothers by name emphasizes their importance and individuality. Azariah is mentioned twice, which may indicate either a common name or a textual variation. Names like Zechariah and Michael are significant as they are also borne by other notable biblical figures, suggesting a cultural or religious significance. The repetition of names within families was common, reflecting the importance of certain names and their meanings, often related to God’s attributes or actions.

Jehoram succeeded his father Jehoshaphat over Judah, beginning a reign that sharply departed from the spiritual direction of his father. Though he was mature in years and had likely observed Jehoshaphat’s administration, he chose the ways of the house of Ahab.
