The Sayings for King Lemuel

Proverbs 31:1

These are the words of King Lemuel—
Lemuel is not mentioned elsewhere in Scripture, though his name, meaning devoted to God, accords with the calling of every ruler to govern under God’s authority. Some have connected Lemuel with Solomon, but the text does not establish the identification. The chapter preserves royal wisdom that begins with reverence for God’s moral order and applies it to the responsibilities of leadership.

the burden that his mother taught him:
His mother entrusted him with a serious message from God, not merely personal advice. Her instruction demonstrates the honored place of faithful parental teaching in Israel, in keeping with Proverbs 1:8 and the command to honor father and mother. A king needed godly wisdom at home before he could exercise justice in public. Her counsel prepares Lemuel to resist corruption, protect the vulnerable, and rule with self-control and righteousness.

Proverbs 31:2

What shall I say, O my son?
King Lemuel’s mother speaks with the urgency of one entrusted by God to shape a son’s character. Her repeated appeal introduces instruction suited to a future ruler, whose private conduct would affect an entire people. Parents bear a solemn responsibility to teach God’s truth diligently, as Deuteronomy 6:6–7 commands, bringing wisdom to bear on the decisions and temptations their children will face.

The mother’s appeal rests on the intimate bond formed through birth. Her concern is neither distant nor merely political; she addresses the child for whom she has labored and whom she deeply loves. God forms life in the womb, as Psalm 139:13–14 declares, and parents are called to receive children as gifts from Him and to nurture them in His ways.

Lemuel had evidently been committed to God through vows or prayers made by his mother. Like Hannah, who dedicated Samuel to the Lord before his birth, she understood that her son belonged ultimately to God. Her instruction therefore presses him toward faithfulness, self-control, and righteous leadership, fitting for one whose life had been consecrated before the Lord.

Proverbs 31:3

Do not spend your strength on women
Sexual sin drains moral resolve, corrupts judgment, and diverts a ruler’s heart from faithful service to God. Solomon’s marriages to foreign women drew him into idolatry despite God’s warning to Israel’s kings (1 Kings 11:1–4; Deuteronomy 17:17). The instruction calls for purity, self-control, and devotion to the Lord rather than surrender to illicit desire (Proverbs 5:3–5; 7:24–27).

Kings possess influence, wealth, and authority that can be destroyed by unchecked appetites and ungodly alliances. David’s sin with Bathsheba brought lasting sorrow and disorder upon his household (2 Samuel 11–12). A ruler must guard his heart, because private compromise can injure families, weaken justice, and bring reproach upon God’s name.

The Virtues of a Noble Woman

Proverbs 31:10

A wife of noble character,
A woman marked by strength, wisdom, faithfulness, and practical skill brings honor to her household and reflects the fear of the Lord. Ruth received this same commendation from Boaz (Ruth 3:11), showing that such character was recognized in godly women who trusted the Lord and served others faithfully.

Such a woman is not measured by outward beauty, social standing, or wealth, but by enduring devotion to God and integrity in daily life. Proverbs repeatedly presents wisdom as a treasure to be diligently sought, and godly character is formed through that same reverent pursuit of the Lord.

She is far more precious than rubies.
Material riches can be lost, but a faithful wife enriches her family with lasting spiritual and moral strength. Her worth surpasses costly jewels because her life displays the wisdom that is more valuable than earthly treasure (Proverbs 8:11) and the imperishable beauty commended in 1 Peter 3:3–4.

Proverbs 31:11

The heart of her husband trusts in her,
Her husband rests securely in her faithfulness, wisdom, and integrity. Their marriage reflects the one-flesh covenant established in Genesis 2:24, where husband and wife are joined in loyal companionship and shared responsibility before God. Her character makes her a trustworthy steward of the home, not merely a helper in domestic matters. Such confidence strengthens the household and illustrates the covenant faithfulness God desires from His people, who are called to trust Him with all their heart (Proverbs 3:5). Marriage also foreshadows Christ’s union with His Church, His redeemed bride, whom He is preparing for Himself in holiness and love (Ephesians 5:25–27).

and he lacks nothing of value.
Her diligence, sound judgment, and fear of the Lord bring blessing rather than loss to her household. In Israel’s agrarian society, a wife’s management of food, clothing, trade, servants, and family resources directly affected the security and prosperity of the home. Her husband is not impoverished by her conduct but enriched by it in material provision, honor, peace, and godly legacy. The abundance described belongs to the broader biblical pattern in which obedience to God brings His care and blessing (Deuteronomy 28:1–14). Yet the greatest wealth is not earthly gain but treasure before God, where faithful service bears eternal fruit (Matthew 6:19–21).

Spinning and weaving were essential domestic labors in Israel, requiring skill, planning, and patience. Linen later becomes an image of purity and righteous deeds (Revelation 19:8), while the care of clothing reflects her practical love for those entrusted to her.

Her readiness reflects disciplined resolve, physical diligence, and strength rooted in fear of the Lord. She makes linen garments and sells them;
Skilled work with flax produced valuable clothing associated with dignity and prosperity. Her labor supplies her household and creates goods for sale, showing diligent stewardship of her abilities and resources. Linen also served in the priestly garments of Israel (Exodus 28:39–42) and later pictures the righteous deeds of the saints (Revelation 19:8), linking fine workmanship with purity and honorable service.

Proverbs 31:25

Strength and honor are her clothing,
Her character is openly marked by moral courage, dignity, and integrity. Strength equips her to labor faithfully, care wisely for her household, and meet hardship without surrendering to fear. Honor reflects the reverence for the Lord that governs her conduct and brings lasting esteem, for “the wise shall inherit glory” (Proverbs 3:35). Like clothing, these virtues are evident in daily life; they are not hidden claims but the visible fruit of a heart shaped by wisdom. Believers likewise are called to be strong in the Lord (Ephesians 6:10) and to put on the graces that reflect their new life in Christ (Colossians 3:12).

and she can laugh at the days to come.
Her joy before an uncertain future rests neither on ease nor on personal control, but on wise preparation and confidence in God’s providence. Having feared the Lord and ordered her life according to His wisdom, she is not ruled by anxiety over tomorrow. Her laughter conveys peaceful assurance rather than carelessness, answering the temptation to worry that Jesus addressed when He taught His disciples to trust their heavenly Father for each day’s needs (Matthew 6:34). Such confidence endures because the future belongs to God, whose faithfulness gives His people reason to face coming days with hope.

Like Solomon, who sought an understanding heart from God (1 Kings 3:9), she demonstrates wisdom that directs both conduct and counsel.

Proverbs 31:27

She watches over the affairs of her household
The godly wife gives careful attention to the needs, work, safety, and spiritual welfare of those entrusted to her care. In Israel, the household was a center of production, hospitality, instruction, and covenant life. Her faithful oversight reflects the stewardship God gave humanity over His creation (Genesis 1:28) and the wisdom that builds rather than tears down a home (Proverbs 14:1). Her care is active, ordered, and protective, seeking the good of her family before trouble gains ground.

and does not eat the bread of idleness.
Her provision does not rest on the labor of others while she neglects her own responsibilities. Bread represents daily sustenance, and she works diligently to help supply it. Her industry accords with the biblical warning that laziness leads to lack (Proverbs 10:4) and with the principle that those able to work should do so (2 Thessalonians 3:10). Her labor is not mere busyness; it is wise service offered for the flourishing of her household and the honor of the Lord.

Proverbs 31:31

Give her the fruit of her hands,
Her labor deserves its fitting return. The virtuous woman has faithfully managed her household, provided for those entrusted to her, and used her strength, skill, and resources with wisdom. Her “fruit” includes the visible provision produced by diligent work, the stability and blessing she brings to her family, and the enduring spiritual influence of a life ordered by the fear of the Lord. Scripture consistently teaches that people reap what they sow (Galatians 6:7), and faithful stewardship receives the Master’s approval (Matthew 25:21). Her reward is not empty praise but the rightful enjoyment and recognition of the good produced through her hands.

Psa 128:2 For when you eat the fruit of your labor,…

Isa 3:10 Tell the righteous it will be well with them…

Gal 6:7 Do not be deceived: God is not to be mocked.…

and let her works praise her at the gates.
City gates served as the center of public life in Israel, where elders heard legal cases, conducted business, and rendered judgments (Ruth 4:1–11). The woman’s godly character and practical service are so evident that her reputation reaches beyond her home into the community. Her works testify publicly to her wisdom, integrity, generosity, and devotion to the Lord. Such honor does not arise from self-promotion but from a life whose fruit cannot be hidden. Her example accords with the truth that genuine faith is demonstrated through obedient works (James 2:26), and it points ultimately to Christ, whose humble service and perfect obedience were followed by the Father’s exaltation of Him (Philippians 2:7–9).
