The scribes, experts in the Law, were present to observe Jesus’ actions. Their role was to ensure that religious practices adhered to the Law of Moses. The phrase indicates their immediate reaction to Jesus’ declaration of forgiveness, highlighting their role as religious authorities and their skepticism towards Jesus’ authority.

The Patches and the Wineskins

Matthew 9:16

No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment.
In a world where garments were costly and commonly repaired, Jesus drew on a familiar reality. A new piece of cloth would shrink when washed, pulling against the worn fabric around it. His teaching came in response to questions about fasting and showed that His presence introduced the promised time of fulfillment. The grace and joy of the kingdom could not be treated as a minor addition to religious routines. Jesus did not discard the Law and the Prophets; He fulfilled them and established the new covenant they anticipated.

Mrk 2:21 No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment.…

For the patch will pull away from the garment,
Attempting to bind the work of Christ to the old covenant’s ceremonial structures would create conflict rather than renewal. The old covenant had a God-given purpose: it exposed sin, guarded Israel, and pointed forward to the Messiah. Yet its sacrifices, priesthood, and ritual obligations could not contain the saving reality brought by Christ. Hebrews 8:13 speaks of the former covenant as becoming obsolete because God had established a better covenant through His Son.

Mrk 2:22 And no one pours new wine into old wineskins.…

Luk 5:37-38 And no one pours new wine into old wineskins.…

and a worse tear will result.
Mixing reliance on the law with trust in Christ’s finished work damages the message of grace. Paul warned that those seeking justification through the law had turned away from grace (Galatians 5:4). Jesus calls His disciples not merely to outward religious adjustment, but to the inward renewal made possible through faith in Him.

Matthew 9:17

Neither do men pour new wine into old wineskins.
In the ancient world, wineskins were commonly made from goat hides. A new skin could stretch as freshly pressed wine continued to ferment, but an old skin had become dry and brittle. Jesus used this familiar practice while answering questions about fasting, showing that His presence as the Bridegroom introduced the promised new age of God’s redemptive work. The grace and life of the new covenant could not be confined within the rigid religious traditions that had grown around the Law.

Mrk 2:22 And no one pours new wine into old wineskins.…

Luk 5:37 And no one pours new wine into old wineskins.…

Fermenting wine produces pressure, and an inflexible skin cannot withstand it. The Pharisees’ legalistic approach to righteousness could not receive the freedom, mercy, and inward renewal Jesus brought. Jesus did not abolish the Law and the Prophets but fulfilled them, bringing their purposes to their appointed goal (Matthew 5:17).

the wine will spill,
Attempting to combine the gospel of grace with a system of self-righteous rule keeping damages the truth of both. Jesus’ healings displayed both His compassion and His authority over the effects of sin in a fallen world. Blindness, lameness, disease, and suffering yielded to His command, fulfilling the hope of Isaiah 35:5–6 and pointing to the Messiah’s redeeming mission. These works confirmed the kingdom message and foreshadowed the final restoration in which death, pain, and sorrow will be no more (Revelation 21:4).

“The harvest is plentiful,
Scripture often portrays God’s gathering of people as a harvest. The crowds throughout Galilee represented many lives ready to hear the good news of the kingdom and respond to the Shepherd sent by God. Jesus saw not an inconvenience or an indistinct mass, but souls in need of truth, mercy, and salvation. The abundance of the harvest reveals both God’s gracious preparation and the urgency of the mission.
