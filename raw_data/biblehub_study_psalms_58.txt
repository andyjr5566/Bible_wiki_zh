God Judges the Earth

Psalm 58:1

For the choirmaster.
The psalm was entrusted for public worship under the direction of Israel’s lead musician. Though David lived before Solomon built the temple, his psalms became central to the worship offered at the tabernacle and later in the temple. The accusation against corrupt judges was therefore sung before the God who sees every hidden act and judges all earthly authority.

Psa 4:1 For the choirmaster. With stringed instruments.…

Psa 5:1 For the choirmaster, to be accompanied by flutes.…

Psa 8:1 For the choirmaster. According to Gittith.…

To the tune of “Do Not Destroy.”
The psalm was to be sung to a familiar melody also named in Psalms 57, 59, and 75. The title accords with David’s repeated dependence on God during seasons when enemies sought his life and powerful men abused their position. God preserves His servants, yet He does not overlook those who destroy others through violence, deceit, and unjust judgment.

Psa 57:1 For the choirmaster. To the tune of “Do Not Destroy.”…

Psa 59:1 Deliver me from my enemies, O my God; protect me…

Psa 75:1 For the choirmaster: To the tune of “Do Not Destroy.”…

A Miktam of David.
David wrote from experience as a man who had suffered under Saul’s unjust hostility and later bore the weight of governing Israel. His words condemn rulers who misuse authority while directing the faithful to trust the Lord as the final Judge. God’s justice does not depend upon the integrity of human courts.

Psa 16:1 A Miktam of David. Preserve me, O God, for in You…

Psa 56:1 Be merciful to me, O God, for men are hounding me;…

Psa 60:1 You have rejected us, O God; You have broken us;…

Do you indeed speak justly, O rulers?
God holds rulers accountable for the words they pronounce and the judgments they render. Israel’s leaders were commanded to reject partiality, bribery, and oppression, because they served under the Lord’s authority. David exposes the moral failure of officials whose public speech claimed justice while their actions protected evil.

Pro 16:10 A divine verdict is on the lips of a king;…

2Sa 23:3-4 The God of Israel spoke; the Rock of Israel said to me, …

Isa 32:1 Behold, a king will reign in righteousness,…

Do you judge uprightly, O sons of men?
Human rulers remain answerable to God despite their rank and influence. Upright judgment reflects God’s own character, defends the innocent, and restrains wickedness. The question anticipates the day when Christ, the righteous King and Judge, will bring perfect justice to every nation and every human heart.

Psalm 58:2

No, in your hearts you devise injustice;
David exposes the hidden source of corrupt judgment: deliberate rebellion formed within the inner life. Those entrusted with authority were called to uphold righteousness, yet they planned evil instead. God sees beyond public words and outward office to the intentions of the heart, which He alone can judge perfectly.

Pro 6:14-18 With deceit in his heart he devises evil;…

Mic 2:1-2 Woe to those who devise iniquity and plot evil on their beds…

Psa 36:4 Even on his bed he plots wickedness; he sets himself…

with your hands you mete out violence on the earth.
The injustice conceived inwardly becomes public action. Rather than weighing cases with fairness, wicked rulers distribute oppression and harm among the people. Their violence corrupts the land, as in the days before the flood, when the earth was filled with violence through human sin. David’s accusation anticipates God’s judgment against every power that rejects His justice.

Isa 59:6-8 Their cobwebs cannot be made into clothing,…

Gen 6:11-13 Now the earth was corrupt in the sight of God,…

Amo 3:10 “For they know not how to do right,” declares the LORD.…

Psalm 58:3

The wicked are estranged from the womb;
David traces human rebellion to the fallen condition inherited from Adam. Sin is not merely learned through society or circumstance; it arises from a heart alienated from God from the beginning of life (Psalm 51:5; Romans 5:12). The wicked rulers condemned in this psalm display outwardly the inward corruption common to fallen humanity. Only God can reconcile sinners to Himself through Christ.

Isa 48:8 You have never heard; you have never understood;…

Psa 51:5 Surely I was brought forth in iniquity; I was sinful…

Gen 8:21 When the LORD smelled the pleasing aroma,…

the liars go astray from birth.
Deceit reveals the departure of sinful hearts from God’s truth. Humanity’s universal guilt and moral failure confirm that all have sinned and fallen short of God’s glory (Romans 3:23). Jesus Christ, who is Himself the truth, came to bear sin’s judgment and to bring those who believe in Him out of estrangement and into fellowship with God.

Psa 7:14 Behold, the wicked man travails with evil;…

Job 15:35 They conceive trouble and give birth to evil;…

Isa 57:4 Whom are you mocking? At whom do you sneer?…

Psalm 58:4

Their venom is like the venom of a snake,
David portrays corrupt rulers as deadly in their speech and conduct. Like venom entering the body and spreading destruction, their lies, violence, and unjust judgments injure those placed under their authority. The serpent’s role in Eden also recalls Satan’s deceitful work, while James warns that the human tongue can be “full of deadly poison” when yielded to sin.

Deu 32:33 Their wine is the venom of serpents, the deadly poison…

Psa 140:3 They sharpen their tongues like snakes; the venom of vipers…

Rom 3:13 Their throats are open graves; their tongues practice deceit…

like a cobra that shuts its ears,
The wicked deliberately resist wisdom, correction, and the fear of the Lord. Their hardness resembles Pharaoh’s repeated refusal to heed God’s command through Moses. Psalm 58 continues by describing their refusal to listen even to a skilled charmer, emphasizing that their rebellion is willful. Those who close themselves to God’s truth remain dangerous until His righteous judgment restrains them.

Psalm 58:5

refusing to hear the tune of the charmer
The wicked are compared to a cobra that cannot be restrained even by a charmer’s practiced attempts. Scripture does not endorse enchantment, which the Law forbids, but uses a familiar ancient image to portray deliberate, hardened resistance. David’s concern is with corrupt rulers who shut themselves against justice, rebuke, and the fear of God.

Jer 8:17 “For behold, I will send snakes among you,”…

Isa 19:3 Then the spirit of the Egyptians will be emptied out…

Ecc 10:11 If the snake bites before it is charmed, there is no profit…

who skillfully weaves his spell.
Even the most skilled effort cannot tame a heart committed to evil. Pharaoh repeatedly resisted God’s commands through Moses despite unmistakable signs of divine power. Likewise, those who reject God’s truth are not hindered by insufficient evidence but by an obstinate will. Only the Lord can remove a heart of stone and give a heart willing to hear and obey Him.

Isa 47:12 So take your stand with your spells and with your sorceries…

Isa 47:9 These two things will overtake you in a moment,…

Mic 5:12 I will cut the sorceries from your hand…

Psalm 58:6

O God, shatter their teeth in their mouths;
David appeals to God as Judge to break the wicked’s power to injure, deceive, and oppress. Their teeth portray the destructive force of unjust rulers and violent enemies. As in Psalm 3:7, deliverance belongs to the Lord, who alone may rightly restrain evil and vindicate the innocent.

Psa 3:7 Arise, O LORD! Save me, O my God! Strike all my enemies…

Job 29:17 I shattered the fangs of the unjust and snatched the prey…

Pro 30:14 there is a generation whose teeth are swords…

O LORD, tear out the fangs of the lions.
Lions depict adversaries whose strength and cruelty make them dangerous. David asks God to remove their ability to devour rather than merely to lessen their threat. The image anticipates God’s final defeat of every hostile power. Satan still prowls like a roaring lion (1 Pet. 5:8), yet Christ has decisively triumphed over the powers of darkness and preserves His people.

Job 4:10 The lion may roar, and the fierce lion may growl…

Dan 6:22 My God sent His angel and shut the mouths of the lions.…

Psa 22:21 Save me from the mouth of the lion; at the horns…

Psalm 58:7

May they vanish like water that runs off;
Water poured onto the dry ground quickly disappears, leaving no lasting strength or benefit. David asks God to cause the power, influence, and violent designs of the wicked to dissipate under His righteous judgment. Their apparent strength is temporary before the Judge of all the earth.

Job 11:16 For you will forget your misery, recalling it only…

2Sa 14:14 For we will surely die and be like water poured out…

Job 6:15-18 But my brothers are as faithless as wadis,…

when they draw the bow,
The bow pictures deliberate hostility and readiness to harm the innocent. The righteous do not deny the reality of evil or its threats; they entrust their defense to the Lord, who sees every hidden intention and every weapon raised against truth.

Psa 11:2 For behold, the wicked bend their bows.…

Jer 50:14 Line up in formation around Babylon, all you who draw the bow!…

Psa 64:3-4 who sharpen their tongues like swords and aim…

may their arrows be blunted.
God can make the attacks of the wicked powerless, causing their schemes to fail and their violence to return without effect. The Lord’s protection ultimately points to Christ’s victory over sin, death, and the powers of darkness, securing His people against every final accusation and assault.

Psa 76:3 There He shattered the flaming arrows, the shield…

Psa 37:15 But their swords will pierce their own hearts,…

Hos 1:5 And on that day I will break the bow of Israel…

Psalm 58:8

Like a slug that dissolves in its slime,
David prays that the wicked would waste away and vanish through the destructive course of their own rebellion. Their apparent strength and progress leave no enduring result before the judgment of God.

Psa 68:2 As smoke is blown away, You will drive them out;…

Psa 22:14 I am poured out like water, and all my bones are disjointed…

Psa 37:20 But the wicked and enemies of the LORD will perish…

like a woman’s stillborn child,
The image portrays a life cut off before it can fulfill its purpose or enjoy the blessings of life. Those who persist in violence and deceit will not establish a lasting legacy, for God brings their evil designs to nothing.

Job 3:16 Or why was I not hidden like a stillborn child,…

Job 10:18-19 Why then did You bring me from the womb?…

Hos 9:14 Give them, O LORD—what will You give?…

may they never see the sun.
To see the sun represents life in God’s world and the enjoyment of His common blessings. David entrusts vengeance to the Lord, pleading for divine justice against rulers and oppressors who pervert judgment and harm the innocent.

Ecc 6:5 The child, though neither seeing the sun nor knowing anything…

Psa 49:19 he will join the generation of his fathers…

Job 3:20-23 Why is light given to the miserable, and life to the bitter…

Psalm 58:9

Before your pots can feel the burning thorns—
Thorn bushes served as readily available fuel for outdoor cooking, producing a brief, intense flame beneath a pot. God’s judgment upon violent and deceitful rulers will arrive before their schemes can yield the results they expect. Their power, like thorn-fire, is momentary and incapable of securing lasting safety.

Ecc 7:6 For like the crackling of thorns under the pot,…

Psa 118:12 They swarmed around me like bees, but they were extinguished…

Isa 33:12 The peoples will be burned to ashes, like thorns cut down…

whether green or dry—
Neither fresh thorns nor dry thorns can delay the Lord’s intervention. The wicked may appear strong and flourishing, or their plans may already be near collapse, but every condition remains under God’s authority. Divine justice is not limited by human readiness, strength, or resistance.

Ezk 20:47 Say to the forest of the Negev: Hear the word…

Luk 23:31 For if men do these things while the tree is green…

He will sweep them away.
The Lord will remove the wicked with decisive force, as wind drives away chaff and a storm scatters what cannot stand. Psalm 58 assures the righteous that evil is neither unnoticed nor permanent; God will judge those who use authority to oppress and destroy.

Psa 35:5 May they be like chaff in the wind as the angel of the LORD…

Job 27:21 The east wind carries him away, and he is gone;…

Psa 1:4 Not so the wicked! For they are like chaff…

Psalm 58:10

The righteous will rejoice when they see they are avenged;
The righteous rejoice because God has publicly upheld His holiness and vindicated those who entrusted themselves to Him. Their joy is not delight in cruelty or private retaliation, but worshipful recognition that the Judge of all the earth has acted rightly. Moses likewise called the nations to rejoice when the Lord avenges His servants (Deuteronomy 32:43), and heaven praises God when His true and righteous judgments overthrow persistent rebellion (Revelation 19:1–2).

Deu 32:41-43 When I sharpen My flashing sword, and My hand grasps it…

Rev 18:20 Rejoice over her, O heaven, and you saints and apostles…

Rev 19:1-3 After this I heard a sound like the roar…

they will wash their feet in the blood of the wicked.
The graphic battlefield image portrays the completeness of God’s victory over unrepentant evil. Ancient armies walked among the fallen after battle, and the psalmist uses that reality to declare that wickedness will not finally prevail. God’s judgment, delayed in mercy, is never forgotten or powerless. The imagery anticipates the day when Christ decisively subdues every enemy, including sin, death, and all opposition to His righteous reign.

Psalm 58:11

Then men will say,
The overthrow of wicked rulers will bring open recognition that God has acted. Those who once doubted His justice will see that He does not ignore violence, deceit, or the suffering of the innocent.

Psa 64:9 Then all mankind will fear and proclaim the work of God;…

Rev 11:13 And in that hour there was a great earthquake,…

Isa 26:9 My soul longs for You in the night; indeed, my spirit…

“There is surely a reward for the righteous!
God’s reward includes His present care, final vindication, and the inheritance promised to those who trust Him. The righteous may endure oppression for a time, but their faithfulness is not forgotten before the Lord.

Pro 11:18 The wicked man earns an empty wage, but he who sows…

Mat 5:12 Rejoice and be glad, because great is your reward…

Isa 3:10 Tell the righteous it will be well with them…

There is surely a God who judges the earth!”
The God of Israel rules over every nation and every ruler, not merely over one land or people. His judgments in history anticipate the final judgment, when Jesus Christ will judge the living and the dead and establish perfect justice.
