He removed foreign altars, high places, sacred pillars, and Asherah poles, calling Judah to seek the LORD and obey His law (2 Chronicles 14:3–4). His reforms showed that faithfulness to God required more than preserving religious forms; it demanded the rejection of idolatry and active obedience.

2 Chronicles 14:9

Then Zerah the Cushite came against them
Zerah led a formidable force from Cush, the region south of Egypt associated with Nubia and areas of present-day Sudan and Ethiopia. His invasion shattered Judah’s season of peace under Asa and tested whether the king’s reforms would be matched by genuine dependence on the LORD. Judah’s security did not rest ultimately in fortifications or military preparation, but in the God Asa had sought.

The vast army and its chariot force presented Judah with humanly impossible odds. Chariots gave an invading army particular strength on the open terrain of Judah’s lowlands. The scale of the threat prepared the way for the LORD to display that no military power, however numerous or well-equipped, can overcome those who rely upon Him. Asa’s later prayer acknowledges this truth: “Lord, there is none besides You to help between the mighty and those who have no strength” (2 Chronicles 14:11).
