Clap Your Hands, All You Peoples

Psalm 47:1

For the choirmaster.
The psalm was entrusted to the appointed leader of Israel’s sacred music for use in public worship. Temple praise involved trained Levites, singers, and instruments, showing that worship was to be offered with order, skill, and reverence before the Lord.

A Psalm of the sons of Korah.
The sons of Korah descended from the Levite who rebelled against Moses and Aaron and came under God’s judgment (Numbers 16). Korah’s descendants did not perish with him (Numbers 26:11), and later generations served faithfully as gatekeepers and musicians in the sanctuary. Their role testifies to God’s mercy and to the possibility of faithful service after a family’s sinful past.

Clap your hands, all you peoples;
Clapping hands summons the nations to visible, united celebration before the God of Israel. The invitation reaches beyond Israel to every people and anticipates the gathering of Gentile believers under the reign of the Messiah. God’s kingship is not limited by national borders, languages, or rulers.

God deserves joyful, audible praise because He reigns in victory over all opposition. Israel knew such victory when the Lord brought down Jericho’s walls amid the people’s shout (Joshua 6:20). The greater victory belongs to Christ, who conquered sin, death, and Satan through His death and resurrection.

Psalm 47:2

How awesome is the LORD Most High,
The LORD inspires reverent fear because His majesty, holiness, and power surpass every earthly ruler and every false god. Israel’s covenant God is “God of gods and Lord of lords” (Deuteronomy 10:17), exalted above all creation and answerable to none. His greatness calls His people to worship with humility and confidence.

the great King over all the earth!
God’s reign extends far beyond Israel’s borders to every nation, land, and people. He governs history, judges the nations, and establishes His purposes without rival. Psalm 47 anticipates the universal reign of Christ, the King of kings, whose authority will be openly acknowledged throughout the earth (Revelation 19:16).

Psalm 47:3

He subdues nations beneath us,
God gave Israel victory over hostile nations as He fulfilled His covenant promises, especially in the conquest under Joshua and in the establishment of David’s kingdom. Israel’s security did not rest in military strength but in the Lord, who rules over kings and empires and directs history according to His purposes. The victories granted to His people displayed His holiness, faithfulness, and judgment against persistent rebellion.

Psa 144:2 He is my steadfast love and my fortress, my stronghold…

and peoples under our feet.
The image recalls Joshua’s commanders placing their feet on the necks of defeated Canaanite kings, a public sign that the Lord had overcome Israel’s enemies. God also promised David that He would cut off his enemies and establish his throne. These historical victories point beyond Israel’s wars to the reign of Christ, the promised Son of David, under whose feet every enemy will finally be placed, including sin, death, and all rebellious powers.

Psalm 47:4

He chooses our inheritance for us,
God assigned Israel the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, establishing their place among the nations by His covenant and power rather than by their own strength. Israel’s possession of Canaan testified that the Lord rules over the earth and gives according to His wise purpose. Believers likewise receive an eternal inheritance through Christ, secured by God’s grace and ordered according to His will.

the pride of Jacob,
The promised land was the honor and glory of Jacob’s descendants, the place where God placed His name, gave His law, and fulfilled His covenant promises. Its value rested not merely in its fertility or security, but in the Lord’s presence and faithfulness among His people.

Psalm 47:5

God has ascended amid shouts of joy,
God is pictured as the victorious King taking His throne amid the jubilant worship of His people. The celebration recalls the bringing of the ark to Jerusalem, when Israel shouted and sounded trumpets before the LORD (2 Samuel 6:15). The scene also anticipates Christ’s ascension after His finished work of redemption, when He was received into heaven and seated in royal authority at the Father’s right hand (Acts 1:9; Psalm 110:1).

the LORD with the sound of the horn.
Yahweh’s covenant name identifies the exalted King as the God who redeemed and chose Israel. The shofar summoned Israel for worship, marked holy gatherings, and announced royal enthronement (Leviticus 23:24; 1 Kings 1:39). Its triumphant sound points beyond Israel’s worship to the return of Christ, when the trumpet of God will accompany His coming and the gathering of His people (1 Thessalonians 4:16).

Psalm 47:6

Sing praises to God, sing praises;
Worship answers God’s sovereign rule and mighty acts on behalf of His people. Israel gathered to praise the Lord with united voices, instruments, and thanksgiving, especially in times of public worship and celebration (2 Chronicles 5:13). Repeated praise calls God’s people to wholehearted devotion rather than silent acknowledgment of His greatness.

Psalm 47:7

For God is King of all the earth;
Israel’s God rules not only over His covenant people but over every nation, ruler, and corner of creation. The earth belongs to the LORD and all who dwell in it (Psalm 24:1). In a world filled with regional deities and competing claims of power, Psalm 47 declares that the living God alone possesses universal authority. His reign anticipates the exaltation of Jesus Christ, before whom every knee will bow and every tongue confess that He is Lord (Philippians 2:9–11).

sing to Him a psalm of praise.
Worship fittingly answers God’s kingship. Singing joins confession, thanksgiving, and trust, declaring that His rule is righteous even amid the unrest of nations and personal affliction. The people of God are called to praise Him with understanding, remembering His mighty acts and submitting gladly to His sovereign rule.

Psalm 47:8

God reigns over the nations;
The Lord’s kingship extends over every people, ruler, and realm, not Israel alone. In a world where nations claimed gods tied to their lands and armies, Israel confessed that all dominion belongs to the LORD (Psalm 22:28). Human kingdoms rise and fall under His providence, but His authority is neither limited by borders nor threatened by rebellion. God’s universal reign anticipates the gathering of worshipers from every nation through the gospel and the final submission of all rule to Christ (Matthew 28:19; Philippians 2:9–11).

Psa 103:19 The LORD has established His throne in heaven…

God is seated on His holy throne.
The heavenly throne reveals God as the exalted King and righteous Judge. His rule is holy in character, untouched by the corruption, injustice, and instability that mark earthly governments. Isaiah saw the Lord high and lifted up upon His throne, before whom even heavenly beings worshiped in reverent awe (Isaiah 6:1–3). The risen Christ, having completed His atoning work, now sits at the right hand of the Majesty on high and reigns until every enemy is subdued (Hebrews 1:3; 1 Corinthians 15:25). God’s throne assures His people that history is governed by perfect holiness and will end in the visible triumph of His kingdom.

Psalm 47:9

The nobles of the nations have assembled
Earthly rulers and representatives of the peoples are pictured as gathered before the Lord, acknowledging the King who reigns over every land. What nations once pursued through conquest, alliances, and power will ultimately be brought under God’s righteous rule. The scene anticipates the worship of the redeemed from every nation in Revelation 7:9 and the promised blessing of all families of the earth through Abraham’s seed (Genesis 12:3).

The God who called Abraham and formed Israel has always purposed to gather worshipers from among all nations. Gentiles are not received as strangers to an unrelated God, but are brought near through the covenant promise fulfilled in Jesus Christ. All who belong to Christ are Abraham’s spiritual offspring and heirs of the promise (Galatians 3:29).
