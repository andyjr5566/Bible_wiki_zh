The Blessed Fear of the LORD

Psalm 112:1

Hallelujah!
“Hallelujah,” a call to praise, opens the psalm because the blessed life begins with God’s glory, not human achievement. Psalm 112 follows Psalm 111, where God’s mighty works and faithfulness are celebrated, and it shows the kind of life formed by reverence for such a God.

God gives enduring well-being to those who belong to Him. The blessing described throughout the psalm includes a steadfast heart, generous conduct, and a lasting testimony, rather than freedom from every earthly hardship.

Psalm 112:2

His descendants will be mighty in the land;
The children of those who fear the LORD receive the benefits of a godly heritage. In Israel, the land was God’s covenant gift to Abraham and his offspring, and strength within it included security, honor, influence, and the ability to fulfill one’s calling among God’s people. The promise recalls God’s pledge to make Abraham into a great nation and to establish his descendants in the land (Genesis 12:2–3; 15:18). Judah’s leadership and Joseph’s fruitfulness also displayed the kind of strength God grants through faithful generations (Genesis 49:8–12, 22–26).

Exo 1:7 but the Israelites were fruitful and multiplied;…

Deu 11:21 as long as the heavens are above the earth,…

Psa 25:13 His soul will dwell in prosperity, and his descendants…

the generation of the upright will be blessed.
Upright parents do not merely leave possessions behind; they pass on the knowledge of God, the pattern of obedience, and the witness of a life ordered by His truth. God’s covenant faithfulness extends to those who love Him and keep His commands (Deuteronomy 7:9). The blessing includes God’s favor, spiritual stability, and a place within the community of His people, though it does not remove each descendant’s responsibility to trust and obey the Lord. Jesus Christ, the perfectly righteous Son, fulfills the hope of blessing for all nations, bringing Abraham’s promised blessing to those who belong to Him by faith (Galatians 3:14).

Psalm 112:3

Wealth and riches are in his house,
The man who fears the Lord and delights in His commandments receives God’s provision for his household. In Israel, such wealth commonly included land, livestock, grain, servants, and goods. Abraham and Solomon also received abundant possessions under God’s blessing. Material provision was intended to strengthen families, enable generosity, and support the needy, not to replace trust in God or become an idol.

and his righteousness endures forever.
The godly man’s faithful obedience and generous deeds leave an enduring testimony because they are established by the Lord. Psalm 112 later joins righteousness with openhanded giving to the poor, a connection Paul applies to believers in 2 Corinthians 9:9. Earthly riches may pass away, but righteousness rooted in the fear of God has lasting value. Such righteousness ultimately rests in God’s grace and finds its fullest ground in Jesus Christ, whose righteousness secures His people before God forever.

Psalm 112:4

Light dawns in the darkness for the upright—
God brings deliverance, guidance, and renewed hope to those who fear Him and walk in His ways. Israel knew both physical and spiritual darkness through wilderness trials, oppression, and exile, yet the Lord remained their light and salvation. The promise finds its fullest fulfillment in Christ, the Light of the world, who overcomes the darkness of sin and death.

Righteousness joins mercy to steadfast obedience to God’s will. The godly do not separate personal holiness from just and loving conduct toward others. Christ alone possesses perfect righteousness, and those who belong to Him are called to display its fruit in lives marked by grace, compassion, and integrity.

Psalm 112:5

It is well with the man who is generous and lends freely,
God’s blessing rests on the person who uses His provision to meet the needs of others. In Israel’s agrarian economy, crop failure, debt, or illness could quickly leave a household without resources. The Law commanded Israelites to lend to needy countrymen without charging interest and without hardening their hearts toward them (Exodus 22:25; Deuteronomy 15:7–11). Such generosity reflected trust that the Lord, not accumulated wealth, secures a person’s future. Giving to the poor honors God, who identifies Himself with their need (Proverbs 19:17). Jesus likewise taught His disciples to give freely, reflecting the mercy they have received from God (Luke 6:35–38).

Psa 37:26 They are ever generous and quick to lend…

Pro 19:17 Kindness to the poor is a loan to the LORD,…

whose affairs are guided by justice.
Generosity must be joined to upright conduct. The godly person manages business, debts, relationships, and responsibilities with fairness, honesty, and concern for those affected by his decisions. Israel’s judges, rulers, and ordinary citizens were commanded to pursue justice because the Lord Himself is righteous and defends the vulnerable (Deuteronomy 16:20; Psalm 146:7–9). Micah summarized covenant faithfulness as doing justice, loving kindness, and walking humbly with God (Micah 6:8). Christ perfectly displayed both mercy and justice, and His people are called to reflect His character in every dealing.

Psalm 112:6

Surely he will never be shaken;
The righteous person stands secure because the Lord sustains those who fear Him and order their lives by His truth. Trials, opposition, and loss may come, but they cannot overthrow one whose confidence rests in God. Psalm 55:22 calls believers to cast their burdens upon the Lord, who promises to sustain them and not permit the righteous to be shaken. Jesus likewise pictured enduring faith as a house built on rock, standing firm when rain, floods, and winds strike (Matthew 7:24–27). Such stability comes not from human resolve but from trustful obedience to God.

the righteous man will be remembered forever.
A godly life leaves a blessing that outlives earthly circumstances. Proverbs 10:7 declares that the memory of the righteous is blessed, for their faithfulness bears witness to God’s work in them. The Lord does not forget those who serve Him; their names and deeds are before Him, even when human recognition fades. The enduring remembrance of the righteous ultimately points to Christ, whose perfect righteousness, saving death, and resurrection are proclaimed throughout every generation.

Psalm 112:7

He does not fear bad news;
The righteous person is not insulated from trouble, loss, or threats, but he is not ruled by them. Israel regularly faced invasion, famine, and political instability, yet the faithful were called to rest in the Lord’s covenant faithfulness. Proverbs 3:25–26 likewise teaches freedom from sudden fear because the Lord is the believer’s confidence. Daniel displayed such confidence when he continued to pray despite the threat of the lions’ den.

his heart is steadfast,
His inner life is firmly established rather than tossed about by changing circumstances. David confessed, “My heart is steadfast, O God” while surrounded by danger (Psalm 57:7). Such stability grows through knowing God’s works, remembering His promises, and ordering one’s life in reverent obedience. Jesus perfectly embodied this steadfast devotion as He set His face toward Jerusalem and endured the cross in obedience to the Father.

trusting in the LORD.
Confidence rests not in favorable circumstances, personal strength, or human protection, but in the Lord Himself. Abraham trusted God’s promise when its fulfillment appeared impossible, and God counted his faith as righteousness. Believers are called to trust the Lord with all their heart rather than lean on their own understanding (Proverbs 3:5–6). Faith in Christ brings this same settled assurance, because the One who gave Himself for His people remains faithful in every trial.

Psalm 112:8

His heart is assured;
The righteous person rests securely in God’s character, promises, and providence. His confidence reaches beyond changing circumstances because the Lord is faithful. Isaiah 26:3 and Philippians 4:7 likewise connect steadfast trust in God with peace that guards the inner life.

Courage grows from assurance in God’s rule and care. Israel often faced hostile nations, war, and uncertainty, yet God repeatedly commanded His people to be strong because He was with them. Faith does not deny danger; it refuses to treat danger as greater than the Lord.

The pattern finds its fullest fulfillment in Christ, who endured the hostility of sinners and triumphed over sin, death, and every enemy through His resurrection.

Psalm 112:9

He has scattered abroad his gifts to the poor;
The righteous person gives freely rather than hoarding what God has provided. Like seed cast broadly over a field, generosity reaches beyond personal comfort to meet real needs among the vulnerable. In Israel, widows, orphans, foreigners, and the poor often depended on such mercy because they possessed little land or protection. Proverbs 19:17 teaches that kindness shown to the poor is a loan to the Lord, who sees and repays faithful compassion. Paul cites Psalm 112:9 in 2 Corinthians 9:9 to encourage cheerful, willing giving among believers. Generosity reflects the character of God, who gives abundantly and calls His people to care for those in need.

his righteousness endures forever;
Mercy and justice are not forgotten before God. Material possessions pass away, but a life marked by obedience to the Lord bears lasting fruit. The righteousness of the godly rests ultimately on God’s grace, and their acts of love reveal the faith that governs their lives. Isaiah 51:8 declares that God’s righteousness will endure forever; therefore, the righteous share in a life whose significance extends beyond earthly reputation. Jesus Christ perfectly fulfilled righteousness, and all who belong to Him stand accepted before God through His righteousness.

his horn will be lifted high in honor.
The horn commonly represented strength, authority, and victorious exaltation in Scripture. God honors the person who fears Him and walks in uprightness, even when generosity brings no immediate earthly recognition. Hannah rejoiced that the Lord lifts up the horn of His anointed (1 Samuel 2:1, 10), anticipating God’s power to vindicate those who trust Him. Christ Himself was exalted after His humble obedience unto death, receiving the name above every name (Philippians 2:9). God’s final judgment will openly honor faithful service that was often unseen in the present age.

Psalm 112:10

The wicked man will see and be grieved;
The wicked will witness the enduring honor, security, and fruitfulness God grants to those who fear Him. Their grief arises from seeing that the life they opposed was upheld by the Lord, while their own rebellion has brought them under judgment. Psalm 37 similarly portrays the wicked plotting against the righteous, yet the Lord knows the day of their accountability is coming.

Gnashing the teeth portrays bitter rage and helpless frustration before God’s righteous rule. Jesus used the same imagery to describe the anguish of those excluded from His kingdom. The wicked cannot overturn God’s purposes or destroy the inheritance He gives His people; their strength, schemes, and influence finally fail.

The closing contrast confirms that the fear of the Lord leads to enduring blessing, but the path of the ungodly ends in loss.
