Zechariah 14:1

Behold, a day of the LORD is coming
God announces a decisive intervention in Jerusalem and among the nations. The day of the LORD brings judgment upon rebellion while preparing the way for the public triumph of God’s kingdom. Zechariah’s prophecy looks beyond the prophet’s own generation to a climactic future conflict in which the LORD vindicates His covenant purposes. Joel 2:31 and Amos 5:18–20 likewise portray that day as terror for the ungodly, yet the wider prophetic hope includes deliverance for those who call upon the LORD.

The scene reflects the brutal realities of ancient conquest, when victors distributed captured wealth as spoil. God does not overlook the sins of His people, and Jerusalem’s distress reveals the seriousness of divine judgment. Yet the city’s defeat is not the end of the story; the following prophecy reveals that the LORD Himself will fight for Jerusalem and establish His reign over all the earth.

Following the Lord’s return and deliverance of Jerusalem, these survivors will live under His visible rule and will acknowledge the God they once opposed.

and to celebrate the Feast of Tabernacles.
The Feast of Tabernacles commemorated God’s faithful provision for Israel in the wilderness and celebrated the harvest He supplied. Its observance by the nations will proclaim their dependence upon the Lord for life, protection, and blessing. The feast also anticipates the joy of God dwelling with His redeemed people, a reality ultimately fulfilled when God makes His dwelling with humanity forever.

And should any of the families of the earth not go up to Jerusalem
The nations that survive the Lord’s judgment at His return will be required to acknowledge Jerusalem as the center of His earthly kingdom. Annual worship will fulfill the prophetic hope that peoples from every nation will come to the mountain of the Lord to learn His ways and walk in His paths (Isaiah 2:2–3). The families of the earth recall God’s promise to Abraham that all peoples would be blessed through his offspring (Genesis 12:3). Israel’s restoration will therefore lead to the public submission of the Gentile nations to the God of Israel.

The judgment reflects the covenant pattern in which obedience brought blessing and rebellion brought drought and barrenness (Deuteronomy 28:23–24). Even during the coming kingdom, the nations will be accountable to the reigning Messiah, and His rule will join perfect justice with the provision needed for life.
