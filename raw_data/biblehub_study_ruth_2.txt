Boaz Meets Ruth

Ruth 2:1

Now Naomi had a relative on her husband’s side,
The narrator introduces the family connection that will bring hope to Naomi’s emptied household. Boaz belonged to Elimelech’s wider family and therefore stood within the circle of those responsible to protect family property and preserve the name of a deceased relative. The laws of redemption provided for a near relative to reclaim land lost through poverty (Leviticus 25:25–28). The duty of raising up offspring for a deceased man belonged specifically to a brother-in-law (Deuteronomy 25:5–10), yet Boaz willingly accepts a related responsibility to preserve Elimelech’s inheritance and provide security for Ruth and Naomi.

a prominent man of noble character
Boaz possessed both material resources and a reputation for strength, integrity, and leadership. His wealth did not make him indifferent to the vulnerable; he used his position to protect Ruth, provide generously for Naomi, and act honorably before the community. His conduct matches the noble character later recognized in Ruth herself (Ruth 3:11). Boaz’s righteousness displays the kind of faith that obeys God’s law with mercy rather than treating duty as a burden.

Elimelech’s clan provided the legal and familial setting for the coming redemption. Naomi’s return to Bethlehem had seemed to end in loss, but God had preserved a relative within Elimelech’s own family who could restore what famine, death, and poverty had taken away.

Boaz becomes God’s instrument of protection and restoration for Ruth and Naomi. The name Boaz means “strength” or “in him is strength,” fitting his role in the narrative. Through his faithful actions, Ruth is received into Israel, Naomi’s family line is renewed, and the inheritance of Elimelech is preserved. Boaz and Ruth become the great-grandparents of David (Ruth 4:21–22) and appear in the genealogy of Jesus Christ (Matthew 1:5). His willing redemption points forward to Christ, who redeems His people at His own cost and brings the outsider near.

Her place in Bethlehem reveals God’s gracious purpose to receive those who come to Him in faith, regardless of ancestry. Ruth’s faithful love for Naomi overcame national and cultural barriers.
