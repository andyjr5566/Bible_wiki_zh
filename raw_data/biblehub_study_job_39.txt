The LORD Speaks of His Creation

Job 39:1

“Do you know when mountain goats give birth?
From the whirlwind, the Lord confronts Job with creatures that live beyond human reach in the high crags. No shepherd or hunter can govern their seasons, guard their young, or trace their hidden births. Yet God knows the appointed time and sustains them in terrain where people scarcely survive. The high mountains are a refuge for wild goats (Psalm 104:18), revealing God’s attentive rule over every part of creation.

Job’s limited knowledge of these creatures exposes the limits of human wisdom, while God’s care for them displays His perfect knowledge and providence.

Job 39:26

Does the hawk take flight by your understanding
God asks Job whether human wisdom enables the hawk’s powerful flight, sharp sight, and skill as a hunter. The hawk’s abilities arise from the Creator’s design, not from human observation or control. Its freedom in the heights exposes the limits of Job’s knowledge and points to the wisdom of God, whose understanding is unsearchable (Isaiah 40:28).

Job 39:27

Does the eagle soar at your command
God confronts Job with a creature whose strength, freedom, and flight lie beyond human control. The eagle rises by the design and sustaining power of its Creator, bearing witness to God’s rule over every living thing. Human wisdom may observe its flight, but only God gives it instinct, strength, and the open heavens. Isaiah 40:31 draws on the eagle’s soaring power to picture the renewal God gives those who wait upon Him.

Pro 30:19 the way of an eagle in the sky, the way of a snake on a rock…

and make his nest on high?
Eagles build their nests on cliffs and elevated places, where their young are sheltered from many dangers and where the parents can survey the land below. God established such instincts and provides the places suited to each creature’s preservation. The inaccessible nest magnifies the contrast between Job’s limitations and God’s complete knowledge of creation. Scripture also portrays the Lord as the secure refuge of His people, whose safety rests not in their own strength but in the protection of the Most High.

Job 39:28

He dwells on a cliff
The eagle makes its home in heights inaccessible to most predators and undisturbed by ordinary danger. Its lofty dwelling displays the wisdom of God’s created order, providing both protection and a broad view of the land below. Scripture often uses high rocks and inaccessible places to picture refuge, and the eagle’s secure habitat reflects the safety found in the Lord, who shelters His people under His care (Psalm 91:4).

and lodges there;
The eagle does not merely pass through the high places but establishes its nest there. Eyries were built on ledges and cliffs where young could be raised with relative security. Such permanence recalls the believer’s confidence in God as a rock and strong tower amid trouble (Psalm 61:2–3). Those who trust Him are called to set their minds above the shifting fears and pursuits of the world.

Job 39:30

His young ones feast on blood;
The eagle’s young are sustained by prey brought to the nest, displaying God’s provision even within the severe realities of a fallen creation. Blood represents life (Leviticus 17:11), and the eagle’s dependence upon it reminds Job that every creature lives by the order God has established. The Creator who feeds the eagle’s brood also sees and sustains His people.
