Elihu Recalls God’s Justice

Job 35:1

And Elihu went on to say:
Elihu, whose name means “He is my God,” continues his final speech, answering Job’s repeated longing for vindication and questioning whether personal righteousness has any benefit before God. As the younger observer who waited while Job and the older friends spoke, Elihu now presses the truth that God’s justice is never dependent on human opinion or circumstance. His words prepare the way for the Lord’s appearance, directing attention from Job’s suffering and self-defense to the greatness, wisdom, and righteous rule of God.

Job 36:1 And Elihu continued:

Job 34:1 Then Elihu continued:

Job 32:6 So Elihu son of Barachel the Buzite declared:…

Job 35:2

“Do you think this is just?
Elihu confronts Job’s repeated insistence that his suffering is undeserved. Job had maintained his integrity before God, yet his protests at times sounded as though God had treated him unfairly. Elihu presses Job to consider whether accusing God of injustice can ever be righteous, since the Lord’s judgments flow from His perfect character.

Gen 18:25 Far be it from You to do such a thing—to kill the righteous…

Deu 32:4 He is the Rock, His work is perfect; all His ways are just.…

Psa 89:14 Righteousness and justice are the foundation of Your throne…

You say,
Elihu responds to the substance of Job’s speeches rather than quoting every word directly. Job had not denied God’s existence or power, but his anguish led him to question why the righteous suffer while the wicked often prosper. The exchange exposes how real sorrow can tempt a faithful person to judge God’s providence by limited human understanding.

Job 33:9 I am pure, without transgression; I am clean…

Luk 18:11-12 The Pharisee stood by himself and prayed, …

Pro 30:12 There is a generation of those who are pure…

‘I am more righteous than God.’
Job never made this claim in those exact words, but Elihu understood Job’s demand for vindication as carrying that implication. If Job were wholly right and God had wronged him, God’s justice would be inferior to Job’s. Scripture consistently rejects such a conclusion, for God is righteous in all His ways and holy in all His works. The Lord’s later appearance to Job confirms that human beings cannot sit in judgment over the wisdom and purposes of their Creator.

Job 40:8-9 Would you really annul My justice? Would you condemn Me…

Job 9:2 Yes, I know that it is so, but how can a mortal…

Ecc 7:20 Surely there is no righteous man on earth…

Job 35:3

For you ask,
Elihu charges Job with reasoning that his integrity has brought no advantage. Job’s grief had led him to observe that calamity can strike both the righteous and the wicked, yet Elihu hears in those complaints an accusation against God’s justice.

Mal 3:14 You have said, ‘It is futile to serve God.…

Job 21:15 Who is the Almighty, that we should serve Him…

Psa 73:13 Surely in vain I have kept my heart pure;…

‘What does it profit me,
The question exposes the temptation to serve God chiefly for visible reward. Job had lost wealth, children, health, and honor, conditions often associated with divine blessing, but righteousness is not a bargain by which man earns ease from God.

Job 34:9 For he has said, ‘It profits a man nothing…

Ecc 2:11 Yet when I considered the works my hands had accomplished…

Ecc 1:3 What does a man gain from all his labor,…

and what benefit do I gain
Elihu presses Job to consider whether he has begun to measure faithfulness only by earthly prosperity. The lasting benefit of righteousness is fellowship with God, a clear conscience before Him, and the hope of His final judgment, even when present circumstances remain painful and unexplained.

1Ti 6:6 Of course, godliness with contentment is great gain.

Psa 19:11 By them indeed Your servant is warned; in keeping them…

Pro 11:4 Riches are worthless in the day of wrath,…

apart from sin?’
Elihu understands Job’s words as implying that innocence leaves a man no better off than wrongdoing. Scripture rejects that conclusion: sin separates man from God and brings judgment, while righteousness, received ultimately through faith in God’s provision, belongs to those who fear Him. Temporary suffering does not erase the moral difference between the righteous and the wicked or overturn God’s final justice.

Rom 6:21-22 What fruit did you reap at that time from the things…

Pro 10:2 Ill-gotten treasures profit nothing, but righteousness…

Jas 1:15 Then after desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin;…

Job 35:4

I will reply to you
Elihu answers Job’s complaint that righteousness seems to bring no advantage when the godly suffer. He seeks to defend God’s justice by showing that human conduct does not place God under obligation. His argument prepares for the Lord’s own speech, which will reveal the limits of Job’s understanding and call him to trust God’s wise rule.

Job 13:22 Then call me, and I will answer, or let me speak,…

Job 38:3 Now brace yourself like a man; I will question you…

Job 40:7 “Now brace yourself like a man; I will question you……

and to your friends as well.
Job’s companions also need correction, for they have wrongly treated suffering as certain proof of personal sin. Elihu confronts both Job’s self-justifying words and the friends’ harsh accusations, insisting that God remains righteous even when His purposes are hidden from human sight.

Job 42:7-9 After the LORD had spoken these words to Job,…

Job 2:11 Now when Job’s three friends came from their homes…

Job 6:14 A despairing man should have the kindness of his friend,…

Job 35:5

Look to the heavens and see;
Elihu directs Job’s attention from his suffering to the created order above him. The heavens display God’s glory and power (Psalm 19:1), reminding Job that the Creator is not measured by human circumstances or judgments. God’s greatness does not make Him indifferent to suffering, but it does expose the limits of human understanding before His wisdom and rule.

Isa 40:26 Lift up your eyes on high: Who created all these?…

Psa 8:3 When I behold Your heavens, the work of Your fingers…

Psa 19:1 For the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. The heavens declare……

gaze at the clouds high above you.
The height of the clouds illustrates the distance between God and man. Elihu’s point in the following verses is that human sin cannot injure God, nor can human righteousness place Him in debt; human actions chiefly affect other people (Job 35:6–8). Yet God remains personally involved with His creation, guiding Israel by the cloud in the wilderness and promising that the Son of Man will come on the clouds with power and great glory (Exodus 13:21; Matthew 24:30).

Psa 68:4 Sing to God! Sing praises to His name. Exalt Him…

Psa 104:3 laying the beams of His chambers in the waters above…

Deu 33:26 There is none like the God of Jeshurun…

Job 35:6

If you sin, what do you accomplish against Him?
Elihu directs Job to God’s transcendent majesty. Human sin does not weaken God, diminish His authority, or overturn His purposes. Yet sin remains a direct offense against God’s holiness, as David confessed in Psalm 51:4, and it brings guilt, judgment, and broken fellowship upon the sinner.

Job 7:20 If I have sinned, what have I done to You?…

Job 22:2-3 Can a man be of use to God? Can even a wise man…

Psa 51:4 Against You, You only, have I sinned…

If you multiply your transgressions, what do you do to Him?
Even repeated rebellion cannot alter the Lord’s character or frustrate His sovereign plan. God does not change (Malachi 3:6), but multiplied sin deepens human corruption and harms others, a point Elihu develops in Job 35:8. God’s patience toward sinners does not excuse transgression; it displays mercy and leaves room for repentance and reconciliation through the redemption He provides.

Jer 7:19 But am I the One they are provoking? declares the LORD.…

Mic 6:3 My people, what have I done to you? Testify against Me…

Pro 9:12 If you are wise, you are wise to your own advantage;…

Job 35:7

If you are righteous,
Elihu reminds Job that even genuine integrity does not place God in human debt. God delights in righteousness and commands it, yet no person’s obedience can equal His holiness or add to His perfection. Human righteousness is always received and sustained by God’s grace, while sin exposes humanity’s need for mercy (Isaiah 64:6).

Job 22:2-3 Can a man be of use to God? Can even a wise man…

Psa 16:2 I said to the LORD, “You are my Lord; apart from You…

what do you give Him,
The Creator possesses all things and depends on no one. As Psalm 50:10–12 declares, every creature and all the earth already belong to Him. Righteous conduct benefits the one who obeys and those affected by that obedience; it does not supply something lacking in God.

1Ch 29:14 But who am I, and who are my people, that we should be able……

Rom 11:35 “Who has first given to God, that God should repay him?”

or what does He receive from your hand?
God is not served as though He needed human gifts or works, for He gives life and breath to all people (Acts 17:24–25). No human offering can secure acceptance before Him. Christ alone offered the perfect obedience and sacrifice humanity could not give, and those who trust Him serve God not to repay Him, but in grateful response to His grace.

Mal 1:10 Oh, that one of you would shut the temple doors…

Act 17:25 Nor is He served by human hands, as if He needed anything,…

Job 35:8

Your wickedness affects only a man like yourself,
Elihu reminds Job that human sin does not weaken, impoverish, or place God in anyone’s debt. God remains exalted above His creatures, yet wickedness brings real injury within human life: it corrupts the sinner, wounds neighbors, disturbs families and communities, and invites God’s righteous judgment. Achan’s sin brought trouble upon Israel, showing that rebellion often carries consequences beyond the one who commits it. God is not indifferent to evil; sin is an offense against His holy character, even though it cannot diminish His sovereign glory.

Job 22:2-3 Can a man be of use to God? Can even a wise man…

Psa 50:12-15 If I were hungry, I would not tell you, for the world is Mine,…

Psa 7:15-16 He has dug a hole and hollowed it out;…

and your righteousness only a son of man.
Genuine righteousness blesses those around the righteous person through justice, mercy, faithfulness, and the fear of God. It cannot obligate the Lord or increase His perfection, but God delights in obedience and rewards those who seek Him. David likewise confessed that his goodness did not enrich God but was directed toward the saints on earth (Psalm 16:2). Elihu’s words challenge the idea that righteousness gives a person grounds to accuse God, while directing attention to the human relationships in which moral choices bear visible fruit. Perfect righteousness is found in Jesus Christ, the Son of Man, whose obedience brought life and blessing to those who believe in Him.

Pro 11:11 By the blessing of the upright a city is built up…

Pro 11:10 When the righteous thrive, the city rejoices,…

Mat 5:16 In the same way, let your light shine before men,…

Job 35:9

Men cry out under great oppression;
Human suffering often produces desperate appeals for help. Israel cried to the Lord under Pharaoh’s brutal bondage in Egypt, and the Psalms preserve many prayers uttered from affliction and injustice. Yet Elihu’s larger argument warns that distress alone does not necessarily bring a person to seek God humbly. The oppressed may protest the cruelty of powerful people while failing to ask where God is, the Maker who gives songs in the night and teaches His creatures.

Exo 2:23-25 After a long time, the king of Egypt died.…

Psa 102:19-20 For He looked down from the heights of His sanctuary;…

Ecc 4:1 I looked, and I considered all the oppression under the sun.…

they plead for relief from the arm of the mighty.
The arm represents the force used by rulers and oppressors to dominate the weak. God sees such violence and will judge those who misuse authority, as the prophets repeatedly declared. Christ came proclaiming liberty to captives and rest to the burdened, but His deliverance reaches deeper than relief from earthly oppression: He rescues sinners from the power of sin and brings them into the righteous care of God.

Isa 49:24-25 Can the plunder be snatched from the mighty,…

Psa 10:15-18 Break the arm of the wicked and evildoer; call him to account…

Psa 37:39-40 The salvation of the righteous is from the LORD;…

Job 35:10

But no one asks,
Elihu describes those overwhelmed by oppression who cry out because of suffering but do not turn their hearts toward God. Affliction often exposes human need, yet pain alone does not always lead people to seek the Lord who alone can deliver and sustain them.

Psa 14:2 The LORD looks down from heaven upon the sons of men…

Psa 53:2 God looks down from heaven upon the sons of men…

Rom 3:11 There is no one who understands, no one who seeks God.

‘Where is God my Maker,
God is not distant from His creatures. He made mankind in His image and remains the righteous Judge and sovereign Lord over every life. Job’s suffering had raised agonizing questions, but Elihu calls attention to the greater question: whether sufferers are earnestly seeking the God who formed them.

Psa 95:6 O come, let us worship and bow down; let us kneel…

Deu 32:6 Is this how you repay the LORD, O foolish and senseless…

Isa 51:13 But you have forgotten the LORD, your Maker…

who gives us songs in the night,
God gives His people grounds for worship even during seasons of darkness, loss, and uncertainty. He brought Israel through the night of bondage, and Paul and Silas sang hymns while imprisoned at midnight. The Lord does not deny the reality of sorrow, but His presence and faithfulness can turn despair into praise.

Psa 42:8 The LORD decrees His loving devotion by day,…

Psa 77:6 At night I remembered my song; in my heart I mused…

Act 16:25 About midnight Paul and Silas were praying.…

Job 35:11

who teaches us more than the beasts of the earth
God created humanity in His image and endowed people with reason, moral accountability, and the capacity to know Him. Unlike animals, which live according to God-given instinct, men and women can seek their Maker, discern His works, and respond to His revelation. Elihu’s point is that sufferers should not merely cry out under oppression; they should ask where God is and seek the wisdom He gives. Dominion over the animals, entrusted to humanity at creation, carries the responsibility to live wisely before the Creator.

Job 12:7-8 But ask the animals, and they will instruct you;…

Pro 6:6-8 Walk in the manner of the ant, O slacker;…

Pro 30:24-28 Four things on earth are small, yet they are…

and makes us wiser than the birds of the air?’
The birds testify to God’s providential care, yet they do not possess the spiritual and moral understanding granted to humanity. God gives wisdom, and He calls people to use that gift by turning to Him in faith and humility. Job’s suffering had raised profound questions, but Elihu insists that God has not left mankind without knowledge of Himself. The capacity to understand God’s instruction makes indifference, pride, and prayerless complaint especially serious.

Job 28:21-28 It is hidden from the eyes of every living thing…

Pro 2:6 For the LORD gives wisdom; from His mouth…

Jas 1:5 Now if any of you lacks wisdom, he should ask God,…

Job 35:12

There they cry out,
This phrase suggests a situation of distress or suffering where individuals are calling out for help. In the context of the Book of Job, this reflects the cries of those who are oppressed or in anguish. The act of crying out is a common biblical motif, seen in Exodus 3:7 where God hears the cries of the Israelites in Egypt. It underscores the human condition of seeking divine intervention in times of trouble.

Psa 18:41 They cried for help, but there was no one to save them—…

Psa 107:13 Then they cried out to the LORD in their trouble,…

Isa 26:16 O LORD, they sought You in their distress;…

but He does not answer,
God’s apparent silence does not mean that He is indifferent to suffering or unaware of the oppressed. Elihu warns that cries centered only on relief, while ignoring God’s righteousness and authority, do not reflect true faith. Job himself had wrestled with such silence, as David later did in Psalm 22:2, but the Lord’s delay never places Him outside His wise and holy purposes.

Pro 1:28 Then they will call on me, but I will not answer;…

Mic 3:4 Then they will cry out to the LORD, but He will not answer…

Job 30:20 I cry out to You for help, but You do not answer…

because of the pride of evil men.
Pride characterizes those who exalt themselves, oppress others, and refuse God’s rule. Their wickedness corrupts both their actions and their prayers, for sin separates people from the fellowship of God (Isaiah 59:2). Pride leads toward ruin (Proverbs 16:18), while God gives grace to the humble who turn to Him.

Psa 10:4 In his pride the wicked man does not seek Him;…

Psa 138:6 Though the LORD is on high, He attends to the lowly;…

Pro 15:29 The LORD is far from the wicked, but He hears the prayer…

Job 35:13

Surely God does not listen to empty pleas,
Elihu insists that God is not moved by cries that arise from pride, falsehood, or self-centered complaint. God hears every word, yet He does not receive as worship prayers that refuse truth, repentance, and humble dependence upon Him. Israel’s prophets likewise warned that unrepentant people could not cover injustice with religious words (Isaiah 1:15). The Lord desires a contrite heart and truth within the person who calls upon Him (Psalm 51:6, 17).

Isa 1:15 When you spread out your hands in prayer…

Psa 66:18 If I had cherished iniquity in my heart, the Lord would not…

Pro 28:9 Whoever turns his ear away from hearing the law…

and the Almighty does not take note of it.
The Almighty cannot be manipulated by repeated words, outward devotion, or human attempts to justify themselves before Him. He sees the heart, weighs motives, and acts in perfect justice (1 Samuel 16:7). Elihu’s words confront the assumption that suffering proves God is indifferent; God remains sovereign and attentive, but He does not grant approval to empty cries. Jesus similarly condemned vain repetition in prayer and taught His disciples to come before the Father with sincere trust (Matthew 6:7–8).

Jer 14:12 Although they may fast, I will not listen to their cry;…

Pro 1:28 Then they will call on me, but I will not answer;…

Mic 3:4 Then they will cry out to the LORD, but He will not answer…

Job 35:14

How much less, then,
Elihu presses his argument from God’s refusal to regard empty cries to Job’s complaint that God seems absent. The Almighty is not subject to human demands, and suffering does not place Him under obligation to explain His purposes immediately.

Job 4:19 how much more those who dwell in houses of clay,…

Job 9:14 How then can I answer Him or choose my arguments?…

Job 25:6 how much less man, who is but a maggot, and the son of man,…

when you say that you do not see Him,
Job’s pain had made God’s silence seem like distance. Yet God’s hiddenness does not mean His absence. The Lord remained aware of Job’s affliction and was accomplishing purposes beyond Job’s present understanding.

Job 9:11 Were He to pass by me, I would not see Him;…

Exo 33:20 But He added, “You cannot see My face, for no one can see…

Jhn 1:18 No one has ever seen God, but the one and only Son…

that your case is before Him
Job’s cause had not been overlooked. God sees every injustice, hears every cry, and judges with perfect knowledge. Though Job could not yet perceive the outcome, his circumstances stood openly before the righteous Judge.

Job 23:4 I would plead my case before Him and fill my mouth…

Psa 142:2 I pour out my complaint before Him; I reveal my trouble…

Isa 43:26 Remind Me, let us argue the matter together.…

and you must wait for Him,
Elihu calls Job to trust God rather than demand an immediate answer. Waiting on the Lord requires faith that His timing is wise and His judgment is sure. Job would eventually learn that God had not abandoned him, even while His purposes remained concealed.

Psa 37:7 Be still before the LORD and wait patiently for Him;…

Psa 62:1 For the choirmaster. According to Jeduthun.…

Mic 7:7 But as for me, I will look to the LORD;…

Job 35:15

and further, that in His anger
Elihu continues his rebuke by insisting that God’s apparent silence does not mean indifference. Divine anger is the holy opposition of God to sin, pride, and false accusations against His righteous rule.

Psa 103:8 The LORD is compassionate and gracious, slow to anger…

Exo 34:6 Then the LORD passed in front of Moses and called out:…

Nam 1:3 The LORD is slow to anger and great in power;…

He has not punished
Job’s suffering had been severe, yet Elihu argues that God had not dealt with Job according to the full measure of his presumptuous words. God’s restraint displays patience, even when He disciplines His servants.

Ecc 8:11 When the sentence for a crime is not speedily executed…

Psa 103:10 He has not dealt with us according to our sins…

2Pe 3:9 The Lord is not slow in keeping His promise…

or taken much notice of folly!
Elihu regards Job’s repeated challenges concerning God’s justice as folly born of grief and limited understanding. Job did not see the whole counsel of God, but neither did Elihu. The Lord would soon answer Job, not by condemning him as a secret hypocrite, but by revealing His wisdom, power, and sovereign care over all creation.

Act 17:30 Although God overlooked the ignorance of earlier times,…

Psa 50:21 You have done these things, and I kept silent;…

Act 14:16 In past generations, He let all nations go their own way.

Job 35:16

So Job opens his mouth in vain
Elihu concludes that Job’s complaints have moved beyond lament into words that seem to charge God with injustice. Job’s suffering was real, and his cries were not ignored by God, yet grief had led him to speak as though the Lord owed him an explanation. God later affirms that Job had spoken more rightly than his friends about Him (Job 42:7), but the Lord also corrects Job’s limited understanding in chapters 38–41.

Job 15:2-3 “Does a wise man answer with empty counsel”…

Job 16:3 Is there no end to your long-winded speeches?…

Ecc 5:2-3 Do not be quick to speak, and do not be hasty…

and multiplies words without knowledge.”
Elihu, the younger observer who speaks after Job’s three friends fall silent, warns that many words cannot supply wisdom where human knowledge is lacking. Job could not see the heavenly purposes behind his trials, including Satan’s challenge and God’s refining work. The Lord’s answer reveals that neither Job nor his companions possessed the knowledge required to judge God’s governance of creation. Suffering calls for honest prayer, but also for humility before the God whose wisdom exceeds human understanding.

Job 34:35 ‘Job speaks without knowledge; his words lack insight.’

Job 38:2 Who is this who obscures My counsel by words…

Pro 10:19 When words are many, sin is unavoidable,…
