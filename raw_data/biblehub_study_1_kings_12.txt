Rebellion against Rehoboam

1 Kings 12:1

Then Rehoboam went to Shechem,
Rehoboam, Solomon’s son and David’s grandson, traveled to Shechem for the public confirmation of his reign. Shechem stood in the hill country of Ephraim, near the center of the land, and carried deep covenant significance. Abraham had built an altar there, Jacob had called his household to put away foreign gods there, and Joshua had renewed Israel’s covenant with the Lord there (Genesis 12:6–7; 35:2–4; Joshua 24:1–25). By meeting at Shechem rather than Jerusalem, Rehoboam came before the northern tribes in a place closely associated with their shared identity and history.

This idolatry endured through the history of the Northern Kingdom, was repeatedly condemned by the prophets, and helped lead Israel toward judgment and exile.

1 Kings 12:31

Jeroboam also built shrines on the high places
Jeroboam established alternative centers of worship throughout his kingdom, rejecting the place God had chosen for Israel’s sacrifices and worship in Jerusalem (Deuteronomy 12:5–14). These shrines supported the calf worship he had introduced at Bethel and Daniel and made religious compromise accessible to the people. Political fear drove Jeroboam to reshape Israel’s worship, but his efforts to secure the kingdom apart from obedience to God became a lasting source of idolatry.

And Jeroboam ordained a feast on the fifteenth day of the eighth month,
Jeroboam established a festival one month after the Feast of Tabernacles, which God had appointed for the fifteenth day of the seventh month (Leviticus 23:34). The altered date was not a harmless adjustment but part of a self-made worship system designed to bind the northern tribes to his rule and keep them from going to Jerusalem.
