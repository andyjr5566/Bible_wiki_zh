Prayer for the Son

John 17:1

When Jesus had spoken these things,
Jesus concluded His farewell teaching in John 13–16, delivered on the night before His crucifixion. He had prepared the disciples for His departure, commanded them to love one another, promised the Holy Spirit, warned of coming persecution, and assured them of His victory over the world. The prayer that follows opens the final movement before His arrest in the garden.

Jhn 16:33 I have told you these things so that in Me…

Jhn 14:31 But I do exactly what the Father has commanded Me,…

Mat 26:1 When Jesus had finished saying all these things,…

He lifted up His eyes to heaven
Jesus prayed openly and reverently before His disciples, directing His attention to the Father who reigns in heaven. His posture displayed dependence, confidence, and communion with the Father at the decisive hour of His earthly mission.

Jhn 11:41 So they took away the stone. Then Jesus lifted His eyes…

Mrk 7:34 And looking up to heaven, He sighed deeply…

Psa 123:1 A song of ascents. I lift up my eyes to You…

and said, “Father,
Jesus addressed God with the intimacy of the eternal Son speaking to the Father. Throughout John’s Gospel, the Father and the Son act in perfect unity: the Father sent the Son, the Son reveals the Father, and the Son accomplishes the Father’s saving will. Through Christ, those who believe are also brought into the family of God.

Luk 10:21 At that time Jesus rejoiced in the Holy Spirit…

Mat 11:25 At that time Jesus declared, “I praise You, Father…

Mat 26:39 Going a little farther, He fell facedown and prayed,…

the hour has come.
The appointed time had arrived for Jesus’ suffering, death, resurrection, and return to the Father. Earlier, Jesus had said that His hour had not yet come; now He willingly enters the events ordained in God’s redemptive purpose. The cross was not an interruption of His mission but its appointed climax.

Jhn 12:23 But Jesus replied, “The hour has come for the Son of Man…

Jhn 12:27 Now My soul is troubled, and what shall I say?…

Mat 26:45 Then He returned to the disciples and said,…

Glorify Your Son,
Jesus asked the Father to reveal His glory through the cross and resurrection. Human eyes would see humiliation, rejection, and death, yet the crucifixion would display the Son’s obedience, love, authority, and sacrificial power. Isaiah’s portrait of the suffering Servant finds fulfillment in the Messiah who is wounded for the sins of His people and afterward exalted.

Jhn 13:31-32 When Judas had gone out, Jesus said, “Now the Son of Man is glorified,…

2Pe 1:17 For He received honor and glory from God the Father…

Heb 2:9 But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower…

that Your Son may glorify You.
The Son’s saving work reveals the Father’s holiness, justice, mercy, and love. Jesus’ obedience unto death accomplishes the Father’s will and makes eternal life possible for all whom the Father gives to the Son. Father and Son are glorified together in the redemption of sinners.

Jhn 14:13 And I will do whatever you ask in My name…

Jhn 15:8 This is to My Father’s glory, that you bear much fruit…

Php 2:11 and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord,…

John 17:2

For You granted Him authority over all people,
The Father has entrusted the incarnate Son with authority over all humanity. Jesus exercises the dominion promised to the Son of Man in Daniel 7:14 and later declared after His resurrection: “All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth” (Matthew 28:18). His authority is given within the unity of the Father’s saving purpose and includes the right to judge, rule, redeem, and preserve His people.

Mat 28:18 Then Jesus came to them and said, “All authority in heaven…

Jhn 3:35 The Father loves the Son and has placed all things…

Dan 7:14 And He was given dominion, glory, and kingship…

so that He may give eternal life
Jesus uses His sovereign authority not merely to condemn sinners but to grant life to those dead in sin. Eternal life begins in knowing the true God and Jesus Christ whom He sent (John 17:3), and it continues forever in resurrection fellowship with Him. The life He gives rests upon His atoning death and victorious resurrection.

Jhn 10:28 I give them eternal life, and they will never perish.…

Jhn 5:21 For just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life,…

1Jn 5:11 And this is that testimony: God has given us eternal life,…

to all those You have given Him.
The Father gives a people to the Son, and the Son receives, saves, and keeps them. Jesus had already promised that all whom the Father gives Him will come to Him and will never be cast out (John 6:37–39). God’s sovereign grace does not weaken the certainty of salvation; it establishes it, for those entrusted to Christ belong securely to His care.

Jhn 6:37 Everyone the Father gives Me will come to Me,…

Jhn 6:39 And this is the will of Him who sent Me,…

Jhn 10:29 My Father who has given them to Me is greater than all…

John 17:3

Now this is eternal life,
Eternal life begins in the present for everyone who receives the Son by faith. It is more than endless existence; it is participation in the life of God, secured through Christ and brought to fulfillment in the resurrection and the age to come.

1Jn 5:11-12 And this is that testimony: God has given us eternal life,…

Jhn 6:47 Truly, truly, I tell you, he who believes has eternal life.

Jesus describes salvation as a real, covenant relationship with the Father. Such knowledge includes trusting God, receiving His revealed truth, obeying His will, and living before Him as His redeemed people.

The Father alone is the living and sovereign God, utterly distinct from idols and every false claim to deity. Jesus’ prayer stands in continuity with Israel’s confession that the Lord is one, while preparing His disciples to understand the Father’s unity with the Son.

whom You have sent.
The Father sent the Son into the world to accomplish redemption through His death and resurrection. Jesus’ completed mission provides the only way sinners may be reconciled to God and receive eternal life.

John 17:4

I have glorified You on earth
Jesus revealed the Father’s holiness, mercy, truth, and saving power through His sinless obedience, teaching, compassion, and signs. As God’s glory rested visibly on Sinai (Exodus 24:16–17), the glory of God was made known in the incarnate Son, who made the Father known to humanity (John 1:18). Jesus fulfilled the servant’s calling to be a light to the nations and to bring God’s salvation to the ends of the earth (Isaiah 49:6).

The Father’s work included proclaiming the kingdom, calling disciples, healing the afflicted, confronting sin, and revealing God’s authority over sickness, demons, nature, and death. Jesus had completed all that was appointed for His public ministry, and His sacrificial death stood immediately before Him as the certain culmination of His obedience. Isaiah 53 foretold the suffering Servant who would bear sin and accomplish God’s redemptive purpose. The cross would bring the work to its fullest completion, as Jesus later declared, “It is finished” (John 19:30).

The Son carried out the mission entrusted to Him by the Father, not according to human ambition but in perfect unity with the Father’s will. Jesus repeatedly testified that He had been sent by the Father and performed the works the Father gave Him (John 5:36; 6:38). His obedience fulfilled God’s covenant promise that through Abraham’s seed all nations would be blessed (Genesis 12:3). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus secured salvation for His people and prepared His apostles to bear witness to Him among the nations.

Jesus turns from praying for His disciples to His own approaching return to the Father. Father reveals the eternal, personal communion between the Son and the Father, a relationship that existed before creation and remains unbroken even as the cross draws near.

Jesus asks to be received openly into the majesty proper to Him as the eternal Son. His death, resurrection, and ascension would reveal His glory through obedient sacrifice and would culminate in His exaltation at the Father’s right hand.

The Son did not begin at Bethlehem. Before taking human flesh, He shared the Father’s glory, honor, and divine life. Though He humbled Himself in the incarnation and accepted the form of a servant, He never ceased to be fully God.

Prayer for the Disciples

John 17:6

I have revealed Your name
Jesus made the Father known by displaying His holy, merciful, righteous, and faithful character in His words and works. God had revealed His name to Israel through His covenant deeds and through His declaration of compassion, justice, and steadfast love in Exodus 34:6–7. In the incarnate Son, the invisible God was seen in human history; whoever had seen Jesus had seen the Father (John 14:9).

The disciples were called from the fallen order of unbelief and rebellion that stands opposed to God. Their faith did not begin in human initiative but in the Father’s gracious purpose. As Israel had been chosen to belong to the Lord among the nations, these men were set apart for Christ and for the mission that would carry the gospel into the world.

The disciples belonged to the Father before they were entrusted to the Son for redemption, instruction, and service. God’s saving purpose rests upon His sovereign claim over His people. Their security and calling were grounded in the Father’s covenant faithfulness rather than their own strength.

The Father’s gift of believers to the Son reveals the perfect unity of purpose within the Godhead. Jesus receives His people as the Good Shepherd, guards them, teaches them, and prepares them to continue His witness after His return to the Father. The disciples would become foundational witnesses of Christ’s death, resurrection, and lordship.

and they have kept Your word.
Their obedience was not sinless, for they would soon falter in fear, yet they had received Jesus’ teaching as God’s truth and remained with Him when many turned away. Keeping God’s word marked genuine faith and covenant loyalty. Their persevering attachment to Christ demonstrated that the Father’s work in them was real and prepared them for the costly service ahead.

John 17:7

The disciples have come to recognize that Jesus’ words, works, and authority reveal the Father who sent Him. Their understanding remains incomplete before the cross and resurrection, yet they have received the central truth that Jesus is not acting independently. The fuller illumination promised through the Holy Spirit would prepare them to bear witness after Pentecost.

The Father entrusted the Son with the words to speak, the works to accomplish, the disciples to keep, and the authority necessary to complete redemption. Jesus’ dependence upon the Father belongs to His incarnate mission, while His unity with the Father reveals the eternal fellowship of the Son with God. His ministry fulfills the Father’s saving purpose rather than a merely human religious mission.

Jesus openly attributes all that He has received in His earthly ministry to the Father’s will and gift. His teaching, miracles, and coming sacrifice therefore carry divine authority. Isaiah foretold the Messiah as the One sent and anointed by the Lord, and Hebrews identifies the Son as the perfect revelation of God’s glory. The disciples’ recognition of the Father as the source of Christ’s mission becomes foundational for their own calling as His witnesses.

Jesus faithfully delivered the revelation entrusted to Him by the Father. His teaching carried divine authority because He spoke in perfect unity with the One who sent Him. Like the prophets who proclaimed God’s word, Jesus revealed God’s truth; yet He did so as the eternal Son who perfectly makes the Father known.

The disciples welcomed Jesus’ teaching as God’s revelation rather than merely human instruction. Their reception involved more than hearing; they accepted His words in faith and began to order their lives around them. The disciples had come to recognize that Jesus’ origin was in the Father. His works, teaching, holiness, and fulfillment of Scripture testified that He was not acting independently or claiming authority for Himself. Their conviction marked a decisive movement from confusion toward faith in His divine mission.

Faith in Jesus includes believing that the Father sent Him into the world to accomplish salvation. Jesus came not merely as a teacher or prophet, but as the promised Messiah and Savior, sent in love to reveal the Father and to give His life for His people.

John 17:9

I ask on their behalf. I do not ask on behalf of the world,
Jesus turns in prayer to the men who had walked with Him, received His teaching, and would soon bear witness to His death and resurrection. Facing His departure through the cross, resurrection, and ascension, He entrusts them to the Father’s care. His prayer reveals the mediating work fulfilled ultimately through His sacrifice and continued in His exalted priesthood (1 Timothy 2:5; Hebrews 7:25). The immediate concern of Jesus’ prayer is not the rebellious world system that rejects the Father and the Son, but the preservation and sanctification of those sent into that hostile setting. Jesus does not deny God’s love for the world or the offer of salvation through His coming (John 3:16–17). Rather, He prays particularly for those who will carry the gospel into the world after His departure.

Jhn 15:19 If you were of the world, it would love you as its own…

1Jn 5:19 We know that we are of God, and that the whole world…

but on behalf of those You have given Me;
The disciples belong to Christ because the Father has drawn and entrusted them to Him. Their calling rests upon God’s purpose rather than human initiative. Though they are weak, confused, and soon to be tested, Jesus’ intercession confirms that their future service depends upon the Father’s preserving grace.

The disciples are the Father’s own covenant people, precious to Him and under His sovereign care. Their belonging to the Father also establishes the unity of the Father and the Son in redemption: those given to the Son are not removed from the Father’s possession, but are kept by both. Their security rests in the God who claims them as His own.

Jesus prays as the eternal Son who possesses nothing independently or competitarily against the Father. All entrusted to Him belongs fully to the Father, revealing perfect unity of will, purpose, and divine nature. The Son’s earthly obedience, including His approaching death, fulfills the Father’s redemptive plan.

Though their faith would soon be tested, Christ would be glorified through their witness, obedience, and proclamation of the gospel. The same remains true for all who belong to Him: His saving work is displayed as His people bear His name and live in faithfulness to His truth.

The disciples received not merely instruction but God’s authoritative message, fulfilled in Christ’s life, death, resurrection, and coming glory.

Jesus came from the Father and carried out a mission governed by the Father’s will, not by earthly power, ambition, or approval. His kingdom is not of this world in origin or character (John 18:36). Those united to Him share His distinct calling: to live faithfully within the world without being conformed to it, awaiting the day when they will be with Him in His eternal kingdom.

John 17:15

I am not asking that You take them out of the world,
Jesus does not seek escape for His disciples but their faithful presence in a hostile world. They would remain to bear witness to His death and resurrection, establish the church, and proclaim the Gospel to all nations. Their mission anticipates the Great Commission and reflects God’s purpose for believers to live among unbelievers without adopting the world’s rebellion against Him.

Jesus entrusts His disciples to the Father’s preserving power against Satan, who seeks to deceive, tempt, accuse, and destroy faith. The disciples would face persecution and spiritual conflict, yet they would not be abandoned to the enemy’s power. God’s protection does not always remove trials, but it guards His people so that they may endure, remain faithful to Christ, and fulfill the work He has given them.

John 17:16

They are not of the world,
Jesus identifies His disciples as belonging to God’s kingdom rather than to the fallen order that resists His rule. Their new identity flows from having received and believed the Father’s word (John 17:6–8). Though they remain among unbelieving people, their loyalties, conduct, and hope are no longer governed by the world’s rebellion, pride, and passing desires (Romans 12:2; 1 John 2:15–17).

The disciples share in Christ’s separation because they belong to Him. Jesus came into the world as the Father’s sent Son, yet He was never shaped by its sin or subject to its claims. His people are called to reflect that holy allegiance while continuing His mission in the world. Their distinct life does not require withdrawal from people, but faithful obedience to God amid a hostile culture, trusting that Christ has overcome the world (John 16:33).

John 17:17

Sanctify them by the truth;
Jesus asked the Father to set His disciples apart for holy service as they carried His message into a hostile world. Their consecration would involve moral purity, but it would also prepare them for the apostolic mission entrusted to them. As priests and sacred objects were dedicated to God under the old covenant, Christ’s followers were to belong wholly to God and serve His redemptive purposes. The Holy Spirit continues this sanctifying work in believers, conforming them to obedience and equipping them for their calling.

Your word is truth.
God’s revealed word is wholly reliable, authoritative, and free from deception. The Scriptures disclose God’s character, expose sin, renew the mind, and direct believers in righteousness. Jesus, the eternal Word made flesh, perfectly embodies the truth God has spoken. In contrast to the falsehood and corruption of the world, God’s word provides the unchanging standard by which His people are formed and sent.

John 17:18

As You sent Me into the world,
Jesus came from the Father in fulfillment of God’s promised redemption, entering humanity without ceasing to be divine. His earthly ministry revealed the Father, proclaimed the kingdom, bore witness to the truth, and led to His sacrificial death for sinners. Isaiah foretold the anointed Servant sent to bring good news, bind the brokenhearted, and proclaim liberty (Isaiah 61:1). In John’s Gospel, Jesus is repeatedly identified as the One sent by the Father, the Light of the World and the Savior whose coming exposes sin while offering eternal life.

Jhn 3:17 For God did not send His Son into the world…

Gal 4:4 But when the time had fully come, God sent His Son,…

I have also sent them into the world.
Jesus commissions His disciples to continue His work among people who remain alienated from God. They are not sent to adopt the world’s rebellion but to bear faithful witness to Christ and proclaim forgiveness through His name. Their mission extends beyond Israel to all nations, as later commanded after the resurrection (Matthew 28:19–20). The Holy Spirit would empower them for this calling, beginning at Jerusalem and reaching the ends of the earth (Acts 1:8). Through the apostles and the church founded on their testimony, Christ’s saving message continues to be carried into the world.

John 17:19

Jesus consecrates Himself to the Father’s redemptive purpose, moving willingly toward the cross. Though entirely holy and without sin, He sets Himself apart as both High Priest and sacrifice, offering His own life for His people (Hebrews 7:26–27). His approaching death is not an accident of history but the fulfillment of the Father’s will and the mission for which He came.

Christ’s self-offering secures the setting apart of His disciples for God. Those who belong to Him are no longer defined by the world from which He has called them, but are made His people, devoted to His service. Their holiness rests first on Christ’s sacrifice and is worked out through the cleansing and preserving ministry of the Holy Spirit (1 Thessalonians 5:23).

The disciples would soon bear witness in a hostile world, and their faithfulness required more than human resolve; they needed to be formed by the Word Christ had given them. Through union with the crucified and risen Lord, believers are set apart to live in obedience to God and to testify to His saving truth.

Prayer for All Believers

John 17:20

I am not asking on behalf of them alone,
Jesus prayed first for the apostles who had received His words, believed that He came from the Father, and would soon witness His death and resurrection. On the eve of the crucifixion, He committed them to the Father’s care and prepared them for their mission in a hostile world. His prayer reveals His continuing priestly ministry for those entrusted to Him (Hebrews 7:25).

but also on behalf of those who will believe in Me through their message,
Jesus included every future believer who would come to faith through the apostolic proclamation of the gospel. The apostles’ message, preserved in Scripture and proclaimed by the church, bears witness to Christ’s death for sins, resurrection, and saving lordship. The faith of later generations rests on the same authoritative testimony that established the early church (Acts 2:42). Jesus’ intercession reaches beyond the upper room to all who believe in Him through the gospel.

John 17:21

that all of them may be one,
Jesus prays for the unity of all who believe through the apostles’ witness. Such unity is spiritual and doctrinal, grounded in the truth He has given, not merely in outward association. The early churches were often threatened by division, pride, and false teaching, as Paul addressed at Corinth and elsewhere. Believers form one body under one Lord, sharing one faith, one Spirit, and one hope.

The unity of Christ’s people is to reflect, though never equal, the perfect fellowship of the Father and the Son. The Father and the Son are distinct persons, yet wholly united in divine nature, will, love, and purpose. Jesus’ prayer reveals His eternal relationship with the Father and confirms that His saving mission proceeded from the Father’s will.

Through faith in Christ, believers are brought into fellowship with the Father and the Son. They remain creatures and do not become divine, yet they receive eternal life, the indwelling Holy Spirit, and a new identity in Christ. This union produces love, obedience, holiness, and mutual care among those redeemed by the same Savior.

The visible love and faithful unity of Christ’s people bear witness that Jesus is the One sent by the Father. A divided church contradicts its message of reconciliation, while a people joined in truth and love displays the power of the gospel. Their shared life points beyond human achievement to the Son whom the Father gave for the salvation of the world.

John 17:22

I have given them the glory You gave Me,
Jesus shares with His disciples the glory entrusted to Him by the Father: the revelation of God’s character, the privilege of knowing Him, and participation in the mission accomplished through the Son. In John’s Gospel, Christ’s glory is especially displayed in His obedient death, resurrection, and return to the Father (John 12:23; 13:31–32). Believers do not become divine, but through union with Christ they are transformed to reflect His holiness, love, and truth. The glory once associated with God’s dwelling in the tabernacle and temple now rests upon the people Christ has redeemed, who become God’s dwelling place by the Spirit (Exodus 40:34–35; 1 Corinthians 3:16).

so that they may be one as We are one—
The Father and the Son possess perfect unity in love, purpose, truth, and will. Jesus prays that His followers will display a corresponding spiritual unity through their shared life in Him. Such oneness is not merely institutional agreement, but faithfulness to the apostolic gospel, mutual love, and obedience to Christ’s commands. The Church’s unity testifies before the world that the Father sent the Son and that His saving work creates one redeemed people from every nation (John 13:35; Ephesians 4:3–6; Revelation 7:9–10).

The Father’s eternal fellowship with the Son becomes the source of the believer’s life in Christ, without erasing the distinction between Creator and creature. Christ lives in His people through the Holy Spirit, producing the new life described in Galatians 2:20.
