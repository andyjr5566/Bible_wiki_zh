Jewish survivors had been scattered beyond Judah through flight, deportation, and the upheaval caused by Babylon’s conquest. Moab, Ammon, and Edom lay east and southeast of Judah and had long-standing, often hostile relationships with Israel. Edom had rejoiced over Jerusalem’s fall (Psalm 137:7; Obadiah 10–14), yet Jews had found refuge even among former enemies. Their dispersion fulfilled the covenant warnings that disobedience would bring scattering among the nations (Deuteronomy 28:64), while their continued existence testified that God had not abandoned His people.
