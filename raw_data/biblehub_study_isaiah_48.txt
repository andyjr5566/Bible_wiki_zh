Israel’s Stubbornness

Isaiah 48:1

“Listen to this, O house of Jacob,
The Lord summons the covenant people to give serious attention to His charge. Jacob’s descendants bear the history of God’s promises, deliverance, and discipline, yet their covenant identity has not produced covenant faithfulness.

Israel was the name God gave Jacob after his wrestling with the Lord, marking him as one who belonged to God and lived under His rule. The nation carried that honored name, but the name itself could not substitute for obedient faith.

Judah identifies the southern kingdom, centered in Jerusalem and the Davidic house. From Judah would come David’s royal line and, in God’s appointed time, Jesus Christ, the promised Messiah. Their heritage placed them near the visible center of God’s covenant worship, making their unfaithfulness especially serious.

Isaiah 48:2

who indeed call yourselves after the holy city
Jerusalem bore God’s name as the place of the Temple, sacrifice, and covenant worship. Israel’s association with the holy city gave them a visible religious identity, yet Isaiah confronts the danger of claiming holiness while resisting the Lord in heart and conduct. Jesus likewise condemned religious leaders whose outward appearance concealed inward corruption (Matthew 23:27–28).

Israel professed dependence on the God who redeemed them from Egypt, gave them the land, and bound Himself to them by covenant. Their history, however, repeatedly revealed divided trust through idolatry, political alliances, and disobedience. Reliance on God is genuine only when it produces faithfulness to His word.

The LORD of Hosts rules over the armies of heaven and every power on earth. His name declares that Jerusalem’s God is not a local deity or an empty religious tradition, but the sovereign King able to judge hypocrisy, preserve His people, and fulfill every promise.

Isaiah 48:3

I foretold the former things long ago;
God had announced His purposes in advance, including Israel’s deliverance from Egypt, the rise and fall of kingdoms, and His covenant dealings with His people. Fulfilled prophecy distinguished the God of Israel from idols, which could neither know the future nor direct the course of history. Israel’s past bore witness that the Lord rules over nations and remains faithful to His covenant promises.

Isa 45:21 Speak up and present your case—yes, let them take counsel…

Isa 46:10 I declare the end from the beginning, and from ancient times…

they came out of My mouth and I proclaimed them.
The Lord did not leave His people without testimony. He made His will known through Moses and the prophets, calling Israel to trust and obey Him. God’s spoken word carries His authority and accomplishes His purpose, just as His command brought creation into being. The prophets spoke what God gave them, and their messages established that history unfolds according to His counsel.

Isa 45:23 By Myself I have sworn; truth has gone out from My mouth,…

Isa 55:11 So My word that proceeds from My mouth will not return to Me…

Suddenly I acted,
God’s appointed time often arrives when human strength and expectation have failed. The exodus came through decisive judgment upon Egypt, and later deliverance from Babylon would come through the unexpected rise of Cyrus. No delay, resistance, or worldly power can prevent the Lord from carrying out what He has determined.

Isa 29:5 But your many foes will be like fine dust…

Isa 30:13 This iniquity of yours is like a breach about to fail…

and they came to pass.
Fulfillment proved that God’s declarations were not empty words. What He promised concerning judgment, preservation, and restoration occurred exactly as He had said. Israel therefore had every reason to abandon idolatry and rely upon the Lord alone. The certainty of His past acts also assures His people that His remaining promises, including the reign of Christ and the final redemption of His people, will be fulfilled.

Isaiah 48:4

For I knew that you are stubborn;
The Lord’s knowledge of Israel’s rebellion was not based merely on past failures but on His perfect knowledge of their hearts. Though He had delivered them from Egypt, sustained them in the wilderness, and bound them to Himself by covenant, they repeatedly turned toward idols and resisted His commands. Their history showed that outward privilege and religious knowledge could not replace a responsive heart.

Israel refused to bow under the gracious rule of God. Scripture repeatedly describes the nation as stiff-necked, especially in connection with the golden calf and its wilderness rebellions. An iron neck pictures determined resistance to correction, submission, and repentance. God calls His people not only to hear His word but to yield themselves to His authority.

Jer 19:15 This is what the LORD of Hosts, the God of Israel, says:…

Their defiance was openly fixed and unashamed, like metal that would not bend or break. Israel’s hardened disposition stood in contrast to the humility God required from those who bore His name. Yet the Lord exposed their obstinacy in order to summon them from self-will and idolatry to genuine faithfulness.

Isaiah 48:5

Therefore I declared it to you long ago;
The Lord had revealed His purposes through His prophets before Israel saw their fulfillment. His advance declaration demonstrated that history unfolds under His rule, not by accident or by the power of nations. The coming Babylonian captivity and the restoration of Israel were known to God and announced according to His covenant faithfulness.

Fulfilled prophecy distinguished the God of Israel from every false god. Babylon would conquer Judah, yet Babylon would not have the final word; the Lord would preserve a remnant and bring His people home. God declares “the end from the beginning” and accomplishes all His purpose (Isaiah 46:10).

Israel’s repeated attraction to idols made such warnings necessary. The Lord removed every excuse for assigning His works to objects made by human hands. His prior announcements called His people to recognize Him alone as the living and sovereign God.

Idols could neither speak, foresee, save, nor govern the course of events. Israel had often borrowed the worship practices of surrounding nations, despite the covenant command to have no other gods before the Lord. God’s acts in judgment and restoration exposed the emptiness of those rival claims.

Psa 115:4-8 Their idols are silver and gold, made by the hands of men.…

Isa 44:9-10 All makers of idols are nothing, and the things…

my carved image and molten god has ordained it.’
Carved wood, shaped stone, and cast metal could not ordain the fall of kingdoms or the return of exiles. The contrast between lifeless images and the Lord who speaks and fulfills His word summons God’s people to wholehearted trust. Jesus Christ, the true image of the invisible God, fully reveals the God whom no idol can represent.

The Lord had repeatedly declared His purposes concerning Israel’s sin, judgment, preservation, and restoration. Israel possessed the testimony of the Law and the words of the prophets, yet familiarity with God’s revelation had not always produced faith or obedience.

The fulfilled events stood as public evidence that the God of Israel alone directs history. His warnings of judgment and His promises of deliverance proved true, calling the people to consider His works rather than credit idols, nations, or human wisdom.

Israel’s release from Babylon, the downfall of Babylonian power, and the Lord’s continued purpose for His people would unfold according to God’s appointed time. Isaiah 48:10

God addressed Israel after exposing her stubbornness, idolatry, and repeated unbelief. The coming captivity in Babylon would be a discipline of covenant love, not a rejection of the people He had chosen. Isaiah 48:11

God would preserve and restore Israel because His covenant name was bound to the people He had chosen. Israel’s repeated rebellion deserved judgment, and the Babylonian exile demonstrated God’s holiness; yet their destruction would have invited the nations to conclude that Babylon’s gods had defeated the Lord. God’s mercy toward His people therefore displayed both His faithfulness and His sovereign purpose. Ezekiel likewise records that the Lord acted for the sake of His holy name among the nations (Ezekiel 20:9, 14, 22).

Israel’s salvation rested ultimately not on its merit but on God’s zeal for the honor of His own name.

Deliverance Promised to Israel

Isaiah 48:12

Listen to Me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I have called:
The Lord addresses the covenant people by the names of their patriarch, Jacob, whom God renamed Israel after preserving and transforming him (Genesis 32:28). Israel’s existence as a nation rested on God’s sovereign call, not human strength or merit. Surrounded by idolatry and facing exile because of persistent rebellion, the people needed to hear again the voice of the God who had chosen them and remained faithful to His covenant.

Isa 44:1 But now listen, O Jacob My servant, Israel…

Isa 43:1 But now, this is what the LORD says—He who created you……

Deu 6:4 Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is One.

I am He;
The Lord alone is the living, self-existent God. He is not like Babylon’s powerless idols, formed by human hands and unable to save. His unchanging identity guarantees the reliability of His warnings, promises, and purpose for Israel.

Isa 43:10 “You are My witnesses,” declares the LORD…

Deu 32:39 See now that I am He; there is no God besides Me…

Isa 46:4 Even to your old age, I will be the same,…

I am the first, and I am the last.
God stands before creation and beyond the end of history, ruling every nation, kingdom, and event. He had declared Israel’s coming deliverance and would accomplish it according to His word. Revelation applies this title to Jesus Christ, the Alpha and Omega, affirming His eternal deity and sovereign authority over all things.

Isa 44:6 Thus says the LORD, the King and Redeemer of Israel…

Isaiah 48:13

Surely My own hand founded the earth,
God identifies Himself as the earth’s Maker, not merely its overseer. The world rests on His purposeful act of creation, as Genesis 1:1 declares. His hand portrays personal power and sovereign care: the Creator is distinct from creation yet actively sustains it. The earth therefore belongs to Him, and every human claim to power, land, or security remains subject to His authority.

Isa 45:12 It is I who made the earth and created man…

and My right hand spread out the heavens;
The heavens, with their vast expanse and ordered lights, display the strength and wisdom of God. Psalm 19:1 celebrates their continual witness to His glory. What appears immeasurable to humanity is the work of God’s right hand, the biblical image of effective power and royal authority. The New Testament identifies the Son as the divine agent of creation, for all things were made through Him and hold together in Him (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16–17).

The earth and heavens answer their Creator as an assembled host responding to its commander. God’s word establishes and governs the created order; no force in heaven or earth can resist His summons. The same Lord who commands the stars directs history toward His appointed end. His unfailing word gives assurance to His people that His promises of redemption, restoration, and judgment will stand.

Cyrus conquered Babylon in 539 BC and permitted Jewish exiles to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple (Ezra 1:1–4). Though Cyrus did not belong to Israel, he stood under God’s sovereign direction and fulfilled God’s purpose in judging Babylon and restoring His people.

The fall of Babylon revealed that no empire can resist the arm of the Lord, and it foreshadows His final victory over every arrogant power opposed to His kingdom.

Isaiah 48:17

Thus says the LORD your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel:
The LORD speaks as Israel’s covenant God, the One who redeemed the nation from Egyptian bondage and remained committed to His promises despite her unfaithfulness. As the Holy One of Israel, He is utterly righteous and distinct from all false gods, yet He has bound Himself to His people in covenant love. His redemption of Israel anticipates the greater redemption accomplished by the Messiah, who delivers His people from sin and judgment.

Isaiah 48:18

If only you had paid attention to My commandments,
The LORD laments Israel’s refusal to heed the covenant law given through Moses. Obedience would have kept the nation in the path of life and blessing set forth in Deuteronomy 28, while persistent rebellion led to judgment and exile under Babylon. God’s commandments were not burdensome restrictions but His righteous instruction for a people called to bear His name among the nations. The appeal exposes both Israel’s guilt and the steadfast mercy of God, who still addresses His disobedient people and calls them back to Himself.

A river supplied life to the land and continued in its course, making it a fitting picture of the abundance Israel forfeited through sin. The promised peace points beyond temporary national prosperity to the reign of the Messiah, the Prince of Peace, who reconciles sinners to God and will establish lasting peace in His kingdom.

Isaiah 48:19

Your descendants would have been as countless as the sand,
God had promised Abraham descendants as numerous as the stars and the sand on the seashore (Genesis 22:17). Israel’s rebellion did not cancel God’s covenant purposes, but disobedience brought discipline and curtailed blessings that could have been enjoyed in fullness. The sand of the seashore portrays a people beyond human calculation, spreading through generations under God’s faithful care.

and your offspring as numerous as its grains;
The Lord had intended Israel to flourish as a holy nation, displaying His wisdom and righteousness before the surrounding peoples (Deuteronomy 4:6–8). Numerous descendants were a covenant blessing, yet the greater purpose was that Abraham’s family would become the channel through which all nations would be blessed (Genesis 12:3). God preserved a remnant through judgment, ensuring that His promises would ultimately stand.

Isaiah 48:21

They did not thirst when He led them through the deserts;
The Lord sustained Israel throughout the wilderness journey after the exodus, where ordinary human resources could not preserve a nation. His guidance through the desert revealed both Israel’s dependence and His covenant faithfulness. Isaiah recalls that history to assure the exiles that the God who led their fathers through barren lands was able to lead them safely from Babylon.

At Horeb, when Israel lacked water, the Lord commanded Moses to strike the rock, and water was provided for the people and their livestock (Exodus 17:1–6). A later provision at Kadesh likewise displayed God’s mercy despite Israel’s rebellion (Numbers 20:1–13). The rock became a lasting witness that God can supply what His people cannot obtain for themselves.

Isaiah 48:22

“There is no peace,”
Peace with God cannot coexist with persistent rebellion against Him. Israel’s idolatry, political unbelief, and refusal to heed God’s servants had brought inner unrest and national instability. The promised restoration would not remove God’s moral distinction between those who return to Him and those who continue in sin. Christ gives His peace to those who receive Him and walk in His truth (John 14:27).
