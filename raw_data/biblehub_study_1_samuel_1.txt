Elkanah and His Wives

1 Samuel 1:1

Now there was a man named Elkanah
Elkanah stands at the beginning of Samuel’s account as a faithful Israelite whose household would become instrumental in a turning point for the nation. His name means “God has created” or “God has possessed.” He was the father of Samuel, whom God raised up as prophet, judge, and leader during Israel’s transition from the period of the judges to the monarchy.

Elkanah’s household reflected a practice permitted in Israel’s fallen social order but never established as God’s design for marriage. From creation, God joined one man and one woman in a covenant union (Genesis 2:24). Multiple wives repeatedly brought grief, jealousy, and division into families, as seen with Abraham, Jacob, and David.

one named Hannah and the other Peninnah.
Hannah, whose name means “grace” or “favor,” is named first, and the account soon centers on her suffering, faith, and prayer. Peninnah, whose name means “pearl” or “coral,” presence intensifies Hannah’s trial, for the two women lived within the same household but did not share the same experience of blessing or peace.

Israel’s men were commanded to appear before the LORD at the appointed feasts each year (Deuteronomy 16:16), and Elkanah’s faithfulness shows that devotion to God was not reserved for moments of crisis. Shiloh was Israel’s central place of worship during the period of the judges and the early life of Samuel. The tabernacle and the ark of the covenant were there, marking the place where God had chosen to make His name dwell among His people. Though no permanent temple yet stood, the LORD’s presence at Shiloh called Israel to approach Him with reverence and obedience.

Hophni and Phinehas held priestly office as sons of Eli, the high priest, but they treated the LORD’s offerings with contempt and exploited those who came to worship (1 Samuel 2:12–17). Their corruption did not lessen God’s holiness or excuse Israel from faithful worship. Their coming judgment would reveal the seriousness of spiritual leadership and prepare the way for Samuel’s faithful service. Their failure also points to the need for the perfect High Priest, Jesus Christ, who is holy, innocent, undefiled, and eternal (Hebrews 7:26–28).
