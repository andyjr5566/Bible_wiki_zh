Luke’s mention of both men exposes a religious leadership deeply entangled with Roman political control. Against that compromised setting, God raised a prophet outside Jerusalem’s institutions.

Luke 3:3

He went into all the region around the Jordan,
John the Baptist carried his ministry throughout the Jordan Valley, calling people from Judea, Jerusalem, and surrounding areas into the wilderness. The Jordan stood at the entrance to the Promised Land, where Israel had crossed under Joshua’s leadership after the waters were divided (Joshua 3:14–17). In the wilderness, away from the religious center of Jerusalem, John summoned Israel to prepare anew for the Lord’s coming. His work fulfilled Isaiah’s prophecy of a voice in the wilderness preparing the way for God’s arrival (Isaiah 40:3). The setting called the nation to leave behind complacency and meet God with humble readiness.

preaching a baptism of repentance
John proclaimed that people must turn from sin and return wholeheartedly to God. Those who received baptism publicly acknowledged their need for cleansing and their determination to live under God’s rule. John’s baptism was not merely a ceremonial washing but a decisive response to his call for repentance. It prepared those who believed his message to receive the Messiah, whose saving work would bring the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Luke 3:16). Genuine repentance would bear visible fruit in changed conduct, justice, mercy, and obedience.

Jesse of Bethlehem was the father of David, Israel’s king and the ancestor of the promised Messiah. From Jesse’s household, God chose David rather than his older brothers, showing that the Lord sees the heart and accomplishes His purposes through His chosen servant. Isaiah foretold that a shoot would rise from Jesse’s stump, pointing beyond David to Christ, the righteous King in whom David’s royal line reaches its fulfillment.
