The Beginning of Knowledge

Proverbs 1:1

These are the proverbs
Proverbs—short, pithy sayings that express general truths or practical precepts—are concise sayings that teach God’s wisdom for the ordinary decisions, relationships, work, speech, and moral choices of life. They state reliable patterns established by God’s order, calling His people to fear the Lord and walk in disciplined obedience.

of Solomon
Solomon received extraordinary wisdom from God after asking for an understanding heart to govern Israel (1 Kings 3:5–12). Scripture records that he spoke three thousand proverbs (1 Kings 4:32). The book preserves a major body of that God-given wisdom, though later chapters also include material from other wise men and royal instruction.

Solomon stood in the Davidic line through which God promised an enduring kingdom (2 Samuel 7:12–16). David’s covenant shaped Solomon’s calling: he was to rule under God’s authority and lead Israel in faithfulness. Jesus Christ, the greater Son of David, fulfills the promised everlasting kingship.

Solomon ruled the united tribes of Israel during a season of peace, prosperity, and international renown. He built the temple in Jerusalem as the central place of Israel’s worship. His royal position gave weight to his instruction, yet the wisdom presented in Proverbs ultimately comes from the Lord, the true King and source of all understanding.

Proverbs 1:2

for gaining wisdom and discipline,
Proverbs was given to form godly character through knowledge rightly applied and correction humbly received. Wisdom begins with reverence for the Lord and directs every area of life—speech, work, family, justice, sexuality, and relationships. Discipline includes the training and rebuke that turn a person from foolish and sinful paths. Solomon presents such instruction as necessary for living faithfully under God’s authority and for contributing to the well-being of the community.

for comprehending words of insight,
God’s wisdom enables believers to discern the moral and spiritual realities beneath circumstances, counsel, and human conduct. Insight receives instruction with understanding rather than merely collecting information. The proverbs train the reader to recognize truth, reject deception, and make decisions consistent with God’s purposes. Such discernment prepares the heart to hear God’s counsel and to walk wisely in a world marked by sin and confusion.

Proverbs 1:3

Wisdom equips God’s people to apply His truth faithfully in the ordinary decisions, relationships, duties, and trials of life. Proverbs presents wisdom as a disciplined way of living under the fear of the Lord, not as mere intelligence or experience. James likewise describes heavenly wisdom as pure, peaceable, gentle, merciful, and sincere (James 3:17).

Righteousness calls for conduct that conforms to God’s holy character and revealed standards. Israel’s covenant life was to display righteousness in private behavior, public leadership, worship, and care for others. Believers pursue such righteousness through union with Christ, who became righteousness for His people (1 Corinthians 1:30).

Jesus rebuked religious leaders who observed outward details while neglecting justice, mercy, and faithfulness (Matthew 23:23).

Proverbs offers moral discernment to the inexperienced, who are vulnerable to deception because they lack tested judgment. Prudence equips them to recognize consequences, resist sinful enticement, and order their conduct in the fear of the Lord. God’s law makes wise the simple (Psalm 19:7), turning teachable hearts from careless living toward righteous understanding.

Solomon’s instruction prepares the next generation to distinguish wisdom from folly before destructive patterns become established. Parents in Israel were commanded to teach God’s words diligently to their children (Deuteronomy 6:6–7), and Proverbs continues that covenant responsibility by forming youth for faithful life before the Lord.

Wisdom requires more than hearing memorable sayings; it calls for discernment to grasp the moral and spiritual realities they reveal. Proverbs communicate God’s ordering of life, while parabolic teaching often brings truth to light through comparison and story. Jesus later used parables to disclose the secrets of God’s kingdom to receptive hearers while exposing the hardness of those who refused His word (Matthew 13:10–17).

Solomon’s God-given wisdom became renowned among the nations (1 Kings 4:29–34), yet the book directs every reader beyond human insight to the fear of the LORD as wisdom’s true beginning (Proverbs 1:7). God invites those who lack wisdom to ask Him in faith, confident that He gives generously (James 1:5).

Proverbs 1:7

The fear of the LORD
Reverent submission to the covenant God of Israel stands at the heart of true wisdom. Such fear recognizes the LORD’s holiness, authority, justice, and mercy, leading His people to trust Him, obey His commands, and turn from evil. Job identified this fear as wisdom itself (Job 28:28), and the psalmist likewise called it the beginning of wisdom (Psalm 111:10).

is the beginning of knowledge,
All sound understanding begins with acknowledging God as Creator, Judge, and Redeemer. Knowledge in Proverbs includes moral discernment and skill for righteous living, not mere accumulation of facts. Israel’s instruction was rooted in God’s revealed law, so wisdom required listening humbly to His word and ordering one’s life accordingly.

The Enticement of Sin

Proverbs 1:8

Solomon addresses his son with the affection and authority of a father charged to form his child in the fear of the Lord. Wisdom was to be taught within the covenant household, where parents repeated God’s commands and applied them to the choices of daily life (Deuteronomy 6:6–7). A father’s instruction was not merely practical advice; it was meant to direct a son away from sin and toward obedience to God.

A mother shared fully in the spiritual and moral formation of her children. Her teaching reinforced the godly instruction of the father and supplied wisdom shaped by faith, experience, and faithful care. Honoring the instruction of both parents accords with God’s command to honor father and mother (Exodus 20:12). Their united guidance provided a foundation for a life marked by discernment, righteousness, and reverence for the Lord.

Proverbs 4:9 repeats the promise that wisdom gives “a crown of glory.” Such honor does not arise from human achievement alone but from walking in the order God has established.

Proverbs 1:16

The wicked do not merely stumble into sin; they choose a path directed toward it. In Scripture, feet often represent the course of one’s life, and running conveys eagerness rather than reluctance. Such willing pursuit of evil reflects humanity’s fallen heart, seen before the flood when “every inclination of the thoughts of the human heart was only evil continually” (Genesis 6:5). Isaiah likewise condemns those whose “feet run to evil” and whose ways bring ruin and destruction (Isaiah 59:7). Wisdom calls God’s people away from companions and habits that make evil seem urgent, profitable, or normal.

Violence quickly follows when greed, hatred, and self-interest rule the heart. Human life bears God’s image and is therefore sacred; God established the seriousness of murder after the flood, declaring that whoever sheds man’s blood must answer to God (Genesis 9:6). The readiness to harm others stands opposite the way of wisdom, which seeks righteousness, peace, and justice. Jesus exposed the root of bloodshed by warning that unrighteous anger and contempt already make a person accountable before God (Matthew 5:21–22).

Wisdom Calls Aloud

Proverbs 1:20

Wisdom calls out in the street,
Wisdom is personified as a woman who openly summons the simple, the foolish, and the scoffing to receive instruction. Her message is not concealed for an elite few but offered where ordinary life unfolds. Streets in Israel’s towns and cities were crowded with travelers, merchants, elders, and laborers, making them fitting places for a public call to fear the Lord. God makes His wisdom known and holds people responsible for their response to it.

Pro 8:1-3 Does not wisdom call out, and understanding raise her voice?…

Isa 58:1 Cry aloud, do not hold back! Raise your voice…

Mat 10:27 What I tell you in the dark, speak in the daylight;…

she lifts her voice in the square;
The city square was the center of commerce, legal judgment, civic discussion, and daily relationships. Wisdom addresses people amid their decisions, ambitions, conflicts, and responsibilities, showing that godly understanding belongs in every part of life. Her public cry resembles the ministry of the prophets, who proclaimed God’s word before the people, and anticipates Christ’s open teaching in towns, synagogues, and the temple courts. The Lord’s truth calls people away from self-rule and toward the path of life.

Wisdom calls in the crowded places where merchants trade, travelers pass, and ordinary life unfolds. Her instruction is not concealed from the poor, unlearned, or socially overlooked. God makes His truth known openly and urgently, confronting people amid the pursuits and distractions of daily life. Like the prophets who proclaimed God’s word before the nation, Wisdom summons the hearer to turn from folly before judgment comes.

Her call reaches everyone entering and leaving the city, pressing each person to choose the path of life. Christ, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge, is also the gate through whom His people enter into salvation and safety.

God’s call carries both patience and urgency, inviting them to leave the ease of folly and pursue the fear of the Lord, where true wisdom begins.

Psalm 1 warns against joining the seat of scoffers, while Proverbs repeatedly shows that scorn closes the heart to repentance. Wisdom still calls, but delight in ridicule cannot continue without judgment.

Israel’s history repeatedly shows the danger of rejecting God’s voice through His law and prophets. Such refusal is not mere ignorance but a deliberate turning away from the light God has given. Christ likewise called sinners to repent and believe, yet many rejected Him despite the clear witness of His words and works.

Proverbs 1:27

when your dread comes like a storm,
Wisdom warns that calamity can arrive with the force and suddenness of a violent storm. Storm imagery throughout Scripture displays God’s sovereign power over creation and His judgment against rebellion (Job 38:1; Psalm 107:29). Those who repeatedly refuse correction may find that the security they trusted vanishes quickly, leaving terror where confidence once stood. The warning follows Wisdom’s rejected invitation in Proverbs 1:24–26, showing that judgment is connected to a persistent refusal to fear the Lord.

and your destruction like a whirlwind,
A whirlwind conveys swift, irresistible devastation. Biblical prophets often used the image for the outpouring of God’s judgment against sin and false security (Isaiah 66:15; Jeremiah 23:19). Destruction is not presented as random misfortune but as the righteous consequence of rejecting God’s instruction. The personal language presses individual accountability: no one can evade responsibility for despising wisdom, reproof, and the fear of the Lord.

Those addressed had heard Wisdom’s call but treated divine correction as unwelcome. Their rejection resembled Israel’s repeated refusal to heed God’s prophets and commandments, choosing human judgment over revealed truth. Such hatred of knowledge is not harmless ignorance; it hardens the heart against repentance and leads to ruin.

Rejecting that fear meant choosing independence from God, like the generations in Judges who did what was right in their own eyes. Moral disorder follows when people refuse to honor the Lord, for wisdom cannot be separated from humble submission to Him.

Such self-reliance stands against the call to trust the Lord rather than one’s own understanding (Proverbs 3:5–6).
