And Rehoboam rested with his fathers
Rehoboam’s death ended a seventeen-year reign over Judah marked by division, conflict, and spiritual decline. His resting with his fathers places him among the kings descended from David and recalls the certainty that every earthly ruler answers to God. Death did not cancel the Lord’s covenant with David, but it exposed the frailty of each king who failed to walk faithfully before Him.

and was buried with them in the City of David;
Burial in Jerusalem’s royal precinct honored Rehoboam as David’s descendant and affirmed the continuing rule of David’s house in Judah. The City of David remained a visible witness to God’s promise that David would have an enduring dynasty, a promise ultimately fulfilled in Jesus Christ.

his mother’s name was Naamah the Ammonite.
Naamah was an Ammonite, a people descended from Lot and frequently opposed to Israel. Her inclusion reflects the foreign marriages introduced during Solomon’s reign, marriages connected with the idolatry that brought God’s judgment upon the united kingdom. Judah’s unfaithfulness under Rehoboam showed the destructive legacy of abandoning wholehearted devotion to the Lord.

And his son Abijam reigned in his place.
Abijam, also called Abijah, inherited Judah’s throne and continued the Davidic line despite the spiritual failures of its kings. God preserved David’s house for His own covenant purposes, even when individual rulers were unworthy. Matthew’s genealogy traces this royal line forward to Jesus Christ, the everlasting King.
