Proverbs 16:1

The plans of the heart belong to man,
God grants people the capacity to think, desire, choose, and form intentions. The heart encompasses the inner life from which motives and decisions arise. Human planning can serve wisdom and faithful stewardship, yet plans formed apart from God may become instruments of pride, as at Babel when people sought to make a name for themselves. Proverbs repeatedly calls God’s people to bring their purposes under His wisdom and righteousness.

but the reply of the tongue is from the LORD.
The Lord remains sovereign over the final answer, outcome, and every purpose accomplished through human words. Balaam intended to profit by cursing Israel, but God placed blessings on his tongue and upheld His covenant promises (Numbers 22–24). God’s rule does not remove human responsibility; it summons believers to humility, prayer, and dependence. Plans should be made diligently, but always with submission to the Lord’s will (James 4:13–15).

Proverbs 16:2

All a man’s ways are pure in his own eyes,
Human hearts easily excuse sin, defend selfish desires, and confuse personal approval with righteousness. Israel’s recurring apostasy showed the danger of moral independence, especially when everyone did what was right in his own eyes (Judges 21:25). Wisdom calls God’s people to submit their judgments, plans, and conduct to the revealed will of God rather than trusting their own perceptions.

Proverbs 16:3

Commit your works to the LORD
Every duty, labor, decision, and ambition belongs under the rule of the covenant God. Committing work to the LORD means entrusting both the effort and the outcome to Him, refusing to treat success as the product of human wisdom alone. Psalm 37:5 similarly calls God’s people to commit their way to the LORD and trust Him to act. Faithful labor is not passive; it works diligently while depending upon God’s guidance, provision, and blessing.

Nothing in creation lies outside His authority or escapes His final design. God’s purposes include the good of His people, whom He is conforming to the image of His Son through every circumstance (Romans 8:28–29).

Proverbs 16:6

By loving devotion and faithfulness iniquity is atoned for,
God calls His people to live in covenant loyalty marked by mercy and truth. Sacrifices appointed under the Law addressed sin before God, yet outward ritual without a faithful heart was unacceptable to Him. Love and faithfulness reflect the character of the Lord and demonstrate genuine repentance, reconciliation, and obedience. Such virtues do not replace the need for God’s provision of forgiveness; they reveal a life turned from sin and ordered by His ways. Jesus Christ perfectly embodied steadfast love and faithfulness, and His sacrifice provides the final and sufficient atonement for all who believe.

God delights in obedient faith, righteous conduct, integrity, and wholehearted devotion. Israel was commanded to fear the LORD, walk in His ways, love Him, and keep His commandments (Deuteronomy 10:12–13). Such obedience does not earn salvation, but it reveals a heart submitted to God. Faith remains essential, for without faith it is impossible to please Him (Hebrews 11:6).

Righteousness before God is worth more than abundance gained at the cost of obedience. The righteous may possess little in worldly terms, yet they have a clear conscience, God’s favor, and the security of walking in His ways. Psalm 37:16 likewise declares that the little belonging to the righteous is better than the riches of many wicked. God calls His people to seek His kingdom and righteousness first, trusting Him rather than making wealth their highest pursuit.

Wealth obtained through fraud, oppression, bribery, dishonest trade, or other wrongdoing cannot bring lasting blessing. Israel’s law required honest weights and measures and protected the vulnerable from exploitation, because the Lord Himself loves justice. Great gain may impress others for a time, but it cannot shield the unjust from God’s judgment. What is received rightly, even when small, is better than prosperity purchased by sin.

Proverbs 16:9

A man’s heart plans his course,
Human beings rightly consider their responsibilities, set goals, and make choices. The heart includes the inner life from which desires, judgments, and intentions arise. Wisdom calls for diligent planning, yet fallen human judgment is limited and often distorted by self-interest. Joshua called Israel to choose whom they would serve, while the prophets repeatedly exposed the danger of trusting one’s own heart rather than God’s word. Many purposes may fill a person’s mind, but they remain accountable to the Lord’s greater purpose.

but the LORD determines his steps.
Yahweh governs the path that finally unfolds, directing circumstances, opening and closing opportunities, and overruling even human evil for His righteous ends. Joseph recognized this truth when he told his brothers that God intended for good what they had meant for evil. God’s sovereignty does not cancel human responsibility; people genuinely choose and act, yet the Lord establishes the outcome according to His will. Believers therefore plan diligently but submit their plans to God, saying with James that they will act “if the Lord wills.” Jesus perfectly embodied such submission when He yielded to the Father’s will in Gethsemane.

Israel’s king ruled as God’s appointed servant and bore a solemn responsibility to administer justice under the Lord’s authority. Solomon’s request for wisdom and his judgment between the two women showed how royal decisions were meant to reflect God’s concern for truth, righteousness, and the protection of the vulnerable (1 Kings 3:9, 28). Royal authority did not make a king independent of God; it made him accountable to God. The ideal king pointed beyond Israel’s throne to the Messiah, whose judgments are altogether true and righteous.

Jesus Christ fulfills the righteous kingship that Israel’s rulers often failed to display. Proverbs 16:11

Honest scales and balances are from the LORD;
God is the source and standard of justice in every human exchange. Scales governed ordinary commerce in Israel, determining the value of grain, produce, metals, and other goods. Fair measurement was therefore not merely a business practice but an act of covenant faithfulness. The Lord required His people to reflect His righteous character in public as well as private life (Leviticus 19:36; Ezekiel 45:10). A merchant who dealt honestly acknowledged that all possessions, labor, and prosperity ultimately belong to God.

Pro 11:1 Dishonest scales are an abomination to the LORD,…

Lev 19:35-36 You must not use dishonest measures of length, weight,…

Ezk 45:10 You must use honest scales, a just ephah, and a just bath.

all the weights in the bag are His concern.
Merchants commonly carried standard weights in a bag for use with a balance scale. Keeping unequal or deceptive weights enabled a seller to take advantage of buyers, especially the poor and vulnerable. God expressly condemned such fraud as an abomination (Deuteronomy 25:13–16; Proverbs 11:1; Micah 6:11). No act of dishonesty escapes His notice, including transactions that appear small or hidden. Christ perfectly embodies the truth and righteousness God requires, and He will judge the world in righteousness (Acts 17:31).

Deuteronomy 17:18–20 required the king to keep and read the Law so that humility, obedience, and reverence for the Lord would govern his rule. Evil in a ruler corrupts courts, oppresses the vulnerable, and invites God’s judgment upon the nation. David’s sin brought severe consequences to his house and kingdom, demonstrating that royal power does not excuse disobedience. Leaders remain accountable to the God who appoints governing authorities and judges every ruler impartially.

Proverbs 16:14

Ancient rulers often possessed authority to execute offenders without appeal, so royal anger could bring swift and irreversible judgment. Xerxes’ fury against Haman ended in Haman’s execution (Esther 7:7–10). Such power warns against provoking rightful authority through rebellion, deceit, or careless speech. Earthly kings, however, only dimly reflect God’s perfect kingship; His wrath against sin is holy, certain, and fully deserved (Romans 1:18).

Wise conduct does not flatter evil or excuse injustice, but seeks peace without compromising righteousness. Proverbs 16:15

When a king’s face brightens,
In ancient Near Eastern cultures, the king was often seen as a representative of divine authority on earth. A king’s demeanor could significantly impact the morale and well-being of his subjects. When a king’s face brightens, it signifies approval, favor, and goodwill. This imagery reflects the importance of a ruler’s disposition in maintaining peace and prosperity. In biblical terms, the brightening of a king’s face can be likened to God’s favor shining upon His people, as seen in the Aaronic blessing in Numbers 6:24-26, where God’s face shining upon someone is a sign of blessing and peace.

Pro 14:35 A king delights in a wise servant, but his anger falls…

Num 6:25 may the LORD cause His face to shine upon you…

there is life;
The presence of life here symbolizes vitality, prosperity, and flourishing. In the context of a king’s favor, it suggests that the well-being of the kingdom is directly tied to the king’s attitude. This concept is echoed in Proverbs 19:12, where a king’s wrath is compared to the roar of a lion, but his favor is like dew on the grass, bringing life and growth. Theologically, this can be seen as a type of Christ, the King of Kings, whose favor brings eternal life to believers.

Spring rain was essential in Israel for ripening grain and securing the harvest. Its arrival promised renewal after the dry season and food for the months ahead. The comparison points beyond human kings to Christ, the King of kings, whose grace brings lasting life and whose coming fulfills the hope of spiritual renewal (Hosea 6:3; John 4:14).

Proverbs 16:18

Pride goes before destruction,
Pride exalts self against God and refuses the wisdom, correction, and dependence that preserve life. Scripture repeatedly shows that those who claim glory belonging to the Lord invite His judgment. Nebuchadnezzar boasted in Babylon’s greatness as the work of his own power, and God humbled him until he acknowledged that the Most High rules over human kingdoms (Daniel 4:30–37). The proud heart is especially vulnerable because it cannot see its own weakness or recognize its need for mercy. Isaiah 14:12–15 portrays the ruin that follows self-exaltation against God’s authority.

A haughty spirit treats others with contempt and acts as though no one, including God, can call it to account. Pharaoh hardened himself against the Lord’s commands, rejected Moses’ warnings, and led Egypt into devastating judgment before his army perished in the Red Sea (Exodus 5–14). God’s opposition to pride is not arbitrary; pride rejects His rightful rule and distorts His image in humanity. James teaches that God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble (James 4:6). Christ displayed the opposite path, humbling Himself in obedient service and death on the cross before the Father exalted Him (Philippians 2:5–11).

Proverbs 16:21

In Scripture, the heart encompasses thought, desire, judgment, and moral purpose. God-given wisdom shapes the inward person so that sound judgment becomes evident in conduct and counsel. Solomon sought such a heart when he asked the Lord for wisdom to govern Israel rightly (1 Kings 3:9–12). The Lord gives wisdom, knowledge, and understanding to those who fear Him and walk in His ways (Proverbs 2:6–7). Discernment recognizes the difference between truth and error, righteousness and sin, and the wise path and the destructive one.

Truth spoken with graciousness makes wise counsel more readily received and remembered. Harsh words may provoke resistance, but fitting and kind words can turn hearts toward correction and understanding (Proverbs 15:1, 23). Wise speech does not dilute truth; it communicates truth in a manner that builds up those who hear. Jesus taught the crowds with authority, clarity, and compassion, revealing the kingdom of God through words suited to their need (Matthew 13:34; John 7:46). Gracious speech remains an essential mark of wisdom, enabling instruction to strengthen the people of God.

Proverbs 16:22

Understanding is a fountain of life to its possessor,
God-given understanding supplies continual direction, preservation, and spiritual renewal. Like a spring in a dry land, wisdom sustains the one who receives it and benefits those around him.
