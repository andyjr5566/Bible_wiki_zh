Seraiah,
Seraiah stands among the priestly leaders who sealed the covenant during Jerusalem’s restoration. His name, meaning “Yahweh is ruler,” accords with the community’s renewed submission to the Lord’s law after the exile. The name also recalls Seraiah the chief priest executed at the fall of Jerusalem (2 Kings 25:18–21), a solemn reminder that priestly office could not preserve the nation when it forsook God. The covenant in Nehemiah marks a new generation’s resolve to serve the same Lord faithfully.

Adonijah,
Adonijah is a name that appears several times in the Old Testament, most notably as the son of King David who attempted to claim the throne before Solomon was anointed king (1 Kings 1:5-10). The name means “Yahweh is my Lord,” reflecting a common practice of the time to incorporate the name of God into personal names, signifying devotion or a reminder of divine authority. In Nehemiah 10:16, Adonijah is listed among those who sealed the covenant, indicating a commitment to the renewed covenant with God during the post-exilic period. This reflects a broader theme of restoration and dedication to God’s laws after the Babylonian exile.

Nehemiah 10:35

We will also bring the firstfruits of our land
Israel’s first and best produce belonged to the Lord as an acknowledgment that the land, harvest, and strength to labor were gifts from His hand. The Law required firstfruits to be presented in worship, accompanied by confession of God’s faithful redemption and provision (Exodus 23:19; Deuteronomy 26:1–11). By giving before the full harvest was secured, the people demonstrated trust that God would continue to provide. Christ’s resurrection fulfills the larger hope pictured in firstfruits, for He is “the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep,” guaranteeing the resurrection of all who belong to Him (1 Corinthians 15:20–23).
