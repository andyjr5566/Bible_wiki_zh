Though later associated with idolatrous worship, Bethel and its surrounding settlements were restored within the community of Judah and Benjamin, pointing to the need for the people to return to the God who had first revealed Himself there.

in Anathoth,
Anathoth lay in Benjamin about three miles northeast of Jerusalem and was assigned to the Levites as one of their cities (Josh. 21:18). Jeremiah came from Anathoth, where his priestly background prepared him for a prophetic ministry that called Judah back to covenant faithfulness (Jeremiah 1:1). Its resettlement showed that God was restoring not only Jerusalem but also the surrounding communities that supported Israel’s worship and spiritual life.

Nob,
Nob had served as a priestly center after the tabernacle left Shiloh. David received consecrated bread there from Ahimelech while fleeing Saul, and Saul’s murder of Nob’s priests revealed his rejection of God’s authority (1 Sam. 21–22). The return of inhabitants to Nob marked the restoration of a place long associated with priestly service and costly faithfulness.

Ananiah,
Ananiah was likely a Benjaminite village near Anathoth and Nob. Its name, meaning “Yahweh has answered,” fittingly reflects the mercy of God in bringing His people back from exile. Even small and less-recorded towns shared in the fulfillment of God’s promise to preserve a remnant and reestablish His people in their land.

Hazor,
The Hazor in Benjamin was distinct from the great northern Canaanite capital destroyed by Joshua. Its resettlement showed that the returned exiles were restoring even smaller Benjaminite communities around Jerusalem, recovering the inheritance God had preserved through judgment and exile.

Ramah,
Ramah stood north of Jerusalem in Benjamin and was associated with Samuel’s ministry and with Judah’s suffering after Jerusalem fell to Babylon. Jeremiah saw captives gathered there, and he later connected Ramah with Rachel’s mourning for her children. Its renewed occupation testified that mourning and devastation had not annulled God’s promise to restore His people.

Lod, and Ono;
These towns lay west of Jerusalem in the Benjaminite region, near important routes connecting the hill country with the coastal plain. Their resettlement strengthened Judah’s presence beyond Jerusalem after the exile. Lod later became known as Lydda, where the apostle Peter healed Aeneas and many turned to the Lord (Acts 9:32–35). Ono also appears when Nehemiah’s enemies tried to draw him away from Jerusalem, but he refused to abandon the work God had given him (Nehemiah 6:2–3).
