Scribes were trained interpreters and teachers of the Law, often associated with the Pharisaic viewpoint. Their joint appearance presents a united challenge from men who held recognized religious authority among the people.

Matthew 15:2

“Why do Your disciples break the tradition of the elders?
The Pharisees and scribes regarded the accumulated oral rulings of respected teachers as binding safeguards around the Law of Moses. These traditions governed many details of daily life and were intended to preserve Israel’s holiness. Yet they had come to be treated with an authority that could rival God’s written commandments. Jesus’ disciples were accused not of violating the Torah, but of disregarding a religious custom elevated by human authority. Jesus would expose the danger of honoring inherited practices while neglecting obedience flowing from a heart devoted to God.

They do not wash their hands before they eat.”
The washing in view was a ceremonial act of purification, not an ordinary concern for cleanliness. The Law required washings for priests and in particular cases of uncleanness, but it did not command every Israelite to perform ritual handwashing before meals. The religious leaders believed such observance protected them from defilement, including contact with what was considered unclean. Jesus answered that moral defilement arises from the sinful heart, not from food or the failure to observe man-made ritual requirements. God seeks purity that reaches the inner person, as Isaiah condemned those who honored Him outwardly while their hearts remained far from Him.

Matthew 15:38

A total of four thousand men were fed,
Jesus miraculously supplied a full meal for an immense crowd after they had remained with Him for three days. The number records the men counted publicly, while the miracle itself displayed the Lord’s care for every person present. Coming after the feeding of the five thousand, this provision confirms that Jesus’ power was neither limited nor accidental. In a setting associated with His ministry among Gentile populations near the Decapolis, the miracle anticipates the extension of God’s saving mercy to all nations. As God gave manna to Israel in the wilderness, the Son provided bread in a desolate place, revealing Himself as the Shepherd who sustains His people.

Mrk 8:20 “And when I broke the seven loaves for the four thousand,”…

besides women and children.
The total number receiving food was far greater than four thousand, since women and children were present in addition to the counted men. Their inclusion shows that Christ’s compassion embraced whole households, not merely those formally numbered in the crowd. The abundant meal points forward to the promised feast of God’s kingdom, prepared for all peoples, and declares that no one who comes to Jesus is overlooked.

Matthew 15:39

After Jesus had dismissed the crowds,
Jesus concluded the feeding of the four thousand by sending the people away in an orderly manner. Having met their physical need with compassion and miraculous provision, He now led His disciples onward. The miracle testified that the Messiah’s mercy and abundance extended beyond Israel into the surrounding Gentile regions.
