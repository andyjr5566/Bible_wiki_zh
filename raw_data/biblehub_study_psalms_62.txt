For the choirmaster.
The psalm was entrusted for use in Israel’s gathered worship under the direction of the temple music leader. David’s personal confidence in God was therefore given to the whole covenant community as a song of faith amid danger and opposition.

Earthly help may fail, but salvation belongs to God alone, ultimately fulfilled in Jesus Christ, through whom sinners are rescued from sin and judgment.

Psalm 62:3

How long will you threaten a man?
David brings the hostility of his enemies before God, refusing to treat their violence as final or unchecked. Their continuing attacks reveal hearts set against the one whom God has appointed, yet David waits for the Lord’s judgment rather than taking vengeance into his own hands.

The righteous suffer not because God has abandoned them, but because rebellion against God frequently takes the form of hostility toward His servants. Jesus also endured the united malice of rulers, religious leaders, and crowds, yet God turned the cross into the means of His victory.

Psalm 62:5

Rest in God alone,
David calls his soul away from every rival refuge. Political power, wealth, human alliances, and personal strength cannot provide the security that belongs to the Lord. Israel lived among nations devoted to many gods, yet the God of Abraham had revealed Himself as the one true God, worthy of undivided trust. His sovereignty over enemies, kingdoms, and the course of life gives His people reason to be still before Him.

Psalm 62:8

Trust in Him at all times, O people;
David calls the whole covenant community to rest their confidence in God through every change of circumstance. Israel’s security did not finally rest in kings, armies, wealth, or fortified cities, but in the Lord who rules over all. Trust is required not only in deliverance from danger but also in seasons of waiting, loss, and uncertainty. Proverbs 3:5–6 likewise directs God’s people to rely on the Lord rather than their own understanding.

The Psalms repeatedly show faithful believers crying to God in distress while continuing to acknowledge His goodness and power. Such prayer is not unbelief; it is the response of those who know that the Lord hears them. Believers are similarly commanded to bring every concern to God in prayer, with thanksgiving, and to receive His peace (Philippians 4:6–7).

Psalm 62:10

Place no trust in extortion
Wealth gained by oppression, threats, or abuse of power cannot provide lasting security. God sees the wrong done to the vulnerable and calls His people to practice justice, mercy, and integrity (Micah 6:8). Those who use power to take from others may gain temporary advantage, but they stand under God’s judgment.

Theft violates God’s command and destroys the trust necessary for life within a covenant community (Exodus 20:15). Ill-gotten wealth offers only a false refuge, for “treasures of wickedness profit nothing” (Proverbs 10:2). God Himself, not dishonest gain, is the sure source of provision and protection.

Rich believers are called to generosity, readiness to share, and hope in God rather than in wealth (1 Timothy 6:17–19).

His steadfast love sustains those who wait quietly for Him rather than trusting in wealth, status, or human strength. God’s mercy reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, whose sacrificial death displays both divine love and righteousness.
