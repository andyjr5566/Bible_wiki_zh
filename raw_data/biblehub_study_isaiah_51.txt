Salvation for Zion

Isaiah 51:1

“Listen to Me,
God summons the faithful remnant to give earnest attention to His word. In a time marked by desolation, opposition, and exile, His authority remains unchanged, and His promises provide the sure ground for hope.

Psa 34:11 Come, children, listen to me; I will teach you…

Psa 78:1 A Maskil of Asaph. Give ear, O my people, to my instruction;…

you who pursue righteousness,
The call is directed to those who desire to live faithfully before God, seeking justice, obedience, and covenant faithfulness. Their pursuit does not earn God’s favor but reflects hearts turned toward Him amid a nation often characterized by rebellion.

2Ti 2:22 Flee from youthful passions and pursue righteousness…

1Ti 6:11 But you, O man of God, flee from these things…

you who seek the LORD:
Seeking the LORD involves dependence on Him in worship, prayer, and obedience. God had promised that those who seek Him wholeheartedly would find Him, and He calls His people to look beyond present circumstances to His enduring purposes.

2Ch 15:2 So he went out to meet Asa and said to him,…

Jer 29:13 You will seek Me and find Me when you search for Me…

Look to the rock from which you were cut,
Israel was to remember Abraham, their father in faith, from whom God raised a people when humanly speaking there was little prospect of a great nation. Abraham’s life testified that God fulfills His promises by His power, not by natural strength or numbers.

Rom 4:17-18 As it is written: “I have made you a father of many nations”…

Gen 12:1-3 Then the LORD said to Abram, “Leave your country…

and to the quarry from which you were hewn.
The image continues in the following verse with Sarah, through whom God gave Abraham the promised son. From one aged couple God formed Israel, demonstrating that He could again restore, multiply, and bless His people. Their history called them to trust the God who had faithfully brought them into being.

Gen 21:1-3 Now the LORD attended to Sarah as He had said…

Gal 4:28 Now you, brothers, like Isaac, are children of promise.

Isaiah 51:2

Look to Abraham your father,
Israel’s hope rested in the God who called Abraham from Ur and led him into the land of promise. Abraham obeyed when he did not yet know where he was going, trusting the Lord to fulfill promises beyond human possibility. His life reminded the faithful that their national existence began not with human strength, but with God’s gracious covenant call.

Isa 41:8-9 But you, O Israel, My servant, Jacob, whom I have chosen,…

Rom 4:11-12 And he received the sign of circumcision as a seal…

and to Sarah who gave you birth.
Sarah shared in the covenant promise as the mother of Isaac, the son born when Abraham and Sarah were beyond ordinary hope of bearing children. The Lord’s gift of Isaac established the promised line through which Israel came. Her barrenness and later motherhood testified that God is able to bring life, fruitfulness, and a future where none appears possible.

Gen 21:1-3 Now the LORD attended to Sarah as He had said…

Gen 17:19 But God replied, “Your wife Sarah will bear you a son,…

When I called him, he was but one;
Abraham had no nation, no settled inheritance, and no son through whom the promise could continue when God first called him. The Lord chose one man and bound Himself to him by covenant, promising descendants, land, and blessing for all nations through his offspring. Israel therefore had reason to trust God even in weakness, exile, and apparent desolation.

Gen 12:1-3 Then the LORD said to Abram, “Leave your country…

Heb 11:12 And so from one man, and he as good as dead,…

then I blessed him and multiplied him.
God gave Abraham Isaac, multiplied his descendants into Israel, and preserved the covenant line through generations. The promise ultimately reaches its fullness in Jesus Christ, Abraham’s promised offspring, through whom believing Jews and Gentiles receive the blessing of faith. The God who made a multitude from one man remained able to restore Zion and fulfill every promise He had spoken.

Gen 22:17 I will surely bless you, and I will multiply…

Heb 6:13-15 When God made His promise to Abraham, since He had no one…

Isaiah 51:3

For the LORD will comfort Zion
Zion, Jerusalem, lay desolate after judgment for sin, yet the Lord’s covenant purposes for His people had not failed. He promised consolation that would reach beyond the return from Babylon, restoring His people to fellowship, worship, and hope. Isaiah later opens his message of restoration with the command to comfort Jerusalem, declaring that the Lord Himself would remove her reproach and gather her people.

Isa 40:1 “Comfort, comfort My people,” says your God.

Isa 66:13 As a mother comforts her son, so will I comfort you,…

and will look with compassion on all her ruins;
Jerusalem’s ruined walls, burned temple, and emptied streets bore witness to the devastation brought by Babylon. The Lord would not overlook the wreckage caused by His judgment. His compassion would move Him to rebuild what had been torn down, a work begun visibly through the return of the exiles under Zerubbabel, Ezra, and Nehemiah.

Psa 102:13-14 You will rise up and have compassion on Zion,…

Zec 1:16-17 Therefore this is what the LORD says: ‘I will return……

He will make her wilderness like Eden
The restoration would be as dramatic as a barren wilderness becoming the fruitful garden God prepared for Adam and Eve. Eden recalls the goodness of creation, the abundance of God’s provision, and unhindered blessing in His presence. The promise anticipates the fuller renewal accomplished under the reign of the Messiah and ultimately fulfilled in the new creation.

Ezk 36:35 Then they will say, ‘This land that was desolate…

Isa 35:1-2 The wilderness and the dry land will be glad;…

and her desert like the garden of the LORD.
The Lord alone can turn desolation into a place of life and fruitfulness. What sin, judgment, and exile had made barren would become a testimony to His faithfulness. The prophets repeatedly connect such transformed land with Israel’s restoration and the coming kingdom of God.

Isa 58:11 The LORD will always guide you; He will satisfy you…

Gen 2:8-10 And the LORD God planted a garden in Eden, in the east…

Joy and gladness will be found in her,
Mourning over devastation and exile would give way to the joy of a restored people. God’s salvation does not merely repair cities and fields; it renews hearts and restores worshiping fellowship with Him. The return of joy reveals that the Lord has turned away His people’s grief and brought them again into blessing.

Jer 31:13 Then the maidens will rejoice with dancing,…

Isa 35:10 So the redeemed of the LORD will return…

thanksgiving and melodious song.
Restored Zion would answer God’s mercy with grateful worship. Songs of praise would once more rise from the city where the Lord had chosen to place His name. The rejoicing of Jerusalem points forward to the redeemed people of God, who will eternally give thanks for His deliverance and reign.

Jer 30:19 Thanksgiving will proceed from them, a sound of celebration.…

Psa 69:30-31 I will praise God’s name in song and exalt Him…

Isaiah 51:4

Pay attention to Me, My people, and listen to Me, My nation;
God calls His covenant people to turn from fear, idolatry, and surrounding voices so that they may receive His saving word. Israel’s identity rests on belonging to the Lord, who chose Abraham, redeemed His people from Egypt, and remains faithful despite their rebellion. The summons also prepares for God’s truth to reach those beyond Israel.

Psa 50:7 Hear, O My people, and I will speak, O Israel,…

Psa 78:1 A Maskil of Asaph. Give ear, O my people, to my instruction;…

for a law will go out from Me,
God will establish His instruction as the authoritative standard for His people and for the world. Isaiah had foretold that the nations would come to Zion to learn the ways of the Lord (Isaiah 2:2–3). God’s revealed will would not remain confined to Israel’s national life but would be made known among all peoples.

Isa 2:3 And many peoples will come and say: “Come, let us go up…

Mic 4:2 And many nations will come and say: “Come, let us go up…

Deu 33:2 He said: “The LORD came from Sinai and dawned…

and My justice will become a light to the nations;
God’s righteous rule brings light because it exposes evil, directs the faithful, and gives hope to those living in spiritual darkness. The Servant of the Lord fulfills this mission as a light to the nations (Isaiah 42:6; 49:6). Jesus Christ proclaimed God’s kingdom, bore sin, and commissioned His disciples to make Him known among every nation.

Isa 42:1 Here is My Servant, whom I uphold, My Chosen One,…

Isa 42:6 I, the LORD, have called you for a righteous purpose,…

Isa 49:6 “It is not enough for You to be My Servant,”…

I will bring it about quickly.
God’s promised deliverance and righteous judgment are certain because He governs history according to His purpose. The return from exile would demonstrate His faithfulness, while the fuller fulfillment points to the coming of Christ and the final establishment of His righteous kingdom.

Rom 9:28 For the Lord will carry out His sentence on the earth…

Isaiah 51:5

My righteousness draws near,
God’s covenant faithfulness was about to be displayed in the restoration of His people and in His judgment of evil. Isaiah’s promise reaches beyond Israel’s return from exile to the Messiah, in whom God reveals His saving righteousness to the world. Jesus Christ fulfills God’s righteous purpose by bearing sin and providing righteousness for all who believe.

Isa 46:13 I am bringing My righteousness near; it is not far away…

Isa 56:1 This is what the LORD says: “Maintain justice…

Psa 85:9 Surely His salvation is near to those who fear Him…

My salvation is on the way,
The Lord’s deliverance was certain because it rested on His own power and promise, not on Israel’s strength. Deliverance from oppression anticipated the greater salvation accomplished through Christ, who rescues His people from sin, death, and judgment.

Isa 62:11 Behold, the LORD has proclaimed to the ends of the earth…

Rom 13:11 And do this, understanding the occasion. The hour has come…

1Pe 1:5 who through faith are shielded by God’s power…

and My arms will bring justice to the nations.
God’s arm represents His irresistible strength in acting for His people and ruling His creation. The coming kingdom of the Messiah will establish true justice among every people, exposing wickedness and defending those who trust in the Lord.

Isa 42:1 Here is My Servant, whom I uphold, My Chosen One,…

Psa 67:4 Let the nations be glad and sing for joy,…

Psa 96:10 Declare among the nations: “The LORD reigns!”…

The islands will look for Me
Distant coastlands and Gentile nations are included in God’s redemptive purpose. Israel’s God would not remain known only within Israel’s borders; through the Messiah, the nations would be summoned to turn from idols and seek the living God.

Isa 60:9 Surely the islands will wait for Me, with the ships of…

and wait in hope for My arm.
The nations would look with expectation for God’s mighty intervention and saving rule. That hope began to be fulfilled in the gospel’s advance among the Gentiles and will be completed when Christ returns in power and glory.

Isaiah 51:6

Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look at the earth below;
God calls His people to consider the whole created order, from the sky above to the ground beneath them. The heavens and the earth, created by His word, testify to His power and authority (Genesis 1:1; Psalm 19:1). Yet even their grandeur cannot provide lasting security apart from the Creator.

Isa 40:26 Lift up your eyes on high: Who created all these?…

Deu 4:39 Know therefore this day and take to heart…

for the heavens will vanish like smoke,
The visible heavens are not eternal. Scripture elsewhere describes them as wearing out and being changed by God, like a garment laid aside for another (Psalm 102:25–26; Hebrews 1:10–12). Their passing will display the permanence of the Lord who made them.

2Pe 3:10 But the Day of the Lord will come like a thief.…

Mat 24:35 Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words…

Psa 102:3 For my days vanish like smoke, and my bones burn…

the earth will wear out like a garment,
The present world bears the effects of the fall and is destined for renewal. God will bring forth new heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells (Isaiah 65:17; 2 Peter 3:13; Revelation 21:1). What appears stable to mankind is temporary before the eternal King.

Psa 102:25-26 In the beginning You laid the foundations of the earth…

Heb 1:11-12 They will perish, but You remain; they will…

and its people will die like gnats.
Human life is brief and fragile, regardless of wealth, strength, or status. The comparison humbles those who trust in earthly permanence and directs attention toward the God whose years have no end. Man’s mortality makes God’s promised salvation all the more urgent.

Psa 144:4 Man is like a breath; his days are like a passing shadow.

Jas 4:14 You do not even know what will happen tomorrow!…

Psa 103:15-16 As for man, his days are like grass—he blooms…

But My salvation will last forever,
God’s deliverance for His people cannot decay or disappear with the world. Isaiah repeatedly points to the everlasting salvation secured by the Lord, ultimately fulfilled through the saving work of Jesus Christ (Isaiah 45:17; John 3:16). Those redeemed by Him receive life that endures beyond the passing order.

Isa 45:17 But Israel will be saved by the LORD…

Heb 5:9 And having been made perfect, He became the source…

and My righteousness will never fail.
God’s righteous character, judgments, and saving purposes remain unshaken. He faithfully accomplishes all He has promised, even when creation itself is transformed. In Christ, God reveals His righteousness and provides righteousness to those who believe (Romans 1:16–17; 2 Corinthians 5:21).

Isaiah 51:7

The Lord summons His people to give careful, obedient attention to His word. Isaiah addresses those who pursue righteousness and look to the Lord, recalling Israel’s calling to hear the one true God and walk in His ways.

God speaks to those who recognize His righteous standards and acknowledge His justice. Their knowledge involves covenant faithfulness, not mere awareness, and distinguishes them from those who reject the Lord’s rule.

God’s law had been given to Israel through Moses, yet true obedience required more than outward conformity. The promise anticipates the new covenant, in which the Lord writes His law upon the hearts of His people and enables willing obedience through His Spirit.

In a culture where public shame carried great weight, the Lord calls His servants to find their identity in Him rather than in human approval. Jesus likewise taught His disciples to rejoice when reviled for righteousness, because God’s judgment and reward are certain.

Isaiah 51:8

For the moth will devour them like a garment,
Human power, wealth, and opposition to God appear durable but are subject to swift decay. Moths threatened valuable garments in the ancient world, quietly ruining what people stored and trusted. The persecutors addressed in Isaiah’s context would not endure, though they seemed strong for a season. Job likewise described human life as wasting away like a moth-eaten garment (Job 13:28), and Jesus warned against storing treasures vulnerable to moth and decay (Matthew 6:19).

Job 13:28 So man wastes away like something rotten…

Jas 5:2 Your riches have rotted and moths have eaten your clothes.

Psa 39:11 You discipline and correct a man for his iniquity,…

and the worm will eat them like wool.
Woolen clothing, another treasured possession, could be consumed and ruined by small creatures. The combined images portray complete and certain destruction of the wicked. God’s enemies may injure His people temporarily, but they cannot preserve themselves against His judgment. Their apparent permanence will vanish, as Psalm 37:20 declares of those opposed to the Lord.

Mrk 9:48 their worm never dies, and the fire is never quenched…

Isa 14:11 Your pomp has been brought down to Sheol…

But My righteousness will last forever,
The Lord’s righteous character, judgments, and covenant faithfulness do not fade with changing nations or generations. His standard remains upright when human rulers and systems collapse. The righteousness God provides for His people also stands secure, ultimately fulfilled through the saving work of Jesus Christ. “Your righteousness is an everlasting righteousness,” the psalmist confessed (Psalm 119:142).

Psa 119:142 Your righteousness is everlasting and Your law is true.

My salvation through all generations.”
God’s deliverance does not expire or belong only to one era of Israel’s history. He preserved a faithful people, fulfilled His redemptive promise in Christ, and continues to save all who call upon Him in faith. His mercy reaches those who fear Him from generation to generation (Luke 1:50), giving God’s people confidence amid every temporary threat.

Zion appeals to the LORD to reveal openly the saving power that had delivered Israel before. God does not sleep or weaken, yet Scripture speaks this way to voice the urgent longing of His afflicted people. The arm of the LORD represents His irresistible power in judgment and redemption, displayed supremely when He brought Israel out of Egypt.

Israel’s memory of God’s former works becomes the ground for present hope. The LORD who acted for Abraham’s descendants in the Exodus, at the Red Sea, and throughout their history had not changed. Recalling His faithfulness calls God’s people to trust that His covenant promises remain sure even in exile and distress.

Rahab is a poetic name for Egypt, the proud power that enslaved Israel and opposed the LORD. The dragon imagery portrays Egypt and Pharaoh as forces of rebellion and chaos subdued by God. At the Red Sea, the LORD shattered Egypt’s military might and made a way of deliverance for His people, proving that no earthly kingdom or spiritual enemy can resist His sovereign power.

Isaiah 51:10

Was it not You who dried up the sea,
Israel’s restoration is grounded in the Lord’s historical deliverance at the Red Sea. By a strong east wind, God divided the waters and exposed dry ground for His people while judging Pharaoh’s pursuing army (Exodus 14:21–28). The God who redeemed Israel from Egypt remains able to rescue Zion from every enemy and exile.

Exo 14:21-22 Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea,…

the waters of the great deep,
The deep waters displayed forces of danger, judgment, and death that no human power could overcome. Yet the Creator who brought order over the waters at creation ruled the sea completely. The exodus revealed that the Lord’s authority extends over all creation and over the nations that oppose His purposes.

God turned an impassable barrier into a path of safety for Israel. Isaiah recalls that road to strengthen faith in God’s promised future salvation, when He would again lead His people home. The deliverance also anticipates Christ, who opened the way to God through His sacrificial death and victorious resurrection.

The redeemed were the Israelites whom the Lord purchased from Egyptian slavery and claimed as His own covenant people (Exodus 6:6; 15:13). Their crossing marked a decisive separation from bondage and the beginning of their journey toward the promised land. Through Christ’s redemption, believers likewise pass from slavery to sin into the freedom and life of God’s kingdom.

The LORD will bring back His people from exile as He once delivered Israel from slavery in Egypt. The promised restoration also anticipates the greater redemption accomplished by Jesus Christ, who delivers His people from sin and death.

The Lord personally claims the role of Comforter for His covenant people. Israel’s hope did not rest in changed political circumstances or human allies, but in the God who redeemed them and would restore them from exile. The assurance recalls Isaiah 40:1 and anticipates the comfort God gives through His presence, His promises, and ultimately the ministry of the Holy Spirit (John 14:16–18). God’s comfort strengthens His people to endure suffering with confidence in His faithfulness.

Those who threaten God’s people may appear powerful for a season, but their authority will end. God’s promises, kingdom, and saving purpose endure without change.

Isaiah 51:13

Israel’s fear revealed a deeper failure to remember the God who formed them, redeemed them from Egypt, and bound them to Himself by covenant. The Maker of His people was not a distant deity but the LORD who had repeatedly acted in their history. Deuteronomy 32:18 similarly rebukes Israel for forgetting the God who gave them birth. Remembering the LORD meant trusting His character, obeying His word, and returning from self-reliance and idolatry.

The God whom Israel neglected possesses absolute authority over all creation. He made the heavens above and established the earth below; therefore, no earthly empire, army, or ruler could stand beyond His rule. Creation itself testified that the LORD’s power was not diminished by Israel’s exile or suffering. The One who established the world could also preserve His people and fulfill every promise He had spoken.

Judah faced the crushing threat of hostile powers, particularly Babylon, whose conquest brought violence, exile, and the destruction of Jerusalem. Their danger was real, yet continual fear had displaced confidence in the LORD. God did not deny the oppressor’s cruelty, but He confronted the assumption that an enemy’s rage could overturn His purposes for His covenant people.

The question directs God’s people away from the immediate terror before them to the certainty of divine deliverance. Human rulers rise and fall, but the Creator keeps His covenant and cannot be defeated.

The covenant God of Israel binds His name to His people, recalling His redemption of them from Egypt and His pledge to dwell among them. The One who gave the law at Sinai is not distant from their affliction or exile; He remains their God, faithful to His promises to Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and David. Israel’s security rests not in political strength or human allies but in belonging to the Lord.

Lev 26:1 You must not make idols for yourselves…

who stirs up the sea so that its waves roar—
The sea, often associated with threatening chaos and hostile powers, is subject to God’s command. He divided the Red Sea for Israel’s escape and brought its waters back upon Pharaoh’s army. The roaring waters that terrify men are no obstacle to the Creator who established their boundaries. God’s authority over the sea assures His people that no force of nature, nation, or enemy can overturn His saving purpose.

Jer 31:35 Thus says the LORD, who gives the sun for light by day…

The Lord commands the armies of heaven and rules over every power in heaven and on earth. As the divine warrior, He defends His people and carries out judgment against those who oppose His purposes. The name LORD of Hosts grounds the promise of restoration in God’s unlimited power and covenant faithfulness.

Isaiah 51:16

I have put My words in your mouth,
God commissions His servant to speak with authority received from heaven, not invented by human wisdom. Jeremiah received a similar calling when the Lord touched his mouth and appointed him to proclaim God’s word to the nations (Jeremiah 1:9–10), while Moses was assured that God would teach him what to say (Exodus 4:12). The messenger must therefore speak faithfully, because God’s revealed word carries His own authority.

Exo 4:12 “Now go! I will help you as you speak, and I will teach”…

and covered you with the shadow of My hand,
The One who sends His servant also preserves him. God’s hand denotes His power, ownership, and personal care, sheltering His servant amid opposition and sustaining him for the work assigned. Israel had known such protection in the wilderness, when the Lord led and guarded the nation by the pillar of cloud and fire (Exodus 13:21–22). Those who live under God’s rule find refuge in His presence (Psalm 91:1).

Isa 49:2 He made My mouth like a sharp sword; He hid Me…

Psa 121:5 The LORD is your keeper; the LORD is the shade…

to establish the heavens,
The Lord who created the heavens directs history toward His appointed restoration. Isaiah looks beyond Jerusalem’s immediate distress to God’s purpose of bringing forth a renewed order in which His righteousness and salvation endure. The promise anticipates the new heavens and new earth, where the effects of sin and judgment will be removed forever (Isaiah 65:17; Revelation 21:1).

to found the earth,
The earth rests upon God’s creative power and remains under His sovereign care. He who laid its foundations is able to restore His people and fulfill every covenant promise despite the ruin brought by rebellion, exile, and human kingdoms. Creation itself points to the certainty that God’s redemptive purposes cannot fail.

Psa 102:25 In the beginning You laid the foundations of the earth…

Zion, the city of Jerusalem and the center of Israel’s worship, receives God’s covenant assurance. Though the nation had endured judgment for sin, the Lord had not abandoned His chosen people. His declaration recalls the covenant promise: “I will be your God, and you shall be My people” (Exodus 6:7). Through the promised Messiah, Gentile believers also share in the blessings of God’s covenant people by faith, while God’s promises to Israel remain secure (Galatians 3:28–29; Romans 11:28–29).

God’s Fury Removed

Isaiah 51:17

Awake, awake!
God summons Jerusalem from the stupor produced by judgment and calls His people to renewed awareness of His restoring work. The doubled command carries urgency, recalling the earlier call for Zion to clothe herself in strength and beauty (Isaiah 52:1).

Isa 52:1 Awake, awake, clothe yourself with strength, O Zion!…

Eph 5:14 So it is said: “Wake up, O sleeper, rise up from the dead…

Rise up, O Jerusalem,
The city represents the covenant people who had been humbled by sin, invasion, and exile. Jerusalem’s fall to Babylon in 586 BC demonstrated the seriousness of rebellion, yet God’s purposes for the city and His people had not failed.

Isa 52:2 Shake off your dust! Rise up and sit on your throne…

Isa 60:1 Arise, shine, for your light has come…

you who have drunk from the hand of the LORD the cup of His fury;
Judah’s suffering came under God’s sovereign discipline, not merely through Babylonian power. The cup portrays the outpouring of divine wrath against persistent idolatry and covenant unfaithfulness, as Jeremiah also proclaimed to the nations (Jeremiah 25:15–17).

you who have drained the goblet to the dregs—
Jerusalem had endured the full bitterness of judgment: siege, famine, death, exile, and disgrace. The punishment was severe, but it was not God’s final word to His people. He would remove the cup of wrath from Zion and place it in the hands of her oppressors (Isaiah 51:22–23).

Ezk 23:34 You will drink it and drain it; you will dash it to pieces,…

the cup that makes men stagger.
God’s judgment had left the nation helpless and disoriented, unable to save itself. The image also points forward to Christ, who willingly received the cup of suffering in the Garden of Gethsemane (meaning “oil press”) in obedience to the Father, bearing sin’s judgment so that all who trust in Him may receive mercy rather than wrath (Matthew 26:39; Romans 5:9).

No human leader could restore Jerusalem or deliver her from the consequences of sin. The image anticipates the Lord’s own intervention, fulfilled ultimately in Christ, who guides His people, bears their weakness, and brings salvation where human strength fails.

Isaiah 51:19

Zion’s sufferings came in full measure under God’s righteous judgment. The disasters were not random events but covenant consequences long warned of for persistent rebellion against the Lord (Deuteronomy 28:47–57).

Jerusalem and Judah were laid waste by Babylon, culminating in the fall of the city and the destruction of Solomon’s temple in 586 BC. National collapse exposed the cost of forsaking the God who had given His people the land and His presence.

Famine consumed the city during the Babylonian siege, while the sword brought death through invasion, warfare, and exile (2 Kings 25:1–11). These judgments, often joined in the prophets, showed that neither Jerusalem’s walls nor its religious heritage could preserve an unrepentant people.

The generation meant to sustain the city has become helpless, unable to resist the calamity brought by covenant rebellion. Their weakness reflects both physical devastation and the spiritual ruin that follows when a people abandon the Lord.

God calls Jerusalem to listen because He is about to announce the end of her season of judgment. The summons follows the picture of Zion drinking the cup of the Lord’s wrath and lying helpless amid devastation. Divine discipline has not canceled God’s covenant purpose; His word of restoration comes to the people He has humbled.
