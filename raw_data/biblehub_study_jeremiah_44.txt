Following the murder of Gedaliah, the appointed governor of Judah, the remnant feared Babylonian retaliation and fled to Egypt despite the Lord’s command to remain in Judah (Jeremiah 42:13–19; 43:1–7). Jeremiah and Baruch were taken with them. Egypt, once the house of Israel’s slavery, had become the refuge they chose in unbelief.

Migdol stood near Egypt’s northeastern frontier, while Tahpanhes was an important fortified settlement in the eastern Delta where Jeremiah announced Babylon’s coming invasion (Jeremiah 43:8–13). Memphis, a leading city of Lower Egypt and a center of Egyptian worship, shows that the Jewish refugees had spread among places deeply marked by idolatry.

Before Jerusalem fell, the LORD repeatedly warned Judah through prophets including Isaiah, Jeremiah, Zephaniah, Habakkuk, and others. God’s servants carried His word at personal cost, calling the nation to abandon sin before judgment came. Even after the surviving Judeans fled to Egypt, the memory of those warnings stood as evidence that God had not judged them without patient instruction and many opportunities to repent. His repeated appeals revealed compassion for His covenant people, as 2 Chronicles 36:15–16 records.

The detestable practice was idolatry, especially the worship of the so-called queen of heaven described later in the chapter. Idolatry rejected the LORD’s exclusive claim upon His people and treated created things as worthy of the worship due to the Creator alone. Such worship was spiritual adultery against the God who had redeemed Israel from Egypt and bound them to Himself by covenant. Judah’s refusal to heed God’s warnings brought the judgment foretold by the prophets, while God’s command still calls His people to turn from every rival allegiance and worship Him alone.

Behold, I will deliver Pharaoh Hophra king of Egypt into the hands of his enemies who seek his life,
Hophra, known in Greek history as Apries, reigned over Egypt during Judah’s final years. Egyptian aid briefly encouraged resistance against Babylon, but Hophra could not preserve Jerusalem or secure his own throne. Internal revolt eventually raised Amasis against him, fulfilling the warning that Egypt’s king was subject to God’s judgment.

Isa 19:4 I will deliver the Egyptians into the hands of a harsh master,…

just as I delivered Zedekiah king of Judah into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon,
Zedekiah’s capture after Jerusalem’s fall in 586 BC had already demonstrated the certainty of God’s warnings. He had resisted Babylon despite Jeremiah’s counsel, fled the city, and was taken to Nebuchadnezzar at Riblah. The Jewish refugees in Egypt could therefore see that reliance upon an earthly ruler could not overturn the Lord’s declared purpose.
