Jesus Heals a Disabled Woman

Luke 13:10

One Sabbath
The Sabbath was Israel’s weekly day of rest, extending from Friday evening to Saturday evening, and commemorated God’s rest after creation (Genesis 2:2–3). It also stood as a covenant sign between the Lord and Israel (Exodus 31:16–17). Jesus did not treat the Sabbath as unimportant; He fulfilled its God-given purpose by bringing mercy, freedom, and restoration. His works of healing exposed the spiritual blindness of leaders who valued restrictive traditions above compassion.

Jesus was teaching
Jesus regularly taught the Scriptures in public, declaring the nearness of God’s kingdom and calling His hearers to repentance and faith. His teaching carried divine authority rather than merely repeating established interpretations (Matthew 7:28–29). The setting prepares for His healing of a woman long afflicted by Satan, showing that His word and His works reveal the liberating reign of God.

Synagogues were local gathering places for prayer, Scripture reading, instruction, and community life. Jesus ministered among His own people within these established settings, bringing the fulfillment of God’s promises into their midst. The synagogue also became the place where opposition arose, as the ruler’s objection to healing on the Sabbath revealed a failure to recognize the Lord’s compassion and authority.

Religious traditions had multiplied safeguards around the command, yet the leader treated an act of deliverance as a violation rather than recognizing God’s mercy at work. Jesus had not diminished the Sabbath; He revealed its purpose as a day suited for life, freedom, and praise.

Psalm 118:26 provided the words shouted by many during Jesus’ entry into Jerusalem, yet the city soon rejected its King. A future generation of Israel will welcome Him in genuine repentance and faith when He returns in glory. Zechariah foretold that Israel will look upon the One whom they pierced, and Paul declared that Israel’s salvation will accompany the coming of the Deliverer from Zion.
