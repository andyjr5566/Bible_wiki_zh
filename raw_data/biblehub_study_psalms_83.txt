O God, Be Not Silent

Psalm 83:1

A song. A Psalm of Asaph.
Asaph served among the Levites appointed by David to lead Israel’s worship before the ark of God (1 Chronicles 6:31–32; 16:4–7). His name also became associated with a guild of temple musicians that continued for generations. Psalm 83 was written for the gathered people of God to sing as a prayer in a time of national danger. The enemies named later in the psalm form a hostile coalition against Israel and, ultimately, against Israel’s God.

O God, be not silent;
Israel’s danger had reached the point where only the Lord’s intervention could preserve His people. God’s silence did not mean that He was unaware or powerless, but it could leave His servants waiting in painful dependence. Asaph appeals to the God who had spoken, judged, and delivered throughout Israel’s history, asking Him to answer the defiance of those who opposed His covenant purposes.

The psalm rests on the conviction that the Lord will act for the honor of His name and the preservation of those whom He has chosen.

Haman’s plot to annihilate the Jews, the Philistines’ schemes against Israel, and the alliances named later in this psalm all reflect this continuing enmity.

Psalm 83:6

the tents of Edom and the Ishmaelites,
Edom descended from Esau and occupied the rugged land south of the Dead Sea. Though Esau and Jacob were brothers, Edom repeatedly treated Israel as an enemy, rejoicing over Judah’s calamity and resisting Israel’s passage through its territory. The tents of Edom picture their military encampments joined to the nomadic Ishmaelites, descendants of Abraham through Hagar. Ishmaelite traders carried Joseph into Egypt, setting in motion the events through which God preserved Jacob’s family during famine. Nations descended from Abraham’s household had become united in hostility against the covenant people, revealing how deeply sin can corrupt even close family ties.

of Moab and the Hagrites,
Moab descended from Lot and lived east of the Dead Sea. Moab opposed Israel during the wilderness journey, hiring Balaam to curse the people whom God had blessed, and later drew Israelites into idolatry at Baal-peor. The Hagrites were likely desert tribes associated with Ishmael’s line and are elsewhere recorded as opponents defeated by the eastern tribes of Israel when they sought God’s help. The gathering of settled kingdoms and wandering tribes portrays a broad conspiracy against Israel. Their strength and numbers could not overturn the Lord’s covenant purposes, for He remains the defender of His people against every power raised against them.
