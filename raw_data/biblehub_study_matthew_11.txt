Matthew 11:13

For all the Prophets and the Law
This phrase refers to the entirety of the Old Testament, which is often summarized as “the Law and the Prophets.” The Law, or Torah, includes the first five books of the Bible, traditionally attributed to Moses. The Prophets encompass the historical and prophetic books that follow. Together, they form the foundation of Jewish religious teaching and are seen as pointing forward to the coming of the Messiah. This phrase underscores the continuity of God’s revelation from the Old Testament to the New Testament.

prophesied
The term “prophesied” indicates that the Law and the Prophets were not merely historical or legal documents but were forward-looking, containing prophecies and foreshadowings of future events, particularly concerning the coming of the Messiah. This aligns with the belief that the Old Testament contains numerous messianic prophecies, such as those found in Isaiah 53 and Micah 5:2, which Christians believe are fulfilled in Jesus Christ.

Matthew 11:27

All things have been entrusted to Me by My Father.
The Father has committed to the Son the authority, judgment, revelation, and saving rule necessary to accomplish God’s redemptive purpose. Jesus receives this commission as the obedient Son, yet His authority is universal and divine, anticipating His declaration that all authority in heaven and on earth belongs to Him (Matthew 28:18). Isaiah foresaw the promised King upon whose shoulders the government would rest (Isaiah 9:6–7), and Daniel saw the Son of Man receiving an everlasting kingdom from the Ancient of Days (Daniel 7:13–14). Jesus stands as the appointed Mediator through whom God reigns, judges, saves, and restores.

The Father alone fully comprehends the eternal identity and glory of the Son. Human observers could see Jesus’ works, hear His teaching, and even acknowledge Him as a prophet, but no created mind can independently grasp the depth of who He is. The Son’s divine person and eternal fellowship with the Father exceed human discovery. Peter’s confession that Jesus is the Christ came through the Father’s revelation rather than human insight (Matthew 16:16–17).

Jesus does not merely speak about God as a messenger reporting what He has learned; He makes the Father known as the One who has come from the Father. John affirms that the Son has revealed the unseen God to humanity (John 1:18). Every true knowledge of the Father is therefore bound to the Son, whose words, works, mercy, holiness, and sacrificial death reveal the Father’s character and will.

Jesus reveals the Father through the gospel, calling sinners to Himself and granting them access to God. Those who receive Him are not left with distant ideas about God; they are brought into the Father’s grace through the Son who alone can reconcile humanity to Him.

Matthew 11:29

Take My yoke upon you
A yoke was a wooden beam that joined animals for labor, an image familiar in the fields of Galilee. Jesus calls people not to independence from all authority, but to willing submission under His rule and in fellowship with Him. Jewish teachers also used the image of a yoke for instruction and obedience. Unlike the Pharisees, who tied up heavy religious burdens for others without helping them bear them (Matthew 23:4), Jesus gives disciples His own teaching, strength, and direction.
