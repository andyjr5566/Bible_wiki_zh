Psalm 107:1

Give thanks to the LORD,
Worship begins with grateful acknowledgment of the LORD’s saving rule over His people. Psalm 107 summons those whom He has redeemed and gathered from distant lands to recount His acts of deliverance. Thanksgiving remembers that Israel’s preservation, restoration, and hope came from God rather than human strength.

The LORD’s goodness is displayed in His righteous character and His merciful dealings with those in need. He delivered Israel from Egypt, sustained them in the wilderness, disciplined them for sin, and did not abandon His covenant purposes. His goodness invites trust even when His people face distress or uncertainty.

God’s redeemed people are called to testify openly to His saving work. Israel’s deliverance from Egypt stood as the foundational Old Testament example of redemption, when the LORD brought His people out of slavery by His mighty power (Exodus 6:6). Worship includes remembering and declaring what God has done, so that His faithfulness is known among His people and before the nations. Those rescued by the LORD have a true witness to bear, not merely an inward gratitude to possess.

Such deliverance points forward to the greater redemption accomplished by Christ, who rescues believers from the dominion of darkness and grants forgiveness through His blood (Colossians 1:13–14).

Israel’s forty-year journey after the exodus revealed both human unbelief and God’s faithful provision. The wilderness was a land of scarcity, danger, and testing, where the Lord humbled His people and taught them to depend on His word rather than visible resources (Deuteronomy 8:2–3). Such wandering also portrays the spiritual condition of people alienated from God, unable to find lasting life apart from His leading.

Trust in the Lord brings the guidance that self-reliance cannot provide (Proverbs 3:5–6).

Their desperate condition recalls Israel’s wilderness journey, when the people lacked bread and water and cried out to the Lord (Exodus 16:3; 17:3). God answered with manna from heaven and water from the rock, showing that life in the wilderness depended entirely on His provision. Physical hunger and thirst also reveal humanity’s deeper need for God. Jesus fulfilled the hope pictured in those provisions by declaring Himself the bread of life and offering living water to all who believe in Him (John 6:35; 4:10, 14).

In an agricultural society, a growing household, like multiplying flocks, signified security, provision, and God’s blessing. The Lord who restores the afflicted also establishes them with descendants and a future, fulfilling the pattern of His covenant faithfulness seen in His promises to Abraham (Genesis 15:5). Children are a heritage from the Lord, and a household strengthened by Him becomes evidence that He can turn deprivation into abundance (Psalm 127:3–5). The image also anticipates Christ’s care for His people, whom He gathers and shepherds as His flock (John 10:11).

Psalm 107:42

The upright see and rejoice,
Those who live in the fear of the Lord recognize His faithful rule in the reversals described throughout Psalm 107. He rescues the needy, humbles the proud, and turns desolation into fruitfulness. Their joy rests in seeing God act with righteousness and mercy.

and all iniquity shuts its mouth.
Evil has no answer when God displays His justice. Job also speaks of the wicked being silenced before the Lord’s righteous judgment (Job 5:16). God’s present acts of deliverance and judgment anticipate the day when every tongue will confess the lordship of Jesus Christ (Philippians 2:10–11).

Psalm 107:43

Let him who is wise
Wisdom recognizes God’s rule over every condition of life. The redeemed are called to look beyond changing circumstances and remember how the LORD gathered wanderers, freed prisoners, healed the afflicted, calmed the storm-tossed, and restored the needy.

pay heed to these things
Careful reflection keeps God’s past mercies from being forgotten. Israel’s history showed that distress often exposed human helplessness, while deliverance revealed the LORD’s readiness to hear those who cry to Him.
