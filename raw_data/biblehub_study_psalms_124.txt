Our Help Is in the Name of the LORD

Psalm 124:1

A song of ascents.
Psalm 124 belongs to Psalms 120–134, a collection used by Israel as pilgrims traveled up to Jerusalem for Passover, Pentecost, and Tabernacles. Jerusalem stood on elevated ground, and the upward journey pictured a people drawing near to the Lord in worship. The song recalls God’s protection and teaches worshipers to remember that their safety rests in Him.

Psa 121:1 A song of ascents. I lift up my eyes to the hills.…

Psa 122:1 I was glad when they said to me, “Let us go…

Of David.
David’s life supplied many occasions for such thanksgiving. The Lord preserved him from Saul, from foreign enemies, from rebellion, and from dangers that exceeded human strength. As Israel’s king, David also knew that the nation’s victories and continued existence depended upon the covenant God who had chosen and defended His people.

Psa 11:1 In the LORD I take refuge. How then can you say to me:…

Psa 12:1 Help, O LORD, for the godly are no more; the faithful…

Psa 14:1 The fool says in his heart, “There is no God.”…

If the LORD had not been on our side—
The covenant name of God directs Israel’s attention to the faithful Lord who had bound Himself to His people. Israel’s deliverance from Egypt, preservation in the wilderness, conquest of Canaan, and survival against surrounding nations all testified that God had fought for them. Without His presence, their enemies would have overwhelmed them.

Psa 118:6 The LORD is on my side; I will not be afraid.…

Rom 8:31 What then shall we say in response to these things?…

Psa 56:9 Then my enemies will retreat on the day I cry for help.…

let Israel now declare—
David summons the whole nation to give public testimony to God’s saving help. Corporate remembrance strengthened Israel’s faith and instructed each generation to acknowledge the Lord rather than credit human power. The gathered people were to confess that their security, worship, and future depended upon the God who remained on their side.

Psalm 124:2

if the LORD had not been on our side
Israel’s preservation rested on the covenant faithfulness of the LORD, not on military strength or national wisdom. From the deliverance from Pharaoh at the Red Sea to victories over hostile nations in the land, the history of Israel testified that God had acted for His people. David calls the congregation to acknowledge that survival belonged to the LORD alone.

Psa 118:6 The LORD is on my side; I will not be afraid.…

2Ch 32:8 With him is only the arm of flesh, but with us is the LORD…

Rom 8:31 What then shall we say in response to these things?…

when men attacked us,
Israel repeatedly faced enemies who sought to enslave, destroy, or dispossess them, including Egypt, the Philistines, Assyria, and Babylon. Human opposition was real and often overwhelming, yet God restrained Israel’s enemies and delivered His people according to His purposes. The confession also directs believers to depend upon the Lord amid every conflict, remembering that God’s presence removes the ultimate power of those who oppose His people.

Psa 56:2 My enemies pursue me all day long, for many…

Psalm 124:3

when their anger flared against us,
David remembers enemies whose hatred had become open aggression. Israel’s history included attacks from Philistines, Assyrians, Babylonians, and other nations determined to destroy God’s people. Such hostility ultimately opposed the Lord who had chosen Israel and bound Himself to them by covenant. The raging of human enemies also foreshadows the spiritual opposition directed against all who belong to God.

Est 3:5-6 When Haman saw that Mordecai would not bow down…

Neh 4:1 Now when Sanballat heard that we were rebuilding the wall,…

Psa 2:1-2 Why do the nations rage and the peoples plot in vain?…

then they would have swallowed us alive,
The picture is of helpless victims consumed suddenly and completely, like prey devoured by a beast or Korah’s company swallowed by the earth (Numbers 16:32). Without the Lord’s protection, Israel would have been overwhelmed beyond recovery. God’s deliverance points beyond Israel’s preservation to Christ, who endured the hatred of sinful men, entered death, and rose victorious so that death would not consume His people.

Pro 1:12 let us swallow them alive like Sheol, and whole…

Num 16:30-33 But if the LORD brings about something unprecedented,…

Psa 55:15 Let death seize them by surprise; let them go down to Sheol……

Psalm 124:4

then the floods would have engulfed us,
Israel’s survival depended wholly on the LORD’s intervention against hostile enemies. Floodwaters portray destructive forces that sweep away every human defense, recalling the waters of judgment in Noah’s day and the Red Sea through which God saved His people. What threatened to consume Israel instead became an occasion for God’s preserving power.

then the torrent would have overwhelmed us,
A rushing torrent pictures danger beyond human control, sudden and irresistible in its force. David directs God’s people to remember that their deliverance was not secured by strength, strategy, or numbers, but by the LORD who restrains chaos and rescues those who trust Him. The same God remains present with His people when they pass through deep waters and overwhelming trials.

Jer 47:2 This is what the LORD says: “See how the waters are rising…

Psalm 124:5

then the raging waters
Israel’s enemies are pictured as a destructive flood, capable of overwhelming the nation apart from the Lord’s help. David draws on the familiar terror of floodwaters and on God’s demonstrated mastery over the sea at the Red Sea, where He delivered Israel from Pharaoh’s army (Exodus 14). Waters also recall the judgment of Noah’s flood, revealing the helplessness of mankind before forces God alone can restrain.

Gen 7:17 For forty days the flood kept coming on the earth…

Psa 18:16 He reached down from on high and took hold of me;…

Isa 8:7-8 the Lord will surely bring against them the floodwaters…

would have swept us away.
The danger was not temporary hardship but complete ruin. Israel’s survival testified that the Lord had intervened against threats beyond human strength. Jesus likewise revealed divine authority over the chaotic sea when He calmed the storm, and He delivers His people from the greater judgment of sin and death.

Exo 14:27-28 So Moses stretched out his hand over the sea,…

Psa 69:15 Do not let the floods engulf me or the depths swallow me up…

Psalm 124:6

Blessed be the LORD,
David calls God’s people to praise the covenant Lord whose power and goodness have preserved them. Israel’s worship repeatedly blesses the LORD in grateful recognition that every rescue comes from His hand (Psalm 103:1–2; Psalm 124:1–5).

Psa 68:19 Blessed be the Lord, who daily bears our burden…

Psa 144:1 Of David. Blessed be the LORD, my Rock,…

Psa 28:6 Blessed be the LORD, for He has heard my cry for mercy.

who has not given us
The LORD did not abandon His people to the designs of their enemies. Israel’s history—from deliverance at the Red Sea to protection amid hostile nations—testifies that God sovereignly restrains those who would destroy His people. His preserving care ultimately finds its fullest assurance in Christ, who keeps His sheep and gives them eternal life (John 10:27–29).

Psa 41:2 The LORD will protect and preserve him; He will bless him…

as prey to their teeth.
Violent enemies are pictured as predators ready to tear apart defenseless prey. The image recalls the real danger Israel faced from surrounding nations and also portrays the spiritual hostility believers endure in a fallen world. The devil seeks to devour, but God provides refuge and strength for those who trust Him (1 Peter 5:8–9).

Psalm 124:7

We have escaped like a bird from the snare of the fowler;
David pictures Israel as a helpless bird caught by enemies who sought its destruction. The Lord repeatedly preserved His people when hostile nations, powerful rulers, and human schemes threatened to overwhelm them. Psalm 91:3 likewise identifies the Lord as the One who delivers His servants from the snare of the fowler.

the net is torn,
Escape came because God broke what human strength could not break. Israel’s deliverance from Egypt, protection in war, and preservation through national threats testify that the Lord can shatter every means of bondage. The torn net also points to the greater freedom secured through Christ, who delivers His people from sin, death, and the power of the devil.

and we have slipped away.
The worshipers confess that their safety was not achieved by their own skill or power but by the Lord’s intervention. God’s people may be surrounded by danger, yet He is able to bring them out of traps that appear inescapable.

Psalm 124:8

Our help is in the name of the LORD,
Israel’s confidence rests in the covenant God whose name reveals His holy character, sovereign authority, and faithful power to save. The worshipers of Psalm 124 had escaped overwhelming danger only because the LORD had been on their side. Calling on His name is not a formula but an appeal to the God who has bound Himself to His people in covenant mercy. His name remains a strong tower for the righteous (Proverbs 18:10), and salvation is ultimately found in Jesus Christ, the name given under heaven by which sinners must be saved (Acts 4:12).

Psa 20:7 Some trust in chariots and others in horses…

Isa 50:10 Who among you fears the LORD and obeys the voice…

Pro 18:10 The name of the LORD is a strong tower;…

the Maker of heaven and earth.
The God who helps His people is no local deity or limited ruler, but the Creator of all things (Genesis 1:1). He possesses absolute authority over nations, waters, enemies, and every circumstance that threatens His people. The One who made heaven and earth does not grow weary or lack understanding (Isaiah 40:28). Through the Son, all things were created and continue to hold together (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16–17), giving believers firm reason to trust the Lord’s power to sustain and deliver.

Psa 121:2 My help comes from the LORD, the Maker of heaven and earth.

Exo 20:11 For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth…
