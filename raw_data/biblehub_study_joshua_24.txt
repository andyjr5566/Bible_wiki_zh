Joshua Reviews Israel’s History

Joshua 24:1

Then Joshua assembled all the tribes of Israel at Shechem.
Near the close of his life, Joshua gathered Israel at Shechem for a decisive renewal of the covenant. Shechem stood between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim, where Israel had previously heard the blessings and curses of the law (Joshua 8:30–35). The location also recalled God’s first promise of the land to Abraham (Genesis 12:6–7) and Jacob’s return to the land under God’s protection (Genesis 33:18–20). Israel’s presence there testified that the Lord had fulfilled what He promised to the patriarchs.

The representatives of every level of Israel’s public life were called to bear responsibility for the nation’s response to God. Elders provided community leadership, judges administered justice, and officers carried out the decisions of the assembly. Joshua did not treat covenant faithfulness as a private concern; the whole nation, from its leaders to its families, was accountable to the Lord.

Having urged Israel to choose whom they would serve, he set before them the lasting obligation of covenant faithfulness. His leadership points forward to Christ, who brings His people into the inheritance secured by God’s grace.

And the bones of Joseph, which the Israelites had brought up out of Egypt,
Joseph had made Israel swear to carry his remains from Egypt when God visited and delivered them (Genesis 50:25). Moses honored that oath at the exodus (Exodus 13:19), and Israel completed it after entering Canaan. Joseph’s burial testifies that he trusted God’s promise of the land more than the honor and security he possessed in Egypt.

were buried at Shechem
Shechem stood near the place where the Lord first appeared to Abram and promised the land to his descendants (Genesis 12:6–7). Jacob later settled there and built an altar to the God of Israel (Genesis 33:18–20). Joseph’s resting place in Shechem joined the patriarchal promises to Israel’s actual possession of the land.

in the plot of land that Jacob had purchased from the sons of Hamor, Shechem’s father,
Jacob’s purchase provided a lawful family holding in Canaan long before the conquest (Genesis 33:19). The burial site preserved a visible connection between Jacob’s household, Joseph’s descendants, and the covenant land. Shechem would later serve as a city of refuge and a Levitical city, marking it as a significant place in Israel’s spiritual and national life.

Joshua 24:33

Eleazar son of Aaron also died,
Eleazar was the third son of Aaron, the first high priest of Israel, and succeeded his father in the priestly role. His death marks the end of an era for the Israelites, as he was a key figure during the conquest of Canaan and the distribution of the land. Eleazar’s role as high priest was crucial in maintaining the spiritual leadership and religious practices of the Israelites, as outlined in the books of Exodus and Leviticus. His death signifies a transition in leadership and a reminder of the mortality of even the most revered leaders.

1Ch 6:4-8 Eleazar was the father of Phinehas, Phinehas…

and they buried him at Gibeah,
Gibeah, in this context, is not to be confused with the more infamous Gibeah of Saul. This Gibeah is a location in the hill country of Ephraim. The act of burial in a specific location often held significant meaning, indicating a place of honor or familial connection. Burial practices in ancient Israel were deeply tied to family and tribal identity, and being buried in one’s own land was considered a blessing and a sign of belonging.

Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, is noted for his zealousness for God’s covenant, as seen in Numbers 25, where he took decisive action against idolatry and immorality. His inheritance of Gibeah signifies the continuation of the priestly line and the blessings associated with faithfulness to God. The land given to Phinehas serves as a tangible representation of God’s favor and the perpetuation of the priestly duties through his lineage.
