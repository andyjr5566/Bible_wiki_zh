I Will Set No Worthless Thing before My Eyes

Psalm 101:1

A Psalm of David.
The superscription identifies David, Israel’s shepherd-king, as the writer. Psalm 101 records the moral commitments of a ruler who knows that his household, court, and kingdom must be governed under God’s authority. David’s own failures make the psalm’s standard especially sobering: righteous leadership depends on continual dependence upon the LORD.

I will sing of Your loving devotion and justice;
David begins with God’s covenant faithfulness and righteous judgment. Israel’s king was called to reflect both qualities in public rule, defending the innocent, restraining evil, and showing faithful care for God’s people. Love without justice would tolerate wickedness, while justice without mercy would misrepresent the LORD’s covenant heart.

to You, O LORD, I will sing praises.
David directs worship to Yahweh, the God who bound Himself to Israel and established David’s throne. Praise is not separated from obedience in Psalm 101; worship of the righteous King must shape the conduct of earthly rulers and all who belong to Him. Jesus Christ perfectly reveals God’s steadfast love and justice, reigning without corruption and receiving the praise due to the Lord alone.

Psalm 101:2

I will ponder the way that is blameless—
David resolves to give careful attention to the path of uprightness rather than drifting according to personal desire or royal privilege. God had called Israel to holiness because He is holy (Leviticus 19:2), and the righteous person delights in God’s instruction and meditates on it continually (Psalm 1:2). A blameless life does not claim sinlessness but reflects undivided devotion, repentance, and obedience before God. David’s concern anticipates the wisdom of Proverbs, where the way of the righteous leads to life while the path of the wicked ends in ruin.

Psa 15:2 He who walks with integrity and practices righteousness…

when will You come to me?
David’s pursuit of integrity is joined to a longing for God’s nearness. Israel’s king could not govern rightly by strength, rank, or military success; he needed the Lord’s favor, guidance, and presence. The Ark of the Covenant had signified God’s dwelling among His covenant people, and David deeply desired to honor the God who had established his throne. The question carries both prayer and expectation: obedience must be sustained by fellowship with God. Believers know God’s presence most fully through Jesus Christ, Immanuel, “God with us.”

I will walk in my house with integrity of heart.
David begins with the hidden sphere of his own household. Public leadership cannot be separated from private character, for a ruler’s conduct at home affects the life of the nation. Integrity of heart requires sincerity before God, not merely outward conformity or reputation. The same principle governs spiritual leadership in the church, where a man’s care for his household bears witness to his ability to serve God’s people (1 Timothy 3:4–5). Jesus Christ alone lived without sin, yet He remains the pattern and source of righteous living for all who seek to walk faithfully before God.

Psalm 101:3

I will set no worthless thing before my eyes.
David resolves to reject whatever is wicked, deceptive, idolatrous, or morally corrupt. What enters through the eyes can shape the mind, affections, and conduct; therefore, holiness requires deliberate restraint. Job made a similar covenant with his eyes (Job 31:1), and Jesus taught that the eye affects the whole person’s spiritual condition (Matthew 6:22–23).

I hate the work of those who fall away;
David opposes the deeds of those who deliberately turn from God’s ways, including the idolatry and corruption that repeatedly drew Israel from covenant faithfulness. His hatred is directed toward rebellion against God, not a denial of mercy toward sinners. God’s servants must refuse to approve, imitate, or participate in works that dishonor Him.

Sin easily entangles those who tolerate it, but David seeks to remain free from its influence and defilement. His resolve anticipates the call to lay aside every sin that clings closely (Hebrews 12:1) and to live in the world without belonging to its evil pattern (John 17:14–16).

Psalm 101:4

A perverse heart shall depart from me;
David resolves to reject inward corruption as well as outward wrongdoing. The heart directs conduct, and a ruler devoted to the Lord cannot tolerate deceit, pride, cruelty, or divided loyalty within himself. Such vigilance accords with Proverbs 4:23, which commands God’s people to guard the heart as the source from which life proceeds.

I will know nothing of evil.
David refuses to welcome, approve, or cultivate wickedness. His commitment anticipates the holiness required of all who belong to God and points to Christ, who was tempted in every way yet remained without sin. God’s people are called to live in the world without becoming companions of its rebellion, being sanctified by His truth.

Psalm 101:5

Whoever slanders his neighbor in secret, I will put to silence;
David vows to remove secret slanderers from his household and rule. Hidden attacks on another person’s name corrupt trust, divide communities, and often escape human justice. God hears every malicious word spoken behind closed doors and defends those harmed by false accusation. His people are called to speak truth, guard a neighbor’s reputation, and refuse participation in gossip and slander.

the one with haughty eyes and a proud heart, I will not endure.
Pride displays itself in contempt for others and self-exaltation before God. Such a heart cannot serve faithfully in a kingdom ordered by righteousness, because it resists correction and seeks its own honor. David’s standard anticipates God’s judgment of the proud and His favor toward the humble. The King who rules under God must not tolerate conduct that dishonors the Lord and injures His people.

Psalm 101:6

My eyes favor the faithful of the land,
David declares that his attention and approval will rest on people marked by covenant loyalty rather than wealth, influence, or flattery. As Israel’s king, he seeks companions and officers whose lives honor the Lord, reflecting God’s concern for justice and righteousness among His people.

that they may dwell with me;
Those admitted to David’s household and court would share close access to the king and influence the life of the kingdom. David recognizes that godly leadership requires a godly community, since the character of those nearest to a ruler affects the direction of the nation.

he who walks in the way of integrity
Integrity describes a life whose conduct agrees with God’s revealed standards. David’s requirement reaches beyond outward reputation to a settled pattern of honesty, purity, justice, and faithfulness. Job likewise was commended as a man who held fast to integrity before God.

David intends to entrust service in his house and kingdom to upright people. Such servants would include counselors, administrators, and attendants whose decisions could either strengthen or corrupt the nation. The standard anticipates the servant leadership fulfilled perfectly in Christ, who came to serve and gave His life for many.

Psalm 101:7

No one who practices deceit shall dwell in my house;
David would not retain deceitful people within his royal household or administration. As Israel’s king, he recognized that a court marked by fraud and manipulation could not reflect the righteous rule of God. The standard reaches beyond public office to every household under God’s authority, for lying lips are an abomination to the Lord (Proverbs 12:22), and God Himself is faithful and without injustice (Deuteronomy 32:4). Those who seek fellowship with the Lord must walk in integrity (Psalm 15:1–2).

no one who tells lies shall stand in my presence.
Standing before the king described the trusted service of courtiers and officials. David refused to grant favor or influence to those whose words could not be trusted. Such judgment anticipated God’s final judgment, where deceit has no place in His holy presence (Revelation 21:8; 21:27). Jesus Christ is the truth (John 14:6), and His people are called to put away falsehood and speak truthfully with one another (Ephesians 4:25).

Psalm 101:8

Every morning
David pledges continual vigilance in the exercise of royal justice. Morning recalls renewed dependence upon God and the fresh mercies He grants to His people (Psalm 5:3; Lamentations 3:22–23). A righteous kingdom cannot treat evil as a matter for occasional concern.

Isa 33:2 O LORD, be gracious to us! We wait for You.…

Lam 3:23 They are new every morning; great is Your faithfulness!

I will remove all the wicked of the land,
The king bears responsibility to restrain those whose settled conduct threatens the covenant community. Israel’s rulers were called to uphold God’s law, punish persistent evil, and protect the innocent (Deuteronomy 17:18–20). Later reforms under kings such as Josiah illustrate the need to remove idolatry and corrupt practices from the land (2 Kings 23:24–25).

that I may cut off every evildoer
David’s resolve concerns those devoted to wickedness rather than isolated sinners seeking mercy and repentance. God’s holiness requires decisive opposition to evil, while His covenant also provides forgiveness for the repentant. The final separation of the wicked from the righteous will occur perfectly under God’s judgment (Matthew 13:49–50).

from the city of the LORD.
Jerusalem was the city God chose for His temple and the center of Israel’s worship and government (Psalm 48:1–2). David understood that public corruption dishonored the God whose name dwelt there. The earthly city points forward to the New Jerusalem, where nothing unclean will enter and God will dwell with His redeemed people forever (Revelation 21:2, 27).
