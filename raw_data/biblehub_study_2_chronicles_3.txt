Temple Construction Begins

2 Chronicles 3:1

Then Solomon began to build the house of the LORD
Solomon began the work David had long prepared for but was not permitted to complete. As David’s son, he fulfilled God’s covenant promise that David’s offspring would build a house for the Lord’s name (2 Samuel 7:12–13). The Temple established the appointed center of Israel’s sacrificial worship, replacing the portable tabernacle while bearing witness that the Lord was present with His covenant people.

The temple replaced the portable tabernacle as Israel’s central place of sacrifice, prayer, and covenant worship. Though God cannot be contained by any building, He chose this house as the appointed place for His name and the visible center of His presence among His people.

The Most Holy Place, or Holy of Holies, stood at the innermost part of the temple as the earthly dwelling place of God’s manifested glory. It continued the arrangement of the wilderness tabernacle, where the veil separated the holy place from the ark of the covenant (Exodus 26:33–34). Only Israel’s high priest entered this chamber, and only once each year on the Day of Atonement, bearing sacrificial blood before the Lord (Leviticus 16:2–34).

The costly colors and fine linen reflected the purity, majesty, and holiness required in the worship of the Lord. The veil also testified that sinful humanity could not casually enter God’s immediate presence; only the high priest entered beyond it, and only once each year with sacrificial blood on the Day of Atonement.
