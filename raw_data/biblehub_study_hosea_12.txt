A Reproof of Ephraim, Judah, and Jacob

Hosea 12:1

Ephraim feeds on the wind
Ephraim, representing the northern kingdom of Israel, sought satisfaction and security in pursuits as empty as feeding on air. Wealth, diplomacy, idolatry, and military strength could not sustain a people who had forsaken the Lord. Like the “striving after wind” described in Ecclesiastes 1:14, Israel’s efforts produced no lasting good because they were detached from reverence for God.

Hos 8:7 For they sow the wind, and they shall reap the whirlwind.…

Ecc 1:14 I have seen all the things that are done under the sun…

Pro 11:29 He who brings trouble on his house will inherit the wind…

and pursues the east wind all day long;
The scorching east wind brought drought, withering, and devastation to the land. Ephraim was not merely pursuing emptiness but was chasing what would bring ruin. Israel’s foreign alliances, conceived as protection from powerful enemies, would become instruments of judgment and captivity. Hosea later portrays the east wind as the force that dries up Ephraim’s springs and plunders its treasures (Hosea 13:15).

Hos 13:15 Although he flourishes among his brothers,…

Jer 18:17 I will scatter them before the enemy like the east wind.…

Psa 48:7 With a wind from the east You wrecked the ships of Tarshish.

he multiplies lies and violence;
National life had become marked by broken promises, dishonest dealings, oppression, and bloodshed. The corruption condemned by Hosea reached from rulers and priests to the wider society. Covenants made with men were unreliable because the nation had already proved unfaithful to its covenant with God. Micah likewise condemned Israel’s wealthy and powerful for violence, deceit, and false speech (Micah 6:12).

Hos 4:1-2 Hear the word of the LORD, O children of Israel,…

Mic 6:12 For the wealthy of the city are full of violence,…

Psa 5:6 You destroy those who tell lies; the LORD abhors…

he makes a covenant with Assyria
Israel looked to Assyria, the dominant military power of the day, for protection and political advantage. Rather than resting in the Lord who had delivered Israel from Egypt and established them in the land, Ephraim sought safety through submission to a pagan empire. Such dependence exposed the nation to Assyrian control and ultimately contributed to its fall.

Hos 5:13 When Ephraim saw his sickness and Judah his wound,…

Hos 7:11 So Ephraim has become like a silly, senseless dove…

2Ki 16:7-9 So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria,…

and sends olive oil to Egypt.
Olive oil was a valuable product of Israel’s land and was sent as tribute or diplomatic payment to secure Egypt’s support. Ephraim attempted to gain favor from both Assyria and Egypt, rival powers whose promises could not save them. Isaiah similarly rebuked Judah for seeking Egyptian aid without seeking the Lord’s counsel (Isaiah 30:1–2). Israel’s divided loyalties revealed a deeper refusal to trust the God who alone could preserve His people.

2Ki 17:4 But the king of Assyria discovered that Hoshea had conspired…

Ezk 17:15-17 But this king rebelled against Babylon by sending his envoys…

Isa 30:1-5 “Woe to the rebellious children,” declares the LORD……

Hosea 12:2

The LORD has a charge to bring against Judah.
Hosea’s ministry addressed the northern kingdom of Israel, yet Judah was not exempt from God’s scrutiny. Jerusalem’s temple and Davidic heritage could not protect a people who tolerated idolatry, injustice, and covenant disobedience. The Lord stands as the righteous Judge and covenant King, holding His people accountable for the obligations they had received.

Isa 1:2 Listen, O heavens, and give ear, O earth…

Mic 6:2 Hear, O mountains, the LORD’s indictment,…

Isa 3:13 The LORD arises to contend; He stands to judge the people.

He will punish Jacob according to his ways
Jacob represents the covenant nation descended from the patriarch, including both Israel and Judah. The divided kingdoms shared a common spiritual inheritance and a common guilt. Jacob’s own history of self-reliance, deception, discipline, and eventual dependence upon God mirrors the nation’s need to turn from its corrupt paths and seek the Lord.

Ezk 18:30 Therefore, O house of Israel, I will judge you…

Ezk 7:3 The end is now upon you, and I will unleash My anger.…

Job 34:11 For according to a man’s deeds He repays him;…

and repay him according to his deeds.
God’s judgment would correspond to the nation’s persistent rebellion rather than arise from arbitrary anger. Israel would soon fall under Assyrian invasion, and Judah would later face Babylonian exile because neither kingdom remained faithful to the Lord. Sin brings real consequences, yet the call to return remains open: “Return to your God; maintain love and justice, and wait for your God always” (Hosea 12:6).

Rom 2:6 God “will repay each one according to his deeds.”

Rev 22:12 “Behold, I am coming soon, and My reward is with Me,…

Psa 62:12 and loving devotion to You, O Lord.…

Hosea 12:3

In the womb he grasped his brother’s heel,
Jacob’s birth alongside Esau fulfilled God’s word that the older would serve the younger (Genesis 25:23–26). His grasp of Esau’s heel foreshadowed a life marked by striving for the birthright and paternal blessing. Jacob’s name, meaning “he who grasps the heel” or “supplanter,” preserved the memory of that birth, yet God would later transform the grasping supplanter into Israel, the father of the covenant nation. Hosea recalls the event to show that Israel’s history began with a man who depended upon God’s promise, not merely human advantage.

Gen 25:24-26 When her time came to give birth, there were indeed twins…

Gen 25:22-23 But the children inside her struggled with each other,…

Gen 27:36 So Esau declared, “Is he not rightly named Jacob?…

and in his vigor he wrestled with God.
Jacob wrestled through the night at Peniel, meaning “face of God,” as he prepared to meet Esau (Genesis 32:24–30). The divine opponent, identified in Hosea 12:4 as the Angel, wounded Jacob’s hip yet blessed him and gave him the name Israel, meaning “he struggles with God.” Jacob’s strength was not displayed in self-reliance but in refusing to let go of God until he received His blessing. The encounter humbled him, redirected his life, and revealed that covenant blessing comes through dependence on God.

Gen 32:24-30 So Jacob was left all alone, and there a man wrestled with him…

Gen 32:28 Then the man said, “Your name will no longer be Jacob,…

Gen 35:10 And God said to him, “Though your name is Jacob.…

Hosea 12:4

Yes, he struggled with the angel and prevailed;
Jacob wrestled through the night beside the Jabbok as he returned to Canaan in fear of Esau’s approach (Genesis 32:24–30). Though the angel touched Jacob’s hip and left him weakened, Jacob would not release Him without a blessing. Genesis identifies the One with whom Jacob wrestled as God, revealing a divine appearance in which the Lord met His covenant servant personally. Jacob’s prevailing came not through physical strength but through clinging faith.

Gen 32:24-28 So Jacob was left all alone, and there a man wrestled with him…

Gen 32:29-30 And Jacob requested, “Please tell me your name.”…

he wept and sought His favor;
Hosea discloses the brokenness behind Jacob’s struggle. His tears expressed dependence, repentance, and urgent need for God’s mercy as he faced the consequences of his former deceit. God changed his name from Jacob to Israel, marking a transformed man who would live by God’s promise rather than manipulation. The blessing Jacob sought was granted by grace.

Gen 32:11-12 Please deliver me from the hand of my brother Esau,…

Jer 31:9 They will come with weeping, and by their supplication…

Psa 126:5-6 Those who sow in tears will reap with shouts of joy.…

he found Him at Bethel
Bethel, meaning “House of God,” was the place where God first appeared to Jacob as he fled from Esau, reaffirming the Abrahamic covenant and promising His presence (Genesis 28:10–19). Jacob later returned there in obedience, put away foreign gods from his household, and worshiped the Lord who had sustained him through exile and danger (Genesis 35:1–7). The House of God testified that the Lord remained faithful despite Jacob’s failures.

Gen 28:10-19 Meanwhile Jacob left Beersheba and set out for Haran.…

Gen 35:1-7 Then God said to Jacob, “Arise, go up to Bethel.”…

and spoke with Him there—
God’s dealings with Jacob were not merely private memories but covenant instruction for Israel, whom Hosea calls to return to the God of their father. The Lord who spoke at Bethel is the God of hosts, unchanging in holiness, mercy, and covenant faithfulness (Hosea 12:5–6). Jacob’s life calls God’s people to seek Him humbly, depend on His grace, and walk in faithful obedience.

Gen 35:9-13 After Jacob had returned from Paddan-aram…

Gen 35:14-15 So Jacob set up a pillar in the place where God had spoken…

Hosea 12:5

the LORD God of Hosts,
Hosea directs Israel from Jacob’s striving to the God who rules every power in heaven and earth. The covenant God who met Jacob at Bethel commands the armies of heaven and stands above kings, nations, and idols. Israel’s security did not rest in alliances with Assyria or Egypt, but in returning to the Lord with repentance, justice, and steadfast trust.

Psa 84:8 O LORD God of Hosts, hear my prayer; give ear…

Amo 4:13 “For behold, He who forms the mountains”…

Amo 5:27 Therefore I will send you into exile beyond Damascus,”…

the LORD is His name of renown.
God’s name declares His enduring faithfulness to the covenant He established with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. His mighty acts in creation, the exodus, judgment, and deliverance had made His glory known among the nations. Israel was called to remember the Lord’s character and live as His people, rather than seeking strength through deceit and self-reliance.

Exo 3:15 God also told Moses, “Say to the Israelites,…

Psa 135:13 Your name, O LORD, endures forever, Your renown, O LORD…

Exo 15:3 The LORD is a warrior, the LORD is His name.

Hosea 12:6

But you must return to your God;
Hosea calls Israel to turn from idolatry, political self-reliance, and covenant rebellion to the Lord who had redeemed and formed them as His people. The covenant God of Sinai remained Israel’s God despite their unfaithfulness, and genuine return required repentance rather than outward religious activity alone.

Deu 4:30 When you are in distress and all these things have happened…

Jol 2:13 So rend your hearts and not your garments,…

Mal 3:7 Yet from the days of your fathers, you have turned away…

maintain love and justice,
Covenant faithfulness must be seen in steadfast love toward others and righteous dealings in public and personal life. Israel’s worship was corrupt because oppression, dishonesty, and exploitation marked the nation. God desires a people whose conduct reflects His own holiness, mercy, and righteousness.

Mic 6:8 He has shown you, O man, what is good.…

Zec 7:9 Administer true justice. Show loving devotion…

Pro 21:3 To do righteousness and justice is more desirable…

and always wait on your God.
Israel had repeatedly sought security through foreign alliances, wealth, and idols rather than trusting the Lord. Waiting on God calls for persevering faith, obedience, and confidence in His timing and salvation. Those who belong to God are to rest their hope in Him rather than in human strength.

Mic 7:7 But as for me, I will look to the LORD;…

Psa 27:14 Wait patiently for the LORD; be strong and courageous.…

Psa 37:7 Be still before the LORD and wait patiently for Him;…

Hosea 12:7

A merchant
Israel had adopted the ways of the surrounding Canaanite culture—the people who inhabited the land before Israel—pursuing gain without regard for God’s law or neighbor’s welfare. Trade was necessary for life in the land, yet commercial success became a tool for oppression when covenant faithfulness was abandoned.

Ezk 27:12 Tarshish was your merchant because of your great wealth…

Rev 18:11-13 And the merchants of the earth will weep and mourn…

Amo 8:4-6 Hear this, you who trample the needy…

loves to defraud
Greed had become a settled desire rather than an occasional failure. The Lord condemns those who enrich themselves by exploiting the weak, because such conduct denies His justice and reveals a heart unwilling to trust His provision.

Mrk 10:19 You know the commandments: ‘Do not murder, ……

Lev 19:13 You must not defraud your neighbor or rob him.…

1Th 4:6 no one should ever violate or exploit his brother…

with dishonest scales
Altered weights enabled sellers to charge for more than they delivered. God had expressly required accurate measures in Israel, linking honest trade to holiness and obedience. Economic deceit was therefore not merely a business offense but rebellion against the Lord who sees every hidden transaction.

Pro 11:1 Dishonest scales are an abomination to the LORD,…

Mic 6:11 Can I excuse dishonest scales or bags of false weights?

Deu 25:13-16 You shall not have two differing weights in your bag,…

in his hands.
The instruments of fraud were held deliberately and used knowingly. Israel’s sin involved personal responsibility: people chose dishonest gain while claiming the privileges of belonging to God’s covenant people.

Pro 16:11 Honest scales and balances are from the LORD;…

Lev 19:35-36 You must not use dishonest measures of length, weight,…

Ezk 45:10 You must use honest scales, a just ephah, and a just bath.

Hosea 12:8

And Ephraim boasts:
Ephraim stands for the northern kingdom of Israel, which had grown prosperous while abandoning covenant faithfulness. Israel’s confidence rested in political strength, commercial success, and false worship rather than in the Lord. Pride blinded the nation to the judgment already approaching through Assyria.

Jer 9:23-24 This is what the LORD says: “Let not the wise man…

Jas 4:16 As it is, you boast in your proud intentions.…

1Co 1:31 Therefore, as it is written: “Let him who boasts…

“How rich I have become!
Material abundance became evidence of supposed security and innocence in Ephraim’s eyes. Yet Hosea had exposed Israel’s dishonest trade, idolatry, and dependence on foreign alliances. Prosperity without obedience did not signify God’s approval; it increased the nation’s accountability before Him.

Rev 3:17 You say, ‘I am rich; I have grown wealthy…

Deu 8:17-18 You might say in your heart, “The power and strength”…

Ezk 28:4-5 By your wisdom and understanding you have gained…

I have found wealth for myself.
Ephraim credited its gain to personal ability and effort, refusing to acknowledge the Lord as the giver of strength and provision. Israel forgot the warning that wealth can lead the heart away from God when gratitude is replaced by self-congratulation.

Luk 12:21 This is how it will be for anyone who stores up treasure…

1Co 4:7 For who makes you so superior? What do you have…

Jas 5:1-3 Come now, you who are rich, weep and wail…

In all my labors,
The nation, Ephraim (meaning “fruitful”), viewed its business and labor as proof of its virtue, though its commerce was marked by exploitation and deceit. Human effort cannot cleanse sin or establish righteousness before God. The Lord requires truth, justice, mercy, and humble dependence on Him.

Jas 4:13-15 Come now, you who say, “Today or tomorrow…

Pro 23:4-5 Do not wear yourself out to get rich; be wise enough…

Psa 127:1-2 A song of ascents. Of Solomon. Unless the LORD builds…

they can find in me no iniquity that is sinful.”
Ephraim declared itself guiltless despite its idolatry, injustice, and covenant betrayal. Such self-deception rejected the Lord’s indictment and removed any sense of need for repentance. Only Jesus Christ is truly without sin, and only His righteousness can cover those who confess their sin and turn to God.

1Jn 1:8 If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves…

Job 33:9 I am pure, without transgression; I am clean…

Pro 30:20 This is the way of an adulteress: She eats…

Hosea 12:9

But I am the LORD your God ever since the land of Egypt.
The LORD grounds His indictment in the Exodus, when He redeemed Israel from slavery and claimed them as His covenant people. The God who defeated Egypt’s gods, led Israel through the sea, and provided in the wilderness had not changed. Israel’s prosperity and idolatry had not canceled His authority or their obligation to worship Him alone. The Exodus also recalls the covenant at Sinai, where redeemed people were called to live in faithful obedience to their Deliverer.

Num 15:41 I am the LORD your God who brought you out of Egypt…

Lev 25:38 I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt…

Lev 11:45 For I am the LORD, who brought you up out of Egypt…

I will again make you dwell in tents,
Israel’s coming judgment would strip away the security and wealth in which Ephraim boasted. Dwelling in tents recalls the wilderness generation, wholly dependent upon God for food, protection, and direction. What had been remembered through worship would become a painful reality as exile and displacement overtook the nation. Yet the wilderness imagery also preserves the truth that the LORD remains able to sustain and restore those whom He disciplines.

Lev 23:42 You are to dwell in booths for seven days.…

Lev 23:43 so that your descendants may know that I made the Israelites…

Neh 8:17 The whole assembly that had returned from exile…

as in the days of the appointed feast.
The appointed feast points to the Feast of Tabernacles, when Israel lived in temporary shelters and rejoiced over God’s provision during the wilderness journey. Hosea turns that memorial into a warning: Israel had celebrated God’s past faithfulness while refusing present faithfulness to Him. The tents would no longer be merely ceremonial reminders of their ancestors’ dependence, but signs of the loss brought by covenant rebellion.

Lev 23:34 Speak to the Israelites and say, ‘On the fifteenth day…

Deu 16:15 For seven days you shall celebrate a feast to the LORD…

Zec 14:16 Then all the survivors will go up year after year…

Hosea 12:10

I spoke through the prophets
God repeatedly addressed Israel through men raised up to declare His word. Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Elijah, and others confronted idolatry, exposed injustice, warned of judgment, and called the covenant people back to faithful worship. The Lord’s messages through them revealed His patience, since Israel had received abundant instruction rather than being left without guidance.

Heb 1:1 On many past occasions and in many different ways,…

2Ki 17:13 Yet through all His prophets and seers, the LORD…

Amo 3:7 Surely the Lord GOD does nothing without revealing His plan…

and multiplied their visions;
God confirmed and unfolded His purposes through many visions, giving His servants glimpses of coming judgment, restoration, and the reign of the Messiah. Symbolic revelations required reverent attention, yet their central message was clear: the Lord knew Israel’s condition and would act according to His righteousness and mercy. Daniel, Ezekiel, Zechariah, and Isaiah testify to the richness of such revelation.

Dan 7:1 In the first year of the reign of Belshazzar…

Ezk 1:1 In the thirtieth year, on the fifth day of the fourth month,…

Num 12:6 He said, “Hear now My words: If there is a prophet among you…

I gave parables through the prophets.
God also used memorable images and enacted illustrations to make spiritual reality plain. Hosea’s marriage itself became a living parable of Israel’s unfaithfulness and God’s pursuing love. Jesus later taught in parables, revealing the truths of God’s kingdom while calling hearers to receive His word with understanding and repentance.

Ezk 17:2 Son of man, pose a riddle; speak a parable…

Psa 78:2 I will open my mouth in parables; I will utter things hidden…

Mat 13:34-35 Jesus spoke all these things to the crowds in parables.…

Hosea 12:11

Is there iniquity in Gilead?
Gilead, east of the Jordan, was renowned for fertile pastureland and healing balm, yet its outward blessings could not conceal its guilt. The Lord’s question exposes a land filled with idolatry, violence, and covenant unfaithfulness. No region’s history, prosperity, or reputation can shield it from God’s judgment when its people persist in sin.

Hos 6:8 Gilead is a city of evildoers, tracked…

Amo 1:13 This is what the LORD says: “For three transgressions…

They will surely come to nothing.
Gilead’s wickedness would produce emptiness rather than security. The wealth, sacrifices, and alliances in which Israel trusted could not preserve a people who had forsaken the Lord. Sin promises gain but ends in ruin, and every work built apart from obedience to God will perish.

Isa 41:29 See, they are all a delusion; their works amount to nothing;…

Jer 10:15 They are worthless, a work to be mocked.…

Do they sacrifice bulls in Gilgal?
Gilgal had once marked Israel’s entrance into the Promised Land and the renewal of covenant faithfulness under Joshua. By Hosea’s day, it had become a center of corrupt worship, where sacrifices continued without repentance or submission to God. Israel retained religious activity while rejecting the God whom its offerings were meant to honor.

Hos 9:15 All their evil appears at Gilgal, for there I hated them.…

Hos 4:15 Though you prostitute yourself, O Israel,…

1Sa 13:9-14 So he said, “Bring me the burnt offering”…

Indeed, their altars will be heaps of stones in the furrows of the field.
The many altars erected for false worship would be torn down and scattered like fieldstones after plowing. What Israel multiplied in rebellion would become evidence of its judgment. God would not preserve religious structures that upheld idolatry, for He desires faithful hearts and worship according to His covenant.

Hos 10:8 The high places of Aven will be destroyed—…

Mic 1:6 Therefore I will make Samaria a heap of rubble…

Jer 26:18 Micah the Moreshite prophesied in the days of Hezekiah…

Hosea 12:12

Jacob fled to the land of Aram
Jacob left Canaan to escape Esau’s intended revenge after obtaining Isaac’s blessing. On the way to Paddan-aram, God met him at Bethel and reaffirmed the covenant promises given to Abraham and Isaac. His exile exposed the consequences of deception, yet God preserved him and continued His covenant purpose through him.

Gen 28:5 So Isaac sent Jacob to Paddan-aram, to Laban son of Bethuel…

Gen 27:43-45 So now, my son, obey my voice and flee at once…

Deu 26:5 My father was a wandering Aramean, and he went down…

and Israel worked for a wife—
Jacob, later called Israel, served his uncle Laban for seven years to marry Rachel, only to receive Leah instead. He then served another seven years for Rachel. The patriarch through whom God would build Israel knew hardship, injustice, and patient endurance in a foreign land.

Gen 29:18-20 Since Jacob loved Rachel, he answered, “I will serve you…

Gen 29:27-30 Finish this week’s celebration, and we will give you…

Gen 31:41 Thus for twenty years I have served in your household—…

for a wife he tended sheep.
Jacob’s labor as a shepherd was humble and demanding, and Laban repeatedly changed his wages during the years that followed. Hosea recalls Israel’s origins to expose the nation’s pride: their forefather did not establish himself by strength or wealth but depended upon God’s preserving grace. The Lord who guarded Jacob in Aram later redeemed his descendants from Egypt and called them to remain faithful to Him.

Gen 31:38-40 I have been with you for twenty years now.…

Gen 30:29-31 Then Jacob answered, “You know how I have served you…

Gen 29:6-10 “Is he well?” Jacob inquired. “Yes,” they answered,…

Hosea 12:13

But by a prophet
God raised up Moses to speak His word, confront Pharaoh, and lead Israel according to divine command. Moses served as God’s appointed mediator, receiving revelation and teaching the nation the covenant law. Israel’s history did not rest on human strength or political power, but on the Lord’s faithful guidance through His chosen servant.

Exo 7:1-2 The LORD answered Moses, “See, I have made you like God…

Deu 18:15-18 The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me…

Num 12:6-8 He said, “Hear now My words: If there is a prophet among you…

the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt,
The Exodus was the Lord’s mighty deliverance of His covenant people from slavery. Through the plagues, the Passover, and the crossing of the Red Sea, God displayed His authority over Egypt’s gods and fulfilled His promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Israel owed its freedom entirely to the Lord. The deliverance from Egypt also foreshadowed the greater redemption accomplished by Jesus Christ, whose sacrifice delivers His people from bondage to sin.

Exo 12:51 And on that very day the LORD brought the Israelites out…

Deu 26:8 Then the LORD brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand…

Psa 105:37 He brought Israel out with silver and gold,…

and by a prophet he was preserved.
Moses continued to shepherd Israel throughout the wilderness, interceding for the people, giving them God’s law, and directing them according to the Lord’s commands. God preserved Israel despite rebellion, hardship, and judgment, sustaining them with manna, water, protection, and covenant instruction. Hosea reminds a self-reliant and unfaithful generation that their national existence depended on God’s mercy and His word, not on their own achievements.

Deu 1:31-33 and in the wilderness, where the LORD your God carried you…

Psa 77:20 You led Your people like a flock by the hand of Moses…

Num 14:19-20 Pardon, I pray, the iniquity of this people,…

Hosea 12:14

Ephraim has provoked bitter anger,
Ephraim, the dominant tribe of the northern kingdom, stands for Israel as a whole. Hosea has exposed Israel’s deceit, idolatry, exploitation of the poor, and reliance on foreign powers rather than the Lord. Their worship of Baal and refusal to hear God’s prophets constituted covenant rebellion. Israel’s sin was not ignorance but a settled rejection of the God who redeemed them from Egypt and sustained them in the land.

Deu 32:21 They have provoked My jealousy by that which is not God;…

Psa 78:58 They enraged Him with their high places…

2Ki 17:11 They burned incense on all the high places…

so his Lord will leave his bloodguilt upon him
The Lord will no longer remove the guilt Israel has stubbornly retained. Bloodguilt includes the violence, oppression, and innocent blood shed within the nation, sins that defiled the land and demanded judgment (Numbers 35:33). Without repentance and atonement, guilt remains before the righteous Judge. The coming Assyrian conquest would reveal that God’s warnings were true and that persistent rebellion brings real consequences.

1Ki 2:32-33 The LORD will bring his bloodshed back upon his own head,…

Num 35:33 Do not pollute the land where you live, …

Ezk 33:6-9 But if the watchman sees the sword coming…

and repay him for his contempt.
Israel treated the Lord’s covenant, worship, and commandments with contempt. God’s repayment accords with His justice, returning upon Ephraim the consequences of its own rebellion. Judgment would vindicate God’s holiness, yet Hosea’s larger message still held out mercy for those who would return to the Lord in humble faith.

Oba 1:15 For the Day of the LORD is near for all the nations.…

Isa 3:11 Woe to the wicked; disaster is upon them!…

Psa 94:2 Rise up, O Judge of the earth; render a reward to the proud.
