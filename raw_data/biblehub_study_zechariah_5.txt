The Vision of the Flying Scroll

Zechariah 5:1

Again I lifted up my eyes
Zechariah received another vision in the continuing sequence of night visions given to him for Judah’s restoration and purification. His lifted eyes mark renewed attention to what the Lord reveals, as God confronts not only the nation’s outward enemies but also the sin within His covenant people.

Zechariah personally witnessed what he was commanded to proclaim. The vision came with divine authority and required interpretation, demonstrating that the coming cleansing of Jerusalem would include God’s direct judgment upon unrepentant wrongdoing.

The large scroll, later described as twenty cubits long and ten cubits wide, carried God’s written curse against thieves and those who swear falsely by His name. Its flight portrays the swift, active, and inescapable reach of divine judgment. God’s word does not remain distant or ineffective; it enters the lives and homes of those who persist in rebellion, exposing sin and removing it from among His people.

Zechariah sees God’s written decree moving swiftly through the land. The scroll carries the covenant Lord’s judgment against persistent sin, especially theft and false swearing in His name. Its flight shows that no home, person, or hidden act lies beyond God’s righteous notice.

The scroll measures about thirty feet by fifteen feet, making its message public and unmistakable. Its dimensions correspond to the entrance porch of Solomon’s temple, recalling the holiness of God’s dwelling and the authority of His covenant law. The coming judgment proceeds from the God who dwells among His people and who will cleanse the restored land from open rebellion.

The flying scroll carries God’s judicial sentence throughout the restored community. Its movement shows that covenant sin cannot remain hidden or confined to private life. The curse recalls the sanctions of Deuteronomy 28, where persistent rebellion against God brings His judgment upon the land and its people. God’s work of restoration after the exile included the cleansing of evil from among His people.

The Vision of the Woman in a Basket

Zechariah 5:5

Then the angel who was speaking with me came forward and told me,
The interpreting angel again guides Zechariah through the night visions, showing that the message comes from God and requires divine explanation. Heavenly messengers minister throughout Scripture as servants of God’s will, bringing revelation to His people. The angel directs Zechariah from the vision of the flying scroll, which proclaimed judgment against covenant-breaking sin, to the vision of the ephah, which portrays wickedness gathered and removed from the land.

“Now lift up your eyes and see
Zechariah is called to give careful attention to what God is revealing. God had similarly commanded Abram to lift his eyes toward the promised land, inviting him to see God’s purpose beyond present circumstances. Spiritual understanding requires receptive attention to God’s word and His work.

what is approaching.”
What comes into view is the ephah, a large measuring basket associated with commerce and daily life. Its appearance introduces God’s exposure of wickedness within the land and His determination to remove it. The approaching vision assures the returned exiles that God sees corruption, judges sin, and will restore holiness among His people.

“What is it?” I asked.
Zechariah sought the angel’s interpretation rather than trusting his own impressions. His question reflects the posture required of God’s servants when confronted with divine revelation: understanding comes through the Lord’s explanation. Like Daniel and John, Zechariah was shown realities that could not be rightly understood apart from God’s appointed messenger.

The basket was an ephah, a standard measure used in Israel’s ordinary commerce. God employed an object familiar from daily life to expose sin embedded in the nation’s public and economic conduct. The basket’s movement indicated that judgment was already proceeding according to God’s purpose.

The ephah represented the pervasive guilt of the people throughout the land of Judah. Sin had not remained private or isolated; it had corrupted the life of the covenant community. The vision continues by identifying wickedness as confined within the basket and removed from the land, revealing God’s determination to cleanse His people and judge rebellion. Honest measures, just dealings, and covenant faithfulness mattered to the Lord, who sees and will ultimately deal with every form of evil.

Nothing hidden from the Lord can remain concealed when He brings sin into the light. The vision follows the flying scroll of judgment and shows that God’s cleansing work reaches beyond individual acts of disobedience to the corrupt power behind them.

God does not overlook such evil, but restrains it and removes it from the land of His covenant people. The vision anticipates the purification necessary for restored worship and fellowship with the Lord.
