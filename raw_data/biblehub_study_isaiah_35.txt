The Glory of Zion

Isaiah 35:1

The wilderness and the dry land will be glad;
Areas marked by curse, hardship, and emptiness will share in God’s restoration. Israel’s wilderness wanderings recalled testing and judgment, yet the Lord also sustained His people there and led them toward the promised land. Isaiah foresees a future reversal in which creation itself reflects the joy of redemption, anticipating the liberation of the created order from corruption (Romans 8:19–21).

Psa 65:12-13 The pastures of the wilderness overflow;…

Isa 43:19 Behold, I am about to do something new; even now it is coming.…

Psa 107:35 He turns a desert into pools of water…

the desert will rejoice
The barren places surrounding Israel, including regions associated with exile and desolation, will no longer testify to judgment alone. God’s saving work brings life where human strength cannot produce it. The renewed land corresponds with the renewed people who will return to Zion in holiness and joy later in the chapter (Isaiah 35:10).

Isa 51:3 For the LORD will comfort Zion and will look with compassion…

Isa 44:23 Sing for joy, O heavens, for the LORD has done this;…

Psa 96:11-13 Let the heavens be glad and the earth rejoice;…

and blossom like a rose.
The sudden flowering of the wilderness portrays the abundance, beauty, and fruitfulness of the Messiah’s kingdom. What was lifeless will display God’s glory openly, as the earth is filled with the knowledge of the Lord (Isaiah 11:9). Christ gives abundant life to those ruined by sin, and His final reign will include the restoration of all that the curse has damaged.

Isaiah 35:2

It will bloom profusely
The wilderness described in Isaiah 35:1 will undergo a real and dramatic transformation under God’s restoring power. Barren places will become fruitful, displaying the life and abundance that flow from the Lord’s blessing. Israel’s return from judgment and exile forms an immediate backdrop, while the complete renewal reaches forward to the Messianic kingdom.

Hos 14:5 I will be like the dew to Israel; he will blossom…

Isa 27:6 In the days to come, Jacob will take root.…

and rejoice with joy and singing.
Creation is pictured as joining God’s redeemed people in celebrating His salvation. Joy and song accompany deliverance throughout Scripture, and Isaiah’s vision anticipates the gladness of those who return to Zion with everlasting joy (Isaiah 35:10).

Isa 12:6 Cry out and sing, O citizen of Zion, for great among you…

Psa 98:4 Make a joyful noise to the LORD, all the earth;…

The glory of Lebanon will be given to it,
Lebanon’s famed cedar forests represented grandeur, strength, and beauty. God will grant such richness to land formerly marked by drought and desolation, revealing that no condition of ruin is beyond His power to restore.

Isa 60:13 The glory of Lebanon will come to you—its cypress…

Psa 92:12-13 The righteous will flourish like a palm tree…

the splendor of Carmel and Sharon.
Carmel’s fruitful heights and the fertile plain of Sharon were well-known images of agricultural abundance in the land of Israel. Their beauty will characterize the restored wilderness, showing the fullness of God’s provision for His people and His land.

Isa 33:9 The land mourns and languishes; Lebanon is ashamed…

Sng 7:5 Your head crowns you like Mount Carmel, the hair of your head…

They will see the glory of the LORD,
The redeemed will behold God’s manifest majesty in the restoration He accomplishes. The glory once revealed in the tabernacle and temple is ultimately displayed in Jesus Christ, and it will be openly known when the Lord completes His saving purposes.

Psa 97:6 The heavens proclaim His righteousness; all the peoples…

Isa 40:5 And the glory of the LORD will be revealed…

the splendor of our God.
God’s holiness, power, and beauty stand behind the promised renewal. The flourishing land will testify that the God of Israel reigns, keeps His covenant, and will bring His people into the joy of His presence.

Psa 96:6 Splendor and majesty are before Him; strength and beauty…

Hab 3:3-4 God came from Teman, and the Holy One from Mount Paran.…

Isaiah 35:3

Strengthen the limp hands
God calls His people to restore courage and usefulness where fear and discouragement have weakened them. Job had once strengthened failing hands (Job 4:3–4), and believers are likewise called to bear one another’s burdens (Galatians 6:2). In Isaiah 35, this encouragement belongs to the promised restoration of God’s people and anticipates the saving work of the coming King.

Job 4:3 Surely you have instructed many, and have strengthened…

2Ch 15:7 But as for you, be strong; do not be discouraged,…

Zec 8:9 This is what the LORD of Hosts says: “Let your hands be…

and steady the feeble knees!
God supplies stability to those shaken by danger, sorrow, or uncertainty. The following command addresses fearful hearts with the assurance that God will come to save His people (Isaiah 35:4). Hebrews applies this call to perseverance among Christians, urging them to rise from weariness and continue in holiness (Hebrews 12:12–13). Christ fulfills this hope by strengthening those unable to stand on their own and by leading His people safely in the way of redemption.

Job 4:4 Your words have steadied those who stumbled;…

Heb 12:12 Therefore strengthen your limp hands and weak knees.

Ezk 21:7 And when they ask, ‘Why are you groaning?’…

Isaiah 35:4

Say to those with anxious hearts:
Isaiah calls God’s people to strengthen those overwhelmed by coming judgment, national danger, and the prospect of exile. Beyond Judah’s immediate crises, the command looks toward the restoration of the redeemed and the security of Zion.

Psa 94:19 When anxiety overwhelms me, Your consolation…

Php 4:6-7 Be anxious for nothing, but in everything, by prayer…

1Pe 5:7 Cast all your anxiety on Him, because He cares for you.

“Be strong, do not fear!
Courage rests in the presence and faithfulness of God, not in Judah’s military power or political alliances. The Lord gave similar encouragement to Moses, Joshua, and Israel when His people faced dangers beyond their strength.

Deu 31:6 Be strong and courageous; do not be afraid or terrified…

Jos 1:9 Have I not commanded you to be strong and courageous?…

Isa 41:10 Do not fear, for I am with you; do not be afraid…

Behold, your God will come with vengeance.
The God who judges the nations will not overlook violence, idolatry, or oppression. Isaiah 34 announces judgment upon the enemies of God and His people, while Isaiah 35 sets that judgment beside the coming renewal of the land and the return of the redeemed.

Isa 61:2 to proclaim the year of the LORD’s favor…

Nam 1:2 The LORD is a jealous and avenging God; the LORD is avenging…

Psa 94:1 O LORD, God of vengeance, O God of vengeance, shine forth.

With divine retribution He will come to save you.”
God’s judgment against evil and His salvation of His people belong together. The Lord would preserve a remnant through exile and bring them home, and the promise ultimately anticipates Christ’s saving work and His future return, when He will fully defeat evil and establish His kingdom.

Isaiah 35:5

Then the eyes of the blind will be opened
Messiah’s coming would bring real healing and restoration to God’s afflicted people. Jesus fulfilled this promise in His earthly ministry by giving sight to the blind, including the man born blind in John 9:1–7. His works identified Him as the promised Christ and displayed the power of God’s kingdom. Physical sight also pointed to spiritual illumination, for Christ delivers sinners from darkness and reveals the truth of God’s salvation.

Jhn 9:1-7 Now as Jesus was passing by, He saw a man blind from birth,…

Isa 42:7 to open the eyes of the blind, to bring prisoners out…

Mrk 8:22-26 When they arrived at Bethsaida, some people brought…

and the ears of the deaf unstopped.
Jesus also healed the deaf, notably in the region of Decapolis (Mark 7:31–37), showing His compassion and divine authority. Those once unable to hear would be restored to full participation in worship and community. The healing anticipates the greater work of grace by which God opens hearts to hear and receive His word. Isaiah’s promise looks beyond individual miracles to the complete renewal that accompanies the redemption of Zion.

Mat 11:5 The blind receive sight, the lame walk…

Isaiah 35:6

Then the lame will leap like a deer
The coming restoration will include real bodily healing, replacing weakness with strength, agility, and joy. Jesus identified the healing of the lame as evidence that the promised kingdom had drawn near through His ministry (Matthew 11:5). The man healed at the temple gate entered the courts “walking and leaping and praising God” (Acts 3:8), offering a visible foretaste of Isaiah’s promise.

Act 3:8 He sprang to his feet and began to walk. Then he went…

2Sa 22:34 He makes my feet like those of a deer…

and the mute tongue will shout for joy.
Those once unable to speak will praise God openly. Jesus healed the mute and demonstrated His authority over every human affliction (Matthew 9:32–33; Mark 7:37). The silence caused by suffering will give way to public worship when the Lord completes His work of redemption.

Luk 1:64 Immediately Zechariah’s mouth was opened…

Mrk 7:35 Immediately the man’s ears were opened…

For waters will gush forth in the wilderness,
The wilderness, long associated with Israel’s testing and dependence on God, will become a place of abundant provision. As God brought water from the rock for Israel in the desert (Exodus 17:6), He will bring life where barrenness once ruled. The outward renewal of the land will accompany the restoration of His people.

Isa 43:20 The beasts of the field will honor Me, the jackals…

Isa 41:18 I will open rivers on the barren heights,…

and streams in the desert.
Flowing streams in an arid land portray the Lord’s power to reverse the effects of curse, desolation, and exile. The image points to the fullness of Messiah’s kingdom, when God’s blessing will reach both His people and the created order. Jesus gives living water to those who believe in Him (John 7:38), and His saving work assures the final renewal promised by the prophets.

Isaiah 35:7

The parched ground will become a pool,
God promises a real reversal of the barrenness that marked the wilderness and the land under judgment. As He brought water from the rock for Israel in the wilderness (Exodus 17:6), He will supply what His redeemed people cannot produce for themselves. The abundance of water also points to the life God gives through Christ, whose living water satisfies forever (John 4:14).

Psa 107:35 He turns a desert into pools of water…

Isa 41:18 I will open rivers on the barren heights,…

Psa 114:8 who turned the rock into a pool, the flint into a fountain…

the thirsty land springs of water.
The land that once lacked life will overflow with provision. Scripture often portrays thirst as humanity’s need for God, and the Lord alone can satisfy that need (Psalm 42:1–2). Jesus identified the life-giving streams flowing from believers with the Holy Spirit (John 7:38–39), joining God’s future restoration with His present work in His people.

Isa 44:3 For I will pour water on the thirsty land,…

Isa 32:2 Each will be like a shelter from the wind…

Psa 84:6 As they pass through the Valley of Baca,…

In the haunt where jackals once lay,
Places inhabited by jackals represented ruin, abandonment, and the aftermath of divine judgment (Jeremiah 9:11). God will transform even such devastated places, demonstrating that no desolation lies beyond His restoring power. The promised renewal belongs to the larger restoration of Zion and of creation under God’s righteous rule.

Isa 34:13-15 Her towers will be overgrown with thorns…

Jer 9:11 And I will make Jerusalem a heap of rubble…

there will be grass and reeds and papyrus.
Vegetation associated with marshes and well-watered ground will replace the wilderness. The change depicts enduring fertility, security, and life where death had prevailed. Isaiah’s vision anticipates the new creation, when God removes the curse’s effects and makes all things new (Revelation 21:1–5).

Isaiah 35:8

And there will be a highway called the Way of Holiness.
God promises a secure, elevated road leading His redeemed people into restored fellowship with Him. Roads prepared for kings in the ancient world announced royal authority and provided direct passage through difficult terrain. The Way of Holiness belongs to the Lord and is reserved for those He has set apart. Isaiah’s vision reaches beyond Judah’s return from exile to the final restoration God accomplishes through the Messiah. Jesus fulfills the promise as the only way to the Father, and all who belong to Him are called to holiness in life and conduct.

Isa 11:16 There will be a highway for the remnant of His people…

Isa 62:10 Build it up, build up the highway; clear away the stones;…

Heb 12:14 Pursue peace with everyone, as well as holiness,…

The unclean will not travel it—
Nothing defiled by sin can enter God’s holy presence. Israel’s laws concerning ritual uncleanness taught that sin separates humanity from the God who is perfectly pure. Christ’s sacrificial death provides the cleansing those laws anticipated. His blood cleanses believers from sin and grants them access to God, not through human merit but through faith in Him.

only those who walk in the Way—
The highway is for God’s redeemed people, whose faith is shown in a continuing pattern of obedience. Walking depicts the whole course of life under God’s direction. The earliest followers of Jesus were known as those belonging to “the Way,” because they followed the risen Christ in faith, worship, and obedience.

and fools will not stray onto it.
Biblical fools reject the fear of the Lord and resist His wisdom. The holy road is not found accidentally by those who remain opposed to God. The Lord preserves and directs His people, giving them discernment to remain on the path that leads to life.

Isaiah 35:9

No lion will be there,
The highway of holiness will be free from every threat that endangers God’s people. Lions and other predators represented real dangers in the ancient world, but they also pictured destructive powers that prey upon the weak. God’s restored order removes both physical danger and all that opposes His peace. Isaiah 11:6–9 similarly portrays creation at peace under the righteous reign of the Messiah.

Job 28:7-8 No bird of prey knows that path; no falcon’s eye…

Ezk 34:25 I will make with them a covenant of peace…

Dan 6:22 My God sent His angel and shut the mouths of the lions.…

and no vicious beast will go up on it.
Nothing hostile, violent, or unclean will enter the way God prepares for His redeemed people. The promise reaches beyond Israel’s return from exile to the final security of God’s kingdom, where evil cannot threaten those who belong to Him. Revelation 21:4 anticipates that consummation, when death, mourning, pain, and every curse are gone.

Ezk 34:28 They will no longer be prey for the nations…

Lev 26:6 And I will give peace to the land, and you will lie down…

Job 5:22-23 You will laugh at destruction and famine,…

Such will not be found there,
God’s protection is complete and enduring. The holiness of the way excludes all that corrupts, destroys, or separates people from the presence of the Lord. The safety of the redeemed rests not in their strength but in God’s sovereign faithfulness to fulfill His covenant promises.

Rev 21:27 but only those whose names are written in the Lamb’s Book…

but the redeemed will walk upon it.
Those delivered by the Lord will travel safely to Zion with joy. The immediate horizon includes Israel’s restoration after exile, when God gathers His scattered people and brings them home. Yet the redemption foretold also points to Christ, whose sacrifice purchases a people for God from every nation. The redeemed walk in fellowship with Him now and will enter His eternal kingdom without fear.

Isaiah 35:10

So the redeemed of the LORD will return
Isaiah looks beyond Israel’s judgment and exile to God’s promised restoration. Those whom the LORD redeems belong to Him because He has delivered them from bondage. The return from Babylon foreshadowed a greater restoration in which God gathers His people and fulfills His covenant promises. Christ’s sacrifice provides the final redemption from sin, yet Israel’s promised return to Zion remains part of God’s unfolding plan.

Isa 51:11 So the redeemed of the LORD will return…

Psa 107:2 Let the redeemed of the LORD say so, whom He has redeemed…

Deu 30:3 He will restore you from captivity and have compassion…

and enter Zion with singing,
Zion, the city of Jerusalem and the place chosen for God’s dwelling, becomes the destination of the redeemed. Their arrival is not marked by fear or mourning but by worshipful celebration. Israel’s songs of deliverance recall God’s faithfulness in the exodus and anticipate the day when the Lord reigns openly from Zion.

Jer 31:12 They will come and shout for joy on the heights of Zion…

Psa 126:1-2 When the LORD restored the captives of Zion,…

crowned with everlasting joy.
Joy will rest upon God’s people as a visible mark of victory and favor. Unlike the fleeting relief that followed earlier deliverances, this joy endures because its source is the presence and reign of God. The promise reaches its fullest horizon in the eternal kingdom, where redemption can never be reversed.

1Th 2:19 After all, who is our hope, our joy, our crown of boasting,…

Gladness and joy will overtake them,
God’s restoration will be so complete that joy replaces the grief accumulated through sin, judgment, exile, and suffering. The redeemed will not merely seek happiness; gladness will come upon them as the sure result of God’s saving work.

and sorrow and sighing will flee.
Every cause of grief will be removed when the Lord completes His redemption. The tears, burdens, and groaning of a fallen world will give way to peace in God’s presence. Revelation 21:4 echoes this promise in the final renewal, when death, mourning, crying, and pain are no more.
