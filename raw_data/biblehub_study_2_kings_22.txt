Josiah Reigns in Judah

2 Kings 22:1

Josiah was eight years old when he became king,
Josiah ascended Judah’s throne after his father Amon was assassinated by his own servants. The people of the land executed the conspirators and established the child king, preserving the Davidic dynasty during a dangerous period of political and spiritual decline. His early accession left him dependent upon court officials and later upon faithful servants of God such as Hilkiah the high priest. God would use Josiah’s youth and receptiveness to His word to bring needed reform to Judah.

2Ki 18:2 He was twenty-five years old when he became king…

and he reigned in Jerusalem thirty-one years.
Josiah ruled from Jerusalem for thirty-one years, approximately 640–609 BC. His reign brought a decisive effort to remove idolatry, restore the temple, and renew covenant obedience after the Book of the Law was found. He also fulfilled the prophecy spoken against Jeroboam’s altar at Bethel, where a king named Josiah would destroy its corrupt worship centers (1 Kings 13:2). Though Judah later returned to rebellion, Josiah’s reign provided a powerful witness to the authority of God’s law and the need for wholehearted repentance.

‘Therefore I will indeed gather you to your fathers,
God promised Josiah that his life would end before Judah experienced the judgment announced against Jerusalem. Being gathered to one’s fathers described death and burial among one’s people, preserving the importance of family, covenant history, and an honorable resting place. Josiah’s humble response to God’s Law distinguished him from the hardened generation around him.

The promised peace meant that Josiah would be spared the national terror of Jerusalem’s fall, the destruction of the temple, and the Babylonian exile. Although Josiah later died from wounds received at Megiddo, he was brought back to Jerusalem, buried in his own tomb, and mourned deeply by Judah and Jerusalem (2 Kings 23:29–30; 2 Chronicles 35:24–25). God granted him rest before the judgment fell.
