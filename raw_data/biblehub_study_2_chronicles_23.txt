2 Chronicles 23:17

So all the people went to the temple of Baal and tore it down.
Following Athaliah’s overthrow and the public coronation of Joash, Judah’s covenant renewal required the removal of the idolatry Athaliah had promoted. Baal worship had gained a foothold in Jerusalem through the influence of Ahab’s house, directly opposing the worship of the Lord at His temple. The united action of the people showed repentance and a renewed allegiance to Yahweh alone, in keeping with the first commandment (Exodus 20:3–5). Like Elijah’s earlier confrontation with Baal worship in the northern kingdom (1 Kings 18), this destruction exposed the emptiness of false worship and affirmed that the Lord would not share His glory with idols.

Baal’s altars and images were not left standing as harmless relics of a former regime. Their complete destruction followed God’s command that Israel tear down the places and objects connected to pagan worship (Deuteronomy 12:3). The action recalled Moses’ destruction of the golden calf after Israel’s rebellion at Sinai (Exodus 32:20). Judah’s restoration could not rest merely on removing Athaliah from the throne; it required the cleansing of the religious corruption that had accompanied her rule.
