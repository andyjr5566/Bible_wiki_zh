Deliver Me, O LORD!

Psalm 3:1

A Psalm of David, when he fled from his son Absalom.
Absalom’s rebellion drove David from Jerusalem and exposed the painful consequences of sin within David’s own household, foretold after his sin against Uriah (2 Samuel 12:10–12). As Israel’s anointed king, David lost public support and fled across the Kidron Valley while many leaders and citizens joined his son (2 Samuel 15:13–30). In personal betrayal and national danger, David turned to the Lord rather than trusting military strength or royal position.

2Sa 16:5-8 As King David approached Bahurim, a man from the house…

O LORD, how my foes have increased!
David brought his distress directly to the covenant God who had chosen and sustained him. Absalom’s supporters included former friends, influential counselors such as Ahithophel, and many Israelites persuaded by Absalom’s ambition. The growing opposition made clear that human authority and past victories could not secure David; his help had to come from the Lord.

How many rise up against me!
The rebellion was an organized assault against God’s appointed king, not merely a private family conflict. David’s enemies sought to remove him from the throne and establish Absalom in his place. The crisis foreshadows the hostility often directed against those who belong to God, yet it also prepares the way for confidence that the Lord sees, protects, and delivers His servants.

Psalm 3:2

Absalom’s rebellion had turned public opinion against David, leaving the king surrounded by enemies and abandoned by many former supporters. Shimei’s curse reflected the belief that David’s suffering proved God had rejected him (2 Samuel 16:8).

David’s adversaries interpreted divine discipline for his sin as permanent abandonment, forgetting God’s covenant promise to preserve David’s house (2 Samuel 7:16). Their scorn anticipates the words directed at Jesus: “He trusts in God. Let God rescue him now” (Matthew 27:43). God’s people may endure severe affliction without being forsaken by God.

Psalm 3:5

I lie down and sleep;
While fleeing Jerusalem during Absalom’s rebellion, David could rest despite the immediate threat to his life (2 Samuel 15:13–17). Sleep placed him beyond his own ability to guard, plan, or defend himself. His rest testified that the Lord remained King when David’s throne, family, and security had been shaken. Psalm 4:8 echoes the same confidence: God alone enables His people to dwell in safety.

I wake again,
Each morning brought evidence that God had preserved David through the night. In a time when enemies sought his life, waking was not presumed but received as mercy. The Lord gives life and breath to His creatures and governs every day entrusted to them (Job 33:4; Acts 17:25).

for the LORD sustains me.
David’s survival did not rest on military strength, loyal supporters, or an escape route, but on the Lord’s continual care. God upheld him physically, strengthened him inwardly, and preserved him for His covenant purposes. The Lord who sustained David also sustains all things through His Son, Jesus Christ (Hebrews 1:3), giving His people reason for confidence amid danger and distress.

Psalm 3:6

David’s confidence rests in the Lord who hears, sustains, and delivers His people. Surrounded by danger during Absalom’s rebellion, he refused to measure his safety by visible circumstances. Such faith recognizes that God remains present even in the valley of the shadow of death and that no earthly threat can overturn His purposes.

Psalm 3:7

Arise, O LORD!
David calls upon the covenant God to act decisively against the danger surrounding him. Fleeing from Absalom’s rebellion, David refuses to place final confidence in military strength, political alliances, or personal survival. The appeal recalls Moses’ cry when the ark set out: “Rise up, O LORD, and let your enemies be scattered” (Numbers 10:35). God’s rising signifies His intervention to defend His people and overthrow those who oppose His righteous purposes.

Save me, O my God!
David’s plea rests on personal trust: the LORD is not distant or unknown, but “my God.” Though his own son had become his enemy and many believed his cause was lost, David entrusted his life to the God who had called, preserved, and established him. Salvation includes deliverance from immediate danger, yet it also reveals God as the lasting refuge of all who belong to Him.

Psalm 3:8

Salvation belongs to the LORD;
David confesses that deliverance from Absalom’s rebellion cannot come through military strength, loyal supporters, or personal wisdom. The God who established David’s kingdom alone can preserve him and restore what has been threatened. Jonah made the same confession from the depths of the sea, and heaven’s redeemed multitude attributes salvation to God alone. God’s saving work reaches its fullness in Jesus Christ, through whom sinners are rescued from judgment and brought into peace with God.

may Your blessing be on Your people.
Though David suffers personally, his prayer embraces the nation entrusted to his care. Absalom’s revolt endangered all Israel, yet David seeks God’s favor upon the people rather than vengeance for himself. The Lord’s blessing includes His protection, covenant faithfulness, forgiveness, peace, and sustaining presence. Such blessing ultimately rests on Christ, who bears the curse of sin so that His people may receive every spiritual blessing from God.
