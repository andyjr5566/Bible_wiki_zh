Solomon’s Prayer for Wisdom

1 Kings 3:1

Later, Solomon formed an alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt by marrying his daughter.
Marriage alliances commonly secured peace between ancient kingdoms, and Egypt’s friendship strengthened Solomon’s international position. Yet Israel’s kings were not to multiply wives or allow foreign loyalties to draw their hearts from the LORD (Deuteronomy 17:17). Pharaoh’s daughter is not identified, and the account does not state her religious convictions, but the marriage anticipates the danger later fulfilled when Solomon’s many foreign wives turned his heart toward other gods (1 Kings 11:1–4). Political wisdom could not replace covenant faithfulness.

The City of David was Jerusalem’s fortified core, captured by David and established as Israel’s royal capital. Solomon temporarily housed Pharaoh’s daughter there while he completed the temple, his royal palace, and Jerusalem’s defenses. These works displayed the peace, wealth, and stability granted during Solomon’s reign and advanced David’s desire to establish a permanent house for the LORD. The temple especially pointed beyond Solomon’s reign to Jesus Christ, who identified His own body as the true temple and who dwells among His people (John 2:19–21). Jerusalem’s walls also pictured the security God provides for His dwelling place, a reality ultimately fulfilled in the holy city of the new creation (Revelation 21:10–27).

1 Kings 3:2

The people, however, were still sacrificing on the high places
Israel’s worship remained dispersed at local elevated shrines, many of which had long been associated with Canaanite religion. Although some sacrifices may have been offered to the LORD, worship on the high places left the nation vulnerable to syncretism and idolatry. The law had directed Israel toward the place God would choose for His Name, where sacrifice and worship would be ordered according to His covenant. Israel’s continued use of high places revealed incomplete obedience, a pattern that later became closely joined to the nation’s spiritual decline.

Its future glory also anticipated Jesus Christ, in whom God came to dwell among humanity and through whom believers now draw near to God.
