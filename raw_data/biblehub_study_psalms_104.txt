Psalm 104:1

Bless the LORD, O my soul!
The psalmist summons his whole inner life to worship the Lord. Praise rises from more than public speech or outward ceremony; it involves the heart, mind, affections, and will. Psalm 103 begins with the same personal call, and Deuteronomy 6:5 commands wholehearted love for God. The soul must remember who God is and respond to Him with grateful adoration.

O LORD my God,
Yahweh is the covenant God who revealed Himself to Israel and faithfully kept His promises. Calling Him “my God” joins reverence with personal trust. The Creator of all things is not distant from His people but receives the worship of those who belong to Him, as David testified when he called the Lord his Shepherd.

God’s greatness exceeds every human ruler, power, and created thing. Psalm 104 unfolds that greatness through His orderly creation, His providential care, and His authority over land, sea, sky, and living creatures. The God who made all things is worthy of the praise later offered before His throne: “You are worthy, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power” (Revelation 4:11).

His royal majesty filled Isaiah’s vision of the temple, and Christ revealed His divine glory on the mount of transfiguration. The Lord reigns over creation in unsurpassed holiness, beauty, and power.

God’s radiant glory reveals His absolute holiness, purity, and majesty. He dwells in unapproachable light (1 Timothy 6:16), yet He graciously makes Himself known through His works and His Word. The light He commanded into being at creation (Genesis 1:3) testifies that He is its source and Lord. Christ manifested that divine glory at the transfiguration, when His face shone like the sun (Matthew 17:2), and He came as the Light of the world to bring salvation to those in darkness (John 8:12).

Psalm 104:15

God’s providence includes the lawful enjoyment of wine, a common fruit of the land and a fitting accompaniment to feasts, sacrifices, and seasons of celebration. Wine was used at Passover and other covenant meals, where it testified to God’s abundance and the joy of fellowship under His blessing. Jesus’ provision of wine at Cana revealed His glory and pointed to the fullness of joy brought through the new covenant. Scripture never commends drunkenness, which dishonors God and destroys wisdom, but it recognizes wine received with gratitude and self-control as one of the Creator’s gifts.

God ordered human life around the rising and setting of the sun. From Adam’s placement in Eden to cultivate and keep the garden, productive work belonged to humanity’s calling before sin entered the world. Each morning summoned Israel’s farmers, shepherds, craftsmen, and household servants to fulfill responsibilities under God’s providence.

Exo 20:9 Six days you shall labor and do all your work,

Ecc 9:10 Whatever you find to do with your hands, do it…

Pro 14:23 There is profit in all labor, but mere talk…

and to his labor
Sin made work painful and wearying, yet labor remains a means by which God supplies daily needs and enables people to serve one another. Scripture commends diligence and receives the fruit of honest labor as God’s gift. Human activity stands in contrast to the animals returning to their dens, displaying the wise order God has established for every creature.

Job 7:1 Is not man consigned to labor on earth?…

Gen 3:19 By the sweat of your brow you will eat your bread,…

Exo 1:14 and made their lives bitter with hard labor…

until evening.
The end of the workday brings rest within the rhythm God ordained in creation. Evening reminds mankind that labor has limits and that provision ultimately comes from the Lord. The daily pattern of work and rest also anticipates the fuller rest promised to God’s people through Christ.

Rut 2:17 So Ruth gathered grain in the field until evening.…

Mat 20:8 When evening came, the owner of the vineyard…

Deu 24:15 You are to pay his wages each day before sunset…

Psalm 104:24

How many are Your works, O LORD!
The psalmist looks upon the ordered world with worshipful astonishment. From the heavens above to the seas, mountains, animals, and human labor described throughout Psalm 104, creation bears witness to the immeasurable range of Yahweh’s works. The covenant God who redeemed Israel is also the Maker and Sustainer of all things, joining His majesty in creation to His personal faithfulness toward His people.

Psa 40:5 Many, O LORD my God, are the wonders You have done,…

Psa 92:5 How great are Your works, O LORD, how deep are Your thoughts…

Job 9:10 He does great things beyond searching out, and wonders…

In wisdom You have made them all;
Nothing in creation exists by accident or apart from God’s purposeful design. Proverbs 3:19 likewise declares that the LORD founded the earth by wisdom, and Genesis 1 records His deliberate ordering of light, land, seas, plants, animals, and mankind. God’s wisdom is also revealed perfectly in Christ, through whom all things were made and in whom all things hold together (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16–17).

Pro 3:19 The LORD founded the earth by wisdom and established the…

Jer 10:12 The LORD made the earth by His power;…

Psa 136:5 By His insight He made the heavens. His loving devotion…

the earth is full of Your creatures.
The abundance of living things displays God’s generous provision and continual care. Each creature depends upon Him for food, breath, and its appointed place in the world (Psalm 104:27–30). Humanity, uniquely made in God’s image, is therefore accountable to exercise faithful dominion over the earth as stewards of what belongs to its Creator.

Psalm 104:25

Here is the sea, vast and wide,
The sea displayed a realm beyond human mastery, feared for its storms, depths, and unseen creatures. Yet it was not outside God’s rule. The Lord gathered the waters in creation and set their boundaries by His command (Genesis 1:9–10; Job 38:8–11). Unlike the pagan nations that attributed the sea to rival deities, Israel confessed that the sea belonged wholly to the Creator. Its immensity magnifies the wisdom and power of God, whose dominion reaches where man cannot.

Psa 77:19 Your path led through the sea, Your way…

Psa 107:23 Others went out to sea in ships, conducting trade…

Job 38:16 Have you journeyed to the vents of the sea…

teeming with creatures beyond number,
God filled the waters with abundant life according to His creative word (Genesis 1:20–22). Much of that life remained unknown to those who lived along the Mediterranean coast, but none of it was hidden from the Lord. The countless creatures of the sea testify that God’s provision extends beyond human sight and need. He sustains every living thing and gives food in its season (Psalm 104:27–28).

Gen 1:20 And God said, “Let the waters teem with living creatures,…

Ezk 47:9 Wherever the river flows, there will be swarms…

Luk 5:6 When they had done so, they caught such a large number…

living things both great and small.
The Creator attends alike to creatures that inspire awe and those scarcely noticed by man. Great sea creatures, including Leviathan, reveal God’s unmatched strength and wisdom (Job 41:1–34; Psalm 104:26). Small creatures reveal the same purposeful care in their design and dependence. The sea therefore joins all creation in declaring that God’s works are manifold, ordered, and good.

Gen 1:21 So God created the great sea creatures…

Psa 148:7 Praise the LORD from the earth, all great sea creatures…

Psalm 104:26

There the ships pass,
Human commerce and travel move across the sea God made. The vessels of merchants and sailors testify that even the waters, vast and dangerous to man, serve within God’s ordered creation. Mediterranean trade linked Israel with Phoenicia, Egypt, Greece, and distant lands, yet every route remained subject to the Lord who set boundaries for the sea.

Psa 107:23-24 Others went out to sea in ships, conducting trade…

Pro 30:19 the way of an eagle in the sky, the way of a snake on a rock…

and Leviathan,
The same waters that carry ships also contain creatures beyond human strength and control. Leviathan displays the greatness of God’s handiwork, as Job 41 vividly confirms. No creature, however fearsome, exists outside the Creator’s rule.

Job 41:1-4 “Can you pull in Leviathan with a hook”…

Psa 74:14 You crushed the heads of Leviathan; You fed him…

which You formed to frolic there.
Leviathan was not an independent force of chaos but a creature deliberately formed by God. Its freedom and power are exercised within the world God governs. The Lord’s creation includes both what humanity can use and what humanity can only behold with humility, leading all who recognize His works to praise Him.

Gen 1:21 So God created the great sea creatures…

Job 41:5-6 Can you pet him like a bird or put him on a leash…

Psalm 104:27

All creatures look to You
Every living thing depends on God’s continual care. He created animals, birds, fish, and mankind, and He sustains the life He has made. Genesis 1:29–30 presents food as God’s gift to humanity and the animals, while Psalm 145:15–16 declares that the eyes of all look to the Lord for provision. Creation’s dependence displays God’s sovereign rule and His faithful care over the world.

Psa 145:15 The eyes of all look to You, and You give them…

Psa 123:2 As the eyes of servants look to the hand of their master…

Job 38:41 Who provides food for the raven when its young…

to give them their food
Food does not ultimately arise from human labor or natural processes alone, but from the hand of God. Israel’s harvests depended upon rain, fertile soil, and seasons governed by the Lord, as Deuteronomy 11:13–15 teaches. God also fed Israel with manna during the wilderness journey, proving that His people live by His provision rather than by their own resources. The same God supplies the needs of all creatures and calls His people to receive His gifts with gratitude and trust.

Psa 147:9 He provides food for the animals, and for the young…

Psa 136:25 He gives food to every creature. His loving devotion…

Mat 6:11 Give us this day our daily bread.

in due season.
God’s provision follows the order He established in creation. Seedtime and harvest, rain and drought, migration and reproduction all remain under His command. Ecclesiastes 3:1 recognizes that God appoints times and seasons, and His wisdom is seen in giving what is needed at the proper time. God’s appointed timing also reaches its fullest purpose in Christ, who came “in the fullness of time” to accomplish redemption (Galatians 4:4).

Gal 6:9 Let us not grow weary in well-doing, for in due time……

Psalm 104:28

When You give it to them, they gather it up;
Every creature receives its food by God’s appointment and responds to His provision. The picture recalls Israel gathering manna each morning in the wilderness, where God supplied enough for every household and taught His people daily dependence upon Him (Exodus 16:4–18). God—Jehovah-Jireh, “The Lord will provide”—provides, yet His gifts ordinarily call forth responsible action. The gathered provision also reflects His concern for the whole created order, not merely for humanity.

Exo 16:4 Then the LORD said to Moses, “Behold, I will rain down bread…

Psa 145:15 The eyes of all look to You, and You give them…

when You open Your hand,
God’s open hand portrays His generous and sustaining rule over all He has made. Psalm 145:16 repeats this image, declaring that He satisfies the desire of every living thing. His bounty is not accidental or impersonal; it comes from the deliberate kindness of the Creator. The Lord who provided a ram for Abraham on Mount Moriah remains Jehovah-Jireh, the God who provides (Genesis 22:14). Jesus likewise revealed the Father’s compassion as He fed the multitudes, healed the needy, and blessed those who came to Him.

Psa 145:16 You open Your hand and satisfy the desire…

Deu 15:8 Instead, you are to open your hand to him…

they are satisfied with good things.
God supplies what His creatures need for life and flourishing. Physical bread points beyond itself to the deeper satisfaction found in fellowship with God. David confessed that the Lord’s shepherding care removed the fear of want (Psalm 23:1), and Christ identified Himself as the bread of life, promising lasting satisfaction to all who believe in Him (John 6:35). God’s gifts are to be received with gratitude and shared generously, as the early church demonstrated by meeting one another’s needs (Acts 2:44–45).

Psalm 104:29

When You hide Your face, they are terrified;
All living creatures depend upon God’s sustaining presence and providential care. His face signifies His favor, blessing, and nearness, as seen in the priestly blessing of Numbers 6:24–26. When He withdraws that favor, creation experiences distress and disorder, for no creature can flourish independently of its Maker. Human sin also brings the terror of estrangement from God, a condition answered only through reconciliation with Him.

Psa 30:7 O LORD, You favored me; You made my mountain stand strong.…

Psa 13:1 For the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. How long, O LORD?…

Psa 44:24 Why do You hide Your face and forget our affliction…

when You take away their breath, they die and return to dust.
God gives life and determines its appointed end. Humanity was formed from dust and received life by God’s breath (Hebrew ruach, meaning spirit, breath, or wind), and death returns the body to the dust from which it came (Genesis 2:7; 3:19). Death entered human experience through sin, yet it does not overturn God’s purpose. Jesus Christ defeated death through His resurrection and will raise those who belong to Him to everlasting life (1 Corinthians 15:21–22).

Psalm 104:30

When You send Your Spirit,
God actively gives life to His creatures by His Spirit, who was present at creation when the Spirit of God hovered over the waters (Genesis 1:2). The Lord is not distant from the world He made; He continually upholds it by His power. The Holy Spirit also gives spiritual life, for “the Spirit gives life” (John 6:63).

Jol 2:28 And afterward, I will pour out My Spirit on all people.…

Ezk 37:14 I will put My Spirit in you and you will live,…

they are created,
New life among every kind of creature comes from God’s sovereign action. Psalm 104 has already declared that creatures return to dust when God takes away their breath (Psalm 104:29), and their existence is renewed when He sends His Spirit. Creation remains dependent upon its Creator in every generation.

God refreshes the earth through the recurring gift of life, growth, and seasons. Such renewal displays His faithfulness despite the curse brought by sin. The Lord’s ongoing care for creation anticipates the final restoration promised in a new heaven and a new earth (Isaiah 65:17; Revelation 21:1), secured through the risen Christ.

Psalm 104:31

May the glory of the LORD endure forever;
The LORD’s glory reveals His majestic holiness, sovereign power, and abiding presence. Creation displays that glory, while the tabernacle and temple were filled with it in visible testimony to His dwelling among His people (Exodus 40:34–35; 1 Kings 8:10–11). Human sin has not diminished God’s glory or altered His purpose. The psalmist looks beyond the present world to the enduring reign of the Creator, fulfilled in the new creation where God’s glory will illuminate the New Jerusalem (Revelation 21:23).

Psa 72:19 And blessed be His glorious name forever; may all the earth…

Psa 86:12 I will praise You, O Lord my God, with all my heart;…

Psa 145:21 My mouth will declare the praise of the LORD;…

may the LORD rejoice in His works.
God declared His completed creation “very good” (Genesis 1:31), showing His delight in all He had made. The heavens, earth, creatures, and humanity testify to His wisdom and power (Romans 1:20). Though creation now groans under the effects of the fall, the LORD will restore what sin has damaged. His final joy in His works will be seen in the renewed heavens and earth, where all creation serves His purposes without corruption or death (Isaiah 65:17–19; Revelation 21:1–4).

Psalm 104:32

He looks on the earth, and it trembles;
The Creator rules over the world He made, and the earth itself responds to His presence. At Sinai, the mountain quaked and smoked when the Lord descended in fire (Exodus 19:18). Such trembling reveals that divine holiness and power are not distant ideas but realities before which creation cannot remain unmoved. God’s sovereign presence calls His people to reverent fear, trusting that the One who governs the earth also faithfully sustains it.

Psa 97:4 His lightning illuminates the world; the earth sees…

Psa 68:8 the earth shook and the heavens poured down rain…

Job 9:6 He shakes the earth from its place, so that…

He touches the mountains, and they smolder.
Mountains appear immovable and enduring, yet they are subject to the slightest act of God’s will. Smoke and fire recall the Lord’s manifestation at Sinai and portray His power to reveal, judge, and purify. The same authority over creation was displayed by Jesus Christ when He commanded the wind and sea to be still (Mark 4:39). Nothing in creation is beyond the rule of its Maker, and no human strength or earthly security can stand independently before Him.

Psa 144:5 Your heavens, O LORD, and come down; touch the mountains…

Nam 1:5 The mountains quake before Him, and the hills melt away;…

Psalm 104:33

I will sing to the LORD all my life;
The psalmist answers the Creator’s sustaining care with lifelong worship. Israel’s songs praised Yahweh for His covenant faithfulness, His works in creation, and His rule over all nations. Worship was offered publicly in the sanctuary and personally in daily life, joining gratitude for God’s provision with trust in His continuing care. Psalm 146:2 echoes the same resolve: praise belongs to the Lord for as long as life endures.

Psa 89:1 A Maskil of Ethan the Ezrahite. I will sing of the LORD…

Psa 63:4 So I will bless You as long as I live; in Your name…

Psa 34:1 Of David, when he pretended to be insane before Abimelech,…

I will sing praise to my God while I have my being.
God is personally known as “my God,” yet He remains the sovereign Lord celebrated throughout Psalm 104. Every breath and every day become an occasion for praise because life itself depends upon His gift. Such worship reaches beyond words or music to include the whole life offered to God, anticipating the call for believers to present themselves as living sacrifices (Romans 12:1). Love for God with heart, soul, and strength finds fitting expression in steadfast praise.

Psa 146:2 I will praise the LORD all my life; I will sing…

Psalm 104:34

May my meditation be pleasing to Him,
The psalmist desires that his inward reflections, like his spoken praise, would be acceptable before the Creator whose works fill heaven and earth. Meditation on God’s mighty acts should lead to worship rather than mere observation. Joshua was commanded to meditate on God’s Law continually, and David prayed that both his words and the meditation of his heart would please the LORD (Joshua 1:8; Psalm 19:14). God sees the heart, weighing motives and thoughts as well as outward conduct (1 Samuel 16:7). Such meditation grows from reverence for God and submits the mind to His revealed truth.

Psa 19:14 May the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart…

Pro 15:8 The sacrifice of the wicked is detestable…

1Ch 29:17 I know, my God, that You test the heart…

for I rejoice in the LORD.
Joy in the LORD is the fitting response to His goodness, power, righteousness, and faithful care for His creation. The psalmist’s delight rests not primarily in the blessings God gives, but in God Himself. This joy remains secure because the LORD does not change. Israel expressed such rejoicing in worship, song, and thanksgiving before the LORD, and believers are likewise commanded to rejoice in Him always (Philippians 4:4). Jesus gives His people abiding joy through fellowship with Him and obedience to His word (John 15:11).

Psalm 104:35

May sinners vanish from the earth
Sin has marred the good creation celebrated throughout Psalm 104. The prayer looks beyond present rebellion to the day when God removes all that defiles His world and establishes the righteousness promised in the new heavens and new earth (2 Peter 3:13).

and the wicked be no more.
God’s judgments in the flood, Sodom, and throughout Israel’s history reveal that evil will not endure forever. The final judgment will bring the complete defeat of Satan and all unrepentant wickedness, while preserving God’s people in His restored creation (Revelation 20:10; 21:27).

Bless the LORD, O my soul.
Contemplation of God’s power in creation, His faithful providence, and His certain justice leads the psalmist back to worship. Personal praise is the fitting response to the Lord who gives life, sustains every creature, and will set all things right.
