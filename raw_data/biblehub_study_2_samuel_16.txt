The conflict exposes the contrast between Absalom’s pursuit of power and the enduring loyalty of those who honored the Lord’s chosen king.

2 Samuel 16:22

So they pitched a tent for Absalom on the roof,
Absalom’s servants prepared a public setting on the palace roof, the place associated with royal visibility and authority. From a rooftop David had looked upon Bathsheba and begun the sin that brought lasting discipline upon his house (2 Samuel 11:2). Absalom now used the palace roof to display open contempt for his father and to proclaim himself king. Ahithophel had urged this act to make reconciliation between David and Absalom impossible and to strengthen the resolve of Absalom’s followers (2 Samuel 16:21).

and he slept with his father’s concubines
Taking possession of a former king’s harem was understood in the ancient world as a claim to his throne and household. Absalom’s action was sexual immorality, rebellion against his father, and a calculated seizure of royal prerogatives. It also fulfilled Nathan’s warning that, because of David’s sin, the Lord would raise trouble from within David’s own house and give David’s wives to another before the sun (2 Samuel 12:11–12). God’s judgment did not excuse Absalom’s evil, but it revealed the bitter consequences of David’s earlier disobedience.
