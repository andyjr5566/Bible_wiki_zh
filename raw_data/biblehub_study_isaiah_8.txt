Darkness and Light

Isaiah 8:19

When men tell you to consult mediums and spiritists who whisper and mutter,
Israel faced continual pressure to imitate the religious practices of surrounding nations. Mediums and spiritists claimed access to the dead and to hidden spiritual knowledge through secret rituals, incantations, and supernatural encounters. God had expressly forbidden such practices because they turned His people from trust in Him toward forbidden spiritual powers (Leviticus 19:31; Deuteronomy 18:10–12). In Isaiah’s day, fear of invasion and political turmoil made such counterfeit sources of guidance attractive. God’s people were not to seek security through occult knowledge but through His revealed word.

Deu 18:10-12 Let no one be found among you who sacrifices his son…

Lev 19:31 You must not turn to mediums or spiritists;…

Lev 20:6 Whoever turns to mediums or spiritists…

shouldn’t a people consult their God instead?
The Lord had bound Himself to Israel in covenant and had given prophets to declare His counsel. He alone knew the future, ruled the nations, and could preserve His people. Seeking direction from mediums denied the sufficiency of God’s presence and His word. Isaiah directs Judah away from voices that conceal truth and toward the God who speaks with authority, a call fulfilled ultimately in Christ, who reveals the Father and gives wisdom to all who seek God in faith.

Isa 31:1 Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help…

Jer 21:2 Please inquire of the LORD on our behalf,……

Why consult the dead on behalf of the living?
Those living under God’s covenant were not to seek counsel from the realm of death. King Saul’s visit to the medium at Endor showed the tragic end of rejecting God’s word while pursuing forbidden guidance (1 Samuel 28). The living God is the source of life, truth, and righteous direction; His people must seek Him through the revelation He has given. Isaiah’s next words point Israel to “the law and to the testimony,” establishing God’s word as the true standard by which every spiritual claim must be tested (Isaiah 8:20).

Isaiah 8:20

To the law and to the testimony!
God directs His people away from mediums, spirits, and human speculation to the revelation He has already given. The Mosaic Law established the covenant standard for Israel, while the prophetic testimony called the nation back to faithful obedience. God’s written word remains the final authority for discerning truth and exposing deception.

If they do not speak according to this word,
Messages claiming spiritual insight must be measured by agreement with God’s revelation. False prophets in Judah offered counsel that suited fearful and rebellious hearts, but their words could not overturn what the Lord had spoken. Scripture likewise commands believers to test every teaching by the truth God has revealed.

The darkness surrounding Judah would culminate in judgment, yet God would later promise light through the coming Messiah. Jesus Christ is the Light of the world, fulfilling the Law and the Prophets and bringing salvation to all who trust in Him.

Isaiah 8:21

They will roam the land, dejected and hungry.
Assyrian invasion would leave the land devastated, its people displaced, fearful, and without provision. Having rejected the Lord’s instruction in favor of political schemes and forbidden spiritual counsel, the nation would experience both physical scarcity and the darkness of spiritual confusion.

Isaiah 8:22

Then they will look to the earth
Having refused the Lord’s counsel and sought guidance from mediums and spirits of the dead, the people will turn their eyes from heaven to earthly resources. Political alliances, military strength, and human wisdom cannot deliver a nation under God’s judgment. Jeremiah likewise warns that the one who trusts in man turns his heart away from the Lord (Jeremiah 17:5–6).

Jer 4:23 I looked at the earth, and it was formless and void;…

and see only distress and darkness
Judah’s coming distress would include invasion, famine, fear, and national humiliation as Assyria swept through the land. Darkness portrays the moral and spiritual condition produced by rebellion against God, as well as the judgment that follows it. Those who reject divine light find no lasting hope in the world they have chosen.

and the gloom of anguish.
The anguish would be more than outward suffering; it would expose the emptiness of a people who had neglected the God who had faithfully revealed Himself to them. Moses had warned that covenant disobedience would bring confusion, blindness, and inability to find the way (Deuteronomy 28:28–29).

Yet the darkness described here prepares for Isaiah’s announcement that a great light would dawn upon those dwelling in darkness (Isaiah 9:1–2), ultimately fulfilled in Jesus Christ, the Light of the world (John 8:12).
