the Jewish Feast of Tabernacles
Also called Sukkot, this seven-day feast, followed by a sacred assembly on the eighth day, commemorated Israel’s wilderness journey and God’s faithful provision (Leviticus 23:33–43). Israelites lived in temporary booths as a reminder that the Lord had sheltered their ancestors in the desert. The feast was marked by rejoicing, sacrifices, and pilgrimage to Jerusalem. Its water ceremonies and temple lights form an important background for Jesus’ later invitations concerning living water and the light of the world (John 7:37–39; 8:12). Zechariah foretold a day when the nations would come to worship the King and celebrate this feast (Zechariah 14:16–19).
