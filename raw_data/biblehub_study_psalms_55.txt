Psalm 55:22

Cast your burden upon the LORD
David calls God’s people to place every weight of fear, sorrow, responsibility, and opposition upon the Lord. Such surrender is deliberate trust in the God who rules over circumstances and cares personally for His servants. Peter repeats this instruction to believers facing suffering: “casting all your anxiety on Him, because He cares for you” (1 Peter 5:7). God does not ask His people to deny the reality of trouble, but to refuse to carry it as though they were without His help.

and He will sustain you;
The Lord supplies the strength needed to endure what He permits. He sustained Israel in the wilderness with manna and water, and He remained faithful despite their weakness and rebellion. His provision reaches beyond material needs; He upholds weary hearts, grants wisdom for obedience, and preserves faith amid adversity. God promised through Isaiah that He would carry His people even into old age (Isaiah 46:4).

He will never let the righteous be shaken.
The righteous are those who belong to the Lord and seek to walk in obedience to Him. They may face betrayal, loss, danger, and seasons of deep distress, as David did, yet God will not allow their ultimate ruin. Their security rests not in favorable circumstances but in the Lord’s abiding presence. David testified, “Because He is at my right hand, I will not be shaken” (Psalm 16:8). A life founded on God’s word stands firm through storms, like the house built on the rock in Jesus’ teaching (Matthew 7:24–25).

Psalm 55:23

But You, O God, will bring them down to the Pit of destruction;
David entrusts his enemies to God’s righteous judgment rather than seeking personal vengeance. The Pit of destruction portrays the grave and the ruin that awaits those who persist in rebellion against the Lord. God sees hidden plots, betrayal, and violence, and no evildoer escapes His authority. Psalm 9:17 likewise declares that the wicked will be turned back to Sheol.

Psa 7:15-16 He has dug a hole and hollowed it out;…

Ezk 32:18-23 Son of man, wail for the multitudes of Egypt,…

men of bloodshed and deceit will not live out half their days.
Those who murder, oppress, and deceive others place themselves under God’s judgment. A long life was recognized as a covenant blessing, while an untimely death could reveal divine discipline against persistent wickedness. David’s words do not deny God’s patience, but they affirm that violent and treacherous people cannot ultimately secure their lives by their schemes. God hates hands that shed innocent blood and tongues that spread lies.
