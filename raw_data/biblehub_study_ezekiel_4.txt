and in despair they will drink water by measure.
Even water would be restricted, intensifying the misery of those trapped within Jerusalem’s walls. The measured bread and water portrayed not merely poverty but the collapse of normal life under God’s discipline. Yet the warning also called the exiles to recognize that the Lord’s judgments are righteous and that life depends wholly upon His mercy.

Ezekiel 4:17

So they will lack food and water;
Babylon’s siege of Jerusalem would cut off supplies and reduce the city’s inhabitants to measured portions of bread and water, as Ezekiel’s earlier actions portrayed (Ezekiel 4:10–11). Hunger and thirst fulfilled the covenant warnings of Deuteronomy 28:48, exposing the cost of Judah’s persistent idolatry and rebellion against the Lord.

they will be appalled at the sight of one another
The people would watch relatives, neighbors, and leaders weakened by famine, disease, and fear. Jerusalem’s suffering would become public and inescapable, bringing horror to a community that had ignored God’s warnings. Lamentations records the dreadful reality that followed the city’s fall (Lamentations 4:9–10).

Isa 13:8 Terror, pain, and anguish will seize them;…

Deu 28:34 You will be driven mad by the sights you see.

wasting away in their iniquity.
Their physical decline revealed the destructive end of unrepented sin. Iniquity had brought the nation under judgment, and no human strength or religious formality could reverse it. Yet the judgment also pointed to humanity’s need for a Savior: Christ bore the sins of His people and endured death so that those who trust Him might receive life rather than perish in their sin.
