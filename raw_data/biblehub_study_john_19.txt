The Soldiers Mock Jesus

John 19:1

Then Pilate took Jesus
Pilate, the Roman governor of Judea, exercised the authority of Rome in Jerusalem. After declaring that he found no guilt in Jesus, Pilate nevertheless yielded to mounting pressure from the chief priests and the crowd. Jesus was transferred from Jewish religious custody to Gentile authority, fulfilling His prediction that He would be delivered to the Gentiles (Matthew 20:19). Jewish leaders and Roman officials, though divided by politics and religion, became united in bringing about the death of the Messiah.

and had Him flogged.
Roman scourging was a savage punishment commonly inflicted before crucifixion. A victim was whipped with leather thongs designed to tear the flesh, producing severe pain, blood loss, and weakness. Pilate may have hoped that this punishment would satisfy the crowd and allow Jesus to be released, but it became part of the path to the cross. Jesus bore this suffering willingly, in keeping with the suffering Servant foretold in Isaiah: “He was pierced for our transgressions… and by His stripes we are healed” (Isaiah 53:5). His wounds reveal both the gravity of human sin and the cost of redemption.

Thorns recall the curse upon the ground after Adam’s sin (Genesis 3:18), and Jesus bore in His suffering the consequences of humanity’s fall. The soldiers intended to ridicule His claim to kingship, yet their cruelty pointed to the true King bearing the curse of sin for His people. He was despised and rejected, as Isaiah foretold of the suffering Servant (Isaiah 53:3).

Myrrh and aloes were costly aromatic spices used in Jewish burial preparations. They honored the dead and helped counter the odor of decay, though Jewish custom did not involve Egyptian-style embalming. Myrrh had appeared at Jesus’ birth among the gifts of the Magi (Matthew 2:11), and its presence at His burial recalls the purpose for which He came: to give His life as a ransom for many. Nicodemus treated Jesus’ body with reverence, acknowledging in death the dignity that many had refused Him in life.
