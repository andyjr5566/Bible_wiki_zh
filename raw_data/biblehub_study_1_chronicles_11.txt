David Anointed King of All Israel

1 Chronicles 11:1

Then all Israel came together to David at Hebron
Representatives from every tribe assembled at Hebron to recognize David as king over the united nation. Hebron, David’s royal city during his seven-year reign over Judah, was also associated with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and stood within the inheritance promised to the patriarchs. The national gathering fulfilled the Lord’s purpose in raising David from shepherd to king and brought the tribes together after years of division under Saul’s house. Israel’s unity under David anticipates the greater gathering of God’s people under Jesus Christ, the Son of David.

David Conquers Jerusalem

1 Chronicles 11:4

Then David and all the Israelites marched to Jerusalem
Having been anointed king over all Israel, David led the united tribes against Jerusalem. The city stood near the boundary between Judah and Benjamin, making it a fitting capital for a nation formerly divided by tribal loyalties and Saul’s declining house. Jerusalem had earlier been associated with Salem, ruled by Melchizedek, priest of God Most High (Genesis 14:18). David’s capture of the city began its rise as Israel’s political center and, later, the place God chose for the temple and the ark.

Jebus was Jerusalem’s Canaanite name and reflected its long occupation by the Jebusites, descendants of Canaan (Genesis 10:16). David’s conquest did not merely change the city’s ruler; it marked the establishment of a new center for the covenant people. From Jerusalem, David would govern Israel, bring the ark of God, and prepare for the temple his son Solomon would build.
