Endow the King with Your Justice

Psalm 72:1

Of Solomon.
Solomon, David’s son and successor, ruled during a period of peace, prosperity, and temple building in Jerusalem (1 Kings 4–8). The psalm presents the God-given ideal for Israel’s king: a ruler whose authority reflects the Lord’s own character. Its hopes reach beyond Solomon’s historical reign to the enduring Davidic King, Jesus Christ.

Endow the king with Your justice, O God,
Israel’s king was accountable to God and charged to govern according to His law, defending the oppressed and judging without partiality (Deuteronomy 17:18–20). Human wisdom and power could not produce the justice required of a righteous ruler; the king needed God’s own judgment to lead faithfully.

Isa 11:3-4 And He will delight in the fear of the LORD.…

and the son of the king with Your righteousness.
As David’s son, Solomon stood within the covenant promise that David’s throne would endure (2 Samuel 7:12–16). The prayer seeks a continuing line of rulers marked by obedience to God. Its fullest fulfillment is found in Jesus Christ, the Son of David, whose reign is perfectly righteous and whose justice will never fail.

Psalm 72:2

May he judge Your people with righteousness
The king of Israel ruled as God’s servant and was accountable to administer God’s law without partiality. Righteous leadership required faithfulness to the covenant, honesty in judgment, and protection from corruption. Deuteronomy 16:18–20 commands Israel’s judges to pursue righteous judgment, because justice among the people reflected the holy character of the Lord. Solomon’s prayer reaches beyond his own reign toward the promised Messiah, whose reign will be marked by perfect righteousness (Isaiah 11:4–5; Jeremiah 23:5–6). Jesus Christ fulfills this hope as the righteous King who judges with complete knowledge and unwavering truth.

Psa 9:8 He judges the world with justice; He governs the people…

and Your afflicted with justice.
The afflicted belong to God and must not be neglected by those entrusted with authority. Israel’s law repeatedly guarded the poor, widows, orphans, and foreigners against oppression, warning that God hears their cries (Exodus 22:21–24). A godly king was to use his authority not for personal gain but to defend those unable to defend themselves. Such justice reflects the Lord’s concern for the humble and oppressed and anticipates Christ’s ministry of bringing good news to the poor and freedom to the burdened (Luke 4:18–19).

Under the righteous reign for which Psalm 72 prays, even the mountains and hills yield their abundance for the good of the land. Peace includes security from enemies, just judgment for the oppressed, fruitful fields, and the settled well-being of God’s people. The enduring strength of the mountains pictures the stability that comes when a king rules in the fear of the Lord. Solomon’s reign foreshadowed such prosperity, yet the fullness of this hope belongs to Christ, whose kingdom will establish lasting peace.

Christ perfectly fulfills this royal ideal, bringing believers peace with God through His righteousness.

Psalm 72:6

May he be like rain that falls on freshly cut grass,
The righteous king is pictured as gentle, life-giving rain upon a field that has been cut and needs renewed growth. In Israel’s agrarian life, rain was God’s essential provision for pasture, grain, livestock, and daily survival. Such rule does not crush the people but restores them, bringing justice, security, and renewed fruitfulness. The image anticipates the reign of the greater Son of David, Jesus Christ, whose righteous kingdom brings life to those made needy and humble before Him.

In the same way, the king prayed for in Psalm 72 would foster peace and prosperity through righteous judgment. The fullness of this hope belongs to Christ, whose reign will ultimately renew the earth and whose word accomplishes God’s life-giving purpose among His people.

May he rule from sea to sea,
The prayer envisions a dominion stretching across the land of Israel and beyond, from the Mediterranean westward boundary to the eastern waters and territories. Solomon’s kingdom briefly reflected this ideal through peace, tribute, and influence among surrounding nations (1 Kings 4:21, 24). The greater fulfillment belongs to the promised Son of David, whose kingdom is not confined to Israel but extends among all peoples. Zechariah foretells the same worldwide reign for the coming King (Zechariah 9:10).

Solomon exercised authority to the Euphrates during the height of his reign, yet no earthly descendant of David permanently possessed all that God promised. Jesus Christ fulfills the Davidic covenant through an everlasting kingdom that cannot be overcome (2 Samuel 7:16; Luke 1:32–33).

The distant shores extend the horizon beyond Israel’s immediate neighbors, portraying rulers from the farthest known lands acknowledging the Davidic king’s authority. Solomon’s international renown partially fulfilled this picture, yet the scope ultimately points to the Messiah, whose inheritance includes the nations and the ends of the earth (Psalm 2:8).

Kings who possess great earthly power must ultimately bow before the Lord’s Anointed, fulfilling Isaiah’s vision that rulers would rise and bow because of the Holy One of Israel (Isaiah 49:7). Jesus Christ bears the title King of kings, and every knee will bow before Him in willing worship or final judgment (Philippians 2:10–11; Revelation 19:16).

The doubled affirmation, amen, amen, meaning truly or so be it, seals the doxology with confident agreement in God’s purpose and promises. God will establish His righteous kingdom, exalt His name, and fill the earth with His glory.

Psalm 72:20

Thus conclude the prayers
The closing note marks the end of Book II of the Psalter and gathers its worship as the prayers of God’s covenant king. David’s psalms give voice to praise, confession, suffering, dependence, and hope in the Lord. The placement after Psalm 72 also directs attention to the promised righteous King whose reign extends to the ends of the earth.

of David
David’s life of prayer arose from real trials, repentance, warfare, exile, and confidence in God’s covenant faithfulness. Although later psalms bear David’s name, this conclusion functions as an editorial marker for the collection associated especially with him. God’s promise that David’s throne would endure finds its ultimate fulfillment in Jesus Christ, the eternal King from David’s line.

son of Jesse.
Jesse of Bethlehem was the father of David, the shepherd chosen and anointed by Samuel to rule Israel. David’s humble family origin magnifies God’s sovereign choice and preserves the connection to Judah, from whom the promised ruler would come. Jesus, born in Bethlehem and called the Son of David, fulfills the hope bound to David’s house.
