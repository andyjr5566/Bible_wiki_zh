God freely set His covenant love on Jacob and his descendants, fulfilling the promise given to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Before the twins were born, the Lord declared that the older, Esau, would serve the younger, establishing that His redemptive purpose rests on His sovereign choice rather than human merit (Genesis 25:23; Romans 9:10–13). Jacob’s name also represents the nation descended from him, Israel, through whom God preserved the promised line leading to the Messiah.

Israel as His treasured possession.
Israel belonged uniquely to the Lord among the nations, not because of its size, strength, or righteousness, but because of His covenant faithfulness (Deuteronomy 7:6–8). At Sinai, God called Israel to live as a holy nation and a kingdom of priests, bearing witness to His character before the world (Exodus 19:5–6). Such privilege carried the responsibility of obedience, worship, and trust. God’s covenant purpose for Israel displays His enduring faithfulness and anticipates the gathering of a redeemed people who proclaim His excellencies through Christ (1 Peter 2:9).

The psalmist’s certainty rests on the LORD’s revealed works in creation, Israel’s deliverance from Egypt, and His faithful rule over His people. God’s greatness is not an abstract idea but is displayed in His holiness, power, mercy, and covenant faithfulness. Israel had seen that no ruler, nation, or force of nature could resist the God who divided the sea, fed His people in the wilderness, and gave them the promised land.

The Lord governs the whole created order, from horizons beyond human sight to the land inhabited by His people. Clouds, rain, and storms are not independent forces but operate under His command. Psalm 135:8

He struck down the firstborn of Egypt,
The tenth plague brought God’s judgment upon Pharaoh’s hardened kingdom and exposed the helplessness of Egypt’s gods (Exodus 12:29–30; 12:12). The death of the firstborn shattered every household, from Pharaoh’s palace to the prison, and compelled Egypt to release Israel from slavery. Israel’s firstborn were spared only through the blood of the Passover lamb, marking redemption through a God-provided substitute. The Passover points forward to Christ, whose sacrificial death delivers His people from judgment and bondage (1 Corinthians 5:7).

Sihon king of the Amorites,
Sihon ruled from Heshbon over territory stretching from the Arnon River to the Jabbok. When Israel sought peaceful passage through his land, he gathered his army and attacked at Jahaz. The Lord gave Sihon and his cities into Israel’s hand, and his former kingdom was later allotted to Reuben and Gad. Heshbon stood as a lasting witness that the Lord had begun to give Israel the inheritance He promised.

Og king of Bashan,
Og ruled the fertile and strongly fortified region north of Sihon’s kingdom. As one of the last of the Rephaim, Og represented a fearful enemy, yet the Lord told Israel not to fear him. Israel defeated Og at Edrei and took Bashan, including its many fortified cities. The land was given to the half-tribe of Manasseh, confirming that no human strength, military power, or imposing reputation could overturn God’s purpose.

Psalm 135:12

God fulfilled His covenant promise to Abraham by giving Canaan to Israel after delivering them from Egypt and defeating the nations that occupied the land (Genesis 15:18–21; Joshua 21:43–45). Israel received the land as God’s gift, not as a reward for superior righteousness (Deuteronomy 9:4–6). Its fields, cities, and boundaries testified to the faithfulness of the Lord who keeps His word across generations.

as a heritage to His people Israel.
The land belonged ultimately to God, yet He assigned it to the tribes of Israel as a lasting covenant possession (Leviticus 25:23; Joshua 13–21). Their inheritance called Israel to live under God’s rule, worship Him alone, and display His holiness among the nations. Israel’s history in the land also showed that covenant privilege brought covenant responsibility; persistent rebellion led to exile, yet God promised a future restoration of His people to their land (Ezekiel 37:21–28). For believers, Israel’s inheritance also points to the greater inheritance secured through Christ, in whom God’s people receive forgiveness, adoption, and an eternal kingdom (Ephesians 1:11; 1 Peter 1:3–4).

God’s renown reaches its fullest public display in Jesus Christ, through whom the promised blessing to Abraham extends to all nations.
