Psalm 77:1

For the choirmaster.
The superscription places Psalm 77 within Israel’s gathered worship, entrusting it to the leader responsible for the temple’s music. Personal anguish was not hidden from the congregation; it was brought before God in prayer and song, teaching God’s people to seek Him together in seasons of distress.

1Ch 15:16 David also told the leaders of the Levites to appoint…

2Ch 29:25 Hezekiah stationed the Levites in the house of the LORD…

According to Jeduthun.
Jeduthun was a Levite appointed by David among the leading temple musicians, serving alongside Asaph and Heman (1 Chronicles 16:41–42; 25:1–6). His family continued in musical ministry, and the notation connects the psalm with the established worship of the sanctuary. The precise musical direction is no longer known, but the psalm was prepared for reverent public use.

1Ch 25:1-3 Additionally, David and the commanders of the army…

1Ch 16:41-42 With them were Heman, Jeduthun, and the rest…

A Psalm of Asaph.
Asaph served as a chief musician under David and ministered prophetically through music (1 Chronicles 25:1–2; 2 Chronicles 29:30). The psalms bearing his name often confront national crisis, divine judgment, and the memory of God’s mighty acts. Asaph’s sorrow does not end in despair, for his prayer turns toward the faithfulness God displayed in Israel’s redemption.

Asaph begins without concealing his trouble. He directs his pain toward God rather than away from Him, recognizing that the Lord alone can sustain and deliver His people. Such prayer reflects confidence that God remains near even when His help seems delayed.

His groaning is not unbelief but the burdened cry of a servant who knows the Lord is able to save. Job likewise groaned beneath suffering he could not explain (Job 3:24), and Elijah’s strength failed after great conflict (1 Kings 19:4). Human strength reaches its limit, but the Lord gives power to the faint and renews those who wait for Him (Isaiah 40:29–31).

Israel’s history testified that the Lord had redeemed, preserved, and restored His people; therefore, permanent rejection would seem contrary to His revealed character. In Christ, God’s promises reach their decisive fulfillment, for all the promises of God are “Yes” in Him (2 Corinthians 1:20).

God used Moses, Israel’s prophet and lawgiver, and Aaron, the first high priest, as His appointed servants in bringing Israel out of Egypt. Their leadership displayed that the Lord works through human instruments while remaining the true Deliverer and Shepherd of His people. Moses and Aaron also anticipate Christ, who perfectly fulfills the offices of prophet, priest, and king and brings His people out of bondage into the promised inheritance.

Psa 105:26-27 He sent Moses His servant, and Aaron, whom He had chosen.…

Mic 6:4 For I brought you up from the land of Egypt…
