Zechariah 3:3

Now Joshua was dressed in filthy garments
Joshua’s soiled clothing portrayed the guilt and defilement of Israel’s restored remnant after the Babylonian exile. As high priest, Joshua represented the people before God, yet he stood clothed in garments unfit for priestly service. Israel’s sin had brought judgment and exile, and return to the land had not removed the need for forgiveness. The image recalls Isaiah 64:6, where human righteousness is compared to polluted garments, and it underscores that cleansing must come from God rather than human effort.

The Lord did not deny Israel’s uncleanness; He removed it and restored Joshua to priestly service. Joshua’s cleansing anticipated the fuller redemption accomplished through Christ, the true High Priest, who intercedes for His people and provides the righteousness they cannot supply.

Zechariah 3:5

Zechariah’s participation shows that he is not merely observing Joshua’s restoration but is drawn into the divine declaration of it. The clean turban restores the high priest’s sacred office, recalling the priestly headdress bearing the inscription, “Holy to the LORD” (Exodus 28:36–39). Joshua, formerly clothed in filthy garments, is now publicly marked as cleansed, consecrated, and accepted for service before God. Israel’s worship and priesthood, defiled through sin and exile, are being renewed by God’s mercy.

The scene anticipates the perfect priesthood of Jesus Christ, who is holy, undefiled, and able to save completely those who draw near to God through Him (Hebrews 7:26–28).

Through Christ, God forms His people into a royal priesthood devoted to His service (1 Peter 2:9).
