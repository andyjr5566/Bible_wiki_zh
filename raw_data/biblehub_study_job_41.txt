The LORD’s Power Shown in Leviathan

Job 41:1

“Can you pull in Leviathan with a hook
Leviathan is presented as a real and fearsome creature under God’s rule, beyond Job’s ability to capture or control. The descriptions that follow emphasize its strength, armor, and terror, whether the creature is understood as a crocodile or as a creature surpassing any animal Job had encountered. Ancient pagan myths often portrayed sea monsters as forces of chaos opposed to the gods, but the Lord presents Leviathan as His creature, wholly subject to His command. Psalms 74:14 and Isaiah 27:1 later use Leviathan imagery for hostile powers and chaos that God will defeat.

This phrase refers to the creature Leviathan, a powerful and fearsome sea monster described in the preceding verses. The imagery of laying a hand on Leviathan suggests an attempt to control or subdue something beyond human capability. This reflects the broader theme of God’s sovereignty over creation, as seen in other biblical passages like Psalm 104:26 and Isaiah 27:1, where Leviathan is also mentioned.

Job 41:27

He regards iron as straw
Leviathan treats humanity’s strongest weapons as though they were dry stalks fit only for crushing. Iron furnished blades, spearheads, and tools of war in the ancient world, yet it offers no protection or advantage against the creature God describes. Job, who had questioned the justice and order of his suffering, is confronted with a being beyond human mastery. Leviathan’s strength exposes the limits of human power and directs attention to the Creator who alone rules all that He has made.

and bronze as rotten wood.
Bronze armor and weapons, valued for their hardness and durability, are no more effective against Leviathan than decayed timber that breaks at a touch. The image denies any confidence that human skill, force, or military resources can overcome what God has placed beyond human control. If no man can stand before Leviathan, Job 41:10 asks, who can stand before the Lord? God’s greatness does not diminish the reality of human weakness; it calls His people to humility, trust, and reverent submission.

Job 41:31

He makes the depths seethe like a cauldron;
Leviathan’s movement churns the waters with such force that the deep appears to boil in a great cooking pot. The sea, often associated with danger and untamed power, cannot resist the strength of this creature. Yet Leviathan is not independent of God; the Lord formed it and rules it. Job is reminded that no human can master what God has made, and therefore no one can contend successfully with the Creator Himself. God’s authority over the sea is also displayed when He stills storms and sets boundaries for the waters (Psalm 107:29; Job 38:8–11).

he makes the sea like a jar of ointment.
The violently stirred sea resembles a vessel of fragrant oil being mixed and brought to the surface. Ointment was valuable in the ancient world, used for healing, hospitality, consecration, and burial. The image does not diminish Leviathan’s terror; it magnifies the visible effect of its power upon the waters. Even the sea’s turmoil remains within God’s providence. The Lord who governs Leviathan also brings order from chaos, ultimately seen in Christ, who rebuked the wind and waves and brought immediate calm (Mark 4:39).
