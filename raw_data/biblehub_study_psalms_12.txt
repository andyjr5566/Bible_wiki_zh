Psalm 12:1

For the choirmaster.
The psalm was entrusted to Israel’s worship leader for use in the gathered worship of God’s people. David’s lament was not merely private grief; it gave the congregation words for praying when corruption and deceit seemed to prevail.

Psalm 12:2

They lie to one another;
David describes a society in which falsehood has become ordinary in human relationships. Such deceit opposes the character of God, who is true and faithful in all His words. Lies destroy trust, conceal sin, and reflect the work of Satan, the father of lies (John 8:44).

Jer 9:5 Each one betrays his friend; no one tells the truth.…

they speak with flattering lips
Flattery uses pleasing words to conceal selfish motives and gain advantage. Rather than speaking truth in love, flattering speech manipulates others and often prepares the way for betrayal. God calls His people to put away falsehood and to speak truthfully with one another (Ephesians 4:25).

Pro 26:28 A lying tongue hates those it crushes…

and a double heart.
A double heart presents one intention outwardly while harboring another within. Such divided loyalty makes a person unstable and untrustworthy before God and man (James 1:8). David’s later prayer for a clean heart (Psalm 51:10) shows that only God can produce the inward sincerity and wholehearted devotion that truthful speech requires.

Psalm 12:3

May the LORD cut off all flattering lips
David appeals to the LORD as the righteous Judge to restrain and silence speech used to deceive, exploit, and gain advantage over others. Flattery conceals hatred or selfish ambition beneath pleasing words, and it can destroy trust within families, communities, and the covenant people. Proverbs 26:28 links a flattering mouth with ruin, while Psalm 5:9 describes the wicked as having no faithfulness in their speech. God’s judgment against such evil protects the vulnerable and establishes the truth that human hearts cannot secure by their own strength.

Pro 26:28 A lying tongue hates those it crushes…

Rom 16:18 For such people are not serving our Lord Christ…

Pro 29:5 A man who flatters his neighbor spreads a net for his feet.

and every boastful tongue.
Proud speech exalts human power, wisdom, or achievement while refusing to acknowledge dependence upon God. Such boasting often accompanies oppression, since those who magnify themselves readily despise others. The LORD opposes the proud and will bring down every self-exalting claim, as Isaiah 2:12 declares. James likewise warns that the tongue, though small, can spread great destruction when governed by sinful ambition. David’s prayer anticipates the day when God will expose arrogant words and vindicate those who trust in Him rather than in human strength.

His word is wholly true, dependable in judgment, and faithful to His pledge to arise for the afflicted and needy. Scripture, breathed out by God, supplies trustworthy instruction, correction, and hope.

Psalm 12:7

You, O LORD, will keep us;
David rests in Yahweh’s covenant faithfulness amid a society marked by deceit, flattery, and oppression. God preserves His people not only from outward harm but from being overcome by the moral corruption surrounding them. His keeping hand sustains those who trust Him when human strength and leadership fail.

God’s protection is steadfast because His character does not change. He watches over His people as the Shepherd who leads, restores, and defends His flock. The Lord’s care reaches beyond immediate trouble and secures all who belong to Him in enduring fellowship with Himself.

Psalm 12:8

The wicked wander freely,
Public restraint weakens when those who fear God are silenced or treated with contempt. Israel repeatedly experienced such disorder during the period of the Judges, when everyone did what was right in his own eyes. Saul’s disobedience, Ahab’s promotion of Baal worship, and Manasseh’s violence show how rebellion against God’s rule spreads from leaders into the life of a nation. The wicked may appear secure and unrestricted, yet their course is spiritually aimless and remains under God’s judgment.

Pro 1:16 For their feet run to evil, and they are swift…

Psa 59:6 They return in the evening, snarling like dogs…

Isa 57:20 But the wicked are like the storm-tossed sea,…

and vileness is exalted among men.
Moral corruption becomes entrenched when evil is honored as wisdom, power, or freedom. Isaiah condemned those who called evil good and good evil, and the prophets warned that idolatry, injustice, and sexual immorality were not private sins but national offenses against the Lord. Such inversion of values anticipates Paul’s description of the last days, when self-love, greed, pride, and cruelty multiply. Jesus Christ reveals the true standard of righteousness and calls His people to stand as salt and light, refusing to celebrate what God condemns.
