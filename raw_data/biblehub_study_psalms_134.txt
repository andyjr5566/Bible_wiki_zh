Bless the LORD, All You Servants

Psalm 134:1

A song of ascents.
Psalm 134 concludes the Songs of Ascents, Psalms 120–134, associated with Israel’s pilgrimage to Jerusalem for the appointed feasts. As worshipers came up to the city set upon Mount Zion, their physical ascent pictured a renewed approach to the presence of the Lord.

Come, bless the LORD,
Pilgrims summon the temple ministers to join in adoration of the covenant God. Praise belongs to the Lord not only in public gatherings but also in the quiet hours when few are present. Psalm 134 closes with the worshipers blessing the Lord and the Lord blessing His people from Zion.

all you servants of the LORD
The servants especially include the priests and Levites appointed to guard, maintain, and minister in the temple. Their service represented Israel’s calling to honor the Lord who had chosen Zion as the place of His name. All who belong to God share this calling to offer their lives in willing obedience and worship.

who serve by night
Night duty required watchfulness in the sanctuary. Priests tended the lamps from evening until morning, and Levites kept watch over the temple precincts. Their faithful service testified that devotion to the Lord did not cease when the crowds departed, and it anticipates the vigilance Christ commands of His people.

in the house of the LORD!
The house of the Lord was the temple in Jerusalem, where God placed His name among His people and where sacrifices were offered according to His covenant. The temple pointed beyond itself to Christ, the true meeting place between God and man, and to the gathered church as God’s dwelling through the Holy Spirit.

Psalm 134:2

Lift up your hands
Worship in Israel involved the whole person. Raised hands expressed reverence, dependence, and an appeal to the God who alone could bless His people. The temple servants were summoned to offer more than routine service; their outward posture was to reflect hearts yielded to the LORD.

Psa 63:4 So I will bless You as long as I live; in Your name…

to the sanctuary
The hands were directed toward the holy place in Jerusalem, where the LORD had placed His name and where priests and Levites ministered before Him. Though God is not confined to a building, the sanctuary testified to His covenant presence among Israel and to the need for approach through His appointed worship.

and bless the LORD!
Those who served through the night were called to praise the God they served. Blessing the LORD acknowledges His holiness, faithfulness, and sovereign goodness. The worshiper who seeks blessing from God is also called to return praise to Him with grateful devotion.

Psalm 134:3

May the LORD,
Yahweh, the covenant God of Israel, is the source of every true blessing. His name recalls His faithfulness to Abraham, Moses, David, and all who belong to Him. The God who receives the worship of His servants also turns in grace toward His people.

Num 6:24 ‘May the LORD bless you and keep you;

Psa 20:1 For the choirmaster. A Psalm of David.…

the Maker of heaven and earth,
The One worshiped in Zion is not a local deity limited to Jerusalem or Israel. He created and rules the entire universe, possessing full power to provide, protect, and sustain. The New Testament identifies the Son as the divine agent through whom all things were made (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16).

Gen 14:19 and he blessed Abram and said: “Blessed be Abram…

Psa 115:15 May you be blessed by the LORD, the Maker of heaven…

bless you
God’s blessing includes His favor, preservation, provision, and peace. The prayer echoes the priestly blessing, in which the LORD places His name upon His people and causes His face to shine on them (Numbers 6:24–26). Those who bless the Lord in worship depend upon Him to bless them in life.

Gen 12:2 I will make you into a great nation, and I will bless you;…

Deu 7:13 He will love you and bless you and multiply you.…

Psa 115:12-13 The LORD is mindful of us; He will bless us.…

from Zion.
Zion was the site of the temple, where God had chosen to make His name dwell among His people. Blessing from Zion points to God’s gracious presence and to His covenant rule. It also anticipates the promised kingdom in which the Lord’s instruction and salvation go forth from Zion (Isaiah 2:2–3), fulfilled ultimately in Christ and consummated in the heavenly Zion (Hebrews 12:22).
