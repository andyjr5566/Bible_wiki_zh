Tyre’s island stronghold, protected by the sea and formidable walls, seemed beyond the reach of any enemy. Its defenses fostered confidence in human strength rather than humility before the Lord. Zechariah 9:4

Tyre’s immense commercial wealth could not shield her from the Lord who governs kingdoms and overturns human pride. Situated on the Phoenician coast, Tyre dominated Mediterranean trade and accumulated riches through shipping, manufacturing, and international alliances. Her judgment fulfilled earlier warnings against Tyre’s arrogance and self-sufficiency (Isaiah 23; Ezekiel 26–28). God’s purposes reached even this powerful maritime city.

The sea that carried Tyre’s merchants and made her prosperous would become the setting of her humiliation. Nebuchadnezzar’s long siege weakened Tyre, and Alexander the Great later captured the island city in 332 BC by constructing a causeway from the mainland ruins. Tyre’s treasures, commerce, and defenses proved unable to preserve her when God brought judgment.

Alexander’s conquest brought slaughter, enslavement, and destruction to Tyre, demonstrating that no earthly power stands secure against divine judgment. The downfall of Tyre also prepared the way for the advance of Alexander toward the land of Israel, while Jerusalem would be preserved by the Lord’s sovereign hand.

The LORD Will Save His People

Zechariah 9:14

The God of Israel will personally defend His covenant people, making His presence known as He did in the Exodus through the pillar of cloud and fire (Exodus 13:21–22). Zechariah presents the LORD not as a distant observer but as the divine Warrior who stands over Judah and delivers His people from their enemies. The promise anticipates the saving presence of Christ, God incarnate, who came to dwell among His people and will return in glory.

God’s judgment will strike with irresistible speed, power, and precision. As the LORD scattered His enemies with lightning and arrows in David’s song of deliverance (Psalm 18:14), He will overthrow those who oppose His kingdom. The image ultimately accords with Christ’s victorious return as the righteous King who judges evil and establishes His rule (Revelation 19:11–16).

God’s trumpet of victory foreshadows the final trumpet associated with Christ’s return, resurrection, and the gathering of His saints (1 Thessalonians 4:16).
