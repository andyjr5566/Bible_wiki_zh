Ezekiel 48:9

The special portion you set apart to the LORD
This phrase refers to a designated area within the land of Israel that is consecrated for divine purposes. In the context of Ezekiel’s vision, this special portion is part of the larger division of the land among the tribes of Israel. The setting apart of land for the LORD emphasizes the importance of holiness and dedication to God. This concept of setting apart is seen throughout Scripture, such as in the consecration of the Tabernacle (Exodus 40:9) and the Temple (1 Kings 8:63). It reflects the idea that certain things are reserved exclusively for God’s use and glory.

Ezk 45:4 It will be a holy portion of the land to be used…

Num 18:20 Then the LORD said to Aaron, “You will have no inheritance…

shall be 25,000 cubits long
The measurement of 25,000 cubits indicates a significant size, suggesting the importance of this portion. A cubit is traditionally understood as the length from the elbow to the tip of the middle finger, approximately 18 inches or 45 centimeters. This would make the length of the portion about 7.5 miles or 12 kilometers. The specific dimensions highlight the precision and orderliness of God’s plans, reminiscent of the detailed instructions given for the construction of the Tabernacle (Exodus 25-27) and the Temple (1 Kings 6). It underscores the idea that God is a God of order and detail.

Ezk 45:1 When you divide the land by lot as an inheritance…

The width of 10,000 cubits, approximately 3 miles or 4.8 kilometers, further defines the area set apart for the LORD. This dimension, combined with the length, forms a rectangular area, which is a common shape for sacred spaces in ancient times. The specific measurements may symbolize completeness and perfection, reflecting God’s perfect nature. The allocation of this land for sacred purposes can be seen as a foreshadowing of the New Jerusalem described in Revelation 21, where the city is also measured with precise dimensions, symbolizing God’s eternal dwelling with His people.

The western boundary matches the other sides of the city, displaying the order and symmetry of the Lord’s appointed dwelling place among His people. Using the ordinary cubit, the length is about 6,750 feet; Ezekiel’s larger temple cubit would make it somewhat greater. Every boundary is established by God, and the city stands as a secure inheritance for restored Israel.

Ezekiel 48:35

The perimeter of the city will be 18,000 cubits,
The city measures 4,500 cubits on each side, for a total perimeter of 18,000 cubits. Using a common cubit of about eighteen inches, the perimeter would be slightly more than five miles. The measured boundaries reveal that the restored order of Israel is neither vague nor symbolic of disorder; God appoints every tribe, portion, gate, and dwelling place according to His righteous purpose. Like the tabernacle and Solomon’s temple, Ezekiel’s city is marked by divine order. The city stands at the center of a restored land where the Lord’s holiness governs the life of His people.

The new name declares the enduring character of the restored city. Israel’s final restoration will not be defined by exile, defilement, or the absence of God’s glory, but by the Lord’s settled presence among His people.

THE LORD IS THERE.”
Yahweh Shammah, “THE LORD IS THERE,” proclaims that the God whose glory departed from Jerusalem because of Israel’s sin will dwell there again. Ezekiel had witnessed the departure of the divine glory from the temple, but he also saw the glory of the Lord return to the restored sanctuary. The city’s greatest blessing is not its size, security, or beauty, but the presence of the Lord Himself. Jesus Christ is Immanuel, “God with us,” and the final dwelling of God with redeemed humanity is fulfilled in the New Jerusalem, where “the dwelling place of God is with man” (Revelation 21:3).
