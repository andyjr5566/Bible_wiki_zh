Psalm 28:1

Of David.
David wrote as a man acquainted with danger, betrayal, warfare, and the responsibilities of ruling Israel. His appeals arise from personal need and from confidence in the God who had called, preserved, and established him.

Psalm 28:3

Do not drag me away with the wicked,
David asks God not to sweep him away in the judgment reserved for those who reject God’s ways. Though all people deserve judgment for sin, the psalmist appeals to the Lord as one who trusts Him and desires to walk uprightly. Scripture consistently distinguishes the righteous from the wicked, not because the righteous are sinless, but because they belong to the Lord and seek His mercy. Abraham appealed to this same justice when he asked whether God would destroy the righteous with the wicked in Sodom (Genesis 18:23–25).

Psa 26:9 Do not take my soul away with sinners, or my life…

Psa 125:5 But those who turn to crooked ways the LORD will banish…

Psa 1:5 Therefore the wicked will not stand in the judgment,…

and with the workers of iniquity,
Those who practice evil as a settled course of life stand under God’s righteous judgment. David does not seek escape from consequences while continuing in sin; he asks to be preserved from identification with those whose actions oppose the Lord. God sees the difference between outward association and the true direction of a person’s heart. Final judgment will bring that distinction fully to light, when Christ separates the righteous from the wicked (Matthew 25:31–46).

Psa 5:5 The boastful cannot stand in Your presence;…

Psa 6:8 Depart from me, all you workers of iniquity,…

Luk 13:27 And he will answer, ‘I tell you, I do not know…

who speak peace to their neighbors
The wicked described here use peaceful language to conceal betrayal. In Israel’s covenant community, words of peace carried the expectation of goodwill, faithfulness, and safety among neighbors. To offer peace while intending harm was a serious violation of justice and brotherly responsibility. Judas’s greeting and kiss in Gethsemane provide a later example of outward friendship masking treachery (Luke 22:47–48).

Jer 9:8 Their tongues are deadly arrows; they speak deception.…

Psa 55:21 His speech is smooth as butter, but war is in his heart.…

Pro 26:25 When he speaks graciously, do not believe him…

while malice is in their hearts.
God judges not only public deeds but the hidden purposes from which deeds arise. A heart ruled by malice may speak kindly, yet its concealed hatred eventually produces destruction. David entrusts himself to the Lord, who searches the heart and exposes deception. Christ likewise taught that moral defilement proceeds from within, calling His people to sincerity, purity, and love that is genuine rather than merely outward.

Pro 26:24 A hateful man disguises himself with his speech…

Psa 12:2 They lie to one another; they speak with flattering lips…

Jer 17:9 The heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure.…

Psalm 28:4

Repay them according to their deeds
David entrusts judgment to the Lord rather than taking vengeance into his own hands. God sees hidden motives and public acts alike, and He judges with perfect righteousness (Proverbs 24:12; Romans 2:6).

Jer 17:10 I, the LORD, search the heart; I examine the mind…

Rom 2:6 God “will repay each one according to his deeds.”

Psa 62:12 and loving devotion to You, O Lord.…

and for their works of evil.
Evil is not merely an inward condition but produces deeds that oppose God’s will and harm others. Persistent rebellion brings real accountability, unless sinners turn from sin and seek God’s mercy.

Col 3:25 Whoever does wrong will be repaid for his wrong…

2Co 5:10 For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ,…

Pro 1:31 So they will eat the fruit of their own way,…

Repay them for what their hands have done;
Human hands carry out the purposes of the heart. Those who use their strength, authority, or influence to deceive, oppress, or destroy cannot escape the judgment of the God who knows every work (Isaiah 59:3).

Jer 25:14 For many nations and great kings will enslave them…

Isa 3:11 Woe to the wicked; disaster is upon them!…

2Ti 4:14 Alexander the coppersmith did great harm to me.…

bring back on them what they deserve.
David appeals to the righteous Judge to give evil its due consequence. The prayer anticipates the final judgment, when God will judge every person according to his works (Revelation 20:12–13), while those redeemed through Christ are saved by grace and no longer bear the condemnation their sins deserve.

Psalm 28:5

Since they show no regard for the works of the LORD
David identifies the root of the wickedness he has described: deliberate refusal to acknowledge God’s rule and deeds. The LORD’s works are displayed in creation, in His preservation of His people, and in His righteous acts within history (Psalm 19:1; Romans 1:20). Israel’s repeated rebellion, including the worship of the golden calf, showed how quickly people could ignore the God who had redeemed them (Exodus 32:1–6). Such disregard hardens the heart against truth and leaves sin unchecked.

Isa 5:12 At their feasts are the lyre and harp…

Psa 111:2 Great are the works of the LORD; they are pondered by all…

or what His hands have done,
The hands of the LORD portray His personal power in creating, sustaining, delivering, and judging. David recognizes that God is not distant from human affairs; He acts with purpose and authority. The heavens are the work of His fingers, and His people are the work of His hands (Psalm 8:3; Isaiah 64:8). To dismiss His works is to reject the Creator’s rightful claim over His creation and to overlook His mercy in redemption.

Isa 64:8 But now, O LORD, You are our Father; we are the clay,…

Psa 95:5 The sea is His, for He made it, and His hands formed…

He will tear them down
God’s judgment answers persistent rebellion. Those who build their lives through violence, deceit, or contempt for God may appear secure for a time, but their security rests on no lasting foundation. The LORD tears down what opposes His righteousness, as He warned concerning nations that refused to turn from evil (Jeremiah 18:7–10). David entrusts judgment to God rather than taking vengeance into his own hands.

Psa 52:5 Surely God will bring you down to everlasting ruin;…

Pro 15:25 The LORD tears down the house of the proud…

and never rebuild them.
The judgment described is decisive because the wicked have set themselves against the One who alone gives life and establishes what endures. God’s overthrow of rebellious powers demonstrates that no human strength can withstand His verdict. The destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah stands as a lasting witness to the seriousness of His judgment against settled wickedness (Genesis 19:24–25).

Isa 25:2 Indeed, You have made the city a heap of rubble…

Psalm 28:6

Blessed be the LORD,
David turns from urgent petition to worship, praising the covenant God who rules in righteousness and acts in steadfast love. The blessing of God rises from a heart that recognizes His goodness and deliverance, as in Psalm 103:1.

for He has heard
The Lord is not distant from His people’s distress. David’s confidence rests in God’s attentive care: the God of Israel receives the prayers of those who seek Him and answers according to His righteous purposes. The assurance anticipates the confidence believers have in approaching God through Christ (1 John 5:14–15).

my cry for mercy.
David had pleaded for God’s compassion amid danger and opposition, refusing to rely on his own strength. Mercy is sought by those who know their need and trust God’s character. Like Bartimaeus, who cried to Jesus for mercy as the Son of David (Mark 10:47), David looks to the Lord alone for help and salvation.

Psalm 28:7

The LORD is my strength and my shield;
David confesses that the covenant God is both the power that sustains him and the defender who stands between him and danger. Israel had seen the LORD reveal such strength in the exodus, when He delivered His people from Pharaoh’s army (Exodus 15:2). A shield was essential in battle, and David’s image declares that safety ultimately rests not in weapons, armies, or human alliances, but in the LORD.

Psa 18:1 For the choirmaster. Of David the servant of the LORD…

Gen 15:1 After these events, the word of the LORD came to Abram…

Psa 84:11 For the LORD God is a sun and a shield;…

my heart trusts in Him, and I am helped.
David’s confidence is personal and wholehearted. He does not merely acknowledge God’s power; he relies on God amid distress. The LORD answers such trust with real help, giving strength, deliverance, and sustaining grace. His faithfulness gives His people reason to obey the call to trust Him rather than their own understanding (Proverbs 3:5–6).

Psa 37:5 Commit your way to the LORD; trust in Him…

Isa 41:10 Do not fear, for I am with you; do not be afraid…

Heb 13:6 So we say with confidence: “The Lord is my helper;”…

Therefore my heart rejoices,
God’s help turns fear into gladness. David’s joy rises from the assurance that the LORD has heard and acted on behalf of His servant. Such rejoicing is deeper than changing circumstances, because it rests in God’s presence, protection, and unfailing character.

Psa 13:5 But I have trusted in Your loving devotion;…

Hab 3:18 yet I will exult in the LORD; I will rejoice…

and I give thanks to Him with my song.
Thanksgiving becomes worship as David publicly praises the God who has delivered him. Song had a central place in Israel’s worship, preserving God’s mighty works and calling His people to remember His faithfulness. Believers likewise give thanks through psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, proclaiming the grace they have received through the Lord.

Psalm 28:8

The LORD is the strength of His people,
Israel’s safety did not rest ultimately in military power, fortified cities, or alliances with neighboring nations, but in the covenant faithfulness of the LORD. David knew that victory over enemies and endurance in distress came from God’s sustaining power. The LORD supplies strength for His people in every calling, enabling faithfulness, courage, and perseverance when human resources fail. He remains the rock, fortress, and deliverer celebrated throughout the Psalms.

Psa 29:11 The LORD gives His people strength; the LORD blesses…

Psa 68:35 O God, You are awesome in Your sanctuary;…

Isa 40:29 He gives power to the faint and increases the strength…

a stronghold of salvation
God Himself is the secure refuge of those who belong to Him. As a stronghold protected inhabitants during invasion, the LORD preserves His people amid danger and delivers them from threats beyond their ability to overcome. His salvation includes rescue in present affliction and the greater redemption from sin and death accomplished through Jesus Christ. Believers therefore face spiritual conflict not in self-reliance, but in the strength God provides.

Psa 18:2 The LORD is my rock, my fortress, and my deliverer.…

2Sa 22:3 My God is my rock, in whom I take refuge…

for His anointed.
David, anointed by Samuel as Israel’s king, depended upon the LORD to establish and preserve his God-given office. The king’s welfare was inseparably connected to the welfare of the nation he represented. David’s anointing also directs attention to the greater Son of David, Jesus Christ (Christ means Anointed One in Greek), the promised Messiah and final Anointed King. In Him God has provided complete salvation, and through Him His people receive the security and strength promised by the LORD.

Psalm 28:9

Save Your people
David pleads for the Lord to deliver Israel from its enemies and preserve the nation He chose by covenant. God had repeatedly shown Himself to be Israel’s Savior, from the exodus out of Egypt to deliverance in times of war and oppression. Jesus Christ brings salvation to its fullest purpose by saving His people from their sins.

Jesus fulfills this image as the Good Shepherd who lays down His life for the sheep and leads them in the way of life.
