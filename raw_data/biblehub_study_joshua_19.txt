Simeon’s Inheritance

Joshua 19:1

The second lot came out for the clans of the tribe of Simeon:
Israel’s inheritance was apportioned by lot before the LORD, acknowledging His authority over the land He had promised to Abraham’s descendants. Simeon, Jacob’s second son by Leah, received its portion according to its clans, preserving both tribal identity and family inheritance.

Their inheritance lay within the territory of Judah
Simeon’s cities were assigned from Judah’s extensive southern territory, since Judah’s allotment proved larger than its population required (Joshua 19:9). The arrangement fulfilled Jacob’s prophecy that Simeon would be scattered in Israel (Genesis 49:7), yet it also provided the tribe with a secure place among God’s people. Simeon remained connected to Judah throughout Israel’s history and shared in the life and defense of the southern kingdom.

Bethul, meaning “house of God,” was another Simeonite town in the Negev, a dry region suited to herding and seasonal movement. Its identification is uncertain, though it may be connected with Bethuel or Chesil in parallel lists (Joshua 15:30; 1 Chronicles 4:30). Even small and now-unidentified towns stood within the covenant inheritance God gave His people.

Hormah,
Hormah, meaning “devotion” or “destruction,” recalled both Israel’s earlier defeat through presumption and its later victory through dependence upon the Lord. Israel had first been struck down there after refusing God’s command and then attempting battle without His presence (Numbers 14:40–45). Later, the Israelites vowed the Canaanite cities to destruction, and the Lord gave them victory (Numbers 21:1–3). Hormah therefore bore lasting witness that God’s promises are received through obedient faith, not human resolve.

Beth-marcaboth,
Beth-marcaboth, meaning “house of chariots,” suggests a place associated with military strength or chariotry. This name indicates the possible presence of a chariot station or a place where chariots were kept or manufactured. The mention of chariots is significant in the biblical context, as they were a symbol of military power and might, often associated with the Canaanites and later the Egyptians. The Israelites’ encounters with chariots, such as in the battle against Sisera (Judges 4:3), highlight the challenges they faced in conquering the land. The exact location of Beth-marcaboth is uncertain, but it is believed to be in the southern part of Judah’s territory.

Hazar-susah,
Hazar-susah, meaning “village of horses,” indicates a settlement associated with horse breeding or stabling. Horses were valuable assets in ancient warfare and transportation, and their presence in a settlement would signify wealth and strategic importance. The mention of horses connects to the broader biblical narrative where horses and chariots are often seen as symbols of earthly power, which the Israelites were cautioned against relying on instead of trusting in God (Psalm 20:7). The location of Hazar-susah is not definitively known, but it is thought to be in the southern region of Judah, similar to the other towns mentioned in this passage.

Simeon received no separate block of land alongside the other tribes but cities and surrounding pasturelands within Judah’s southern allotment. Yet the Lord did not abandon Simeon; He gave the tribe a recognized place in the Promised Land.

Zebulun’s Inheritance

Joshua 19:10

The third lot came up for the clans of the tribe of Zebulun:
The land was distributed before the Lord by lot, so Zebulun’s inheritance was received as God’s appointed portion rather than gained by human preference or power (Numbers 26:55–56; Joshua 18:6–10). Zebulun, Leah’s sixth son, received territory in northern Canaan among the tribes that would later be associated with Galilee.

The border of their inheritance stretched as far as Sarid.

Zebulun’s territory lay near productive valleys and important travel routes, providing opportunities consistent with Jacob’s blessing concerning commerce and access toward the coast (Genesis 49:13). In later generations, the region of Zebulun became closely connected with Galilee, where Jesus was raised and began His public ministry. Isaiah’s promise that light would shine upon the land of Zebulun found its fullest fulfillment in Christ’s ministry among the people of Galilee (Isaiah 9:1–2; Matthew 4:13–16).

Its productive fields and strategic location contributed to the tribe’s later importance in Israel’s history. Men of Issachar were recognized for understanding the times and knowing what Israel should do (1 Chronicles 12:32), demonstrating that God’s gifts to His people included both material provision and wisdom for faithful service.

When they had finished distributing the land into its territories,
The allotments, assigned by lot before the Lord at Shiloh, completed the major division of Canaan among Israel’s tribes. God’s promise to Abraham that his descendants would possess the land had come to visible fulfillment. Israel had moved from wilderness wandering into an ordered national life, with each tribe receiving a place of inheritance under God’s covenant.

the Israelites gave Joshua son of Nun an inheritance among them,
Joshua received his portion only after the people had been provided for, displaying the humility and faithfulness that marked his leadership. By the Lord’s command, he received Timnath-serah in Ephraim’s hill country and rebuilt the city for his home. Joshua’s name, Yeshua, means “the Lord is salvation,” and his selfless service anticipates Christ, who humbled Himself to secure an eternal inheritance for His people.

The people honored Joshua’s long and faithful service by granting him an inheritance after the tribes had received their portions. Timnath-serah, called Timnath-heres in Judges 2:9, became the home and later the burial place of Israel’s servant-leader.

in the hill country of Ephraim,
Joshua belonged to the tribe of Ephraim, and his inheritance lay within its rugged central territory. From this region, near Shiloh where the tabernacle stood, Israel’s life of worship and national order would be centered for generations.

Joshua restored Timnath-serah and made it his home, entering the rest God had promised after years of wilderness wandering and warfare. His settled inheritance foreshadows the lasting rest God gives His people through the greater Joshua, Jesus Christ.

Joshua 19:51

These are the inheritances
The allotted territories gave each tribe a permanent place within the land God had promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Israel received the land as God’s gift, not merely as the result of military conquest, and each inheritance carried the responsibility to live faithfully under the Lord’s covenant.

that Eleazar the priest, Joshua son of Nun, and the heads of the families
Eleazar represented the priestly authority established through Aaron, while Joshua carried the leadership entrusted to him by Moses. The tribal heads represented their clans and protected the interests of their people. Their united service joined worship, civil order, and tribal accountability under God’s authority.

distributed by lot to the tribes of Israel
The casting of lots placed the final determination in the Lord’s hands, preventing Israel’s strongest tribes or leaders from claiming territory by preference or influence. Each tribe received its appointed portion according to God’s purpose, while Levi received no tribal territory as its primary inheritance because the Lord and His priestly service were its inheritance.

at Shiloh
Shiloh became Israel’s central sanctuary after the land was subdued. The Tent of Meeting and the ark remained there for much of the period of the judges, making Shiloh the gathering place for national worship, judgment, and covenantal decisions. Its location in Ephraim’s territory also placed the sanctuary among the tribes settled in the central highlands.

before the LORD
The division of the land took place under the searching authority of the covenant God who had brought Israel out of Egypt and into Canaan. The inheritance belonged ultimately to Him, and Israel’s possession of it depended on obedient faith rather than military strength alone.

The entrance of the tabernacle was the appointed place where Israel drew near to God through priestly ministry. Conducting the allotments there declared that the nation’s boundaries, leadership, and future were to be ordered by the Lord’s presence.

The completion of the allotments marked the fulfillment of God’s long-standing promise to give Abraham’s descendants the land of Canaan. Israel had moved from wilderness wandering into settled life, yet each tribe still needed to drive out remaining enemies and guard its inheritance through continued obedience to the Lord.
