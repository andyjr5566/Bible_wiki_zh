A Remnant in Zion

Isaiah 4:1

In that day
Isaiah looks beyond the judgment announced against Jerusalem and Judah, when the Lord’s discipline would bring devastating loss. The immediate setting includes the fall of the city’s men in battle and the removal of its leaders, leaving society wounded and disordered.

Zec 14:9 On that day the LORD will become King over all the earth—…

Jol 3:18 And in that day the mountains will drip with sweet wine…

Hos 2:16 In that day,” declares the LORD, “you will call Me…

seven women will take hold of one man
War would so reduce the number of surviving men that women would urgently seek the protection and recognized standing of marriage. The picture fulfills the warning that Judah’s pride, injustice, and spiritual rebellion would bring consequences reaching into every household.

Isa 13:12 I will make man scarcer than pure gold,…

Deu 28:56-57 The most gentle and refined woman among you,…

Jer 31:22 How long will you wander, O faithless daughter?…

and say, “We will eat our own bread and provide our own clothes.
The women would not request the normal material support of a husband. Their willingness to bear their own expenses reveals the depth of the social crisis and their desire for the security associated with a marital household.

Gen 28:20 Then Jacob made a vow, saying, “If God will be with me…

Exo 21:10 If he takes another wife, he must not reduce the food,…

Mat 6:31 Therefore do not worry, saying, ‘What shall we eat?’…

Just let us be called by your name.
To bear a man’s name meant public identification with his family and the legal and social standing of marriage. Their request was not for romance or provision, but for the removal of public reproach.

Jer 14:9 Why are You like a man taken by surprise, …

Deu 28:10 Then all the peoples of the earth will see you are called…

2Ch 7:14 if My people who are called by My name humble themselves…

Take away our disgrace!”
In Israel, remaining unmarried and childless could bring deep sorrow and social shame. The scene portrays the outward devastation caused by divine judgment, while also exposing the greater disgrace of a people who had forsaken the Lord. God’s later promises of restoration show that He alone can remove the reproach produced by sin and bring lasting renewal to His people.

Isaiah 4:2

On that day
The Day of the LORD will bring both the cleansing described in Isaiah 4:3–4 and the restoration of Zion after judgment. God’s purposes for His people will not end in devastation; He will preserve a holy remnant and establish His promised kingdom.

Zec 14:9 On that day the LORD will become King over all the earth—…

Jol 2:31 The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood…

Isa 2:11-12 The proud look of man will be humbled…

the Branch of the LORD
The Branch points to the coming Messiah, the Davidic King promised in Isaiah and later identified more fully in Jeremiah 23:5 and Zechariah 3:8. Jesus Christ fulfills this hope as the righteous King whose life, reign, and salvation bring new life to His people.

Jer 23:5-6 Behold, the days are coming, declares the LORD,…

Jer 33:15 In those days and at that time I will cause to sprout…

Zec 6:12 And you are to tell him that this is what the LORD of Hosts…

will be beautiful and glorious,
Christ’s glory will be openly displayed after the humiliation and rejection associated with His first coming. His righteous reign will reveal the beauty of God’s holiness, justice, mercy, and faithfulness.

Isa 33:17 Your eyes will see the King in His beauty.…

Psa 50:2 From Zion, perfect in beauty, God shines forth.

Psa 96:6 Splendor and majesty are before Him; strength and beauty…

and the fruit of the land
The restored land of Israel will yield abundant provision under the Messiah’s blessing. Agricultural fruitfulness will testify that the curse and devastation brought by sin and judgment have given way to God’s favor and restoration.

Deu 8:7-10 For the LORD your God is bringing you into a good land,…

Jol 2:22-24 Do not be afraid, O beasts of the field,…

Amo 9:13 “Behold, the days are coming,” declares the LORD,…

will be the pride and glory of Israel’s survivors.
The survivors will be the faithful remnant preserved through judgment and brought into the blessings of God’s kingdom. Their honor will rest not in human achievement but in the presence, provision, and reign of the LORD.

Isa 37:31-32 And the surviving remnant of the house of Judah…

Jer 33:9 So this city will bring Me renown, joy, praise…

Zep 3:19-20 Behold, at that time, I will deal with all who afflict you.…

Isaiah 4:3

Whoever remains in Zion
The survivors of the Lord’s judgment will form a purified remnant in Jerusalem. Zion, the city of David and center of Israel’s worship, had become marked by pride, injustice, and moral corruption. Yet God would not abandon His covenant purposes. As in Isaiah 10:20–22, preservation would rest on God’s grace and faithfulness rather than on the strength of the nation.

Jol 2:32 And everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will be saved…

Oba 1:17 But on Mount Zion there will be deliverance…

Mic 4:7 And I will make the lame into a remnant, the outcast,…

and whoever is left in Jerusalem
Jerusalem would face devastation, but the Lord would preserve those appointed to remain. The immediate setting anticipates cleansing after judgment, while the larger prophetic horizon points toward Jerusalem’s future restoration under God’s rule. Survival is not presented as accidental; the Lord knows and keeps His people.

Isa 37:32 For a remnant will go forth from Jerusalem,…

Neh 11:1-2 Now the leaders of the people settled in Jerusalem,…

Zec 8:4-5 The LORD of Hosts says: “Old men and old women…”…

will be called holy—
Those preserved will be set apart for the Lord. Isaiah joins deliverance with purification: the inhabitants of restored Zion are not merely spared from destruction but cleansed from the corruption that brought judgment. Isaiah 4:4 identifies the means of this cleansing as the Lord’s washing and burning work. God’s dwelling among His people requires holiness, a truth fulfilled ultimately in the redeemed people who belong to Christ.

Isa 62:12 And they will be called the Holy People,…

Exo 19:6 And unto Me you shall be a kingdom of priests…

1Pe 2:9 But you are a chosen people, a royal priesthood…

all in Jerusalem who are recorded among the living—
God’s record distinguishes those whom He preserves for life from those removed in judgment. Scripture elsewhere speaks of names written in God’s book as belonging to those who are His, especially in Daniel 12:1 and Revelation 20:12–15. The Lord’s knowledge of His people is personal and secure; those He appoints for life will share in the holy community He establishes.

Psa 69:28 May they be blotted out of the Book of Life…

Rev 3:5 Like them, he who overcomes will be dressed in white.…

Dan 12:1 At that time Michael, the great prince who stands watch…

Isaiah 4:4

when the Lord has washed away the filth of the daughters of Zion
The Lord will cleanse Jerusalem’s inhabitants from the moral uncleanness that had defiled the city. The “daughters of Zion” portray the people of Jerusalem, especially those humbled by the judgments described in the preceding chapter. Washing depicts God’s removal of sin and shame, restoring a people who cannot cleanse themselves. David’s plea, “Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity” (Psalm 51:2), reflects the same need for divine mercy.

Isa 1:16 Wash and cleanse yourselves. Remove your evil deeds…

Zec 13:1 On that day a fountain will be opened to the house of David,…

Ezk 36:25 I will also sprinkle clean water on you,…

and cleansed the bloodstains from the heart of Jerusalem
Jerusalem’s bloodguilt included violence, oppression, and the shedding of innocent blood. Though chosen as the place of God’s temple and worship, the city had repeatedly rebelled against Him. The Lord’s cleansing reaches the center of the nation’s life, removing guilt and establishing righteousness. The sacrifices of the Law foreshadowed the final cleansing accomplished through the blood of Christ, which cleanses believers from all sin (1 John 1:7).

Jer 4:14 Wash the evil from your heart, O Jerusalem,…

Lam 4:13-14 But this was for the sins of her prophets…

Ezk 22:2-4 “As for you, son of man, will you judge her?”…

by a spirit of judgment
God’s judgment exposes sin, brings rebellion to account, and separates the unrepentant from the remnant He preserves. His discipline is holy and purposeful, preparing His people for restoration. The judgments upon Jerusalem would not overturn God’s covenant purposes but would purge the city for the future glory promised in Isaiah 4:2–6.

Isa 28:6 a spirit of justice to him who sits in judgment…

Isa 11:3-4 And He will delight in the fear of the LORD.…

Jhn 16:8 And when He comes, He will convict the world…

and a spirit of fire.
Fire portrays the consuming holiness of God, which destroys impurity as metal is refined in a furnace. The Lord’s presence cannot coexist with persistent corruption. His cleansing work anticipates the transforming ministry of the Holy Spirit and the holiness required of those who belong to Christ.

Mat 3:11 I baptize you with water for repentance…

Luk 3:16 John answered all of them: “I baptize you with water”…

Act 2:3-4 They saw tongues like flames of fire that separated…

Isaiah 4:5

Then the LORD will create over all of Mount Zion
After cleansing His people and establishing the Branch of the LORD in glory, the LORD promises a new manifestation of His presence in Jerusalem. Mount Zion, the former Jebusite stronghold captured by David, became the city of David and the site of the temple. The language of creation points to God’s sovereign work in restoring His holy people and dwelling among them.

Psa 132:13-14 For the LORD has chosen Zion; He has desired it…

Psa 48:1-2 A Psalm of the sons of Korah. Great is the LORD…

Jol 3:17 Then you will know that I am the LORD your God,…

and over her assemblies
God’s protection will rest not only on the city itself but on every gathering of His worshiping people. The redeemed community will no longer be marked by the sin and defilement described earlier in the chapter, but by the Lord’s holy presence and covenant care.

Lev 23:2 “Speak to the Israelites and say to them,…

Exo 12:16 On the first day you are to hold a sacred assembly,…

Deu 31:12-13 Assemble the people—men, women, children…

a cloud of smoke by day and a glowing flame of fire by night.
The cloud and fire recall the LORD’s visible guidance of Israel in the wilderness after the Exodus (Exodus 13:21–22). As He led and guarded the nation on its journey from Egypt, He will again make His presence known among His restored people. The imagery also anticipates the day when God dwells permanently with His people in the New Jerusalem (Revelation 21:3, 23).

Exo 13:21-22 And the LORD went before them in a pillar of cloud…

Num 9:15-16 On the day that the tabernacle, the Tent of the Testimony,…

Exo 40:38 For the cloud of the LORD was over the tabernacle by day,…

For over all the glory there will be a canopy,
The divine glory will not expose God’s people to judgment but will become their covering and shelter. Like the protective cloud over Israel and the covering associated with covenant joy, the canopy portrays the security, peace, and nearness of God’s kingdom.

Exo 40:34-35 Then the cloud covered the Tent of Meeting,…

Psa 18:11 He made darkness His hiding place, and storm clouds a canopy…

Psa 27:5 For in the day of trouble He will hide me in His shelter;…

Isaiah 4:6

a shelter to give shade from the heat by day,
Zion’s restored holiness includes the Lord’s visible protection over His people. The shelter recalls the pillar of cloud that accompanied Israel through the wilderness, providing guidance and covering from the desert sun (Exodus 13:21–22). God’s presence, which had dwelt above the tabernacle and later the temple, will again rest over Mount Zion and its assemblies (Isaiah 4:5). The promised shade portrays more than relief from physical heat; it reveals the security of a people cleansed from sin and gathered under the care of their covenant God. Jesus Christ invites the weary to find rest in Him, fulfilling God’s saving purpose for all who come in faith (Matthew 11:28–30).

Isa 25:4 For You have been a refuge for the poor…

Psa 121:5-6 The LORD is your keeper; the LORD is the shade…

Psa 91:1 He who dwells in the shelter of the Most High…

and a refuge and hiding place from the storm and the rain.
Storm and rain often portray judgment, danger, and the hardships that expose human weakness. The Lord Himself will be the secure dwelling place of the remnant who remain in Zion after His purifying judgment. Israel’s history repeatedly showed that safety was found not in walls, armies, or alliances, but in trusting the Lord (Psalm 46:1; Isaiah 28:16). The protection promised for Zion anticipates the final peace of God’s kingdom, when His people will dwell under His rule without fear. Christ is the refuge from divine judgment and the faithful protector of His people amid every trial.

Isa 32:2 Each will be like a shelter from the wind…

Psa 55:8 I would hurry to my shelter, far from this raging tempest.”
