Belshazzar’s Feast

Daniel 5:1

Later, King Belshazzar
Belshazzar, whose name means “Bel, protect the king,” was the son of Nabonidus, Babylon’s last reigning king, and served as co-regent during his father’s lengthy absences from the capital. His offer to make Daniel the “third ruler” in the kingdom later in the chapter reflects this arrangement: Nabonidus was first, Belshazzar second, and Daniel could be elevated to third. Daniel calls Nebuchadnezzar Belshazzar’s “father” in the sense of royal predecessor or ancestor, emphasizing the continuity of Babylon’s accountability before God. Belshazzar inherited a mighty kingdom, but he did not humble himself under the lessons God had already taught Nebuchadnezzar.

The feast displayed Babylon’s wealth, confidence, and political strength while the Medo-Persian forces threatened the city. Babylon’s massive walls and stored provisions may have encouraged Belshazzar and his nobles to treat danger with contempt. What appeared to be a demonstration of security became a setting for judgment. Human power, military defenses, and public celebration cannot protect a kingdom that defies the God who rules over kings.

Belshazzar
Belshazzar had known how God humbled Nebuchadnezzar, yet he exalted himself against the Lord of heaven by profaning the vessels taken from God’s temple. As co-regent under his father Nabonidus, he ruled in Babylon during its final days. His death revealed the fatal consequence of rejecting known truth and treating holy things with contempt.

Daniel 5:31

and Darius the Mede
Darius the Mede assumed authority over Babylon after its fall to the Medo-Persian forces in 539 B.C. His precise identification remains uncertain, though he may have governed Babylon under Cyrus the Great. Daniel presents him as a real ruler through whom God transferred dominion from Babylon to the Medes and Persians, fulfilling the interpretation of Nebuchadnezzar’s dream that another kingdom would arise after Babylon (Daniel 2:39).

received the kingdom
Babylon did not fall beyond God’s control; He had announced its judgment through Isaiah and Jeremiah because of its pride, violence, and idolatry (Isaiah 13:17–22; Jeremiah 51:24–26). The kingdom was handed over in a single night, confirming that God removes kings and establishes kings according to His purpose (Daniel 2:21). Darius’s rule also prepares for Daniel’s continued witness before a new empire, showing that the Lord’s servant remained secure even when earthly governments changed.
