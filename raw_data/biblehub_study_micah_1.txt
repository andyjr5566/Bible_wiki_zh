Judgment to Come

Micah 1:1

This is the word of the LORD
Micah’s message came by divine revelation, not human insight or political observation. The Lord spoke through His prophet with the same authority seen in the ministries of Isaiah, Jeremiah, and the other prophets. God’s word exposes sin, announces judgment, and preserves hope for those who return to Him.

that came to Micah the Moreshite
Micah came from Moresheth, a town in Judah’s Shephelah, the lowland region between the Judean hill country and the Philistine plain. His rural background placed him near communities vulnerable to exploitation by powerful landowners and officials. His concern for justice, covenant faithfulness, and the oppressed reflects the conditions he witnessed in Judah.

in the days of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah—
Micah ministered during a period of spiritual decline, international danger, and later reform in Judah. Jotham generally did what was right, though idolatrous worship continued among the people. Ahaz led Judah into deep apostasy and sought security through alliance with Assyria. Hezekiah restored proper worship of the Lord, yet the nation’s underlying sins and the warnings of judgment remained serious. Micah’s ministry overlapped with Isaiah’s and extended through the years leading to Assyria’s destruction of the northern kingdom.

what he saw regarding Samaria and Jerusalem:
God gave Micah a prophetic vision concerning Samaria, capital of Israel, and Jerusalem, capital of Judah. Both cities represented the political and religious leadership of the divided covenant people, and both had become centers of idolatry, injustice, and rebellion against the Lord. Samaria fell to Assyria in 722 BC, confirming God’s judgment, while Jerusalem would later face Babylonian destruction because Judah persisted in sin. Yet Micah’s prophecies also point beyond judgment to the Lord’s future restoration of His people and the coming reign of the Messiah.

Assyrian conquest brought captivity and scattering to many in the land, demonstrating the reality of God’s covenant warnings. Exile meant removal from family, inheritance, worship, and the familiar places God had given Israel. Yet judgment would not be God’s final word; Micah would later proclaim the gathering and restoration of God’s remnant under the coming Ruler from Bethlehem.
