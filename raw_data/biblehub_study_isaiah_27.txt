The LORD’s Vineyard

Isaiah 27:2

In that day:
Isaiah looks beyond judgment to the day when the Lord will defeat His enemies, remove evil, and restore His people. The surrounding prophecy includes the punishment of Leviathan, portraying God’s final victory over the powers that oppose His rule. Israel’s restoration will follow divine judgment, anticipating the kingdom blessings promised throughout Isaiah and fulfilled under the reign of the Messiah.

Isa 12:1 In that day you will say: “O LORD, I will praise You.…

Isa 25:9 And in that day it will be said, “Surely this is our God;…

Zec 14:9 On that day the LORD will become King over all the earth—…

“Sing about a fruitful vineyard.
Israel, once compared to a vineyard producing only wild grapes and facing desolation (Isaiah 5:1–7), will become fruitful under the Lord’s faithful care. The song replaces mourning with praise because God Himself guards, waters, and preserves His people (Isaiah 27:3). The restored vineyard points to Israel’s future renewal and also displays the life that comes from abiding in Christ, the true vine, through whom His people bear lasting fruit (John 15:1–5).

Isaiah 27:3

I, the LORD, am its keeper;
The vineyard depicts restored Israel, which will take root, blossom, and fill the world with fruit (Isaiah 27:6). Unlike the neglected vineyard of Isaiah 5, God Himself assumes its care. His covenant commitment does not rest on Israel’s strength but on His faithful purpose to preserve and restore His people.

Psa 17:8 Keep me as the apple of Your eye; hide me in the shadow…

I water it continually.
God supplies all that His people need for life, growth, and fruitfulness. In a land where water meant survival, continual watering portrayed unfailing provision. The Lord nourishes His people through His word, His Spirit, and His sustaining grace, giving living water that does not fail (John 4:14).

Psa 65:9-10 You attend to the earth and water it; with abundance…

Ezk 34:26 I will make them and the places around My hill a blessing.…

Deu 11:11-12 But the land that you are crossing the Jordan to possess…

I guard it night and day
God watches over His people without interruption. He neither slumbers nor sleeps, and His protection extends through every danger, visible and unseen (Psalm 121:3–4). The Lord’s care includes discipline when needed, yet His discipline serves restoration rather than abandonment.

Psa 121:3-4 He will not allow your foot to slip; your Protector…

Psa 91:5-6 You will not fear the terror of the night,…

so no one can disturb it;
No enemy can finally overthrow those whom God keeps. Nations may oppose Israel, and spiritual forces may assault God’s people, but the Lord remains sovereign over every threat. The promise points beyond temporary deliverance to the secure peace of God’s coming kingdom, where His people will dwell safely in His presence.

Isaiah 27:4

I am not angry.
God’s wrath against His covenant people is not His final purpose. After announcing judgment, Isaiah reveals the Lord’s settled intention to preserve and restore His vineyard, Israel. His mercy does not ignore sin, but provides a way for repentance, cleansing, and renewed fellowship. The promise anticipates the peace secured through the Lord’s saving work, when those who return to Him find forgiveness rather than condemnation.

Isa 54:9 For to Me this is like the days of Noah,…

Jer 3:12 Go, proclaim this message toward the north:…

Mic 7:18 Who is a God like You, who pardons iniquity…

If only thorns and briers confronted Me,
Thorns and briers recall the curse upon the ground after Adam’s sin and picture all that resists God’s holy rule. They represent open rebellion, corrupting influences, and hostile powers that threaten the fruitfulness of God’s people. Unlike the vineyard itself, which the Lord tends and protects, thorns and briers have no place in His restored land.

Jos 23:13 know for sure that the LORD your God will no longer…

Num 33:55 But if you do not drive out the inhabitants of the land…

Isa 5:6 I will make it a wasteland, neither pruned nor cultivated…

I would march and trample them,
The Lord stands as the divine Warrior, fully able to overthrow every enemy of His purposes. No nation, idol, ruler, or rebellious force can prevent His plan to preserve a holy people. God’s protection of His vineyard includes His decisive opposition to whatever seeks to destroy it.

Deu 33:29 Blessed are you, O Israel! Who is like you?…

Psa 60:12 With God we will perform with valor, and He will…

Mal 4:3 Then you will trample the wicked, for they will be ashes…

I would burn them to the ground.
Fire portrays the completeness of divine judgment against persistent rebellion. God will remove what is evil rather than allow it to remain among His people. The judgment of thorns clears the way for the vineyard to flourish, pointing to the coming restoration in which God’s people will bear fruit for His glory.

Isa 33:12 The peoples will be burned to ashes, like thorns cut down…

Psa 118:12 They swarmed around me like bees, but they were extinguished…

Isaiah 27:5

Or let them lay claim to My protection;
The Lord offers refuge even to those who stand exposed to His judgment. Within Isaiah’s vineyard song, God guards His restored people while confronting all that threatens their fruitfulness. Those who would otherwise be consumed as thorns and briers may instead take hold of His strength and receive His protection. Israel’s history testified that safety did not rest in alliances, armies, or surrounding nations, but in faithful dependence upon the covenant God.

Psa 18:2 The LORD is my rock, my fortress, and my deliverer.…

Psa 91:2 I will say to the LORD, “You are my refuge and my fortress…

Pro 18:10 The name of the LORD is a strong tower;…

let them make peace with Me—
Peace requires the removal of hostility created by sin, rebellion, and idolatry. God’s invitation calls for repentance and submission rather than continued resistance. He does not delight in destruction but opens a way for those under judgment to be reconciled to Him. The covenant Lord who disciplined Israel also promised restoration to those who returned to Him.

Job 22:21 Reconcile now and be at peace with Him;…

Rom 5:1 Therefore, since we have been justified through faith…

yes, let them make peace with Me.”
The repeated appeal reveals both the certainty of God’s offer and the urgency of responding to it. God Himself provides the ground of reconciliation, fulfilled ultimately through Jesus Christ, whose death brings peace with God to all who believe. Those who take refuge in Him are no longer His enemies but belong to His protected and fruitful people.

2Co 5:18-20 All this is from God, who reconciled us to Himself…

Eph 2:14-16 For He Himself is our peace, who has made the two one…

Isaiah 27:6

In the days to come,
God points beyond Israel’s present discipline to a future restoration. The promised renewal belongs to the larger pattern of prophetic hope: the Lord will preserve His covenant people, restore them under His righteous King, and display His faithfulness before the nations.

Jer 30:24 The fierce anger of the LORD will not turn back…

Hos 3:5 Afterward, the people of Israel will return…

Jacob will take root.
Israel, descended from Jacob, will be firmly established rather than repeatedly uprooted by judgment, exile, and invasion. The image recalls God’s covenant promises to the patriarchs concerning the land, descendants, and enduring blessing.

Isa 37:31 And the surviving remnant of the house of Judah…

Psa 80:8-11 You uprooted a vine from Egypt; You drove out the nations…

Jer 17:7-8 But blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD,…

Israel will bud and blossom
The nation will move from barrenness and chastening to visible life, strength, and fruitfulness. Isaiah elsewhere joins such renewal to the reign of the Messiah and to the transformation of creation under God’s saving rule.

Hos 14:5-7 I will be like the dew to Israel; he will blossom…

Num 17:8 The next day Moses entered the Tent of the Testimony…

Isa 35:1-2 The wilderness and the dry land will be glad;…

and fill the whole world with fruit.
Israel’s restoration will serve God’s purpose of blessing the nations, fulfilling the promise that through Abraham’s offspring all peoples of the earth would be blessed. The spiritual fruit of God’s redemption will extend outward to the whole world, ultimately centered in the Messiah, the true King of Israel and Savior of the nations.

Isaiah 27:7

Has the LORD struck Israel as He struck her oppressors?
Israel’s discipline was severe because her idolatry and rebellion violated the covenant, yet the Lord did not abandon the people He had chosen. Assyria and Babylon were permitted to afflict Israel, but they exceeded their appointed role in pride and cruelty and later came under God’s judgment. The Lord’s purpose in chastening Israel was to remove sin and preserve a remnant, not to erase Jacob from the earth. Jeremiah 30:11 confirms that God would correct Israel in measure while making a full end of the nations among whom she was scattered.

Jer 46:28 And you, My servant Jacob, do not be afraid…

Jer 30:11 For I am with you to save you, declares the LORD.…

Psa 78:38 And yet He was compassionate; He forgave their iniquity…

Was she killed like those who slayed her?
Israel endured invasion, exile, and great loss, but her national existence was not extinguished as were many of the powers that oppressed her. Egypt, Assyria, and Babylon each stood under divine accountability for their violence and arrogance. God’s covenant faithfulness ensured that judgment would lead ultimately to restoration, gathering, and renewed blessing. Isaiah’s message points beyond temporary chastisement to the day when Israel will be restored and the Lord’s righteous rule will be openly displayed.

Isaiah 27:8

God disciplined Israel for covenant unfaithfulness, using invading armies and removal from the land rather than destroying the nation completely. The Assyrian and Babylonian exiles displayed the seriousness of idolatry and injustice, yet preserved a remnant through whom God would fulfill His promises.

The scorching east wind pictures desolation and loss, fitting the devastation brought by invasion and exile. Yet the judgment described in Isaiah 27 leads toward the removal of guilt and the restoration of worship, ultimately fulfilled in the saving work of Christ.

Isaiah 27:9

Therefore Jacob’s guilt will be atoned for,
Jacob represents the covenant people of Israel. God’s judgment would not end in their destruction but would serve His purpose of cleansing and restoration. The sacrificial system taught that sin required atonement through God’s appointed provision, ultimately pointing to Christ, who secured eternal redemption by His own blood.

Isaiah 27:10

Human strength, walls, and military preparation cannot preserve a people under God’s judgment. Jerusalem’s later devastation under Babylon displayed the truth Isaiah proclaimed: covenant unfaithfulness brought ruin even upon a city associated with God’s temple.

Isaiah 27:12

In that day
Isaiah looks ahead to the appointed time when the LORD intervenes decisively to judge evil, restore His people, and fulfill His covenant purposes. The promised restoration follows the judgment described throughout Isaiah and points beyond Israel’s immediate historical circumstances to God’s final kingdom.

Isa 11:10-11 On that day the Root of Jesse will stand as a banner…

Jer 30:7-8 How awful that day will be! None will be like it!…

the LORD will thresh
The LORD is pictured as a farmer separating valuable grain from what must be discarded. His judgment is never careless; He knows those who belong to Him and removes what corrupts and opposes His holy purposes. The image also conveys purification, for the remnant of Israel will be brought through discipline into renewed fellowship with their God.
