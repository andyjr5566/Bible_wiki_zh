Pharaoh Necho II removed Jehoahaz, Josiah’s son, after his brief three-month reign in 609 BC. Judah had become caught between Egypt and Babylon as the old Assyrian empire collapsed. Necho’s authority over Jerusalem revealed Judah’s weakened condition following Josiah’s death and fulfilled covenant warnings that persistent unfaithfulness would bring foreign domination. Necho carried Jehoahaz to Egypt, where he died, and installed Eliakim, renaming him Jehoiakim, as Judah’s tributary king.

Zedekiah Reigns in Judah

2 Chronicles 36:11

Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king,
Originally named Mattaniah, Zedekiah was a son of Josiah and the uncle of Jehoiachin. Nebuchadnezzar II installed him as Babylon’s vassal after removing Jehoiachin in 597 BC and changed his name, demonstrating Babylon’s authority over Judah. Yet the name Zedekiah, meaning “The LORD is righteousness,” stood as a witness against the king’s later unfaithfulness to the God whose name he bore. His appointment came during Judah’s final years, when the nation needed humble obedience rather than political maneuvering.

World empires changed rulers, yet the Lord governed their course for the preservation of His covenant people.
