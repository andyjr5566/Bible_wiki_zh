Jeremiah 46:2

concerning Egypt and the army of Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt,
Egypt had long been a major power affecting the life of Judah. Pharaoh Neco II reigned from 610 to 595 BC and sought to extend Egyptian influence northward after the collapse of Assyria. He killed King Josiah at Megiddo in 609 BC (2 Kings 23:29–30), an event that deeply wounded Judah and accelerated its political decline. The Lord’s message addresses not merely a foreign army but a proud kingdom and its ruler, showing that kings and nations stand accountable before Him.

2Ki 23:29-30 During Josiah’s reign, Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt…

which was defeated at Carchemish on the Euphrates River
Carchemish was a fortified strategic city on the Euphrates, controlling a major route between Mesopotamia, Syria, and the Mediterranean coast. Neco marched toward Carchemish during the events surrounding Josiah’s death (2 Chronicles 35:20–24), but Egypt’s effort to secure control of the region ended in decisive defeat. The battle in 605 BC broke Egyptian power in Syria-Palestine and removed the protection Judah had hoped to find through alliance with Egypt.

2Ch 35:20 After all this, when Josiah had set the temple in order,…

by Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon
Nebuchadnezzar II gained renown through this victory and began Babylon’s rise as the dominant empire of the ancient Near East. His conquest of Egypt’s forces at Carchemish opened the way for Babylonian control over Judah and the surrounding nations. Nebuchadnezzar would later besiege Jerusalem, carry Judah into exile, and destroy the temple in 586 BC (2 Kings 25:1–21). His military success was not independent of God’s rule, for the Lord used Babylon as an instrument of judgment against persistent rebellion.

The defeat at Carchemish exposed the futility of trusting earthly strength and marked the beginning of Judah’s subjection to Babylon.
