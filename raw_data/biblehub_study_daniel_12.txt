Daniel 12:2

And many who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake,
Daniel looks beyond Israel’s immediate deliverance to the final resurrection, when God will summon the dead from the grave. Death is pictured as sleep because it is not the final condition of God’s creatures; those whose bodies return to dust, as Genesis 3:19 declares, will be raised by divine power. Isaiah 26:19 anticipates the same hope, and Jesus confirms that all who are in the tombs will hear His voice and come out (John 5:28–29). The resurrection will involve real persons whose bodies are restored for God’s appointed judgment.

Isa 26:19 Your dead will live; their bodies will rise.…

Jhn 5:28-29 Do not be amazed at this, for the hour is coming…

some to everlasting life,
God will raise the righteous into unending fellowship with Him. Everlasting life is not merely continued existence but the full enjoyment of God’s kingdom, holiness, and peace. The promise finds its fulfillment in Jesus Christ, whose resurrection guarantees the resurrection of all who belong to Him (1 Corinthians 15:20–23). Faith in the Son brings the assurance that death cannot overturn God’s saving purpose (John 3:16).

God’s resurrection judgment also reveals the final destiny of those who reject His rule. Shame exposes sin before the holy Judge, and everlasting contempt describes an irreversible condition of judgment and exclusion from God’s blessing. Jesus likewise speaks of eternal punishment alongside eternal life (Matthew 25:46), and Revelation portrays the final judgment before God’s throne (Revelation 20:11–15). Daniel’s vision establishes that human history ends not in oblivion but in resurrection, justice, and eternal accountability.

Daniel 12:4

But you, Daniel, shut up these words and seal the book until the time of the end.
God commanded Daniel (“God is my judge”) to preserve the revelation without expecting its full fulfillment or understanding in his own generation. Sealed documents protected their contents and confirmed their authority until the proper time for opening. The prophecy concerns the appointed end of human history, when God will judge evil, deliver His people, and establish His kingdom. Revelation portrays a related image in the sealed scroll that only the Lamb is worthy to open, showing that Jesus Christ (“Yahweh saves,” “Anointed One”) directs the fulfillment of God’s purposes.

Daniel 12:11

And from the time the daily sacrifice is abolished
The removal of the regular morning and evening offerings brought a devastating interruption to the worship God had appointed for Israel (Exodus 29:38–42). Antiochus IV Epiphanes halted the temple sacrifices in 167 BC during his persecution of the Jewish people. That historical crisis foreshadowed a greater future desecration connected with Israel’s final time of distress.

Dan 8:11-12 It magnified itself, even to the Prince of the host;…

and the abomination of desolation set up,
Antiochus defiled the temple by establishing pagan worship, including an altar associated with Zeus. His actions revealed the hatred of idolatry toward the true God and His covenant people. Jesus later identified the abomination of desolation spoken of by Daniel as still possessing future significance, warning those in Judea to flee when it appears in the holy place (Matthew 24:15–21). The final fulfillment will involve a blasphemous ruler who exalts himself against God and profanes what is holy (2 Thessalonians 2:3–4).

The additional days point to events surrounding the defeat of evil, the cleansing of desecration, and the establishment of God’s righteous rule.
