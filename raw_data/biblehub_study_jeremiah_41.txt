Jeremiah 41:3

Ishmael also killed all the Jews who were with Gedaliah at Mizpah,
Ishmael’s slaughter extended beyond Gedaliah to the Judeans gathered under his protection. Gedaliah had encouraged the remnant to submit to Babylon and cultivate the land in peace, in keeping with Jeremiah’s message that Babylonian rule was God’s discipline for Judah’s sin. By murdering these fellow Jews, Ishmael destroyed the small community through which restoration and stability might have begun. His royal lineage did not excuse his violence; instead, it displayed the continuing corruption and rivalry that had helped bring Judah to ruin.

near Bethlehem,
The location stood near David’s hometown and within the territory of Judah, linking the distressed remnant with the Davidic heritage of the land. Bethlehem had long been a recognized and settled community, making it a natural gathering point before a dangerous journey. The town would later be identified by Micah as the birthplace of the promised Ruler and by Matthew as the birthplace of Jesus Christ (Micah 5:2; Matthew 2:1).

in order to proceed into Egypt
The group intended to flee Egypt because they feared the Babylonians would hold them responsible for Ishmael’s assassination of Gedaliah. Egypt had often served as a refuge during famine, political danger, and war, yet it also represented the nation from which God had delivered Israel. Their plan revealed reliance on human protection rather than confidence in the Lord’s care. God would soon command the remnant to remain in Judah and warn that seeking safety in Egypt would bring the very judgment they feared (Jeremiah 42:10–16).
