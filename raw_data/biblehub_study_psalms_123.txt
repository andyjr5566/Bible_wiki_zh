I Lift Up My Eyes to You

Psalm 123:1

A song of ascents.
Psalms 120–134 formed a collection associated with Israel’s worship as pilgrims went up to Jerusalem for the feasts commanded by the Law. Jerusalem’s elevation and the temple’s central place in Israel’s life pictured a greater movement of faith toward the presence of God.

Psa 120:1 A song of ascents. In my distress I cried to the LORD,…

Psa 122:1 I was glad when they said to me, “Let us go…

Psa 134:1 A song of ascents. Come, bless the LORD, all you servants…

I lift up my eyes to You,
The worshiper turns from human contempt and helplessness to the Lord for mercy and deliverance. Such dependence recalls Psalm 121:1–2, where help comes from the Maker of heaven and earth.

Psa 121:1 A song of ascents. I lift up my eyes to the hills.…

Psa 141:8 But my eyes are fixed on You, O GOD the Lord.…

Psa 25:15 My eyes are always on the LORD, for He will…

the One enthroned in heaven.
God rules over all nations from His heavenly throne, though He made His name dwell in the temple at Jerusalem. His reign surpasses every earthly power and assures His people that their cries are heard. Isaiah saw the Lord high and exalted upon His throne, and the New Testament reveals the risen Christ seated at the Father’s right hand, reigning and interceding for His people.

Psa 103:19 The LORD has established His throne in heaven…

Psa 2:4 The One enthroned in heaven laughs; the Lord taunts them.

Psa 11:4 The LORD is in His holy temple; the LORD is on His throne…

Psalm 123:2

As the eyes of servants look to the hand of their master,
Servants watched their master’s hand for direction, provision, and protection, ready to respond to the slightest command. The image portrays humble attentiveness rather than passive resignation. God’s people acknowledge that their welfare rests in the Lord’s wise and sovereign care.

Psa 145:15 The eyes of all look to You, and You give them…

Psa 104:27-28 All creatures look to You to give them their food…

Luk 12:37-38 Blessed are those servants whom the master finds on watch.…

as the eyes of a maidservant look to the hand of her mistress,
A maidservant likewise remained alert to the needs and instructions of the household. The parallel includes all the worshiping community in a shared posture of dependence. Neither status nor strength removes the need to wait upon God.

Pro 30:23 an unloved woman who marries, and a maidservant…

Luk 1:48 For He has looked with favor on the humble state…

so our eyes are on the LORD our God
Israel turns from human masters to the covenant Lord, who alone possesses ultimate authority and mercy. The plural language joins the pilgrims in a corporate appeal, fitting the Psalms of Ascents sung by worshipers coming before the Lord. Believers also look to Christ in obedient faith, for He humbled Himself as a servant and now reigns as Lord.

2Ch 20:12 Our God, will You not judge them? We are powerless…

Psa 25:15 My eyes are always on the LORD, for He will…

Psa 141:8 But my eyes are fixed on You, O GOD the Lord.…

until He shows us mercy.
The people wait with confidence because God’s mercy flows from His covenant faithfulness. Their need is real, but their hope is not placed in human relief or changing circumstances. God’s mercy finds its fullest display in the saving work of Jesus Christ, who delivers sinners from judgment and brings them near to God.

Isa 30:18 Therefore the LORD longs to be gracious to you;…

Psa 130:7-8 O Israel, put your hope in the LORD, for with the LORD…

Lam 3:25-26 The LORD is good to those who wait for Him,…

Psalm 123:3

Have mercy on us, O LORD, have mercy,
The repeated cry rises from a people who know that relief cannot be secured by human strength. Israel appeals to YHWH, the covenant-keeping God who had bound Himself to His people and repeatedly shown compassion toward the undeserving. David voiced a similar appeal after his sin in Psalm 51, grounding his hope in God’s abundant mercy. The prayer also anticipates Christ, through whom God’s mercy was finally and fully displayed in His death and resurrection.

Mat 20:30-31 And there were two blind men sitting beside the road.…

Luk 17:13 and raised their voices, shouting, “Jesus, Master”…

Mat 9:27 As Jesus went on from there, two blind men…

for we have endured much contempt.
The worshipers had borne prolonged scorn from those who lived at ease and trusted in their own power, as the following verse explains. Israel often faced such ridicule from hostile nations that mocked the Lord and questioned His faithfulness, especially in seasons of oppression and exile. The suffering also foreshadows the contempt endured by the Servant in Isaiah 53 and by Jesus, who was mocked, rejected, and crucified. God’s people may endure reproach for trusting Him, yet their hope rests in His righteous vindication.

Psa 44:13-14 You have made us a reproach to our neighbors…

Psa 79:4 We have become a reproach to our neighbors,…

Lam 3:45 You have made us scum and refuse among the nations.

Psalm 123:4

We have endured much scorn from the arrogant,
God’s people had long suffered the ridicule of nations and rulers who trusted in military power, wealth, and human wisdom. During exile and oppression, Israel’s enemies treated the covenant people as abandoned and powerless. Nehemiah faced similar mockery from Sanballat and Tobiah while rebuilding Jerusalem’s walls (Nehemiah 4:1–3). Such scorn tested Israel’s faith but also drove the faithful to lift their eyes to the Lord for mercy.

Psa 119:51 The arrogant utterly deride me, but I do not turn…

Psa 73:8 They mock and speak with malice; with arrogance…

Pro 1:22 “How long, O simple ones, will you love your simple ways?”…

much contempt from the proud.
The proud despise those who depend upon God because they rely on themselves and reject His authority. Scripture repeatedly warns that pride precedes destruction, while God gives grace to the humble (Proverbs 16:18; James 4:6). Israel’s suffering anticipated the rejection endured by Christ, who was despised by religious leaders and Roman authorities yet vindicated by the Father. Believers may endure contempt without surrendering hope, knowing that God sees injustice and will judge the proud in righteousness.

Psa 31:18 May lying lips be silenced—lips that speak…

Pro 21:24 Mocker is the name of the proud and arrogant man…

Pro 3:34 He mocks the mockers, but gives grace to the humble.
