Sing to the LORD a New Song

Psalm 98:1

A Psalm.
Psalm (a song sung to the accompaniment of a harp or lyre) 98 summons the covenant people to public worship because the Lord has revealed His saving power. Israel’s praise rests on God’s acts in history, not on human achievement. The psalm also looks beyond Israel, calling all the earth to rejoice in the righteous King.

Sing to the LORD a new song,
A new work of salvation calls forth renewed praise. God’s people are not confined to memories of former deliverance; they answer His present faithfulness with worship shaped by what He has done. The same summons appears in Psalms 33 and 96 and anticipates the redeemed singing before the Lamb in Revelation 5:9.

The Lord’s wonders include His creation, His judgments, His deliverance of Israel from Egypt, and His preservation of His people according to His covenant. His mighty works disclose His holiness, mercy, and sovereign rule. Every deliverance points forward to the greater salvation accomplished through Christ, who conquers sin and death.

His right hand and holy arm
God is spirit, yet Scripture speaks of His hand and arm to reveal His active, irresistible power. His strength is holy: He saves without injustice and judges without corruption. Isaiah likewise declares that the Lord bares His holy arm before all nations so that the ends of the earth may see His salvation.

have gained Him the victory.
The Lord wins salvation by His own power, receiving glory that belongs to no human ruler or army. Israel’s victories were ultimately His victories, and the final triumph belongs to Christ, who rose from the dead and will reign over all His enemies.

Psalm 98:2

The LORD has proclaimed His salvation
God has made known His saving power through His mighty acts, beginning with His covenant promise that all families of the earth would be blessed through Abraham’s offspring. Israel’s deliverance from Egypt displayed that salvation before the surrounding world, and the prophets looked beyond temporal rescue to the coming Messiah. Jesus Christ fulfilled that hope through His death and resurrection, providing forgiveness of sins and eternal life for all who believe in Him.

and revealed His righteousness
God’s righteousness is seen in His perfect justice, covenant faithfulness, and holy mercy. He keeps His promises without overlooking sin. At the cross, God revealed His righteousness by judging sin in Christ while justifying those who trust in Him. The salvation He provides does not compromise His holiness; it demonstrates it.

to the nations.
God’s purpose has always extended beyond Israel to every people and land. Isaiah foretold that the Servant of the LORD would be a light to the nations, and Christ commanded His disciples to proclaim the gospel to all nations. The worldwide spread of the gospel fulfills God’s promise that His saving righteousness would be openly displayed before the whole earth.

Psalm 98:3

He has remembered His love and faithfulness to the house of Israel;
God acted openly to fulfill His covenant promises to Israel, displaying the steadfast mercy pledged to Abraham and reaffirmed through Moses. Israel’s exodus from Egypt, preservation in the wilderness, and inheritance of the land testified that the Lord does not abandon His people. His covenant faithfulness reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, the promised Messiah, through whom God fulfills the Law and brings the blessings promised through Abraham.

all the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God.
God’s saving acts on Israel’s behalf were intended to make His power and glory known among the nations. The deliverance celebrated in the psalm anticipates the Servant’s mission to bring God’s salvation to the ends of the earth. Through the gospel, Christ’s death and resurrection are proclaimed among every people, and the final fulfillment will be seen when a redeemed multitude from every nation worships before God’s throne.

Psalm 98:4

Make a joyful noise to the LORD,
Worship rises from glad recognition of the LORD’s saving power, righteous rule, and faithful love. Israel celebrated God with singing, shouts, and instruments, as David and the people did when the ark was brought toward Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6:5). Reverent joy belongs to those who know that the LORD reigns.

The LORD is Creator and King over every people, nation, and land; therefore, no part of humanity stands outside His rightful claim to worship. Israel’s praise anticipated the gathering of the nations through the gospel, when disciples of Jesus Christ would be made from every nation (Matthew 28:19).

Praise is portrayed as an unrestrained response to God’s greatness rather than a merely private sentiment. Such rejoicing recalls Israel’s victorious shout at Jericho, where the LORD displayed His power to save His people and overthrow their enemies (Joshua 6:20).

Psalm 98:5

Sing praises to the LORD
Israel answered God’s saving acts with public worship. Psalm 98 celebrates the LORD’s victory, righteousness, and faithfulness to His covenant people, calling the whole earth to rejoice. Praise belongs to Yahweh because He alone reigns over creation and accomplishes salvation. The redeemed respond not merely with private gratitude but with voices joined in reverent joy.

Psa 96:1 Sing to the LORD a new song; sing to the LORD…

with the lyre,
Stringed instruments accompanied the songs of Israel’s worship, especially in the service of the tabernacle and temple. David appointed Levites to lead musical praise before the ark, recognizing that skill, beauty, and order could be offered to God’s glory. Instruments did not replace heartfelt devotion; they gave fitting expression to faith in the God who had revealed Himself and delivered His people.

in melodious song
Worship offered to the LORD is marked by joyful intention rather than empty performance. The music of God’s people declares His mighty works and trains the heart to remember His mercy. Such praise anticipates the fuller song of redemption through Christ, whose death and resurrection secure salvation for people from every nation.

with the harp.
The harp was closely associated with David, Israel’s shepherd-king and psalmist, whose songs directed the nation toward the LORD. David’s royal line ultimately leads to Jesus Christ, the greater Son of David, who reigns forever. Earthly instruments accompany the praise of God’s people now, while Scripture looks ahead to the final worship of the redeemed before God’s throne.

Psalm 98:6

With trumpets and the blast of the ram’s horn
Trumpets and the shofar summoned Israel to worship, announced holy gatherings, and signaled God’s intervention in battle. The shofar sounded at Sinai, on the Day of Atonement in the Jubilee year, and in celebrations of the Lord’s reign. Their sound declares that the God who redeemed His people rules over them in power and holiness.

Jos 6:4-5 Have seven priests carry seven rams’ horns…

shout for joy before the LORD, the King.
Israel’s worship is directed before the personal presence of the Lord, not toward an abstract ideal. He is King over Israel and all the earth, worthy of public, wholehearted praise for His saving victories. The royal language finds its fullest fulfillment in Jesus Christ, the promised Son of David, whose kingdom will never end.

Psalm 98:7

Let the sea resound,
The sea, often associated with untamed power and danger, is summoned to proclaim the Lord’s kingship. The God who set boundaries for the waters at creation and divided the Red Sea for Israel rules what humanity cannot control. Its roar becomes praise before the righteous Judge who is coming to govern the earth.

and all that fills it,
Every creature and treasure within the sea belongs to the Lord who made it. Creation is not independent or purposeless; it bears witness to its Creator’s wisdom, power, and sustaining care. The whole created order joins the celebration of God’s saving acts and coming judgment.

Psalm 98 looks forward to the universal acknowledgment of the Lord’s reign, fulfilling the promise that through Abraham’s offspring all nations would be blessed. God’s salvation is proclaimed among the nations because His authority extends over every land.

Psalm 98:8

Let the rivers clap their hands,
Rivers, which sustain life and mark the fruitfulness of God’s provision, are pictured as joining the praise of their Maker. The image anticipates the day when God’s righteous judgment brings deliverance and restores creation from the effects of sin. Isaiah likewise portrays rivers, trees, and mountains rejoicing when the Lord redeems His people (Isaiah 55:12).

Psa 96:11-12 Let the heavens be glad and the earth rejoice;…

let the mountains sing together for joy
Mountains, enduring witnesses to God’s power and presence, unite in celebration before the King who comes to judge the earth in righteousness. Sinai trembled at the Lord’s descent (Exodus 19:16–20), yet Psalm 98 presents creation not in terror but in joyful expectation. The praise of rivers and mountains points to the universal reign of Christ, before whom all creation will acknowledge God’s holy and just rule.

All creation is summoned to rejoice in the presence of its Maker and King. The Lord’s nearness calls forth reverence, worship, and glad expectation, for no nation or person stands outside His authority.
