Joshua 4:1

When the whole nation had finished crossing the Jordan,
Israel entered Canaan as a complete people, with no tribe or family left behind. The LORD had restrained the flooded river until the nation crossed safely, recalling the Red Sea crossing and confirming that the God who delivered Israel from Egypt would also give them the land promised to Abraham. Passing through the Jordan marked the end of wilderness wandering and the beginning of covenant inheritance. The event also points forward to baptism as a decisive passage from an old life into a new course of obedience under God’s provision.

The Camp at Gilgal

Joshua 4:19

On the tenth day of the first month
Israel entered Canaan on the same date when each household had selected the Passover lamb before the Exodus (Exodus 12:3). Nisan marked Israel’s redemption from Egypt; now it marked the beginning of life in the land promised to Abraham. The timing joined the crossing of the Jordan to the crossing of the Red Sea and testified that the God who delivered His people from slavery was fulfilling His covenant promise.

Their passage ended forty years of wilderness wandering and began the possession of their inheritance. As at the Red Sea, God made a way where none existed, showing that His presence remained with His people under Joshua’s leadership.

Gilgal became Israel’s first camp and principal base during the early conquest of Canaan. There the twelve stones taken from the Jordan were erected as a lasting memorial of God’s power. Gilgal also became a place of covenant renewal, where the reproach of Egypt was rolled away and the people kept the Passover in the land (Joshua 5:9–10).
