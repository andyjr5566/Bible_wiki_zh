Similar reverence marked Moses, Isaiah, Ezekiel, and John when they encountered God’s glory or angelic messengers. Daniel could not presume to speak freely while overcome by the weight of the revelation.

The vision had drained Daniel physically, revealing the sharp contrast between human weakness and the power of God’s presence. Earlier, Daniel had fallen into a deep sleep and lost his strength at the angel’s appearance (Daniel 10:9–10). God’s servants are not sustained by natural ability when called to receive divine revelation; they must be strengthened by God Himself.

Daniel 10:18

Again the one with the likeness of a man
The heavenly messenger appeared in a form Daniel could endure, though the encounter still revealed the overwhelming holiness and power of God’s presence. The being is not named in this chapter, yet his humanlike appearance recalls Gabriel’s earlier ministry to Daniel (Daniel 8:15–16; 9:21). Angels often appeared as men when sent to deliver God’s word, as with the visitors to Abraham and the angels who came to Lot. God made His message accessible without reducing the majesty of the heavenly realm.

God’s servants are not merely given revelation; they are sustained to receive it. God had heard Daniel’s prayer from the first day of his mourning and regarded him as greatly beloved, as He had already declared in Daniel 9:23. Daniel’s value before God rested not on personal strength, but on God’s gracious regard for a faithful servant.

Daniel 10:20

“Do you know why I have come to you?” he said.
The heavenly messenger came in answer to Daniel’s mourning, fasting, and prayer for understanding concerning Israel’s future. God had heard Daniel from the first day, yet the delivery of the message involved conflict in the unseen realm. Daniel’s visions concerned coming kingdoms, persecution, and God’s final deliverance of His people; the messenger’s arrival confirmed that these matters proceeded according to God’s appointed purpose.

“I must return at once to fight against the prince of Persia,
The prince of Persia was a hostile spiritual ruler connected with the Persian Empire, not the human Persian king. The angel had already encountered resistance from this power for twenty-one days until Michael came to help him (Daniel 10:13). Persia remained the ruling empire during Daniel’s lifetime and would permit Jewish exiles to return and rebuild Jerusalem, yet spiritual opposition continued even within events God governed for Israel’s good. Earthly rulers and empires operate under God’s sovereignty, while spiritual powers seek to oppose His purposes.

Greece would rise after Persia, as Daniel had previously been shown in the visions of the great image, the beasts, and the ram and goat (Daniel 2; 7; 8). Alexander the Great’s conquests would bring the Greek Empire to power and eventually lead to severe oppression of the Jewish people under Antiochus IV. The coming of another hostile prince reveals that the conflict would continue as empires changed. God’s people are not abandoned amid such warfare, for the Lord directs the course of kingdoms and appoints Michael to stand for His people.

God’s decrees stand with the same certainty as His record of the redeemed in the Book of Life.
