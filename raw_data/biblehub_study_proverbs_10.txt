Solomon’s Proverbs: The Wise Son

Proverbs 10:1

The proverbs of Solomon:
Solomon, the son of David and Bathsheba, received extraordinary wisdom from God after asking for discernment to govern Israel (1 Kings 3:5–14). His reign brought peace, prosperity, and the building of the temple in Jerusalem. Proverbs 10 begins a major collection of brief sayings associated with Solomon, intended to train God’s people in righteousness, judgment, diligence, speech, family life, and the fear of the Lord. These instructions reveal that wisdom is not merely intellectual ability but the godly ordering of life under God’s authority.

A wise son brings joy to his father,
A child who receives instruction, fears the Lord, and walks uprightly becomes a source of deep joy within the home. Such wisdom honors a father’s labor in teaching and preserves the family’s name and stability. The principle reaches beyond earthly families: obedience brings pleasure to God, the Father of all who trust Him. Jesus Christ perfectly fulfilled the wisdom and obedience that every human child fails to display, and the Father declared His delight in Him (Matthew 3:17).

but a foolish son grief to his mother.
Foolishness rejects correction and pursues a path that brings sin, shame, and destructive consequences. A mother who has nurtured and instructed her child bears particular sorrow when that child turns from the way of life. The contrast warns that private rebellion does not remain private; it wounds families and dishonors God. Wisdom brings life and joy, while folly produces grief because it resists the Lord, the true source of understanding.

Proverbs 10:2

Ill-gotten treasures profit nothing,
Wealth gained through theft, fraud, oppression, bribery, or exploitation carries no lasting benefit. Israel’s Law forbade stealing and coveting, while the prophets condemned those who enriched themselves by crushing the poor and using dishonest scales. Achan’s stolen goods brought judgment upon his household rather than security or honor. Riches cannot purchase peace with God, escape His judgment, or preserve life when He calls a person to account.

Jer 17:11 Like a partridge hatching eggs it did not lay…

Mat 16:26 What will it profit a man if he gains the whole world…

but righteousness brings deliverance from death.
A life marked by obedience, justice, mercy, and faith in God leads away from the destructive path of sin. Righteous conduct often preserves life by avoiding the consequences of evil, but its fullest meaning reaches beyond physical danger. God delivers His people from eternal death through the righteousness He provides in Jesus Christ. Those who trust Him are called to walk uprightly, showing that true treasure is found in fellowship with God rather than dishonest gain.

Proverbs 10:3

The LORD does not let the righteous go hungry,
God faithfully sustains those who fear Him and walk in obedience to His Word. Israel experienced this care in the wilderness, where the Lord gave manna day by day and taught His people to depend on Him rather than stored-up security (Exodus 16). David likewise testified that he had not seen the righteous forsaken or their children begging for bread (Psalm 37:25). God’s provision does not promise a life free from hardship, famine, or persecution, but it assures His covenant people that He sees their needs and remains their sufficient help. The deepest fulfillment is found in Christ, the Bread of Life, who gives eternal life to all who come to Him in faith (John 6:35).

but He denies the craving of the wicked.
Those who reject God may pursue wealth, pleasure, power, and self-rule, yet their desires cannot bring lasting satisfaction. The Lord may restrain their ambitions, expose the emptiness of their pursuits, or allow sinful desires to produce their bitter consequences. Psalm 34:10 declares that those who seek the LORD lack no good thing, while those who refuse Him remain spiritually impoverished regardless of outward abundance. Wickedness promises satisfaction but leaves the heart hungry; righteousness finds its true provision in the God who gives what is good and withholds what would destroy.

Proverbs 10:4

Idle hands make one poor,
Laziness brings real loss. In Israel’s agrarian society, neglected fields, delayed planting, and unrepaired tools could quickly lead to hunger and debt. Proverbs repeatedly contrasts the sluggard with the diligent, calling God’s people to responsible labor and wise stewardship. The warning also reaches the heart: neglect of prayer, Scripture, obedience, and service leaves a person spiritually impoverished and vulnerable to sin. Paul likewise commanded believers who were able to work not to live in idleness at the expense of others (2 Thessalonians 3:10).

but diligent hands bring wealth.
Steady, faithful labor normally produces provision, usefulness, and increased responsibility. Such wealth includes more than money; it may involve a well-ordered household, a good reputation, practical skill, and resources to bless others. Diligence does not guarantee earthly riches, since hardship and injustice remain in a fallen world, but God honors faithful work. Believers labor as servants of Christ, trusting Him with the outcome and using what He supplies for His glory.

Proverbs 10:5

He who gathers in summer is a wise son,
Summer gathering required diligence, foresight, and faithful stewardship of the provision God had given. A son who worked while the crop was ready protected his household from want and honored his parents. Such wisdom grows from the fear of the Lord, which teaches people to recognize God’s order and fulfill their responsibilities in the proper season (Proverbs 1:7). Christ perfectly fulfilled the work given Him by the Father, gathering His people for the kingdom and completing His mission without delay (John 4:34–35).

but he who sleeps during harvest is a disgraceful son.
Harvest offered a limited opportunity; neglected labor could bring hunger and shame upon an entire household. Sleep pictures laziness, indifference, and failure to act when duty is clear. The disgraceful son stands opposite the ant, which prepares its food in the appointed season (Proverbs 6:6–8). Believers are likewise called to spiritual vigilance and readiness, lest complacency leave them unprepared for the Lord’s return (Matthew 25:1–13).

Proverbs 10:6

Blessings are on the head of the righteous,
God’s favor rests upon those who fear Him and order their lives by His wisdom. The head represents the person as a whole, so the blessing reaches every area of life under God’s care. Anointing upon the head marked kings and priests as recipients of divine appointment and honor; likewise, the righteous receive the Lord’s approval and sustaining grace. Psalm 23:5 portrays such favor as abundant provision in the presence of enemies.

Pro 3:33 The curse of the LORD is on the house of the wicked…

but the mouth of the wicked conceals violence.
Wickedness often hides behind peaceful words, flattering speech, and outward respectability while violence, deceit, and oppression remain in the heart. Such hypocrisy cannot remain hidden before God, who judges both words and motives. Jesus condemned religious leaders who appeared righteous outwardly while harboring corruption within. The righteous receive blessing because their lives are directed toward God, while the wicked ultimately face the exposure and consequences of the evil they conceal.

Proverbs 10:7

The memory of the righteous is a blessing,
Those who fear the Lord leave an enduring testimony of faith, integrity, and service to others. Their influence continues after death because their lives have displayed the wisdom and goodness of God. Abraham, Moses, David, and the faithful saints remembered in Scripture continue to strengthen later generations through their trust in God and obedience to His word. Psalm 112:6 likewise promises that the righteous will be remembered forever. Such remembrance does not depend merely on public reputation, but on a life that bears fruit for God’s kingdom and blesses those who follow.

Heb 11:4 By faith Abel offered God a better sacrifice than Cain did.…

but the name of the wicked will rot.
A wicked person’s reputation ultimately decays because rebellion against God produces shame, destruction, and death. Even when evildoers gain power or wealth for a time, their deeds cannot establish an honorable legacy. King Saul’s disobedience brought the loss of his kingdom and left his name associated with rejection of the Lord’s command. The contrast warns that lasting honor belongs not to those who pursue themselves, but to those whose lives are governed by the fear of the Lord.

Proverbs 10:8

A wise heart will receive commandments,
A wise heart humbly welcomes God’s instruction and submits to His authority. Wisdom begins with the fear of the Lord and is shown by obedience, not mere knowledge (Proverbs 1:7). Such receptiveness marked those who heard God’s law and sought to walk in His ways. Jesus likewise taught that the one who hears His words and acts on them builds on a secure foundation (Matthew 7:24).

but foolish lips will come to ruin.
Foolish speech reveals a heart that resists correction and disregards God’s truth. Careless, proud, or deceitful words bring destructive consequences, often turning a person’s own speech against him. Proverbs repeatedly warns that the tongue has power over life and death (Proverbs 18:21). The contrast shows that obedience leads toward stability, while unrestrained folly leads to downfall.

Proverbs 10:9

He who walks in integrity walks securely,
A whole and upright life brings the stability that comes from living before God without divided loyalties. Integrity joins private conduct to public profession and orders daily choices by God’s commands. Such security is deeper than freedom from trouble; it is the settled confidence of one who has no hidden course to defend. Psalm 25:21 likewise joins integrity and uprightness with the Lord’s preserving care. Wisdom guards the faithful from the fear and instability produced by secret sin.

but he who perverts his ways will be found out.
Deliberately turning from what is right eventually brings exposure. Sin may be concealed from people for a time, but it remains open before the Lord, who searches the heart and judges every work. Israel’s history repeatedly showed that concealed rebellion affected families and communities and could not escape God’s notice. Numbers 32:23 warns that sin will find out the guilty. The contrast calls God’s people to walk honestly, trusting that righteousness leads to lasting peace while deceit ends in shame and accountability.

Proverbs 10:10

He who winks the eye causes grief,
Winking the eye portrays concealed malice and covert cooperation in evil. Proverbs 6:12–14 likewise describes a corrupt person who signals with his eyes while devising trouble in his heart. Secretive deceit injures its victims, spreads distrust, and often draws others into sin. God calls His people to walk openly in truth, with motives and conduct that can endure His searching judgment.

and foolish lips will come to ruin.
Reckless, deceitful, and boastful speech exposes the folly within the heart and finally brings destruction upon the speaker. Words can wound families, divide communities, and damage a person’s standing before others. Proverbs repeatedly joins wisdom with truthful, restrained speech, while James warns that an uncontrolled tongue can spread great harm. Christ, who is truth, calls His followers to speak with integrity and to give account for every careless word.

Proverbs 10:11

The mouth of the righteous is a fountain of life,
Those who fear the Lord speak in ways that refresh, strengthen, and direct others toward truth. Their words flow from hearts shaped by God’s wisdom and become a source of counsel, comfort, correction, and hope. Proverbs repeatedly connects wisdom with a fountain of life (Proverbs 13:14), and Jesus fulfills that image as the giver of living water to all who believe in Him (John 4:14; 7:38). Righteous speech does not merely offer pleasant words; it conveys life because it is governed by truth, righteousness, and reverence for God.

Wicked speech often hides malicious purposes beneath flattering, deceptive, or careless words. What appears harmless may conceal hatred, manipulation, or plans that injure others. Jesus taught that the mouth speaks from the abundance of the heart (Matthew 12:34), so destructive speech reveals a heart opposed to God’s rule. The contrast warns that words carry moral weight: the righteous use speech to promote life, while the wicked use it to disguise and advance violence.

Proverbs 10:12

Hatred stirs up dissension,
Hatred nourishes grievances, interprets another’s actions with suspicion, and turns offenses into open conflict. Saul’s hatred of David, fed by jealousy, divided his household and brought violence into Israel. Such enmity opposes God’s command to love one’s neighbor and produces the disorder associated with selfish ambition.

Gal 5:15 But if you keep on biting and devouring one another…

but love covers all transgressions.
Love does not deny sin or protect evil; it refuses to spread offenses, seek revenge, or keep a record of wrongs. It seeks repentance, forgiveness, and restored fellowship. God’s saving love provides the fullest pattern, for Christ bore sin to reconcile sinners to God. Believers who have received such mercy are called to pursue peace and to deal with others’ failures in patience, truth, and forgiveness.

Proverbs 10:13

Wisdom is found on the lips of the discerning,
God-given wisdom becomes evident in speech that is truthful, timely, righteous, and useful for guiding others. The discerning person recognizes the fear of the Lord as the beginning of wisdom and applies God’s truth to the realities of daily life. In Israel, elders, judges, parents, and leaders were expected to speak with such understanding because their words shaped families and communities. James likewise directs believers to ask God for wisdom, trusting Him to give generously (James 1:5). Wise speech flows from a heart instructed by the Lord.

Foolishness brings correction because it rejects instruction and leads toward destructive choices. The rod portrays deserved discipline, whether administered by parents, governing authorities, or ultimately by God Himself. Proverbs repeatedly presents correction as an expression of care intended to turn a person from ruin toward wisdom. Hebrews 12:6 teaches that the Lord disciplines those He loves; His correction is never careless, but aims to produce righteousness in those who receive it.

Proverbs 10:14

The wise store up knowledge,
Wisdom begins with the fear of the Lord, receiving God’s revealed truth with humility and preserving it for faithful obedience. The wise do not merely collect information; they treasure instruction, discern God’s ways, and build their lives upon His commands. Proverbs portrays such knowledge as a treasure more valuable than silver or gold, acquired through diligent listening, correction, and meditation on God’s Word. Jesus likewise compared obedience to His teaching with building a house upon rock, a foundation that endures testing. Knowledge stored in the heart equips believers to speak rightly, resist sin, make righteous judgments, and pass God’s truth to the next generation.

A fool’s speech reveals a heart unwilling to submit to God’s wisdom. Careless, proud, deceitful, or angry words can wound others, stir conflict, expose sin, and bring ruin upon the speaker and those near him. Nabal’s insulting response to David endangered his entire household until Abigail intervened with wisdom and humility. James warns that the tongue, though small, can set great destruction in motion when it is not governed by God. The believer is therefore called to seek wisdom from God, to be slow to speak, and to exercise the self-control produced by the Holy Spirit.

Proverbs 10:15

The wealth of the rich man is his fortified city,
Wealth can provide food, shelter, influence, and protection from many earthly hardships. Like the walls surrounding an ancient city, riches may make a man feel secure and independent. Yet such security is limited and can become deceptive when wealth replaces trust in the Lord. Proverbs 18:11 repeats the image and exposes the rich man’s tendency to imagine his possessions as an unassailable wall. Riches cannot preserve a life from death, remove guilt before God, or secure eternal salvation.

Pro 18:11 A rich man’s wealth is his fortified city;…

Ecc 7:12 For wisdom, like money, is a shelter, and…

but poverty is the ruin of the poor.
Poverty leaves people exposed to hunger, debt, exploitation, and social vulnerability. The poor in Israel were therefore to receive justice, generous aid, and protection under God’s law (Deuteronomy 15:7–11). Poverty is not presented as proof of personal wickedness; Job’s suffering and Jesus’ (meaning “the Lord saves”) concern for the needy forbid such judgment. The contrast calls God’s people to recognize the crushing realities faced by the poor while remembering that the Lord, not material security, is the true refuge of both rich and poor.

Proverbs 10:16

The labor of the righteous leads to life,
Righteous labor includes the whole course of obedient living—work, choices, speech, worship, and service offered under the fear of the Lord. God’s covenant with Israel joined obedience with blessing, as Deuteronomy 28 demonstrates, while Psalm 1 portrays the righteous as a fruitful tree sustained by God. Such life is more than material provision; it is the fullness, stability, and fellowship with God that belong to those who walk in His wisdom. Righteous works do not earn salvation, but they reveal living faith and its fruit, as James teaches. Eternal life is finally found in Christ, whose righteousness gives life to all who believe.

Wealth, influence, or success gained through sin may appear secure for a time, yet it carries guilt and moves toward God’s judgment. Achan’s stolen spoil brought disaster upon his household, and Haman’s pride and malice ended in his own downfall. The wicked reap what they sow because God sees every unjust gain and will judge sin. Apart from repentance, sin’s course ends in death; through Christ, who bore sin’s punishment, the guilty may receive forgiveness and life.

Proverbs 10:17

Whoever heeds instruction is on the path to life,
Receiving godly discipline keeps a person on the way that leads to spiritual health, righteous conduct, and fellowship with God. Moses set before Israel life and death, blessing and curse, calling the people to choose life by loving the Lord and obeying His voice (Deuteronomy 30:19–20). Proverbs presents life as the outcome of fearing the Lord and walking in His wisdom. Jesus ultimately reveals Himself as the way, the truth, and the life; those who follow Him walk in the light rather than darkness (John 14:6; 8:12).

Correction exposes sinful patterns and calls the erring person back to the right way. Rejecting it is not independence but a departure from wisdom and obedience to God. Israel repeatedly suffered when it refused the Lord’s warnings through His law and prophets. Proverbs warns that those who reject reproof eventually face the destructive consequences of their chosen path (Proverbs 1:24–31). Yet God seeks those who wander, as a shepherd seeks a lost sheep, and His discipline is an expression of fatherly love that restores His children to faithful obedience (Luke 15:4–7; Hebrews 12:5–11).

The one who conceals hatred has lying lips,
Hidden hatred corrupts both the heart and the words spoken before others. God commanded Israel not to hate a brother in the heart but to deal honestly with sin and offense (Leviticus 19:17–18). Outward kindness that masks inward malice creates a false appearance of peace. Jesus taught that evil proceeds from within and defiles a person (Mark 7:21–23), exposing the need for repentance and genuine love.

Proverbs 10:19

When words are many,
This phrase highlights the potential danger of excessive speech. In biblical times, as today, the spoken word held significant power. Proverbs often contrasts the wise and the foolish, and here, verbosity is linked to folly. The culture of ancient Israel valued wisdom and discernment, often conveyed through concise and thoughtful speech. The emphasis on the quantity of words reflects a broader biblical theme found in Ecclesiastes 5:2, which advises against hasty speech before God.

Ecc 5:2 Do not be quick to speak, and do not be hasty…

Jas 1:19 My beloved brothers, understand this: Be quick to listen…

Pro 17:27 A man of knowledge restrains his words, and a man…

sin is unavoidable,
The inevitability of sin with excessive talking underscores the human tendency to err, especially when not exercising self-control. James 3:2-12 elaborates on the power of the tongue, describing it as a small part of the body that can cause great harm. The historical context of Proverbs, a book of wisdom literature, often addresses the moral and ethical conduct expected of God’s people. The idea that sin is unavoidable with many words serves as a warning to guard one’s speech, aligning with the broader biblical narrative of the fallibility of human nature.

Restraint in speech is portrayed as a virtue. In the cultural context of the ancient Near East, where honor and shame were significant societal values, controlling one’s words was a sign of wisdom and maturity. This restraint is echoed in the New Testament, where believers are encouraged to be “quick to listen, slow to speak” (James 1:19). The ability to hold one’s tongue is seen as a mark of self-discipline and spiritual maturity, reflecting the character of those who seek to live righteously.

Proverbs 10:20

The tongue of the righteous is choice silver,
Silver was a precious and refined metal in the ancient world, valued in commerce and treasured for its purity. Righteous speech carries similar worth because it proceeds from a heart submitted to God. Such words give wise counsel, bring healing, defend truth, and strengthen those who hear. Proverbs elsewhere compares the mouth of the righteous to a fountain of life, showing that godly speech refreshes rather than corrupts (Proverbs 10:11). Jesus taught that words reveal the abundance of the heart, so speech marked by truth, grace, and wisdom reflects a life shaped by God’s righteousness (Matthew 12:34–35).

Psa 12:6 The words of the LORD are flawless, like silver…

Psa 37:30 The mouth of the righteous man utters wisdom…

Pro 15:7 The lips of the wise spread knowledge…

but the heart of the wicked has little worth.
The wicked person’s inner life is impoverished because it rejects the fear of the Lord, the beginning of wisdom. From such a heart come deceit, cruelty, pride, and corrupt speech, producing harm rather than lasting good. Though wickedness may appear successful for a time, it has no enduring spiritual value before God. The contrast directs attention beyond outward words to the heart from which words arise. God desires inward purity, and a heart made right before Him produces speech that serves life and truth.

Proverbs 10:21

The lips of the righteous feed many,
Righteous speech imparts wisdom, correction, comfort, and instruction that strengthens others. In Israel’s covenant community, parents, elders, priests, and wise teachers nourished others through faithful counsel grounded in the fear of the Lord. Words possess the power to direct lives toward blessing or ruin; therefore, the righteous use speech to build up rather than deceive or destroy. Jesus Christ, the perfectly righteous One, gives life through His words and remains the true bread of life for all who believe in Him.

Proverbs repeatedly warns that a path chosen apart from God may appear right but ends in death. Christ is the wisdom of God, and those who receive Him and heed His instruction find the life that folly continually forfeits.

Proverbs 10:22

The blessing of the LORD enriches,
True prosperity comes from the LORD, who gives more than material increase. His blessing brings wisdom, peace, fruitful labor, covenant fellowship, and the ability to use His gifts rightly. Abraham received land, descendants, and divine favor so that all families of the earth might be blessed through him (Genesis 12:2–3). Solomon received wisdom and also riches and honor from God (1 Kings 3:13). Such abundance was never meant to terminate on the recipient but to display God’s faithfulness and serve His purposes. Jesus directs His people to seek treasures in heaven, where lasting wealth is found in knowing and obeying God (Matthew 6:19–21).

The LORD may lead His people through trials, and faithful believers are not promised an easy life; yet His blessing itself does not produce the destructive misery attached to sinful gain. His provision remains consistent with His fatherly goodness, and every good gift comes from above (James 1:17).

The fear of the LORD is reverent submission to the holy and sovereign God. Wisdom begins with such fear because it leads a person away from destructive paths and into obedience, prudence, justice, and peace. Israel’s covenant life joined faithful obedience with God’s blessing in the land, as Deuteronomy 5:33 teaches: those who walk in God’s ways may live and prosper. Proverbs 10:28

The hope of the righteous is joy,
Biblical hope is a confident expectation grounded in God’s character and promises. Those who fear the Lord possess a settled assurance that He will fulfill His purposes, even when present circumstances include suffering or uncertainty. Their joy begins in fellowship with God and His favor, as Psalm 16:11 declares, and it reaches its fullness in the salvation He provides. Abraham believed God and was counted righteous by faith (Genesis 15:6), establishing the pattern of trust by which God’s people live. Jesus fulfills this hope, giving His people enduring joy through union with Him and the promise of eternal life (John 15:11; Romans 5:1–2).

Israel’s cities of refuge illustrated God’s concern for justice and protection, though they could only provide temporary earthly safety. Those who fear the Lord stand on a foundation that suffering, opposition, and earthly loss cannot destroy. Wisdom from above is pure, peaceable, merciful, and sincere, revealing the character of God in ordinary speech and conduct (James 3:17).

Proverbs 10:32

The lips of the righteous know what is fitting,
Those who fear the Lord learn to speak with wisdom, truth, and grace. Their words are shaped by obedience to God’s commands and concern for the good of others. Scripture joins righteous speech with justice, mercy, and humility before God (Micah 6:8), because a heart submitted to Him does not treat words lightly. Appropriate speech may bring comfort to the burdened, correction to the wandering, counsel to the uncertain, and peace where conflict threatens to grow. Such discernment comes from seeking God’s wisdom rather than relying on natural impulse (James 1:5). A word rightly spoken has lasting value and reflects the order and goodness of God (Proverbs 15:23; 25:11).

Pro 16:23 The heart of the wise man instructs his mouth…

but the mouth of the wicked is perverse.
Those who reject God’s rule corrupt speech into an instrument of deception, slander, manipulation, and harm. Their words reveal hearts that resist truth and delight in what is crooked (Proverbs 6:12–14). Jesus taught that the mouth speaks from the abundance of the heart (Matthew 12:34); therefore, corrupt language is not merely an outward failure but evidence of inward rebellion. Perverse speech injures others and finally brings ruin upon the speaker (Proverbs 18:7). God calls His people to guard their hearts and tongues, speaking in a manner consistent with Christ, who is Himself the truth and the perfect righteous One (John 14:6).
