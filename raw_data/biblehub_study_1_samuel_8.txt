Names in biblical times often carried significant meaning, indicating the parents’ faith or hopes for their children. Joel’s name suggests a recognition of God’s sovereignty, which is ironic given the later actions of Samuel’s sons. This name is also shared by the prophet Joel, who authored the Book of Joel, emphasizing the importance of divine judgment and repentance.

1 Samuel 8:6

But when they said, “Give us a king to judge us,”
Israel sought a visible human ruler like the surrounding nations, even though the LORD Himself was their King and had governed them through judges He raised up. Their request rejected the distinctive calling given at Sinai, where Israel was to be God’s treasured possession and a kingdom of priests among the nations (Exodus 19:5–6). God had already made provision for a future king who would rule under His law (Deuteronomy 17:14–20), yet Israel demanded a king from distrust and a desire for national conformity rather than humble submission to God. The request would lead to Saul’s anointing and ultimately to David’s royal line, through which the promised Messiah would come (2 Samuel 7:12–16; Luke 1:32–33).
