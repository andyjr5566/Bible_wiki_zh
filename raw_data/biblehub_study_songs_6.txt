Song of Solomon 6:2

My beloved has gone down to his garden,
The bride speaks with confidence of her beloved’s presence in a place that belongs to him and delights him. The garden portrays ordered beauty, fruitfulness, and intimate fellowship, recalling Eden as a place prepared by God for human life and communion with Him. In its immediate setting, the beloved comes to enjoy the devotion and loveliness of his bride. In the larger biblical pattern, Christ dwells among His people, whom He cultivates as His own.

The bride rejoices in an exclusive covenant bond marked by love, faithfulness, and security. Her identity is bound to the one she loves, portraying marriage as a union of wholehearted devotion. Scripture also uses such marital faithfulness to portray the Lord’s covenant love for His people (Isaiah 54:5).

Song of Solomon 6:4

You are as beautiful, my darling, as Tirzah,
Tirzah was a renowned and pleasant city in the hill country of Ephraim and later became the first capital of Jeroboam’s northern kingdom (1 Kings 14:17; 15:33). Its beauty and prominence make the comparison a fitting affirmation of the woman’s loveliness and worth. The husband’s tender address reveals delight, covenant affection, and personal devotion.

The beloved is praised not merely for outward attractiveness but for a beauty marked by dignity and honor. Song of Solomon 6:5

Turn your eyes away from me, for they have overcome me.
The bridegroom is overwhelmed by the loving gaze of his bride. Her eyes convey devotion, beauty, and personal knowledge, drawing his heart to her anew after their recent separation. Love within marriage involves more than outward attraction; it is strengthened by the attentive, faithful regard of one person for another. The intensity of the bridegroom’s response reflects the joy of restored fellowship. Scripture also portrays the Lord as delighting in His people, not because of their natural worthiness, but because He has made them His own in covenant love.
