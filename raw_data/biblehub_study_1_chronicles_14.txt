1 Chronicles 14:6

Nogah,
Nogah is one of the sons of David born to him in Jerusalem. The name Nogah means “brightness” or “splendor,” which may reflect the hope and promise associated with David’s reign. Although Nogah is not mentioned elsewhere in the biblical narrative, his inclusion in the genealogical record underscores the importance of lineage and heritage in Israelite culture. This emphasis on genealogy is seen throughout the Bible, highlighting the fulfillment of God’s promises through specific family lines, ultimately leading to the lineage of Jesus Christ as detailed in the Gospels.

Nepheg,
Nepheg is another son of David, and his name means “sprout” or “shoot.” This imagery of growth and new life is significant in the biblical context, often symbolizing God’s ongoing work and the flourishing of His people. The mention of Nepheg, like Nogah, serves to document the expansion of David’s family and the establishment of his dynasty in Jerusalem. The city itself, chosen by God as the political and spiritual center of Israel, plays a crucial role in biblical history and prophecy, pointing forward to the coming of the Messiah, who would establish an eternal kingdom.

Gibeon stood near Jerusalem and had a significant place in Israel’s earlier history, while Gezer guarded important routes leading toward the coastal plain. The extent of the defeat demonstrates that the Philistines were not merely repelled from a battlefield but driven back from the region, establishing David’s kingdom and bringing greater safety to Israel.

1 Chronicles 14:17

And David’s fame went out into every land,
David’s victories over the Philistines established Israel as a secure and rising kingdom among the surrounding peoples. God had anointed David to shepherd Israel and had promised to make his name great (2 Samuel 7:8–9). News of his reign spread beyond Israel’s borders through military campaigns, trade, diplomacy, and the defeat of nations that had long threatened God’s people. The Tel Daniel Stele’s reference to the “house of David” confirms that David’s dynasty became known in the ancient Near East. His growing renown testified that the Lord was fulfilling His covenant purposes through the king He had chosen.

and the LORD caused all nations to fear him.
The surrounding nations recognized that David’s strength was not merely human achievement. The Lord Himself placed dread upon Israel’s enemies, much as He had caused the nations of Canaan to fear Israel after the Exodus (Exodus 15:14–16; Joshua 2:9–11). Such fear restrained hostile powers and gave Israel security during David’s reign. David’s authority foreshadowed the greater reign of Jesus Christ, the Son of David, before whom every nation and every person will ultimately bow (Philippians 2:9–11).
