The Transfiguration

Matthew 17:1

After six days
Jesus brought His disciples from the confession at Caesarea Philippi and His prediction of suffering, death, and resurrection to a decisive revelation of His glory. Mark records the same interval, while Luke’s “about eight days” likely includes both the first and last days. The timing recalls the period of waiting before God revealed Himself to Moses on Sinai, preparing the disciples to see the Son’s majesty.

Matthew 17:2

There He was transfigured before them.
On the mountain, Peter, James, and John were permitted to see Jesus in unveiled majesty. His glory was not borrowed or newly received; it revealed the divine splendor that belonged to Him as the eternal Son. The Transfiguration gave these disciples a foretaste of the kingdom glory Jesus had promised would be seen by some before death (Matthew 16:28). The event likely occurred near Caesarea Philippi, possibly on Mount Hermon, though Mount Tabor became an early Christian tradition. As Moses encountered God’s glory on Sinai, Jesus stood on the mountain as the greater Moses, not merely reflecting God’s glory but displaying it in Himself.

Moses’ face shone after speaking with the Lord and had to be veiled before Israel (Exodus 34:29–35), but Jesus’ glory came from His own divine person. The brightness also anticipates the risen and exalted Christ, whose face shines “like the sun shining in full strength” (Revelation 1:16). Peter, James, and John saw that the One who would suffer and die was also the Lord of glory.

Jesus stood before His disciples as the sinless Savior, perfectly holy and worthy to bear the sins of His people. His revealed glory assured them that the cross would not be defeat, but the path through which the King would accomplish redemption and enter His glory.
