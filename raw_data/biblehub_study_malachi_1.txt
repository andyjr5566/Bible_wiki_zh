Malachi 1:2

“I have loved you,” says the LORD.
God begins His message to the returned community of Judah with covenant love. He had chosen Israel through Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, redeemed them from Egypt, sustained them in the wilderness, and preserved them through exile. Their weakness and repeated disobedience had not canceled His commitment to the promises He made to their fathers. Deuteronomy 7:6–8 grounds Israel’s election in the LORD’s love and faithfulness rather than in Israel’s size, strength, or merit.

Esau and Jacob were twin sons of Isaac and Rebekah, sharing the same family line and covenant heritage through Abraham. Yet before their birth, God declared that the older would serve the younger, establishing Jacob as the heir through whom the covenant promise would continue (Genesis 25:23). Israel’s existence as a covenant people rested on God’s sovereign choice, not on natural privilege or human worthiness.

God’s love for Jacob includes His enduring favor toward Jacob’s descendants, Israel. Through Jacob came the twelve tribes, the land promises, the priesthood, the kingdom of David, and ultimately the Messiah, Jesus Christ. The LORD’s choice of Jacob shows that His redemptive purposes move forward by grace and faithfulness, even when His people struggle to recognize His care.

Malachi 1:3

God’s choice of Jacob over Esau began before the twins were born, when He declared that the older would serve the younger (Genesis 25:23). Jacob became the father of Israel, the covenant people through whom the promised Seed would come. Esau became the father of Edom. God’s hatred denotes His judicial rejection of Esau’s line from the covenant privilege given to Jacob, not arbitrary personal hostility. Paul cites these words in Romans 9:13 to uphold God’s sovereign freedom in carrying forward His redemptive purpose.

The devastation of its land fulfilled prophetic warnings given through Jeremiah and Obadiah and demonstrated that no natural strength can preserve a nation under God’s judgment.

The Polluted Offerings

Malachi 1:6

“A son honors his father, and a servant his master.
God appeals to obligations Israel readily recognized. Children owed honor to their fathers, and servants owed faithful respect to those placed over them. Israel’s covenant life rested upon such ordered relationships, including the command to honor father and mother. Yet the Lord had a greater claim upon His people than any earthly father or master.

The Lord was Israel’s Father as Creator, Redeemer, and covenant Lord. He had delivered them, preserved them through judgment and exile, and restored them to their land. Their worship should have reflected gratitude, trust, and willing obedience. Instead, the priests permitted conduct that treated God as unworthy of costly devotion.

God possessed absolute authority over the priests and the nation. Reverence for Him should have governed every sacrifice offered at His altar. Their careless service revealed that they feared human opinion and personal inconvenience more than the holy God whom they represented.
