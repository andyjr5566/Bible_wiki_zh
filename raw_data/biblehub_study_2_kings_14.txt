Amaziah Reigns in Judah

2 Kings 14:1

In the second year of the reign of Jehoash son of Jehoahaz over Israel,
This phrase sets the historical context for the events described. Jehoash, also known as Joash, was the king of Israel, the northern kingdom, during this time. His reign is noted in 2 Kings 13:10-25, where he is described as doing evil in the sight of the Lord, continuing the sins of Jeroboam. The synchronization of reigns between Israel and Judah is a common method in the biblical narrative to provide a timeline. This period is marked by political instability and frequent conflicts between the two kingdoms. Jehoash’s reign is significant for his military campaigns and his interactions with the prophet Elisha, who prophesied victories for Israel.

Amaziah son of Joash became king of Judah.
Amaziah succeeded his father in the Davidic line after Joash was assassinated by his own servants. He began with acts of justice and obedience to the Law, but his devotion to the Lord was not wholehearted. His early victory over Edom became a turning point when pride led him to challenge Jehoash of Israel, bringing Judah humiliating defeat. Amaziah’s reign demonstrates that outward obedience and early success cannot replace enduring humility and faithful dependence on God.

2 Kings 14:7

Amaziah struck down 10,000 Edomites in the Valley of Salt.
The Valley of Salt lay south of the Dead Sea near the boundary of Edom, a region associated with salt deposits and earlier Israelite victories. Amaziah’s defeat of Edom renewed Judah’s authority over a people descended from Esau who had repeatedly opposed Israel. David had also conquered Edom in this region (2 Samuel 8:13–14). The ten thousand slain records a decisive historical victory, granted by God as Amaziah obeyed the prophetic instruction not to rely on Israelite mercenaries (2 Chronicles 25:7–10).

Amaziah ruled Judah after defeating Edom and capturing Sela, a victory that appears to have fostered dangerous confidence in his own strength. Jehoash ruled the northern kingdom of Israel and descended from Jehu, whom God had used to destroy Ahab’s dynasty and confront Baal worship. Although Israel remained marked by idolatry, God had recently granted Jehoash victories over Syria because of His covenant mercy toward Israel (2 Kings 13:22–25). The conflict exposed the continuing rivalry between the divided kingdoms, whose shared ancestry and covenant heritage did not prevent them from turning against one another.
