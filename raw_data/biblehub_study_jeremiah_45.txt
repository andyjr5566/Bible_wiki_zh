Jeremiah’s Message to Baruch

Jeremiah 45:1

This is the word that Jeremiah the prophet spoke to Baruch son of Neriah
Jeremiah served as the Lord’s faithful messenger during Judah’s final years before the Babylonian exile. Baruch was more than a secretary; he shared in Jeremiah’s costly ministry by preserving and proclaiming the warnings God gave. His family appears to have held social standing in Jerusalem, and his brother Seraiah served King Zedekiah (Jeremiah 51:59). The Lord’s personal word to Baruch reveals His care for a servant burdened by the national crisis and by the demands of prophetic service.

Jer 36:4 So Jeremiah called Baruch son of Neriah,…

Jer 32:12 I gave this deed to Baruch son of Neriah,…

Jer 43:3 Rather, Baruch son of Neriah is inciting you against us…

when he wrote these words on a scroll at the dictation of Jeremiah
Baruch recorded the prophecies Jeremiah dictated, likely the collection described in Jeremiah 36. The scroll contained God’s warnings against Judah, Jerusalem, and the surrounding nations. When King Jehoiakim later cut up and burned the scroll, the Lord commanded Jeremiah and Baruch to write it again, with further words added (Jeremiah 36:27–32). Human opposition could not erase God’s revealed word, and Baruch’s faithful labor helped preserve it for succeeding generations.

Jer 36:32 Then Jeremiah took another scroll and gave it to the scribe…

Jer 36:18 “It was at his dictation,” Baruch replied.…

Jer 36:2 Take a scroll and write on it all the words I have spoken…

in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah:
The fourth year of Jehoiakim was about 605 BC, when Babylon rose to supremacy after defeating Egypt at Carchemish. Judah faced the approaching judgment Jeremiah had long announced, yet Jehoiakim rejected God’s warnings. The setting gives urgency to the Lord’s message to Baruch: personal sorrow and disappointed hopes had to be viewed in light of God’s greater purpose of judging Judah while preserving a remnant.

God addresses Jeremiah’s faithful scribe personally. After recording Jeremiah’s warnings during Jehoiakim’s reign, Baruch carried the burden of serving God in a time of national ruin. The Lord knew his sorrow and fear, and He would command him not to seek personal greatness but to trust God for preservation.

Jeremiah 45:3

You have said,
Baruch’s complaint is recorded by the Lord, revealing the burden carried by Jeremiah’s faithful scribe during Judah’s final years. As he wrote and proclaimed messages of coming judgment, he bore personal grief alongside the national crisis.

Psa 27:8 My heart said, “Seek His face.” Your face, O LORD,…

Lam 3:57 You drew near when I called on You; You said…

‘Woe is me because the LORD has added sorrow to my pain!
Baruch recognized that the calamities overtaking Judah stood under God’s sovereign judgment. His distress was not merely disappointment or hardship; it arose from living in a rebellious nation marked for Babylonian invasion and exile. Faithful service did not spare him from sorrow, yet the Lord had not abandoned him.

Isa 6:5 Then I said: “Woe is me, for I am ruined,…

Lam 1:12 Is this nothing to you, all you who pass by?…

Mic 7:1 Woe is me! For I am like one gathering summer fruit…

I am worn out with groaning
The weight of fear, opposition, and prolonged grief had exhausted Baruch. He had witnessed Jeremiah’s rejection, helped preserve unpopular prophecies, and faced the consequences of identifying with God’s message. His groaning reflects the anguish of one who remained near the Lord’s word while surrounding events seemed to unravel.

Psa 6:6 I am weary from groaning; all night I flood…

Job 3:24 I sigh when food is put before me, and…

Psa 69:3 I am weary from my crying; my throat is parched.…

and have found no rest.’”
Judah had forfeited the peace associated with obedience to God, and Baruch felt that unrest personally. The Lord would soon correct Baruch’s ambition and promise to preserve his life amid judgment (Jeremiah 45:4–5). Lasting rest is ultimately found in the Lord, who calls the weary to Himself and gives rest to those who trust Him (Matthew 11:28–29).

Jeremiah 45:4

Thus Jeremiah was to say to Baruch:
Baruch had faithfully served as Jeremiah’s scribe, recording prophecies, reading them publicly, and enduring the dangers attached to Jeremiah’s ministry. God’s word addressed Baruch personally in a season when Judah’s collapse made private hopes and public security increasingly uncertain.

“This is what the LORD says:
The message came with the authority of YHWH, Israel’s covenant God. Jeremiah was not offering counsel drawn from political observation; he delivered God’s settled purpose concerning Judah and the nations.

Jer 28:2 This is what the LORD of Hosts, the God of Israel, says:…

Zec 1:3 So tell the people that this is what the LORD of Hosts says:…

Throughout the land I will demolish what I have built
God had established Judah in the land, given Jerusalem its temple, and preserved David’s line according to His covenant promises. Yet persistent rebellion brought covenant judgment. Babylon’s coming invasion would overthrow institutions and protections that the people wrongly assumed could not fall.

Jer 1:10 See, I have appointed you today over nations and kingdoms…

Jer 31:28 Just as I watched over them to uproot and tear down…

Jer 18:7-10 At any time I might announce that a nation or kingdom…

and uproot what I have planted.
The Lord who plants also has the right to uproot. Judah’s exile would remove the people from the land God had given them, demonstrating the seriousness of sin and the certainty of His judgment. Still, uprooting was not God’s final purpose; He later promised to build and plant His restored people again (Jeremiah 31:28).

Jeremiah 45:5

But as for you, do you seek great things for yourself?
Baruch had faithfully served Jeremiah by writing and reading the Lord’s words during the reign of Jehoiakim, yet the burden of Judah’s collapse may have stirred desires for security, honor, or advancement. God redirected him from personal expectations to humble service. In a nation facing judgment, lasting hope could not be found in position or recognition, but in submission to the Lord and His purposes. Jesus likewise calls His followers to seek first God’s kingdom rather than earthly gain.

Pro 25:27 It is not good to eat too much honey or to search…

Psa 131:1 A song of ascents. Of David. My heart is not proud, O LORD, my eyes are not haughty.…

Pro 25:6-7 Do not exalt yourself in the presence of the king,…

Stop seeking!
Baruch was not to build his hopes on plans that God’s coming judgment would soon overturn. The command called him to relinquish self-directed ambition and entrust his future to the sovereign God. Faithfulness in a season of loss often means continuing to serve without demanding the rewards one expected.

Php 2:21 For all the others look after their own interests,…

1Co 10:24 No one should seek his own good, but the good of others.

Luk 12:29-31 And do not be concerned about what you will eat or drink.…

For I will bring disaster on every living creature, declares the LORD,
The disaster included the Babylonian invasion and the devastation that would fall upon Judah, Jerusalem, and the surrounding nations. No social standing, political alliance, or religious office could shield those under God’s righteous judgment. The Lord’s declaration made clear that the coming calamity was not accidental history but His response to persistent rebellion, idolatry, and refusal to heed His word.

Gen 6:17 And behold, I will bring floodwaters upon the earth…

Zep 1:2-3 “I will completely sweep away everything,” declares the LORD.…

Isa 24:1-3 Behold, the LORD lays waste the earth and leaves it…

but wherever you go, I will grant your life as a spoil of war.”
Baruch would not be spared every hardship, but the Lord promised to preserve his life through the destruction. Like a soldier receiving spoil after battle, Baruch’s survival would be a gift granted by God amid widespread loss. The promise revealed both the severity of judgment and the Lord’s faithful care for His servant, who was called to remain obedient wherever the path of exile or danger might lead.
