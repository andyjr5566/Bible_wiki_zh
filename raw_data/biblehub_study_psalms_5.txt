Psalm 5:2

Attend to the sound of my cry,
David brings his distress openly before the Lord, confident that God hears the cries of His people. Israel’s deliverance from Egypt began with God hearing their groaning under oppression (Exodus 3:7). Prayer is not an attempt to inform God of need, but a faithful appeal to the God who sees, knows, and acts.

Psalm 5:3

In the morning, O LORD, You hear my voice;
David began the day by turning consciously to the covenant God who listens to His people. Morning prayer acknowledges that every new day belongs to the Lord and depends upon His sustaining mercy. Abraham rose early to stand before the Lord after interceding for Sodom (Genesis 19:27), and Jesus withdrew before dawn to pray to His Father (Mark 1:35). God’s mercies are new every morning, giving His servants reason to seek Him with renewed trust (Lamentations 3:22–23).

Psalm 5:4

For You are not a God who delights in wickedness;
David grounds his plea for protection in the holy character of the Lord. Israel’s God stands utterly apart from the false gods worshiped among the surrounding nations, whose myths often attributed deception, violence, and sexual immorality to their deities. The Lord is righteous in all His ways and never approves sin or derives pleasure from human rebellion. His purity explains both His judgment of evil and His mercy toward those who repent. Scripture consistently declares that God is light, and in Him there is no darkness at all (1 John 1:5; Habakkuk 1:13).

Pro 15:9 The LORD detests the way of the wicked…

no evil can dwell with You.
Wickedness cannot remain in God’s favorable presence as an accepted companion or worshiper. The restricted access to the Holy of Holies under the Mosaic covenant displayed this reality: even the high priest entered only once each year, with sacrificial blood and appointed purification (Leviticus 16). Human sin creates a separation that no person can remove by personal effort. Jesus Christ alone is the mediator between God and men, bringing sinners to God through His atoning death and resurrection (1 Timothy 2:5; 1 Peter 3:18). The final kingdom will fully reveal this holiness, for nothing unclean will enter the New Jerusalem (Revelation 21:27).

Psalm 5:5

The boastful cannot stand in Your presence;
God’s holy presence exposes and excludes human pride. The arrogant trust in themselves, resist God’s rule, and cannot claim a place before Him on their own merit. Scripture repeatedly joins pride with coming judgment, for “pride goes before destruction” (Proverbs 16:18). God gives grace to the humble, but He opposes those who exalt themselves (James 4:6). David’s confidence rests not in personal strength or royal status, but in the mercy of the Lord.

You hate all workers of iniquity.
God’s hatred is His righteous opposition to evil and to those who persist in it. Sin is not a harmless weakness before a holy God; it is rebellion against His character and rule. God’s eyes are too pure to approve evil (Habakkuk 1:13), and His justice requires judgment upon unrepentant sinners. Yet the same God provides forgiveness through Jesus Christ, who bore judgment for sin and calls all people to repentance and faith.

Psalm 5:6

You destroy those who tell lies;
God’s judgment against falsehood reveals His unwavering commitment to truth. Lying violates the character of the God of truth and corrupts the trust necessary for covenant life and human community. The ninth commandment forbids false witness, especially the kind of deceit that perverts justice and harms one’s neighbor (Exodus 20:16). Scripture repeatedly joins lying with rebellion against God, warning that unrepentant deceivers face final judgment (Proverbs 6:16–19; Revelation 21:8). David’s words also acknowledge that deceit brings ruin upon the deceiver, whether through God’s temporal discipline or His righteous final judgment.

the LORD abhors the man of bloodshed and deceit.
Murder and treachery belong together because violence often depends upon concealed hatred, false testimony, betrayal, or calculated schemes. Cain’s murder of Abel began with sinful jealousy that he refused to master, and Judas betrayed Jesus under the cover of friendship for personal gain. God’s hatred of such evil reflects His care for human life, made in His image, and for justice among His people (Genesis 9:6). The Lord sees crimes hidden from human courts and will judge those who shed innocent blood. Christ’s victory over sin and death assures believers that violent and deceitful evil will not endure, while His grace calls sinners to repentance, truth, and reconciliation.

Psalm 5:7

But I will enter Your house by the abundance of Your loving devotion;
David approaches the sanctuary trusting not in his own righteousness but in God’s covenant mercy. God’s loving devotion, revealed in His faithful dealings with Israel, provides the only basis for entering His presence. The tabernacle was the appointed place where the LORD made His dwelling known among His people, yet even the king came as a dependent worshiper before the holy God.

During David’s lifetime, worship centered on the tabernacle and its sacred precincts, while the permanent temple would later be built by Solomon. The earthly sanctuary pointed beyond itself to Christ, through whom believers receive access to the Father and may draw near with confidence because of His grace.

Human wisdom cannot reliably discern the right path amid danger, temptation, and pressure; the LORD leads His people in a manner consistent with His truth. Such dependence anticipates Proverbs 3:5–6 and accords with God’s call for His people to reflect His holiness (Leviticus 19:2).

His prayer does not seek personal revenge but asks God to expose and condemn those who deliberately oppose His rule. Scripture reserves vengeance and recompense for God alone (Deuteronomy 32:35; Romans 12:19).

Psalm 5:12

For surely You, O LORD, bless the righteous;
Yahweh faithfully bestows His favor upon those who belong to Him and walk in obedience to His revealed will. Righteousness does not mean sinless perfection, but a life marked by faith, repentance, and submission to God. The blessings promised under the Mosaic covenant included provision, security, fruitfulness, and peace for Israel’s obedience (Deuteronomy 28:1–14). God’s goodness toward the righteous ultimately rests upon His covenant faithfulness and finds its fullest fulfillment in Christ, through whom believers receive every spiritual blessing (Ephesians 1:3).

God’s favor provides complete protection, like a shield carried into battle and held on every side. David knew that earthly enemies, danger, and opposition could not overturn the care of the Lord. His protection does not guarantee freedom from suffering, but it assures His people that no enemy can finally separate them from His love or defeat His purposes. Through Christ, God’s favor surrounds all who trust Him, giving confidence amid spiritual conflict and secure hope in every circumstance (Romans 8:31–39; Ephesians 6:16).
