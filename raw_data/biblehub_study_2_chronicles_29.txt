Hezekiah Cleanses the Temple

2 Chronicles 29:1

Hezekiah was twenty-five years old when he became king,
Hezekiah succeeded his father Ahaz, whose reign had brought Judah into grave idolatry and had even closed the doors of the temple. Though still a young man, Hezekiah acted with conviction and spiritual courage. His accession introduced a decisive renewal in Judah, beginning with the restoration of the Lord’s house and the call to covenant faithfulness.

His reign centered in Jerusalem, the city God had chosen for His name and the seat of David’s dynasty. During these years Hezekiah removed idols, restored temple worship, celebrated the Passover, and led Judah through the Assyrian threat. His faithful rule demonstrated the blessing of seeking the Lord, while his dependence on God during crisis anticipated the greater reign of the Son of David, who restores true worship and delivers His people.

Hezekiah began his reign by giving immediate attention to the worship of the LORD. Nisan marked the beginning of Israel’s religious year and included the Passover season (Exodus 12:2–6), making the timing especially fitting for national renewal. Ahaz had led Judah into idolatry and had closed the temple doors (2 Chronicles 28:24–25). Hezekiah’s first public concern was therefore not military strength or political security, but restored fellowship with God.

The reopened doors restored access to the temple, where God had chosen to place His name and where Israel was to bring sacrifices, prayer, and praise. Repairs were necessary because the temple had been neglected during Ahaz’s reign. Hezekiah’s work prepared the way for cleansing the sanctuary, renewing the priesthood’s service, and observing Passover according to God’s command. The restored temple worship anticipated the greater access to God provided through Jesus Christ, the true temple and the final mediator between God and His people (John 2:19–21; Hebrews 9:11–12).
