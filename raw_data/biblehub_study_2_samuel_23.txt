Ahithophel later joined Absalom’s rebellion against David, yet Eliam’s place among the mighty men records his own faithful service. The family connection deepens the tragedy of Ahithophel’s betrayal and reveals how closely David’s military, household, and royal court were intertwined.

Naharai the Beerothite,
Naharai was from Beeroth, a Gibeonite city associated with the covenant Israel made in Joshua’s day (Joshua 9:17–20). Although the Gibeonites entered Israel’s midst through deception, Israel remained bound by its sworn commitment. Naharai’s military service generations later reflects the enduring consequences of covenant obligations and the incorporation of the Gibeonites into Israel’s life.

2 Samuel 23:39

and Uriah the Hittite.
Uriah, though of Hittite ancestry, served David with exemplary covenant loyalty. His conduct during the siege of Rabbah exposed David’s sin: Uriah refused the comforts of home while Israel’s army remained in danger, but David arranged his death to conceal adultery with Bathsheba (2 Samuel 11:6–17). Nathan later charged David with killing Uriah by the sword of the Ammonites (2 Samuel 12:9). Uriah’s inclusion among the mighty men preserves the honor of a faithful soldier whom David wronged and shows that God holds even His anointed king accountable for bloodshed and injustice.

Their exploits helped establish and defend the kingdom God gave David. Yet the final name, Uriah, reminds readers that military strength and royal success could not excuse moral failure.
