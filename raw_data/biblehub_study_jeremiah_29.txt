Jeremiah 29:7

Seek the prosperity of the city to which I have sent you as exiles.
Jeremiah addressed the Judeans deported to Babylon in 597 BC after King Jehoiachin surrendered to Nebuchadnezzar. Though Babylon had acted violently against Jerusalem, the exile was also God’s judgment for Judah’s persistent covenant unfaithfulness. The exiles were not outside God’s rule or forgotten by Him; He had sent them there and would govern both their discipline and their restoration. Rather than encourage revolt or withdrawal, God commanded His people to promote the peace, stability, and welfare of the Babylonian society in which they lived. Their conduct would display the character of the Lord among a nation devoted to idols and imperial power. God’s purpose for Abraham’s descendants included blessing the nations, even when His people lived as strangers in foreign lands.

“Send a message telling all the exiles
Jeremiah was to address the Judean community deported to Babylon after Nebuchadnezzar’s campaign against Jerusalem in 597 BC. Though far from Jerusalem and the temple, the exiles remained under the LORD’s covenant care and accountable to His word.

Ezk 3:10-11 “Son of man,” He added, “listen carefully…”…

Jer 24:5-7 This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says:…

what the LORD says concerning Shemaiah the Nehelamite.
Shemaiah lived among the exiles and opposed Jeremiah’s instruction that Judah should settle in Babylon during the appointed period of exile. He sought to discredit Jeremiah’s message and encouraged resistance to the discipline God had decreed.

Jer 28:15-17 Then the prophet Jeremiah said to the prophet Hananiah,…

Ezk 13:2-3 Son of man, prophesy against the prophets of Israel…

Because Shemaiah has prophesied to you—though I did not send him—
Prophetic authority depended upon God’s calling and message, not personal influence or popular hopes. Shemaiah claimed to speak for the LORD while contradicting the revelation God had given through Jeremiah concerning the seventy years of Babylonian rule.

Deu 18:20-22 But if any prophet dares to speak a message in My name…

Jer 23:21 I did not send these prophets, yet they have run…

and has made you trust in a lie,
Shemaiah’s teaching offered false hope of an immediate return and turned the exiles from humble submission to God’s purposes. False prophecy is spiritually destructive because it replaces confidence in God’s true word with confidence in human invention.

Jer 7:8 But look, you keep trusting in deceptive words to no avail.

‘I will surely punish Shemaiah the Nehelamite and his descendants.
Shemaiah had sent a letter from Babylon urging the priest Zephaniah to silence Jeremiah for proclaiming the seventy-year exile. By opposing God’s revealed word, he deceived the exiles and encouraged resistance to the Lord’s discipline. His household would bear the temporal consequences of his rebellion.

He will have no one left among this people,
Shemaiah’s family line would not endure within the restored covenant community. For an Israelite, the loss of descendants meant the loss of inheritance, name, and continuing place among God’s people.
