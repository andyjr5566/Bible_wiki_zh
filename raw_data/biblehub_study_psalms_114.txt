A Psalm of Exodus

Psalm 114:1

When Israel departed from Egypt,
God redeemed Israel from slavery through mighty acts of judgment against Pharaoh and Egypt’s gods. The Exodus established Israel as the people God had delivered for His own possession and became the defining pattern of divine salvation throughout Scripture. Passover commemorated the night when the blood of the lamb shielded Israel from judgment, anticipating Christ, the true Passover Lamb, whose sacrifice delivers His people from sin and death.

the house of Jacob
Jacob, renamed Israel, received the covenant promises first given to Abraham and Isaac. His twelve sons became the heads of Israel’s tribes, so the nation’s departure from Egypt demonstrated God’s faithfulness to His covenant across generations. The Lord had not forgotten His people during their long affliction, but brought them out to worship Him and inherit the land He promised.

Their liberation showed that no human power, national boundary, or spiritual darkness could prevent God from accomplishing His purpose. The deliverance also points forward to the gathering of redeemed people from every nation and language through Christ.

Psalm 114:2

Judah became God’s sanctuary,
This phrase highlights the special status of Judah as the tribe from which the Davidic line and ultimately the Messiah, Jesus Christ, would come. The term “sanctuary” signifies a holy place where God’s presence dwells. Historically, the temple in Jerusalem, located in Judah, was the central place of worship for the Israelites, symbolizing God’s dwelling among His people. This reflects the fulfillment of God’s promise to dwell with His people, as seen in Exodus 25:8. The choice of Judah as the sanctuary also points to the prophecy in Genesis 49:10, where the scepter is promised to Judah, indicating leadership and divine favor.

Israel His dominion.
The nation redeemed from Egypt belonged to the Lord as His covenant people and lived under His righteous rule. At Sinai, God set Israel apart as a kingdom of priests and a holy nation (Exodus 19:5–6), governing them by His Law and leading them by His presence. Israel’s deliverance and worship proclaimed that the Lord alone is King, a truth fulfilled ultimately in Christ’s eternal reign.

Psalm 114:3

The sea observed and fled;
The Red Sea parted before Israel at God’s command, opening a dry path through the waters as Pharaoh’s army pursued them (Ex. 14:21–22). The sea is pictured as fleeing before its Creator, unable to resist His presence and power. Israel’s deliverance from Egypt displayed God’s faithfulness to His covenant and His authority over every force that opposed His people. Paul later connects the passage through the sea with baptism, portraying it as a decisive break with the old bondage and identification with the deliverance God provides (1 Cor. 10:1–2).

the Jordan turned back;
The Jordan River stopped its overflowing course when the priests carrying the ark entered its waters, allowing Israel to cross into Canaan on dry ground (Josh. 3:14–17). At flood stage, the river presented an impossible barrier, yet God made a way as He had at the Red Sea. The ark went before the people, declaring that their inheritance depended upon the presence and leading of the Lord. The crossing marked the transition from wilderness wandering to the possession of God’s promised land, foreshadowing the fullness of life and inheritance secured through Christ.

Psalm 114:4

the mountains skipped like rams,
At the Lord’s presence, even the fixed mountains of Sinai responded as though alive. Israel’s deliverance from Egypt was accompanied by God’s descent upon Sinai in fire, cloud, thunder, and earthquake (Exodus 19:18). The mountains’ movement proclaims that the Creator rules over every power that appears immovable. His presence brought judgment upon Egypt, opened the sea for His people, and established His covenant with them in the wilderness.

the hills like lambs.
The smaller hills join the mountains in joyful, trembling response to the God of Jacob. Lamb-like imagery conveys lively movement before the Lord whose saving power renews what sin has brought under bondage. Creation’s response anticipates the day when Christ’s completed redemption will be revealed in a liberated creation, no longer subject to decay (Romans 8:19–21).

Psalm 114:5

Why was it, O sea, that you fled,
The Red Sea opened before Israel when the LORD drove back its waters through Moses’ outstretched hand, bringing His redeemed people safely out of Egypt on dry ground (Exodus 14:21–22). The sea’s retreat displayed the Creator’s authority over all creation and removed the barrier between Israel and freedom. Egypt’s pursuing army could not overturn the deliverance God had accomplished; the waters returned in judgment upon those who sought to enslave His people. This exodus became Israel’s defining testimony that the LORD saves, and it points forward to the greater redemption secured through Christ, who delivers His people from sin and death.

Psa 66:6 He turned the sea into dry land; they passed…

O Jordan, that you turned back,
The Jordan ceased its flow when the priests carrying the ark of the covenant stepped into its overflowing waters, allowing Israel to enter the land God had promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (Joshua 3:14–17). The same God who brought Israel out of Egypt also brought them into their inheritance, proving His faithfulness across generations. The ark’s presence at the river testified that the LORD Himself went before His people. Israel’s passage through the Jordan marked the end of wilderness wandering and the beginning of life in the promised land, anticipating the believer’s entrance into the full inheritance secured by Christ.

Psalm 114:6

O mountains, that you skipped like rams,
Mount Sinai trembled violently when the LORD descended in fire to give His Law to Israel (Exodus 19:18). The psalm portrays even the strongest features of creation as yielding before the God who made them. Mountains that appear fixed and immovable respond with the energy of rams before His presence. The Lord who delivered Israel from Egypt rules over the earth itself, and all things continue by the sustaining power of Christ (Colossians 1:16–17).

O hills, like lambs?
The smaller hills join the mountains in trembling and leaping before the Creator. Rams and lambs convey vigorous movement throughout the flock, showing that no part of creation is indifferent to God’s majesty. The earth’s response anticipates the day when creation will be fully released from its bondage and share in the glory of God’s redeemed people (Romans 8:19–21).

Psalm 114:7

Tremble, O earth,
All creation stands under the authority of its Creator. The trembling recalls Mount Sinai, where the mountain shook greatly when the Lord descended in fire to give His law to Israel (Exodus 19:18). Earth, sea, mountains, and nations cannot remain unmoved before the God who rules them all.

at the presence of the Lord,
The Lord’s manifested presence brings both holy fear and covenant comfort. He revealed His glory in the tabernacle and above the ark of the covenant, dwelling among the people He redeemed (Exodus 25:22; 40:34–38). Isaiah saw that holiness and cried out over his own uncleanness (Isaiah 6:1–5), yet the same Lord provided cleansing and forgiveness.

He preserved Jacob, renamed him Israel, and fulfilled His promises through Israel’s deliverance from Egypt and entrance into the promised land. His covenant purposes culminate in Jesus Christ, the promised King from Jacob’s line, whose kingdom will endure forever (Luke 1:32–33).

Psalm 114:8

who turned the rock into a pool,
God supplied Israel with water at Horeb and later at Kadesh when the people thirsted in the wilderness (Exodus 17:6; Numbers 20:11). The miracle revealed that Israel’s life depended not on the barrenness of the land but on the faithfulness of the Lord. Paul identifies the rock as a picture of Christ, the spiritual Rock from whom God’s people receive sustaining grace (1 Corinthians 10:4).

the flint into a fountain of water!
Even unyielding flint obeyed its Creator and became a source of abundant water. God brought life from what appeared incapable of giving it, displaying His authority over creation and His power to meet His people in their need. The water from the rock anticipates the living water Christ gives, which satisfies the deepest thirst and springs up into eternal life (John 4:14).
