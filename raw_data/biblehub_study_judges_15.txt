Judges 15:17

And when Samson had finished speaking,
Samson’s victory song followed the Lord’s powerful deliverance of Israel through an unlikely weapon. Like Moses after the Red Sea and Deborah after the defeat of Sisera, Samson gave public expression to a victory that displayed God’s strength against Israel’s enemies. Yet the following events show that Samson’s strength did not make him independent of the Lord; he remained wholly dependent on God for life and deliverance.

The jawbone had served its purpose and was discarded after the battle. Israel’s deliverance did not rest in the weapon itself, but in the Spirit of the Lord who had come mightily upon Samson. The discarded jawbone testified that God can use weak and ordinary means to overthrow overwhelming opposition, as David later demonstrated when he rejected Saul’s armor and faced Goliath in faith.

Judges 15:19

So God opened up the hollow place in Lehi,
Samson’s desperate cry was met by the Lord’s direct and miraculous provision. At Lehi, meaning “jawbone,” the place associated with Samson’s victory by the jawbone, God caused water to flow from a hollow place in the ground. The Lord who had given Samson strength for battle did not abandon him when exhaustion and thirst brought him near death. As God brought water from the rock at Horeb for Israel, He showed again that no wilderness need is beyond His power.

Exo 17:6 Behold, I will stand there before you by the rock at Horeb.…

Water in a barren setting revealed God’s sustaining mercy and preserved the life of His servant. Samson’s victory over the Philistines had come through the power of the Spirit, and his recovery likewise came from God rather than human strength. The provision anticipates the greater spiritual refreshment Christ gives to those who come to Him in faith, for He is the source of living water.

Judges 15:20

And Samson judged Israel
Samson served as Israel’s judge through a God-given calling to begin delivering Israel from Philistine oppression (Judges 13:5). His leadership was chiefly expressed through personal acts of deliverance rather than the gathering of an army. Though his sins brought grievous consequences, the Lord used Samson’s extraordinary strength and Nazirite consecration to confront Israel’s enemies.

Jdg 3:10 The Spirit of the LORD came upon him…

Jdg 12:7 Jephthah judged Israel six years, and when he died…

1Sa 7:15 So Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life.

for twenty years
Samson’s judgeship extended through a significant period of Philistine domination and is confirmed at his death (Judges 16:31). Israel did not gain full freedom from the Philistines during those years, but Samson’s resistance restrained their power and foreshadowed the later deliverance brought through Samuel, Saul, and David.

in the days of the Philistines.
The Philistines controlled much of Israel’s western territory and possessed military advantages that made them formidable oppressors (1 Samuel 13:19–22). Their rule exposed Israel’s continuing spiritual compromise, yet God raised Samson within that oppression as a reminder that His purposes are not hindered by the strength of Israel’s enemies or the weakness of His servants.
