A Warning to the Cities of Judah

Jeremiah 26:1

At the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim son of Josiah king of Judah,
Jehoiakim came to the throne after Josiah’s death at Megiddo in 609 BC. Unlike his father, who led Judah in covenant renewal and removed idolatry, Jehoiakim resisted the Lord’s prophets and pursued evil. Egypt initially controlled Judah, but Babylon soon became the dominant power after its victory at Carchemish in 605 BC. The opening of Jehoiakim’s reign marks a critical moment when Judah still had opportunity to repent before the Babylonian judgment became unavoidable.

Although it had once been the appointed center of Israel’s worship, the Lord abandoned Shiloh because of His people’s persistent unfaithfulness, and the ark was captured by the Philistines (Psalm 78:60–61; 1 Sam. 4). Jerusalem’s temple would receive no exemption from judgment merely because God’s name had been associated with it. Sacred buildings cannot protect a people who reject the God they claim to worship.

Prophecy in the LORD’s name carried the claim of divine commission and required faithfulness to the word God had given (Deuteronomy 18:18–22). The presence of another prophet preaching the same warning showed that Jerusalem had received repeated and confirmed testimony from God.

Uriah’s family and hometown identify him as a known prophet within Judah. Kiriath-jearim lay in Judah and had once housed the ark of the covenant for many years after its return from Philistine territory (1 Samuel 7:1–2).
