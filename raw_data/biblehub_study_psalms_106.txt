Psalm 106:1

Hallelujah!
Praise (Hallelu Yah, meaning “Praise the LORD”) opens Psalm 106, calling God’s people to worship before recounting Israel’s repeated rebellion and the LORD’s enduring mercy. Israel’s failures never diminished God’s right to receive glory.

Give thanks to the LORD,
Gratitude acknowledges that every deliverance, provision, and covenant blessing comes from the LORD. David used these words when the ark was brought to Jerusalem, joining thanksgiving to the worship of God’s presence among His people (1 Chronicles 16:34).

for He is good;
The LORD’s goodness is seen in His holy character and gracious actions. He rescued Israel from Egypt, sustained them in the wilderness, and remained faithful despite their unbelief. God’s goodness reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, who gave Himself to save sinners.

Psalm 106:2

Who can describe the mighty acts of the LORD
The LORD’s works in creation, in the deliverance of Israel through the Red Sea, and in His judgments and mercies throughout history exceed human measure. Psalm 106 will recount Israel’s repeated rebellion alongside God’s enduring faithfulness, showing that His mighty acts include both righteous judgment and covenant mercy. Job 38–41 likewise reminds humanity that the wisdom and power displayed in creation cannot be fully comprehended by finite minds. Jesus revealed the same divine glory through His signs, culminating in His resurrection from the dead.

Psa 71:18 Even when I am old and gray, do not forsake me, O God,…

or fully proclaim His praise?
No human voice can adequately declare all that God deserves, yet His people are called to continual worship. Israel praised the LORD in song after He defeated Pharaoh’s army, and the Psalms summon every generation to remember His works with gratitude. The unsearchable riches of Christ and the unending praise of heaven show that worship will never exhaust the greatness of God. Faithful praise rises not from complete understanding, but from humble recognition of His holiness, power, mercy, and steadfast love.

Psalm 106:3

Blessed are those who uphold justice,
God’s blessing rests on those who order their lives by His righteous standards and defend what is right. Israel’s law required impartial judgments, honest dealings, and protection for the poor, foreigners, widows, and orphans. Justice reflects the character of the Lord, the Judge of all the earth, who neither overlooks evil nor shows favoritism. Psalm 106 recalls Israel’s repeated rebellion and God’s enduring mercy; amid that history, the psalm honors faithful obedience as the proper response to divine grace.

who practice righteousness at all times.
Righteousness is not limited to public worship, legal decisions, or moments of opportunity. God calls His people to faithful integrity in every relationship, responsibility, and season of life. The prophets condemned religious activity divorced from righteous conduct and commanded God’s people to seek justice, correct oppression, and walk humbly with Him. Such steadfast obedience cannot be produced by outward conformity alone. Jesus Christ perfectly fulfilled all righteousness, and those who belong to Him are called to reflect His character through enduring obedience.

The psalmist asks God to turn His covenant mercy toward him personally. God’s remembrance is never mere awareness; it leads to faithful action, as when He remembered Noah in the ark and brought the floodwaters to recede (Genesis 8:1). The plea rests on dependence upon the Lord’s grace rather than personal merit.

The psalmist does not seek blessing apart from Israel but identifies himself with the people to whom God gave His promises, worship, and inheritance. Exodus 14:31 records that the people feared the Lord and believed in Him and in Moses His servant. Psalm 106 also exposes the weakness of this response, for Israel soon forgot God’s works and rebelled against His guidance. Moses therefore commanded Israel to remember God’s deeds and teach them to their children (Deuteronomy 4:9).

Israel’s history repeatedly displayed the Lord’s covenant mercy. He brought His people out of Egypt, preserved them in the wilderness, gave them victory in Canaan, and raised up judges when surrounding nations oppressed them. Deliverance came not because Israel deserved it, but because God remembered His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and acted for the honor of His name.

Neh 9:27 So You delivered them into the hands of enemies…

Jdg 2:18 Whenever the LORD raised up a judge for the Israelites,…

2Ki 13:5 So the LORD gave Israel a deliverer, and they escaped…

but they were bent on rebellion
After receiving God’s help, Israel repeatedly returned to idolatry, unbelief, and disobedience. The period of the judges especially reveals this pattern: the people forsook the Lord, suffered oppression, cried out in distress, and were rescued again. Their rebellion was deliberate resistance to God’s revealed will, showing the deep corruption of the human heart and the inability of external blessings alone to produce faithfulness.

Deu 31:27 For I know how rebellious and stiff-necked you are.…

Jdg 2:19 But when the judge died, the Israelites became corrupt…

2Ki 17:14 But they would not listen, and they stiffened their necks…

and sank down in their iniquity.
Persistent sin brought humiliation, defeat, and bondage upon the nation. Israel’s decline eventually culminated in exile, when both kingdoms fell under God’s judgment. Yet the record of their failure also magnifies the need for the greater Deliverer, Jesus Christ, who rescues sinners not merely from earthly enemies but from sin, judgment, and death.

Psalm 106:44

Nevertheless He heard their cry;
Israel’s rebellion and idolatry had brought severe discipline, yet the Lord did not abandon the people of His covenant. He heard them as He had heard their groaning in Egypt, remembering His promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Divine mercy did not excuse their sin; it provided deliverance for a people who had repeatedly proved unable to save themselves. God’s readiness to hear the afflicted reveals His faithful character and His commitment to restore those who turn to Him.

He took note of their distress.
The Lord saw both the oppression Israel endured under its enemies and the deeper misery produced by its disobedience. His knowledge was personal and active, leading Him to raise deliverers during the period of the judges and to preserve Israel despite its repeated failures. God’s compassion toward His distressed people anticipates the saving work of Christ, the Good Shepherd who knows His sheep, bears their sin, and delivers them from the bondage they cannot escape on their own.

Psalm 106:45

And He remembered His covenant with them,
God acted according to His binding promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and to the covenant relationship He established with Israel. His remembering denotes purposeful intervention, as when He heard Israel’s groaning in Egypt and moved to deliver them (Exodus 2:24). Israel’s rebellion had brought deserved discipline, yet God did not abandon the people He had chosen. His faithfulness rested on His own character and pledged word.

Exo 2:24 So God heard their groaning, and He remembered His covenant…

and relented by the abundance of His loving devotion.
God withheld the full judgment Israel deserved because His mercy is abundant and enduring. He had disciplined them through oppression and exile, but His covenant love moved Him to preserve a remnant and provide hope for restoration. The same mercy reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, through whom God forgives repentant sinners and restores them to fellowship with Himself.

Psalm 106:46

He made them objects of compassion
Even under judgment for persistent rebellion, Israel was not abandoned by the covenant God. The Lord restrained the severity of their enemies and moved captors to show mercy, as He had done for Joseph in Egypt (Genesis 39:21) and later for the exiles in Babylon. Cyrus’s decree allowing Judah’s return demonstrated that kings and empires remain under God’s rule (Ezra 1:1–3). Divine discipline served restoration, preserving the people through whom the promised Messiah would come.

Exo 12:36 And the LORD gave the people such favor…

Gen 39:21 the LORD was with him and extended kindness to him…

Dan 1:9 Now God had granted Daniel favor and compassion…

to all who held them captive.
Israel’s bondage under foreign powers exposed the consequences of idolatry and covenant unfaithfulness, yet captivity could not cancel God’s promises. The Lord who turned the hearts of Israel’s captors also accomplished the greater deliverance in Christ, who frees His people from slavery to sin and brings them into the mercy of God.

Psalm 106:47

Save us, O LORD our God,
Israel appeals to Yahweh as the covenant God who alone can deliver His people. The psalm remembers repeated rebellion, judgment, and mercy, yet ends in confidence that God remains faithful to His covenant. Israel’s need for rescue points beyond political deliverance to the deeper human need for salvation from sin. Jesus fulfills that hope as the Savior who delivers His people from judgment and brings them to God.

2Ki 19:19 And now, O LORD our God, please save us from his hand,…

Psa 79:9 Help us, O God of our salvation, for the glory of Your name…

Psa 28:9 Save Your people and bless Your inheritance;…

and gather us from the nations,
The petition anticipates the regathering of Israelites scattered through conquest and exile, especially among the nations that carried them away. God promised through Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Ezekiel to restore His people to their land and renew them under His rule. The return from Babylon gave an initial fulfillment, while the prophetic hope ultimately reaches toward God’s final restoration and the gathering of redeemed people from every nation through Christ.

that we may give thanks to Your holy name,
Deliverance is sought not merely for relief, but so that God’s people may worship Him rightly. God’s holy name represents His revealed character—His faithfulness, mercy, righteousness, and power. A restored people should publicly acknowledge that their salvation came from the Lord rather than from human strength.

that we may glory in Your praise.
The desired end of salvation is joyful worship centered on God’s greatness. God rescues His people so that they may delight in Him and declare His praise before the world. All redemption finds its proper goal when those whom God has saved live for His glory.

Psalm 106:48

Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, from everlasting to everlasting.
Psalm 106 closes with worship after recounting Israel’s repeated unbelief, rebellion, and deliverance. The God who judged sin also preserved His covenant people, proving Himself faithful across every generation. His reign has no beginning or end; He remains the same God revealed in creation, in Israel’s history, and in Jesus Christ, whose kingdom will never pass away.

Let all the people say, “Amen!”
Israel was summoned to join in affirming the truth of God’s praise and the certainty of His covenant faithfulness. “Amen” was not a passive response but the congregation’s agreement that the LORD alone is worthy of trust, obedience, and worship. God’s redeemed people are called to confess together what He has done.
