The Bride and Her Beloved

Song of Solomon 5:1

I have come to my garden, my sister, my bride;
The bridegroom answers the bride’s invitation to enter her garden, portraying the mutual delight and rightful intimacy of marriage. The garden, previously described as enclosed and fruitful, now belongs to him in covenant love. “My sister, my bride” joins the language of familial devotion with marital union, emphasizing a bond marked by purity, permanence, and affection. The marriage relationship also provides a fitting picture of Christ’s faithful love for His Church, His redeemed and cherished people.

I have gathered my myrrh with my spice.
The garden yields costly fragrances, expressing the bride’s beauty, faithfulness, and readiness to please her husband. Myrrh and spices belonged to celebrations, royal settings, anointing, and burial customs. The richness of the imagery points beyond ordinary pleasure to love that is precious and costly. Christ’s love for His people was displayed supremely through His sacrificial death, where myrrh again appears in connection with His burial.

Song of Solomon 5:6

The bride finally responds, but her delay has brought painful consequences. In the love poetry of the Song, the scene portrays a real separation between husband and wife after hesitation disrupted their fellowship. Her opened door reveals renewed willingness, yet the beloved is no longer present. Believers are likewise called to receive Christ promptly and faithfully, not treating communion with Him as a matter for delay (Revelation 3:20).

Grief overtakes the bride as she recognizes what her reluctance has cost. The heart, understood as the center of thought, desire, and will, is shaken by the loss of the beloved’s nearness. Spiritual neglect does not cancel Christ’s covenant love, but it can diminish the joy and assurance of fellowship with Him. The psalmist knew a similar thirst for God’s presence (Psalm 42:1–2).

O daughters of Jerusalem.
The bride’s testimony is spoken before the women of Jerusalem, who have heard her longing and now receive her confession of love. Love rightly recognized as faithful and pure bears witness before the community. The redeemed people of God will ultimately rejoice together as the bride prepared for Christ, the heavenly Bridegroom (Revelation 21:2).
