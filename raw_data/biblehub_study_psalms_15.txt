Who May Dwell on Your Holy Mountain?

Psalm 15:1

A Psalm of David.
King David, once a shepherd in Bethlehem and later Israel’s king in Jerusalem, wrote from a life marked by both deep fellowship with God and serious personal failure. His kingship established Jerusalem as Israel’s political and worship center, giving particular force to the longing for fellowship with God in Zion.

Act 4:25 You spoke by the Holy Spirit through the mouth of Your servant,…

Mat 22:43 Jesus said to them, “How then does David in the Spirit…

O LORD,
David addresses Yahweh, Israel’s covenant God, whose holiness governs access to His presence. Israel did not approach a local deity or an impersonal power, but the living God who redeemed His people and dwelt among them.

Psa 86:1 A prayer of David. Incline Your ear, O LORD, and answer me,…

Psa 5:1-2 For the choirmaster, to be accompanied by flutes.…

Psa 130:1-2 A song of ascents. Out of the depths I cry to You, O LORD!…

who may abide in Your tent?
The tent recalls the tabernacle, where God made His dwelling known among Israel and where sacrifice was offered according to His command. David asks who may be received as a welcome guest in God’s presence, recognizing that sinners cannot claim such access on their own terms.

Psa 61:4 Let me dwell in Your tent forever and take refuge…

Psa 27:5 For in the day of trouble He will hide me in His shelter;…

Psa 23:6 Surely goodness and mercy will follow me…

Who may dwell on Your holy mountain?
God’s holy mountain points to Mount Zion, Jerusalem’s chosen place of worship and the future site of Solomon’s temple. The question reaches beyond physical proximity to the sanctuary and concerns enduring fellowship with God. The righteous character described in the following verses reflects the holiness required of those who would live before the Lord, ultimately fulfilled through the cleansing and righteousness provided by Jesus Christ.

Psa 24:3-4 Who may ascend the hill of the LORD? Who may stand…

Psa 48:1-2 A Psalm of the sons of Korah. Great is the LORD…

Psa 2:6 “I have installed My King on Zion, upon My holy mountain.”

Psalm 15:2

He who walks with integrity
The worshiper welcomed into God’s presence lives with a whole and blameless character before Him. Job was likewise called blameless and upright because he feared God and turned from evil (Job 1:1). Integrity unites public conduct and private life, refusing hypocrisy and seeking faithfulness in every relationship.

Pro 20:7 The righteous man walks with integrity;…

Pro 10:9 He who walks in integrity walks securely,…

Pro 28:6 Better a poor man who walks with integrity…

and practices righteousness,
Righteousness is shown through obedience to God’s revealed will, just treatment of others, and active rejection of evil. Israel received God’s law so that His people would reflect His holy character in daily life. Christ perfectly fulfilled righteousness, and those who belong to Him are called to display the transformed life that flows from faith.

1Jn 3:7 Little children, let no one deceive you:…

1Jn 2:29 If you know that He is righteous, you also know…

Isa 56:1 This is what the LORD says: “Maintain justice…

who speaks the truth from his heart,
Truthful speech proceeds from an inner life governed by God rather than deceit or self-interest. In Scripture, the heart encompasses the mind, will, and affections; therefore honest words reveal sincere devotion. Jesus taught that the mouth speaks from the abundance of the heart (Matthew 12:34), and God’s people are to reflect the truthfulness of the God who cannot lie.

Psa 51:6 Surely You desire truth in the inmost being;…

Isa 33:15 He who walks righteously and speaks with sincerity,…

Eph 4:25 Therefore each of you must put off falsehood…

Psalm 15:3

who has no slander on his tongue,
Righteousness reaches to a person’s speech, refusing lies, malicious reports, and words intended to damage another’s reputation. Such integrity fulfills the command against false witness and reflects the wisdom that recognizes the tongue’s power to wound or to give life. Jesus, who is Himself the truth, showed perfect faithfulness in every word.

Lev 19:16 You must not go about spreading slander among your people.…

Pro 10:18 The one who conceals hatred has lying lips…

Jas 4:11 Brothers, do not slander one another…

who does no harm to his neighbor,
Love for God is demonstrated in conduct toward those nearby. Israel’s covenant life depended on protecting the well-being, property, honor, and peace of one’s neighbor. Jesus reaffirmed this obligation as part of the greatest commandment, and the Good Samaritan showed that neighborly love extends beyond social, ethnic, and religious boundaries.

Pro 3:29 Do not devise evil against your neighbor,…

Zec 8:17 do not plot evil in your hearts against your neighbor…

who casts no scorn on his friend,
The citizen of God’s dwelling does not spread reproach, expose another’s shame, or delight in a companion’s failure. In a culture where public honor deeply affected a person’s life, such contempt could cause lasting harm. Love seeks restoration rather than disgrace, following the mercy of Christ, who endured scorn yet prayed for those who mocked Him.

Psalm 15:4

who despises the vile
The righteous person refuses to admire, excuse, or imitate those who persist in wickedness. Such rejection is moral discernment, not personal hatred or cruelty, and it accords with the fear of the LORD, which hates evil (Proverbs 8:13).

but honors those who fear the LORD,
Reverence for God marks those who order their lives by His word. The godly recognize and esteem faithful people, strengthening a community shaped by holiness, obedience, and mutual encouragement (Hebrews 10:24–25).

who does not revise a costly oath,
A promise made before God remains binding even when keeping it brings loss or hardship. Israel regarded oaths as solemn matters because the Lord hears every word. Such integrity reflects the faithfulness of God, whose promises never fail, and finds perfect fulfillment in Christ, who remained obedient even unto death.

Psalm 15:5

who lends his money without interest
The Law required Israelites to aid impoverished covenant members without profiting from their distress (Exodus 22:25; Leviticus 25:35–37). Such generosity recognized that the land and its provision belonged to the Lord and cultivated mercy within the community.

Exo 22:25 If you lend money to one of My people among you…

and refuses a bribe against the innocent.
God forbade bribes because they corrupt judgment and deny justice to those without power or influence (Exodus 23:8; Deuteronomy 16:19). Integrity protects the innocent and reflects the righteous judgment of the Lord.

Deu 16:19 Do not deny justice or show partiality…

He who does these things will never be shaken.
A life ordered by reverence for God stands secure amid changing circumstances. The righteous possess enduring stability because their confidence rests in the Lord, ultimately fulfilled in Christ, the sure foundation of all who trust in Him.
