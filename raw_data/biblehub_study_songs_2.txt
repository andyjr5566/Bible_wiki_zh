The Bride’s Admiration

The beloved woman speaks of her beauty with the imagery of a wildflower growing on the fertile coastal plain of Sharon, a land celebrated for its richness and abundance. Her words suggest natural grace rather than pride, for flowers flourish by the provision of God. Solomon’s response in the following verse honors her as uniquely precious among others. The beauty of creation points beyond itself to the wisdom and generosity of the Creator, who clothes the fields with splendor.

The bridegroom sees his beloved as beautiful and undefiled amid a fallen world marked by hardship and sin. Thorns recall the curse pronounced after Adam’s disobedience, when the ground brought forth “thorns also and thistles” (Genesis 3:18). Her loveliness is not diminished by the thorns around her; it stands out more clearly against them. The image anticipates the calling of God’s people to live in holiness within a corrupt world.

The beloved is pictured as strong, graceful, and swift in movement. Earlier, he was seen coming over the mountains in eager pursuit of his bride (Song of Solomon 2:8–9). Love is not portrayed as passive; it moves toward the beloved with delight and devotion.

Hab 3:19 GOD the Lord is my strength; He makes my feet like those…

on the mountains of Bether.
The location is otherwise unknown, though its name may suggest divided or rugged mountains. The image emphasizes the distance and barriers that stand between the lovers during the night. Yet the beloved is called to cross every obstacle, anticipating the joy of their reunion.
