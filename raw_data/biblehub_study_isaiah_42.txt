Here Is My Servant

Isaiah 42:1

“Here is My Servant,
Isaiah presents the promised Servant as the One sent by the Lord to accomplish His redemptive purpose. Israel had been called to be God’s servant and light among the nations, yet failed through sin and unbelief. Jesus Christ fulfills in perfect obedience all that Israel was called to be.

Mat 12:18 Here is My Servant, whom I have chosen…

Isa 52:13 Behold, My Servant will prosper; He will be raised…

whom I uphold,
The Father sustains the Servant throughout His earthly ministry, including His rejection, suffering, death, and resurrection. Jesus depended wholly upon the Father and carried out the work entrusted to Him.

Isa 41:10 Do not fear, for I am with you; do not be afraid…

Psa 63:8 My soul clings to You; Your right hand upholds me.

My Chosen One,
The Servant is uniquely appointed by God to bring salvation and establish His kingdom. Jesus is the Messiah chosen before the foundation of the world, sent to redeem His people and reign over all nations.

in whom My soul delights.
The Father’s delight rests on the Son because of His perfect holiness and obedience. At Jesus’ baptism and transfiguration, the Father openly declared His pleasure in Him, identifying Jesus as the beloved Son.

The Holy Spirit descended upon Jesus at His baptism, marking Him for His public ministry. Empowered by the Spirit, Jesus proclaimed the kingdom, healed the afflicted, cast out demons, and fulfilled the Father’s will without measure.

The Servant brings God’s righteous rule beyond Israel to every people. Jesus began this work through His ministry and the proclamation of the gospel among the Gentiles, and He will complete it when He returns to judge evil, vindicate His people, and establish lasting righteousness.

Isaiah 42:2

He will not cry out
The Lord’s Servant will not advance His mission through clamor, intimidation, or self-exaltation. Unlike earthly rulers who demand attention and enforce their claims by displays of power, Jesus (the Lord saves) came in humility, submitting to the Father’s will and serving those in need.

or raise His voice,
His authority rests in His identity as God’s chosen Servant and in the truth He speaks, not in coercion. Jesus taught openly and with authority, yet He did not manipulate crowds or compel faith by force. His gentleness revealed strength governed by perfect obedience and love.

Mat 27:12-14 And when He was accused by the chief priests and elders,…

Psa 39:2 I was speechless and still; I remained silent…

nor make His voice heard in the streets.
The Servant would not seek public acclaim or political popularity. Matthew 12:19 applies these words directly to Christ, whose ministry fulfilled Isaiah’s prophecy. Although multitudes followed Him, Jesus often withdrew for prayer and sometimes commanded those He healed not to publicize His works. His purpose was to establish God’s justice and bring salvation, not to gain the passing praise of the crowds.

Isaiah 42:3

A bruised reed He will not break
The Servant deals tenderly with those weakened by sin, suffering, failure, or oppression. A damaged reed growing among Israel’s marshes had little practical value and could easily be discarded, yet the Servant does not treat wounded people as worthless. He understands human frailty and receives the brokenhearted with mercy. Jesus displayed this compassion toward the sick, the outcast, repentant sinners, and those burdened by shame. His ministry fulfilled the Father’s compassion described in Psalm 103:13–14, and He calls the weary to find rest in Him.

Mat 12:20 A bruised reed He will not break, and a smoldering wick…

Psa 147:3 He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds.

Ezk 34:16 I will seek the lost, bring back the strays…

and a smoldering wick He will not extinguish;
The Servant also preserves the faintest remaining evidence of faith and hope. A wick producing smoke rather than light would ordinarily be put out and replaced, but He patiently supplies what is needed for the flame to burn again. Jesus did not reject struggling disciples, though their faith was often fearful and weak. He restored Peter after his denial, received the father who cried, “I believe; help my unbelief,” and strengthened His followers for service. His gentleness does not excuse sin, but it leads repentant people toward restoration rather than despair.

The Servant’s compassion is joined to unwavering righteousness. He will establish God’s justice in a world marked by evil, falsehood, violence, and oppression. Jesus fulfilled this mission through His obedient life, sacrificial death, and resurrection, providing a righteous basis for the forgiveness of sinners. His kingdom will ultimately bring the full restoration of God’s order, when evil is judged and righteousness reigns.

His justice includes the judgment of evil, the vindication of the oppressed, the removal of sin’s curse, and the establishment of lasting peace. The promised kingdom of the Messiah will fulfill the prophecy that He will reign on David’s throne with justice and righteousness forever.

Distant coastlands and nations beyond Israel will look to the Servant for salvation and instruction. The Gospel has carried Christ’s authority and saving promise throughout the world, gathering people from every tribe and language into His kingdom. Under the new covenant, God writes His law upon the hearts of His people, and the nations find their hope in the King who will one day renew all creation.

Isaiah 42:6

“I, the LORD, have called you for a righteous purpose,
The Lord Himself appoints His Servant according to His holy plan. Isaiah 42:1–4 identifies the Servant as the One in whom God delights, upon whom He places His Spirit. Matthew applies this passage to Jesus Christ and His ministry (Matthew 12:18–21). Christ came in perfect obedience to accomplish the Father’s righteous will and to bring justice without failing or being discouraged.

The Father’s sustaining presence accompanies the Servant in every part of His mission. Jesus walked in dependence upon the Father, doing the works and speaking the words entrusted to Him. Neither opposition, temptation, suffering, nor death could turn Him from the work appointed for Him.

God preserved His Servant until the appointed hour of His sacrifice. Attempts to destroy Jesus before His time did not succeed, because His life was laid down according to the Father’s purpose and His own willing obedience. Even through death, the Father vindicated Him by raising Him from the grave.

By His blood, He secured forgiveness of sins, reconciliation with God, and the gift of the Holy Spirit for all who believe. Isaiah 42:8

I am the LORD; that is My name!
Yahweh revealed Himself to Moses as the eternal, self-existent God who would redeem Israel from Egypt and remain faithful to His covenant promises (Exodus 3:14–15). The name declares His unmatched authority over creation, nations, and history. Israel’s God was not one deity among many but the living Lord who alone saves, judges, and restores.

Exo 3:14-15 God said to Moses, “I AM WHO I AM. This is what you are”…

I will not yield My glory to another
God alone possesses supreme majesty, honor, and power. His glory cannot rightly be assigned to kings, nations, angels, or false gods. The Lord’s exclusive claim stands against every form of polytheism and fulfills the confession that “the LORD is one” (Deuteronomy 6:4). His people are therefore called to give Him undivided worship and trust.

Isa 48:11 For My own sake, My very own sake, I will act;…

Psa 115:1 Not to us, O LORD, not to us, but to Your name be the glory…

Isa 45:5 I am the LORD, and there is no other; there is no God but Me…

or My praise to idols.
Idols were lifeless images fashioned by human hands, yet Israel repeatedly faced the temptation to seek help from them, as in the worship of the golden calf (Exodus 32). Isaiah exposes their helplessness and folly in contrast to the Lord who speaks, acts, and foretells what will come (Isaiah 44:9–20). Praise belongs to God alone, and true worship is rendered to Him in spirit and truth (John 4:23–24).

Isaiah 42:9

God’s fulfilled judgments and deliverances testify that He governs history and keeps His word. The fall of kingdoms, the Assyrian threat against Judah, and Jerusalem’s deliverance had occurred according to His announcements. Fulfillment marks the Lord as the true God, unlike idols that can neither foresee nor accomplish events.

God now reveals the advancing work of redemption, centered in His Servant, who will bring justice, light, and salvation beyond Israel to the nations. The promise reaches toward the new covenant, in which God forgives sin and writes His law on the hearts of His people.

God’s saving work through His Servant calls forth worship suited to the greatness of His redemption. The new song celebrates the Lord’s revealed power and anticipates the salvation that reaches its fullness in the Messiah and the new covenant He establishes. Isaiah 42:15

I will lay waste the mountains and hills
The Lord declares His power to bring down every strength that stands against His purposes. Mountains and hills, symbols of permanence and human might, cannot endure before the Creator. High places also served as centers of pagan worship in Israel and the surrounding nations; their destruction portrays the overthrow of idolatry and proud powers. Isaiah elsewhere connects lowered mountains with the humbling of all that exalts itself before God (Isaiah 40:4).

and dry up all their vegetation.
The removal of vegetation pictures the withdrawal of life, abundance, and agricultural provision. In a land dependent on seasonal rain, such devastation meant famine and national collapse. God had promised fruitful fields as a covenant blessing for obedience, while drought and barrenness accompanied rebellion (Deuteronomy 28:23–24). The judgment reaches beyond rulers and armies to the resources on which rebellious nations depend.

The Lord rules the waters as fully as He rules the nations. He divided the Red Sea to deliver Israel from Egypt and stopped the Jordan for Israel’s entrance into the Promised Land (Exodus 14; Joshua 3). Here the drying of rivers announces judgment upon those who oppose Him, while also preparing a way for His people. No natural barrier can prevent the Lord from accomplishing redemption or judgment.

Isaiah 42:16

I will lead the blind by a way they did not know;
The Lord promises to take the initiative for people unable to find the path of truth and safety by their own wisdom. Israel’s blindness had been exposed through unbelief and disobedience, yet God had not abandoned His covenant people. The Servant introduced earlier in the chapter is appointed to open blind eyes (Isaiah 42:6–7), pointing ultimately to Jesus Christ, the Light of the world, who gives spiritual sight to those who come to Him.

God led Israel through the wilderness by the pillar of cloud and fire, bringing them through territory they could not navigate alone (Exodus 13:21–22). He remains faithful to direct His people when the way ahead is hidden, calling them to trust His wisdom rather than rely on their own understanding (Proverbs 3:5–6).
