True Riches

Proverbs 23:1

When you sit down to dine with a ruler,
A meal at a ruler’s table could bring honor, advancement, and opportunity, but it also placed a person within the ruler’s influence. Royal hospitality often carried expectations of loyalty, gratitude, or compromise. Daniel faced such a setting in Babylon when he was assigned food from the king’s table, yet he refused to defile himself and remained faithful to God (Daniel 1:8). Wisdom requires humility before human authority and unwavering allegiance to the Lord above every earthly ruler.

The food may be freely offered, yet the banquet can conceal enticements that appeal to appetite, ambition, and fear. The following warnings against gluttony and deceptive delicacies show that self-control is necessary when prosperity is offered by those with power (Proverbs 23:2–3). A believer must judge every opportunity by God’s standards, refusing benefits that demand silence toward sin or participation in evil.

Proverbs 23:4

Do not wear yourself out to get rich;
Diligent labor is commended in Proverbs, but life must not be consumed by anxious striving for wealth. Riches can be received as God’s provision, yet they are uncertain and cannot secure the soul. Israel’s covenant life called God’s people to trust Him, care for others, observe Sabbath rest, and remember that the Lord gives power to gain wealth. The love of money leads many into temptation and spiritual ruin, while wisdom is more valuable than material abundance.

Proverbs 23:5

When you glance at wealth, it disappears,
Material wealth is unstable and cannot provide lasting security. Riches in the ancient world included land, livestock, grain, silver, and gold, yet all could be diminished through drought, famine, war, theft, sickness, or poor judgment. Ecclesiastes 5:10 warns that the love of money never satisfies, because possessions continually promise more than they can deliver. God does not condemn diligent labor or responsible provision, but He forbids trusting riches as though they could preserve life or secure the future.

for it makes wings for itself
Wealth may depart through circumstances beyond human control, exposing the weakness of confidence placed in earthly possessions. The rich fool in Luke 12:16–21 stored abundance for himself but died before he could enjoy it, having failed to be rich toward God. Prosperity can become spiritually dangerous when it produces self-reliance, forgetfulness of God, or indifference toward others. Wisdom holds possessions with gratitude and open hands, recognizing God as the giver and sustainer of every good provision.

Lasting security rests not in what can be gained or kept, but in God’s faithful care and the eternal inheritance of those who belong to Christ.

Saying 10

Proverbs 23:9

Do not speak to a fool,
Wisdom should not be offered indiscriminately to a person who is committed to rejecting correction and mocking what is right. Solomon does not forbid compassionate witness or necessary rebuke, but he warns against fruitless argument with one whose heart is hardened. Proverbs 26:4–5 likewise calls for discernment in answering folly. Jesus gave a similar warning when He spoke of not casting what is holy before those who will trample it underfoot (Matthew 7:6).

Pro 14:7 Stay away from a foolish man; you will gain no knowledge…

Pro 29:9 If a wise man goes to court with a fool,…

for he will despise the wisdom of your words.
A fool’s rejection of sound counsel reveals a deeper resistance to God, from whom true wisdom comes (Proverbs 1:7). Rather than receiving instruction, he treats it with contempt and may turn against the one who offers it. The message of the cross is also foolishness to those who are perishing, yet Christ is the wisdom and power of God to those who believe (1 Corinthians 1:18, 24). Believers should speak truth faithfully while recognizing that only the Lord can give a receptive heart.

Saying 11

Proverbs 23:10

Do not move an ancient boundary stone
Boundary stones marked the inherited limits of family land in Israel. Moving one secretly stole from a neighbor and violated the allotments established under God’s covenant. Deuteronomy 19:14 and 27:17 condemn such fraud, recognizing that land often sustained a household for generations. Respect for boundaries upheld justice, protected inheritance, and acknowledged God as the ultimate giver of the land.

Deu 19:14 You must not move your neighbor’s boundary marker…

Deu 27:17 Cursed is he who moves his neighbor’s boundary stone.…

or encroach on the fields of the fatherless,
Orphans were especially vulnerable because they lacked a father to defend their legal and economic interests. Taking part of their land exploited weakness and invited God’s judgment. The Lord identifies Himself as Father of the fatherless and defender of the oppressed (Psalm 68:5). Proverbs 23:11 warns that their Redeemer is mighty; He will contend against those who defraud them.

God Himself defends the fatherless and vulnerable whose inheritance or property has been threatened. In Israel, a kinsman-redeemer protected a relative’s land, family line, and legal rights, as Boaz did for Ruth and Naomi. The Lord is not limited by human weakness or social standing; His covenant faithfulness makes Him the powerful guardian of those who have no earthly protector. His redeeming care ultimately points to Jesus Christ, who delivers His people from sin and secures their eternal inheritance.

Christ also intercedes for His people before the Father, assuring believers that their final defense rests in the righteous judgment of God.

Asaph confessed this struggle when he saw the prosperity of the wicked, but worship restored his perspective on their final end (Psalm 73:16–19). Earthly success apart from God is temporary, while sin brings judgment and separation from Him. Israel’s history repeatedly showed the danger of admiring the practices and fortunes of surrounding pagan nations rather than trusting the LORD.

Proverbs 23:20

Do not join those who drink too much wine
Wine was a common part of life in Israel, but Scripture consistently condemns drunkenness and the loss of judgment it produces. The warning concerns companionship as well as personal conduct: those who make intoxication their habit can draw others into the same bondage. Proverbs calls the son to choose companions who strengthen wisdom, self-control, and reverence for the Lord rather than those whose appetites govern them. Drunkenness dulls discernment, weakens moral restraint, and often brings shame, conflict, and waste. Believers are called to be sober-minded and filled with the Spirit rather than controlled by wine.

Meat was often connected with feasts, sacrifices, and occasions of prosperity rather than ordinary daily meals. Gorging on it portrays unchecked indulgence and a life directed by bodily appetite. Proverbs 23:21 explains that drunkards and gluttons come to poverty, for habitual excess consumes resources and destroys diligence. The danger reaches beyond food and drink: a heart devoted to pleasure can become indifferent to wisdom, responsibility, and the needs of others. God calls His people to receive His gifts with gratitude while exercising the discipline that keeps earthly desires in their proper place.

Alcoholic excess and habitual self-indulgence consume resources, weaken judgment, and erode the discipline required to provide for oneself and one’s household. God gives material goods as blessings to be received with gratitude and managed faithfully, not squandered in pursuit of temporary pleasure. Such indulgence often brings not only financial loss but also spiritual dullness and broken responsibilities.

Saying 17

Proverbs 23:22

Listen to your father who gave you life,
God ordinarily gives children their first instruction, discipline, and example through their parents. A father bears responsibility to lead his household in the fear of the Lord, teaching God’s commands diligently in daily life (Deuteronomy 6:6–7). Listening involves receiving correction with humility rather than rejecting the wisdom gained through a parent’s years and duties. Such honor belongs to the moral order established by God and reflects the command to honor father and mother (Exodus 20:12).

Honor for a mother does not diminish when age brings weakness, dependence, or changing needs. Israel’s family life required children to care for aging parents rather than treating them as burdens or meeting them with contempt. God commands reverence for both father and mother (Leviticus 19:3), and neglect of either violates the spirit of His law. Jesus Himself provided for His mother from the cross, entrusting her to John’s care (John 19:26–27), showing that faithful love remains active through the end of a parent’s life.

Proverbs 23:23

Invest in truth
Truth rests in the character of God, whose word is wholly true and endures forever (Psalm 119:160; John 17:17). Israel repeatedly suffered when false prophets promised peace while concealing God’s judgment (Jeremiah 14:14). God’s people must seek His revealed will and order their lives by it.

Pro 3:3-4 Never let loving devotion or faithfulness leave you;…

Jhn 17:17 Sanctify them by the truth; Your word is truth.

and never sell it—
Truth must not be surrendered for comfort, approval, advantage, or temporary relief. Esau’s sale of his birthright showed the lasting loss that can follow a momentary bargain (Hebrews 12:16–17). No earthly gain can equal the value of faithfulness to God (Matthew 16:26).

1Ti 6:20 O Timothy, guard what has been entrusted to you.…

2Jn 1:8 Watch yourselves, so that you do not lose…

in wisdom
Wisdom enables believers to apply God’s truth rightly in conduct, judgment, and relationships. Solomon received wisdom from God because he desired to govern according to God’s ways rather than pursue riches or honor (1 Kings 3:9–12). Wisdom leads toward life because it begins with reverence for the Lord (Proverbs 9:10).

Pro 2:6 For the LORD gives wisdom; from His mouth…

Jas 1:5 Now if any of you lacks wisdom, he should ask God,…

and instruction
God forms His people through teaching, correction, and discipline. The Law commanded Israel to pass God’s commands faithfully from parents to children, making instruction central to covenant life (Deuteronomy 6:1–9). Loving correction guards the heart from folly and directs it toward righteousness (Proverbs 3:11–12).

Pro 1:8-9 Listen, my son, to your father’s instruction,…

2Ti 3:16-17 All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for instruction,…

and understanding.
Understanding gives discernment to recognize God’s purposes amid life’s choices and pressures. It grows through attention to God’s word, obedience, and the illumination God gives His people (Ephesians 1:17–18). Truth, wisdom, instruction, and understanding belong together; they are treasures to acquire diligently and preserve faithfully.

A child’s godly character brings profound joy to his parents, honoring both the household and the Lord. In Israel, a son’s conduct affected the standing and legacy of his family, yet the deeper blessing was to see him fear God and walk in obedience. Such joy reflects the Father’s delight in those who belong to Him and bear the fruit of righteousness through faith.

Proverbs repeatedly joins wisdom with the fear of the Lord, pointing ultimately to Christ, “the wisdom of God,” in whom God’s people learn to walk wisely.

Proverbs repeatedly connects parental joy with a son or daughter who fears the Lord, rejects sin, and walks in wisdom. In Israel, a family’s faithfulness affected its standing within the community, so the godly life of a child became a visible blessing to father and mother alike.

Saying 18

Proverbs 23:26

My son, give me your heart,
Wisdom begins with willing devotion. The father seeks more than outward compliance; he calls for the son’s trust, affections, judgment, and will to be shaped by godly instruction. Proverbs repeatedly presents the father’s counsel as a safeguard against sexual sin, greed, drunkenness, and companionship with the wicked. Such wholehearted receptiveness ultimately belongs to the Lord, who commands His people to love Him with all their heart, soul, and strength (Deuteronomy 6:5). A divided heart leaves room for temptation, but a heart yielded to God is prepared to receive correction and walk in truth.

Pro 3:1 My son, do not forget my teaching, but let your heart…

Deu 6:5 And you shall love the LORD your God with all your heart…

and let your eyes delight in my ways.
The son must not merely hear wisdom but gladly fix his attention on the path it sets before him. His eyes will otherwise be drawn toward alluring but destructive ways, especially the adulteress described in the surrounding context (Proverbs 23:27–28). God’s ways lead in a clear and righteous path, illuminated by His Word (Psalm 119:105). Delight in that path grows as obedience reveals the goodness of God’s commands and preserves the believer from the sorrow produced by sin.

Proverbs 23:27

For a prostitute is a deep pit,
Sexual sin is portrayed as a concealed trap that draws a person downward into bondage, shame, and ruin. Like the cistern into which Joseph was cast or the muddy pit that confined Jeremiah, a deep pit offered little ability to escape without help. Illicit intimacy may appear inviting, but it separates the sinner from wisdom, damages households, and hardens the heart against God. Proverbs repeatedly warns that the path of sexual immorality leads toward death rather than life.

Pro 22:14 The mouth of an adulteress is a deep pit;…

Pro 7:22-27 He follows her on impulse, like an ox going to the slaughter…

and an adulteress is a narrow well.
A narrow well could endanger anyone who leaned too far over its opening or descended without secure footing. Adultery promises satisfaction while narrowing a person’s life through secrecy, fear, broken trust, and judgment. Marriage is God’s covenantal gift, intended to reflect faithful love and mutual devotion; adultery profanes that covenant and harms all who are joined to it. Deliverance from such entanglement requires repentance, self-control, and obedience to the Lord.

Sexual immorality conceals its ruin beneath promises of pleasure, secrecy, and escape. Like bandits who watched isolated roads for vulnerable travelers, the immoral woman seeks an unsuspecting victim and draws him toward destruction. Proverbs repeatedly warns that adultery does not remain a private indulgence; it brings dishonor, shattered households, loss of possessions, and judgment before God. The danger requires vigilance, wisdom, and a heart guarded by the fear of the Lord.

Those who yield to such temptation become treacherous toward God, spouses, families, and covenant obligations. Sin rarely confines its damage to one person, for unfaithfulness spreads through broken trust, corrupt counsel, and harmful example. God calls His people to faithfulness in heart and conduct, knowing that moral compromise can enslave and ruin those who disregard His commands.

The people described are those who remain at drink long after legitimate refreshment has become intoxication. Wine was common in Israel, but Scripture repeatedly condemns the loss of judgment, self-control, and moral restraint that drunkenness produces. Proverbs 23:29–35 links such indulgence with sorrow, conflict, wounds, distorted vision, and enslaving desire.

Proverbs 23:31

Do not gaze at wine while it is red,
Wine was a common part of ancient meals and celebrations, yet its beauty could awaken a desire that mastered the drinker. The warning addresses deliberate fascination with what promises pleasure while concealing danger. Scripture does not treat wine itself as inherently evil, but repeatedly condemns drunkenness and the loss of judgment it brings (Proverbs 20:1; Ephesians 5:18).

The wine’s appearance is presented as an enticement, much as forbidden fruit appeared “pleasing to the eye” before it brought ruin (Genesis 3:6). Self-control, produced by the Holy Spirit, guards believers from pleasures that promise ease but lead to bondage (Galatians 5:22–23).

Proverbs 23:33

Your eyes will see strange things,
Alcohol clouds perception and weakens judgment, producing distorted sights, reckless imaginings, and a false sense of security. Proverbs portrays drunkenness as a deceiver that promises pleasure but ends in pain, conflict, and shame (Proverbs 23:29–35). What the intoxicated person believes he sees or understands cannot be trusted. Such impairment also illustrates the spiritual danger of surrendering discernment to any influence that draws the heart away from truth. God calls His people to sobriety, vigilance, and clear-minded obedience (1 Peter 5:8).

Deu 28:34 You will be driven mad by the sights you see.

Pro 20:1 Wine is a mocker, strong drink is a brawler,…

and your mind will utter perversities.
A mind ruled by drink gives voice to thoughts that restraint and conscience would otherwise suppress. Corrupt, foolish, cruel, or sexually immoral words may proceed from a heart no longer governed by wisdom. Scripture joins sober thinking with godly speech, since the tongue reveals what fills the inner person (Matthew 12:34; James 3:6–10). Drunkenness therefore damages more than the body; it compromises moral judgment and can lead a person to speak and act against the fear of the Lord.

Proverbs 23:34

You will be like one sleeping on the high seas
Drunkenness leaves a person exposed to danger while dulling the ability to recognize it. Like a sailor trying to sleep amid the heaving waves, the drunkard loses stability, judgment, and control. Proverbs 23:29–35 describes the sorrow, wounds, strife, and deception produced by wine when it is pursued for pleasure rather than received with self-control. The restless sea also pictures the condition of the wicked, who cannot find peace apart from God (Isaiah 57:20). Christ alone gives secure rest amid life’s storms (Matthew 8:23–27).

or lying on the top of a mast:
A man lying high on a ship’s mast would be vulnerable to every movement of the vessel and could fall without warning. The image exposes the false security of intoxication: the drunkard may feel carefree, yet he stands in immediate peril. Wisdom calls God’s people to sobriety, vigilance, and a life built on the sure foundation of Christ rather than on pleasures that weaken the mind and enslave the heart (1 Corinthians 3:11; 1 Peter 5:8).

Proverbs 23:35

“They struck me, but I feel no pain!
Intoxication dulls the drunkard’s awareness of bodily harm and moral danger. He has lost the self-control needed to recognize the damage being done to him. Sin likewise hardens the heart when repeatedly indulged, making a person less responsive to correction, conscience, and the word of God.

Pro 20:1 Wine is a mocker, strong drink is a brawler,…

They beat me, but I did not know it!
The imagined blows reveal that drunkenness brings real consequences even when the victim refuses to acknowledge them. The drunkard is injured, shamed, and endangered, yet alcohol has blinded him to his condition. Such blindness resembles the condition described in Isaiah 6:9–10, where people see and hear God’s truth but remain unwilling to perceive its meaning.

Rather than awakening to repentance, the drunkard’s first desire is to return to what has enslaved him. The craving exposes the deceitfulness of addiction: what promises relief produces misery, yet still demands more. No created pleasure can satisfy the thirst of the soul. Jesus alone gives the living water that brings lasting life to those who come to Him in faith (John 4:13–14).
