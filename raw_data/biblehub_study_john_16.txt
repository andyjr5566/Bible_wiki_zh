Persecution Foretold

John 16:1

“I have told you these things
Jesus had warned the disciples that the world would hate them because it hated Him first (John 15:18–21). His forewarning included hostility from religious leaders, rejection by society, and opposition that would intensify after His departure. The disciples were not to interpret persecution as evidence that Jesus had failed or abandoned them. His words prepared them to recognize suffering for His name as part of faithful discipleship.

Mrk 13:23 So be on your guard; I have told you everything in advance.

so that you will not fall away.
Jesus sought to guard His followers from apostasy when hardship came. Jewish authorities would expel believers in Christ (the Anointed One) from the synagogue (assembly), and some would even believe that killing Christians served God (John [Yahweh is gracious] 16:2). Such trials would test whether the disciples trusted Christ’s promises or yielded to fear and public pressure. Their perseverance would rest not in human resolve alone, but in the abiding presence and witness of the Holy Spirit, whom Jesus promised to send (John 16:7–15).

John 16:2

They will put you out of the synagogues.
Expulsion from the synagogue brought religious, social, and often economic isolation, since synagogue life shaped the identity and community of Jewish families. Jewish believers who confessed Jesus as the Messiah could be treated as dangerous dissenters, as seen in the fear of the man born blind’s parents (John 9:22). Jesus prepared His disciples for rejection even from those closest to them, showing that allegiance to Him could divide households and communities.

Jhn 9:22 His parents said this because they were afraid of the Jews.…

Jhn 12:42 Nevertheless, many of the leaders believed in Him.…

In fact, a time is coming when anyone who kills you will think he is offering a service to God.
Religious zeal without submission to God’s truth can become a justification for cruelty. Saul of Tarsus persecuted believers with such conviction before meeting the risen Christ, seeking to imprison and destroy those who called on Jesus’ name (Acts 8:1–3; 9:1–2; 26:9–11). Stephen’s martyrdom and later persecutions fulfilled Jesus’ warning. Those who opposed Christ’s followers believed they were defending God’s honor, yet they were resisting the God who had revealed Himself in His Son.

Sin is humanity’s rebellion against God, displayed in disobedient actions, corrupt desires, and refusal to live under His authority. From Adam’s fall in Genesis 3 onward, sin has brought guilt, death, and separation from God. The Law exposed sin’s seriousness and demonstrated that no sinner can establish righteousness before God by personal effort. Jesus came to bear sin’s judgment, grant forgiveness, and reconcile believers to the Father. The Holy Spirit convicts the world by uncovering its true condition before God and its need for the Savior.

Although the disciples had followed Him for about three years and received extensive instruction, His death, resurrection, ascension, and the establishment of the church would reveal further dimensions of God’s redemptive plan. God’s revelation had long unfolded progressively, from His promises to the patriarchs through the Law, the prophets, and finally the coming of His Son. The apostles would receive further instruction through the risen Christ and the Holy Spirit, who would lead them to proclaim the completed gospel and preserve its truth in the New Testament Scriptures.
