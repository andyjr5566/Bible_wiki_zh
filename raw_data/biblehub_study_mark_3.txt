His repeated presence showed His commitment to proclaiming God’s kingdom within the covenant community. The Sabbath had been given as a gift of rest and worship, directing Israel to remember the Creator and Redeemer (Exodus 20:8–11; Deuteronomy 5:12–15). Jesus would show that the Sabbath was never meant to prevent acts of love or leave suffering untouched. Doing good, preserving life, and extending mercy accord with God’s purpose for the day He made holy.

Mountains repeatedly mark divine revelation in Scripture: Moses received the Law at Sinai, and Elijah heard the Lord at Horeb. Jesus now acts with divine authority as He prepares to appoint the Twelve, a number recalling the twelve tribes of Israel and signaling the gathering of God’s renewed people.

Jesus knew the men He called and intended to shape them into witnesses of His ministry, death, resurrection, and kingdom.
