Elijah Flees from Jezebel

1 Kings 19:1

Now Ahab told Jezebel everything that Elijah had done
Ahab reported the events on Mount Carmel to Jezebel, including the Lord’s answer by fire, the confession of the people, and Elijah’s decisive confrontation with Baal’s prophets. Although Ahab had witnessed the Lord’s unmistakable power, he did not lead Israel in repentance or remove Baal worship from the kingdom. Jezebel remained the dominant force behind Israel’s idolatry and had previously sought to destroy the prophets of the Lord. Her response would reveal that the victory at Carmel had not yet brought national reformation.

Their death accorded with the covenant judgment against those who led Israel into idolatry (Deuteronomy 13:1–5). Baal had been exposed as powerless, while the Lord had shown Himself to be the living God who alone commands the fire, rain, and allegiance of His people. Yet Jezebel’s determination to preserve Baal worship prepared the way for renewed conflict between the prophet and the royal house.

By sending a royal messenger, Jezebel delivered an urgent, official threat against the prophet who had called Israel back to the LORD.

Elijah’s journey from the northern kingdom to this southern boundary reveals the extent of his retreat. Though he sought physical safety, the Lord remained his true refuge and strength.

The Call of Elisha

1 Kings 19:19

So Elijah departed and found Elisha son of Shaphat.
Elijah obeyed the Lord’s command to appoint a successor (1 Kings 19:16). Elisha’s identification as Shaphat’s son places him within a known family and emphasizes that God called him from an established household rather than from isolation or poverty. His name, “God is salvation,” suited the ministry he would later exercise among a nation marked by idolatry and spiritual decline. God preserved prophetic witness in Israel by preparing Elisha to continue the work entrusted to Elijah.

He was plowing with twelve teams of oxen, and he was with the twelfth team.
Twelve yoke of oxen indicate that Elisha’s family possessed significant land and resources. Elisha was not merely overseeing the work; he was laboring with the final team, demonstrating diligence and readiness to serve. The twelve teams also recall the twelve tribes of Israel, among whom Elisha would soon minister. God called him from ordinary faithfulness in daily work into a public ministry of confronting sin, proclaiming God’s word, and extending mercy.

Like other servants called by God, Elisha was summoned to leave the security of his former life and follow the path appointed for him.
