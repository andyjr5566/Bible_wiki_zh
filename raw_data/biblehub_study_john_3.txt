Jesus and Nicodemus

John 3:1

Now there was a man of the Pharisees
The Pharisees were an influential religious movement known for careful observance of the Law of Moses and the traditions developed around it. Many sought sincerely to preserve Israel’s holiness, yet Jesus repeatedly exposed the danger of honoring religious customs while neglecting mercy, faithfulness, and the heart’s need for God.

Nicodemus (whose Greek name means “victory of the people”) appears only in John’s Gospel. He came to Jesus at night, later urged fairness toward Him before the council, and finally helped prepare Jesus’ body for burial. His journey reveals a man drawn from spiritual uncertainty toward open identification with the crucified Christ.

He came to Jesus at night
Nicodemus, a Pharisee and member of the Jewish ruling council, sought Jesus privately under cover of darkness. Fear of other leaders may have contributed to his caution, though nighttime also allowed for an uninterrupted conversation. John’s Gospel repeatedly contrasts darkness with the light brought by Christ, making Nicodemus’s visit a fitting picture of a respected teacher beginning to seek the One who is the light of the world. His later appearances show a growing willingness to identify with Jesus (John 7:50–52; 19:39).

John 3:35

The Father loves the Son
The Father’s eternal love for the Son reveals the perfect unity of the Godhead and stands behind the Son’s saving mission in the world. Jesus came not as a reluctant servant but as the beloved Son, sent by the Father to give eternal life to all who believe in Him. That divine love is displayed in the cross, where the Father gave His Son for sinners, and in the Son’s willing obedience to the Father.

The Father has entrusted the Son with supreme authority over creation, salvation, judgment, and the destiny of every person. Jesus fulfills the promised reign of the Messiah, receiving dominion that belongs to God alone. John the Baptist therefore directs his disciples away from himself and toward Christ, whose authority demands faith and whose hands hold both eternal life and final judgment.

John 3:36

Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life.
Saving faith rests personally in Jesus Christ, God’s eternal Son and the promised Messiah. Eternal life begins now through reconciliation with God and continues forever in His presence. John has already declared that God gave His Son so that believers would not perish but have eternal life (John 3:16). Isaiah foretold that the Servant would bear sin and justify many (Isaiah 53:11); Jesus fulfills that promise through His death and resurrection.

Whoever rejects the Son will not see life.
To reject Jesus is to refuse the One whom the Father sent as Savior and King. Such unbelief leaves a person separated from God and unable to receive the life found only in His Son. Moses had set before Israel life and death, calling God’s people to love and obey Him (Deuteronomy 30:19–20). The apostles likewise proclaimed that salvation is found in no other name than Jesus Christ (Acts 4:12).

Instead, the wrath of God remains on him.”
God’s wrath is His holy and righteous judgment against sin and rebellion. Apart from Christ, humanity remains under condemnation because sin has not been forgiven. Christ bore judgment for all who believe in Him, delivering them from the wrath to come (Romans 5:9). The promise and warning together reveal the urgency of responding to the Son with faith.
