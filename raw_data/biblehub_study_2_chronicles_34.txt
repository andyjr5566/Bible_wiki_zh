Josiah was eight years old when he became king,
Amon’s assassination had exposed the moral and political disorder produced by Judah’s long rebellion. The people of the land removed the conspirators and placed Josiah on the throne, preserving David’s royal line. His youth made faithful guidance essential, and the Lord raised up servants such as Hilkiah the priest to aid the king’s later reforms. Like Joash, who also began reigning as a child, Josiah demonstrates that God is not limited by human age or circumstance.

Josiah’s reform exposed the emptiness of false worship and called Judah back to the God who had revealed Himself through His covenant and His word.

The severity of the judgment showed that false worship was not a harmless addition to devotion to the Lord but a covenant-breaking rebellion. By destroying its priests, altars, and sacred objects, Josiah rejected every attempt to blend the worship of Yahweh with pagan practice.

2 Chronicles 34:33

And Josiah removed all the abominations from all the lands belonging to the Israelites,
Josiah carried the covenant reforms beyond Judah into territories once governed by the northern kingdom. With Assyrian power declining, he was able to dismantle shrines, images, altars, and other idolatrous practices that had corrupted Israel’s worship. The Law required Israel to destroy the places devoted to false gods rather than blend their worship with devotion to the LORD (Deuteronomy 12:2–3; 7:25–26). Josiah’s work expressed the truth that covenant faithfulness demanded the removal of rivals to God, not merely the addition of proper religious observances.

and he required everyone in Israel to serve the LORD their God.
Josiah summoned the people under his influence to exclusive service of the LORD, the God who had redeemed their fathers from Egypt and given them the land. His leadership followed the pattern of covenant renewal under Joshua, who called Israel to put away foreign gods and serve the LORD alone (Joshua 24:14–25). The king’s authority was to be exercised under God’s Law, guiding the nation toward obedience rather than establishing worship according to human preference (Deuteronomy 17:18–20). Josiah’s call also anticipates Christ’s universal summons to worship and serve God alone (Matthew 4:10).
