Psalm 82:1

A Psalm of Asaph.
Asaph served as a leading Levite musician under David and continued in temple worship during Solomon’s reign. His psalms proclaim God’s holiness, justice, and rule over nations and rulers. Psalm 82 confronts every authority that resists God’s righteous standards.

God presides in the divine assembly;
The Lord stands as King over the heavenly court, surrounded by spiritual beings who remain subject to His command. Job 1:6 and 1 Kings 22:19 also portray heaven as the place of God’s sovereign rule and judgment. No power in heaven or earth exists independently of Him.

He renders judgment among the gods:
God judges beings entrusted with authority who have corrupted justice and failed to defend the weak. The “gods” may include heavenly rulers, yet the psalm’s concern for oppressed people also exposes the guilt of earthly authorities who mirror such rebellion. Jesus cited Psalm 82:6 in John 10:34–36, affirming the authority of Scripture while revealing Himself as the One uniquely sanctified and sent by the Father.

Psalm 82:2

“How long will you judge unjustly
God confronts Israel’s rulers for a settled pattern of corrupt decisions. Judges were appointed to administer God’s law without distortion, protecting the poor, the resident alien, the fatherless, and the widow (Deuteronomy 16:18–20; 24:17). Their failure was not merely political; it was rebellion against the Lord, who judges the nations in righteousness and holds every earthly authority accountable (Psalm 9:7–8).

Lev 19:15 You must not pervert justice; you must not show partiality…

Deu 1:17 Show no partiality in judging; hear both small and great…

Pro 17:15 Acquitting the guilty and condemning the righteous…

and show partiality to the wicked?
Favoring those who defy God’s ways overturns the purpose of civil authority and leaves the vulnerable without protection. Israel’s law forbade favoritism toward either the poor or the powerful and prohibited bribes that blinded judges (Leviticus 19:15; Deuteronomy 16:19). The following commands call these rulers to defend the weak and rescue the needy from the hand of the wicked (Psalm 82:3–4), reflecting the same demand for justice voiced by Isaiah and Amos (Isaiah 1:17; Amos 5:24).

Psalm 82:3

Defend the cause of the weak and fatherless;
God calls those entrusted with authority to protect people who lack power, family support, or legal standing. In Israel, the fatherless were especially exposed to loss of property, exploitation, and neglect. The Law repeatedly forbade taking advantage of widows, orphans, foreigners, and the poor (Exodus 22:22–24; Deuteronomy 24:17). Human judges were to reflect the Lord, who “defends the cause of the fatherless and the widow” (Deuteronomy 10:18). Justice requires more than avoiding wrongdoing; it requires active advocacy for those unable to secure justice for themselves.

Job 29:12 because I rescued the poor who cried out…

Isa 1:17 Learn to do right; seek justice and correct the oppressor.…

Psa 10:14 But You have regarded trouble and grief;…

uphold the rights of the afflicted and oppressed.
The afflicted and oppressed include all who suffer under poverty, violence, corruption, or unjust treatment. Psalm 82 rebukes rulers and judges who had used their positions to favor the wicked rather than restrain evil. God holds every authority accountable for administering justice impartially, because all earthly authority is exercised beneath His supreme rule. Jesus displayed this concern throughout His ministry, bringing mercy and truth to those burdened by sin, sickness, exclusion, and oppression. His people are therefore called to pursue justice with integrity, defending the vulnerable without partiality or self-interest.

Psalm 82:4

Rescue the weak and needy;
God commands those entrusted with authority to defend people who lack power, resources, or social standing. His concern for the widow, orphan, foreigner, and poor runs throughout the Law and Prophets (Deuteronomy 10:18; Isaiah 1:17). Justice requires more than impartial judgment; it calls for active protection when the vulnerable are being harmed. God’s people reflect His character by giving practical help and by refusing to ignore oppression. Such mercy also points to humanity’s deeper need for deliverance from sin and spiritual poverty.

Pro 31:8-9 Open your mouth for those with no voice,…

save them from the hand of the wicked.
Wicked people often use power, wealth, false judgment, or violence to control those unable to resist. Israel knew God as the Deliverer who brought His people out from Pharaoh’s oppressive hand (Exodus 3:7–8). Earthly rescue anticipates the greater salvation accomplished by Jesus Christ, who delivers believers from the dominion of darkness and brings them into His kingdom (Colossians 1:13–14).

Psalm 82:5

They do not know or understand;
The unjust rulers lack the wisdom that begins with the fear of the Lord (Prov. 1:7). Though entrusted with authority to defend the weak and judge rightly, they have rejected God’s law and become blind to the demands of justice. Israel’s history repeatedly showed the danger of leaders who lacked knowledge of God, for their corruption brought suffering upon those under their care (Hosea 4:6).

Isa 1:3 The ox knows its owner, and the donkey its master’s manger…

they wander in the darkness;
Separated from God’s truth, these leaders move without moral direction and perpetuate oppression rather than righteousness. Darkness portrays rebellion, ignorance, and alienation from the Lord, who is the source of light and life. Their wandering recalls Israel’s wilderness judgment, when unbelief and disobedience delayed the people’s entrance into the promised land.

all the foundations of the earth are shaken.
God established human authority to uphold justice and restrain evil. When rulers pervert judgment, the moral order of society is destabilized, and the vulnerable are left unprotected. The Lord will judge corrupt authorities and establish His righteous reign, fulfilled ultimately in Christ, the sure cornerstone of God’s kingdom.

Psalm 82:6

I have said, ‘You are gods;
Israel’s judges and rulers bore a delegated authority that reflected God’s own judgment on earth. They were not divine by nature, but their decisions carried serious responsibility because they represented God before His people. Their corruption therefore dishonored the One whose justice they were called to administer.

Exo 7:1 The LORD answered Moses, “See, I have made you like God…

you are all sons of the Most High.’
God had granted these leaders a privileged position under His covenant rule, calling them to display the character of the Most High in wisdom, impartiality, and mercy. Their title heightened their accountability, for those given authority by God must answer to Him for the way they use it.

Psalm 82:7

But like mortals you will die,
God addresses unjust judges and rulers who had been entrusted with authority to defend the weak, the fatherless, the poor, and the oppressed. Though they stood in places of honor and were accountable to administer God’s justice, they remained mortal men under His judgment. No office, wealth, or public esteem can shield a person from death or from answering to the Lord. Their end recalls God’s sentence upon Adam: “For dust you are, and to dust you shall return” (Genesis 3:19).

Job 14:1-2 “Man, who is born of woman, is short of days…”…

Ecc 3:19-20 For the fates of both men and beasts are the same:…

Psa 49:12-14 But a man, despite his wealth, cannot endure;…

and like rulers you will fall.”
Earthly leaders may appear secure, yet God brings down those who exalt themselves and use power to preserve injustice. Pharaoh’s resistance ended in the Red Sea, Nebuchadnezzar was humbled until he acknowledged God’s rule, and Herod was struck down after receiving divine honors (Exodus 14; Daniel 4; Acts 12:21–23). Human authority is temporary and delegated; God alone is the righteous Judge over every nation and ruler.
