The Day of Trouble

Psalm 20:1

For the choirmaster.
The superscription assigns Psalm 20 to the leader responsible for Israel’s sacred music, the choirmaster, a leader of music in the temple, indicating its use in public worship. The congregation likely sang this prayer as they sought God’s help for the king and the nation, especially in the shadow of military danger.

A Psalm of David.
David composed this royal prayer as king, warrior, and worshiper of the LORD. His life had taught him that Israel’s security did not rest in armies or royal strength but in God’s covenant faithfulness. The psalm also points beyond David to Christ, the greater Son of David and the King whose victory secures His people.

May the LORD answer you in the day of trouble;
Israel prays that the LORD will hear and deliver the king when danger comes. The day of trouble includes the pressures of conflict, opposition, and need that expose human weakness and call for God’s intervention. The LORD remains present with His people in their distress and acts according to His covenant mercy.

Israel’s confidence rests in the God who chose their fathers, redeemed His people, and remains able to defend all who trust in Him.

Psalm 20:2

May He send you help from the sanctuary
Israel sought the Lord’s aid from the place where He had made His presence known among His covenant people. In David’s day, the sanctuary on Zion was associated with the ark of the covenant and the sacrifices offered before God. The prayer recognizes that victory, protection, and deliverance do not rest ultimately on military strength or human planning, but on the holy God who receives His people’s worship. Jesus Christ fulfills the saving nearness of God as the true mediator who intercedes for His people.

and sustain you from Zion.
Zion stood for Jerusalem, the city God chose as the center of Israel’s worship and the seat of David’s kingdom. Sustaining strength comes from the Lord who reigns in faithfulness and keeps His covenant promises. The earthly Zion also directs hope toward God’s coming kingdom, where the redeemed will dwell with Him in the heavenly Jerusalem.

Psalm 20:3

May He remember all your gifts
Israel brought offerings to the Lord as acts of worship, thanksgiving, repentance, and covenant devotion. In Psalm 20, the congregation prays that God will take notice of the king’s faithful worship as he faces a time of need. God’s remembrance is not mere recollection; it is His gracious attention that leads to action on behalf of His people. God remembered Noah in the flood and acted to preserve him, and He remembers His covenant promises to those who call upon Him.

and look favorably on your burnt offerings.
Burnt offerings were wholly consumed on the altar, portraying complete dedication to God and the need for atonement through a substitute. The prayer asks God to accept the king’s sacrifice and grant him favor before battle. Such offerings could not ultimately remove sin, but they pointed forward to Jesus Christ, whose once-for-all sacrifice secured forgiveness and access to God for all who believe.

Psalm 20:4

May He give you the desires of your heart
David prays that the king’s deepest purposes would be granted as he seeks the Lord’s honor and Israel’s good. The heart includes the seat of will, devotion, and counsel before God. Such desires are not independent of God’s rule; they are shaped by delight in Him and submission to His purposes (Psalm 37:4). The Lord looks beyond outward appearance to the heart (1 Samuel 16:7), knowing whether a request arises from faith, obedience, and righteous concern.

and make all your plans succeed.
The petition anticipates a time of national danger, asking God to establish the king’s counsels and grant victory according to His covenant faithfulness. Israel’s success did not rest ultimately on military strength, strategy, or human resolve, but on the Lord who directs events and gives deliverance. Plans committed to Him are established under His sovereign will (Proverbs 16:3). Jesus Christ perfectly fulfilled the Father’s saving purpose, completing the work entrusted to Him and securing eternal salvation for His people (John 17:4).

Psalm 20:5

May we shout for joy at your victory
Israel’s people anticipated celebrating the king’s deliverance as the Lord’s gift. Military success was not credited merely to weapons or numbers, but to God’s covenant faithfulness and His presence with His people. Such rejoicing recalls the song of Moses and Israel after the Lord overthrew Pharaoh’s army in the Red Sea (Exodus 15:1–21). The greater fulfillment belongs to Christ, whose resurrection secured victory over sin, death, and every enemy of God’s kingdom.

and raise a banner in the name of our God.
Banners identified armies and gathered soldiers around a common leader in battle. Israel’s banner declared allegiance to the Lord rather than confidence in human strength. After victory over Amalek, Moses built an altar and named it “The LORD Is My Banner” (Exodus 17:15). The Lord Jesus stands as the true banner for His people and for the nations, drawing those who trust in Him into His victorious kingdom (Isaiah 11:10).

Such petitions rest on confidence that the Lord hears prayers offered according to His will. Christ, the greater King, perfectly seeks the Father’s will and intercedes for all who belong to Him.

Psalm 20:6

Now I know that the LORD saves His anointed;
David expresses settled confidence that the Lord preserves the king He has appointed to lead His covenant people. Israel’s kings were anointed with oil as a public sign that they belonged to God and served under His authority (1 Samuel 16:13). The Lord’s deliverance of David repeatedly confirmed His covenant promise to establish David’s house. The anointed king also anticipates Jesus Christ, the Messiah, whose victory over death in His resurrection reveals the Father’s saving faithfulness.

He answers him from His holy heaven
The God who reigns from heaven hears the petitions offered on earth and acts for His appointed servant. Heaven is the seat of His holy rule, yet His exaltation does not make Him remote from His people. David’s confidence rests not in military advantage but in the Lord’s attentive presence and sovereign authority.

God’s right hand represents His irresistible strength in judgment, rescue, and victory, as displayed in Israel’s deliverance from Egypt (Exodus 15:6). The Lord supplies the power His anointed king cannot produce for himself. Jesus Christ now sits at the Father’s right hand, reigning with all authority and securing the final salvation of all who belong to Him.

Psalm 20:7

Some trust in chariots
Chariots represented the strongest military technology of the ancient world and were central to the armies of Egypt, Assyria, and other powerful nations. Israel was repeatedly warned not to measure its security by such weapons or to seek deliverance through military alliances.

Isa 31:1 Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help…

Psa 33:17 A horse is a vain hope for salvation; …

Pro 21:31 A horse is prepared for the day of battle,…

and others in horses,
Horses supplied speed, force, and prestige in battle, but they could not secure victory apart from the Lord. The psalm stands in the setting of prayer for Israel’s king as he faces conflict, reminding God’s people that armies and resources are never a substitute for obedient dependence on God.

but we trust in the name of the LORD our God.
The name of the LORD reveals His covenant character, sovereign authority, and faithful power to save. Israel’s confidence rested in the God who had redeemed them, kept His promises, and fought for His people. Human strength eventually fails, but the Lord remains the sure refuge of all who call upon Him.

Psalm 20:8

They collapse and fall,
Those who boast in chariots, horses, weapons, alliances, and human strength eventually discover that earthly power cannot secure victory against the Lord. Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, and other mighty nations possessed armies Israel could not match, yet God repeatedly showed that He rules over kings and battles. The downfall of the proud reveals the weakness of every refuge built apart from God.

The people who call upon the name of the Lord are sustained when others fail. God grants stability, courage, and deliverance to those who trust Him rather than visible strength. This confidence finds its fullest fulfillment in Jesus Christ, who conquered sin and death through His resurrection and establishes His people on an unshakable foundation.

Psalm 20:9

O LORD, save the king.
Israel prayed for the preservation and success of the Davidic king, whose faithfulness and leadership affected the welfare of the whole nation. The king was the Lord’s anointed servant, yet he remained dependent on God for victory, wisdom, and protection. The prayer ultimately directs hope beyond every earthly ruler to Jesus Christ, the greater Son of David, whose saving reign cannot fail.

The people joined their king in calling upon the covenant God during times of danger and need.
