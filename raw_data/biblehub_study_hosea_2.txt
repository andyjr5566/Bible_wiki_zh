Israel’s Adultery Rebuked

Hosea 2:1

“Say of your brothers, ‘My people,’
God reverses the judgment announced in Hosea 1, where Israel was called “Not My People” because of persistent covenant rebellion. The restored name declares that the Lord has not abandoned His covenant purposes. Though the northern kingdom had pursued idols and suffered division from Judah, God would gather a remnant and renew them as His own people. The promise reaches beyond Israel’s national restoration to all who are brought near through Christ, including Gentiles once separated from the covenant promises.

God also reverses the name “Not Loved,” revealing His mercy toward those who deserved judgment. Israel’s unfaithfulness had brought discipline, but the Lord’s steadfast love remained greater than her sin. The family language joins brothers and sisters in one restored people, bound together by God’s compassion. Christ fulfills this mercy by giving Himself for His people and making the church His beloved bride.

The mother represents corporate Israel, whose idolatry had corrupted her households and communities. God’s prophets were not permitted to ignore such unfaithfulness; Elijah, Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Hosea alike called the people to turn from false worship and return to the LORD.

The marriage image reveals both the closeness of God’s covenant love and the gravity of Israel’s betrayal. God’s words announce real judgment and the loss of covenant blessings, yet the surrounding prophecy makes clear that His disciplinary action serves His purpose of bringing His people to repentance and eventual restoration.

Deuteronomy 7:13 identifies these products as gifts God promised to His covenant people, while Deuteronomy 8:11–18 warns against forgetting the Giver when enjoying His gifts.
