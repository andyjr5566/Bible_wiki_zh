Daniel Removed to Babylon

Daniel 1:1

In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah,
Jehoiakim, son of Josiah, reigned over Judah from 609 to 598 BC. Unlike his father, he rejected the reforms that had called Judah back to covenant faithfulness and led the nation in evil (2 Kings 23:36–24:7). The third year of his reign was about 605 BC, when Babylon replaced Egypt as the dominant power in the region after the battle of Carchemish. Judah’s political crisis exposed a deeper spiritual rebellion against the Lord.

Nebuchadnezzar II became Babylon’s most prominent ruler and expanded its empire through decisive military campaigns. He was God’s appointed instrument of judgment against Judah, as Jeremiah had foretold (Jeremiah 25:8–11). Though Nebuchadnezzar acted from imperial ambition, the Lord remained sovereign over his victories and over the discipline of His covenant people.

The judgment fulfilled prophetic warnings, yet it did not cancel God’s promises. Even in exile, the Lord preserved a faithful remnant and demonstrated that His authority extends over every kingdom and nation.

until the first year of King Cyrus.
Cyrus conquered Babylon in 539 BC, and his decree soon permitted Jewish exiles to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple (Ezra 1:1–4; 2 Chronicles 36:22–23). The transition from Babylonian to Persian rule fulfilled God’s word concerning the seventy years of exile (Jeremiah 29:10). Cyrus had also been named as the Lord’s instrument of restoration long before his birth (Isaiah 44:28; 45:1). Daniel’s continued presence into Cyrus’s reign testifies to God’s preservation of His servant and His control over kingdoms. Daniel 10:1 shows that Daniel lived beyond Cyrus’s first year. Cyrus’s release of the captives foreshadows the greater deliverance accomplished by Christ, who frees His people from bondage to sin.
