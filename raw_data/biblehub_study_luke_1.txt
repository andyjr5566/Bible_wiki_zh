Dedication to Theophilus

Luke 1:1

Many have undertaken
Several written accounts and carefully preserved oral reports concerning Jesus were already circulating among the early believers. The extraordinary works, teachings, death, resurrection, and ascension of Christ had created an urgent need for faithful testimony. Luke did not dismiss these earlier efforts; he recognized the broad concern among Christians to preserve the events by which God brought salvation.

Ecc 12:12 And by these, my son, be further warned:…

to compose an account
Luke presents the Gospel as an orderly historical narrative rather than a collection of religious reflections. His work records real events involving identifiable people, places, rulers, and witnesses. Guided by the Holy Spirit, Luke investigated these matters so that believers could know the certainty of what God accomplished through His Son.

of the things that have been fulfilled
Jesus’ coming fulfilled God’s promises to Israel and accomplished His redemptive purpose foretold in the Old Testament. His birth from the line of David, His ministry, His suffering, and His resurrection were not isolated events but the realization of God’s sovereign plan. The Gospel therefore proclaims that God has acted decisively in history through Christ.

The saving events of Jesus’ earthly ministry occurred publicly within the world of those who first received the Gospel. Apostles, disciples, women who followed Jesus, religious leaders, Roman officials, and crowds all encountered His words and works. The faith of the church rests on God’s action in the incarnate Son, who lived among His people and made the Father known.

The Gospel accounts rest on carefully preserved testimony transmitted within the earliest Christian community. Jewish culture valued the faithful passing on of sacred history and teaching, and the apostles guarded what they had received concerning Jesus’ words, works, death, and resurrection. Luke writes as one receiving this established testimony and examining it diligently for an orderly account.

The first witnesses had seen Jesus’ ministry, heard His teaching, observed His miracles, and many had encountered Him risen from the dead. The apostles’ proclamation was grounded in events they personally knew, not in invented accounts or distant legends. Their witness agrees with the testimony of John, who declared what the disciples had heard, seen, and touched concerning the Word of life (1 John 1:1–3).

Herod the Great ruled Judea from 37 to 4 BC under Roman authority. His reign brought major building projects, including the enlargement of the Jerusalem temple, but it was also marked by cruelty, suspicion, and the murder of family members and political rivals. Israel lived under foreign domination and longed for the promised King from David’s line. God began the fulfillment of His saving purposes in a dark and politically troubled age.

Luke 1:10

And at the hour of the incense offering,
The hour of the incense offering refers to a specific time in the Jewish temple ritual, typically occurring twice daily, in the morning and in the evening (Exodus 30:7-8). This was a sacred time when the priest would burn incense on the altar, symbolizing the prayers of the people ascending to God (Psalm 141:2; Revelation 8:3-4). The incense offering was a central part of the temple worship, highlighting the importance of prayer and intercession in the life of Israel. This practice took place in the Holy Place, just outside the Holy of Holies, indicating the closeness to God’s presence.

Luke 1:76

And you, child, will be called a prophet of the Most High;
Zechariah, filled with the Holy Spirit, turns from praising God’s salvation to address his newborn son, John. John would stand in the line of Israel’s prophets as God’s appointed messenger, yet his ministry would uniquely mark the transition from promise to fulfillment. The Most High, sovereign over Israel and all nations, had chosen John to summon His people back to covenant faithfulness. Jesus later affirmed John’s greatness among the prophets and identified him as the messenger promised in Scripture.

John’s calling was to precede the public ministry of Jesus Christ. Malachi foretold a messenger who would prepare the way before the Lord, and Isaiah spoke of a voice calling in the wilderness to make ready the way of the Lord. By applying these prophecies to John and Jesus, Luke identifies Jesus with the Lord whose coming Israel had awaited. John did not draw attention to himself but directed the people to the One coming after him.

John prepared the way through preaching repentance, baptizing those who confessed their sins, and calling Israel to bear fruit consistent with a changed heart. As roads were repaired for the arrival of a king, hearts were to be made ready for the Messiah. His ministry exposed sin, confronted religious hypocrisy, and awakened expectation for God’s promised salvation. John’s work found its purpose when he pointed to Jesus as the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world.

Zechariah praises God for sending the Messiah as the dawning light upon a world blinded by sin and alienated from its Creator. Isaiah foretold that “the people walking in darkness” would see a great light (Isaiah 9:2), a prophecy fulfilled in Jesus’ ministry, especially among the spiritually neglected regions of Galilee (Matthew 4:13–16). Jesus is the Light of the World, revealing the Father, exposing sin, and calling people from unbelief into salvation (John 8:12; John 1:4–9).

Luke 1:80

And the child grew
John developed physically under the care of Zechariah and Elizabeth, bearing witness that the prophet sent before Christ entered the world in true human weakness and dependence. His maturing recalls Samuel, who “grew on, and was in favor both with the LORD, and also with men” (1 Samuel 2:26), and anticipates Jesus’ growth in wisdom, stature, and favor (Luke 2:40, 52). God’s sovereign purpose for John unfolded through the ordinary course of life.

The Holy Spirit had set John apart even before his birth (Luke 1:15), forming in him the courage, devotion, and spiritual resolve required for his calling. Like Elijah, whose ministry John would reflect, he would stand before a resistant generation with God-given boldness and call the nation back to the Lord.
