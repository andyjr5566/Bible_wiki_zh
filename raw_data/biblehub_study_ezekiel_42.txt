The western side matched the other boundaries in length, forming a vast and ordered sacred square around the temple. Ezekiel’s measuring cubit was longer than the ordinary cubit, consisting of a cubit plus a handbreadth (Ezekiel 40:5). The repeated measurements reveal neither accident nor human invention, but the completeness, order, and holiness of God’s future sanctuary.

five hundred cubits long and five hundred cubits wide,
The equal dimensions formed a complete and balanced enclosure, reflecting the perfection and stability of God’s order. The detailed measurements recall the tabernacle and Solomon’s temple, where every dimension testified that worship must be governed by God’s instruction rather than human preference.

to separate the holy from the common.
Israel’s priests were commanded to distinguish between holy and common, clean and unclean (Leviticus 10:10). The wall embodied that calling, declaring that God is holy and that His people must honor the distinction between what belongs to Him and what is ordinary. Through Christ’s sacrifice, believers are sanctified and given access to God, yet they remain called to lives marked by reverence and holiness (Hebrews 10:10, 19–22).
