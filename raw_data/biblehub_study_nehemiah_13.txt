The Sabbath Restored

Nehemiah found Judah returning to practices the people had pledged to forsake when they renewed the covenant (Nehemiah 10:31). Treading winepresses was demanding agricultural labor, directly contrary to God’s command to remember the Sabbath and keep it holy (Exodus 20:8–11; 34:21). Sabbath rest declared that Israel belonged to the Lord and depended on His provision rather than continual labor.

Nehemiah 13:30

Thus I purified the priests and Levites from everything foreign,
Nehemiah removed influences that compromised the holiness of those appointed for temple service. His reforms addressed marriages, alliances, and practices that had drawn Israel toward idolatry and disregard for God’s law. Priests and Levites were called to model covenant faithfulness, teach the Law, and guard the worship of the Lord (Leviticus 10:10–11; Malachi 2:7). Their cleansing upheld the distinction between what was holy and what was common, protecting the restored community from repeating the unfaithfulness that had contributed to the exile.

and I assigned specific duties to each of the priests and Levites.
Nehemiah restored the ordered service God had established for the temple, ensuring that sacrifices, offerings, music, gatekeeping, and instruction were carried out faithfully. Each servant’s assigned responsibility promoted accountability and preserved the regular worship of God. Order in temple ministry reflected God’s own order and reminded the people that worship was not governed by convenience or personal preference, but by obedience to His revealed commands.

Nehemiah 13:31

I also arranged for contributions of wood at the appointed times,
Nehemiah ensured a regular supply of wood for the altar, where the fire was never to go out (Leviticus 6:12–13). The people had previously pledged to provide wood according to an ordered schedule (Nehemiah 10:34). Such practical provision guarded the continuity of sacrifice and demonstrated that restored worship required faithful administration as well as spiritual devotion.

and for the firstfruits.
Firstfruits acknowledged that the land, harvest, and every good gift belonged to God (Exodus 23:19; Deuteronomy 26:1–11). Nehemiah’s reforms called Judah back to covenant obedience after neglect had weakened the temple service. The firstfruits also point forward to Christ, the firstfruits of the resurrection, who guarantees new life for all who belong to Him (1 Corinthians 15:20–23).
