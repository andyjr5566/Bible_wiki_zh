You Showed Favor to Your Land

Psalm 85:1

For the choirmaster.
The psalm was entrusted to the leader responsible for directing Israel’s sacred music in corporate worship. Its concerns belonged not merely to private devotion but to the gathered people of God, who remembered His mercy together and sought His renewed blessing.

A Psalm of the sons of Korah.
The sons of Korah were Levites appointed for temple service and became known as worshipers and singers. Though their ancestor Korah rebelled against Moses and perished under God’s judgment, his descendants were preserved and later served before the Lord. Their place in Israel’s worship displays God’s grace and calls His people to draw near to Him with reverence.

You showed favor to Your land, O LORD;
The land was God’s covenant gift to Abraham’s descendants and the setting in which Israel was to live under His rule. Prosperity, security, fruitful harvests, and peace in the land were marks of the Lord’s favor, while judgment and exile followed persistent covenant unfaithfulness. The psalm begins with grateful remembrance that the LORD had acted mercifully toward His people.

You restored Jacob from captivity.
Jacob represents the whole covenant nation descended from Israel’s patriarch. The Lord had brought His people back from a condition of loss, oppression, or exile, demonstrating that His judgment was not His final word toward those He had chosen. Israel’s restoration anticipates the greater redemption accomplished by Christ, who delivers His people from the bondage of sin and will finally restore all things under His righteous reign.

Psalm 85:2

You forgave the iniquity of Your people;
God had dealt mercifully with Israel’s covenant rebellion, removing the guilt that their disobedience deserved. Their restoration after judgment, including the return from Babylonian exile, displayed His faithfulness to the people He had disciplined. The sacrifices appointed through Moses taught that forgiveness required atonement, anticipating the greater sacrifice of Christ, who bore the sin of His people.

You covered all their sin.
God did not merely overlook Israel’s sins but provided atonement for them according to His covenant mercy. The repeated sacrifices of the old covenant pointed beyond themselves to Jesus Christ, whose blood cleanses from sin completely and permanently. God’s forgiveness reaches every confessed sin; no guilt remains before Him for those redeemed through Christ.

Psalm 85:3

You withheld all Your fury;
God’s mercy restrained the judgment Israel’s sins deserved. Psalm 85 recalls His restoration of His people after seasons of discipline, likely with the return from exile in view. Throughout Israel’s history, the Lord judged idolatry and rebellion yet preserved His covenant people, repeatedly providing deliverance and calling them back to Himself. His patience revealed both His holiness and His faithfulness. God’s withheld wrath ultimately points to Christ, who bore the judgment of sin so that those justified by His blood are saved from wrath (Romans 5:9).

Psa 78:38 And yet He was compassionate; He forgave their iniquity…

Isa 57:16 For I will not accuse you forever, nor will I be angry;…

You turned from Your burning anger.
God’s anger against sin is righteous, but He provides reconciliation rather than abandoning His people. Israel’s restoration depended not upon its own merit but upon the Lord’s covenant mercy. The prophets joined forgiveness with repentance, calling the nation to return to the compassionate God who is “gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love” (Joel 2:13). Through Jesus Christ, God reconciles believers to Himself, granting peace with God through the sacrifice that removed the barrier of sin (2 Corinthians 5:18–19).

Psalm 85:4

Restore us, O God of our salvation,
Israel appealed to the covenant God who had forgiven iniquity and withdrawn wrath in earlier generations. National restoration required more than relief from outward trouble; it required renewed fellowship with the Lord, whose salvation included deliverance, forgiveness, and the return of His favor. The prayer anticipates the fuller redemption accomplished by Jesus Christ, through whom sinners are reconciled to God.

God answered the deepest need for reconciliation in the atoning death of Christ, who bore divine judgment so that all who trust Him may receive peace with God.

Psalm 85:5

Will You be angry with us forever?
Israel’s recent restoration had not removed every sign of covenant discipline, and the psalmist pleads for God to turn fully from His anger. God’s wrath against sin is righteous, yet His covenant mercy assures His people that judgment is not His final purpose. The Lord revealed Himself as “merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love” (Exodus 34:6). Israel’s exile demonstrated the seriousness of rebellion, but their return showed that God had not abandoned His promises.

Mic 7:18 Who is a God like You, who pardons iniquity…

Will You draw out Your anger to all generations?
The prayer looks beyond the present generation and asks God not to let the effects of judgment continue among their children. Sin brings lasting consequences, but God’s covenant faithfulness extends farther than judgment. He keeps steadfast love “to a thousand generations” of those who love Him (Deuteronomy 7:9). God ultimately answered the need for reconciliation through Jesus Christ, who bore the judgment sin deserved and secured peace with God for all who trust in Him.

Psalm 85:6

Will You not revive us again,
Having recalled the Lord’s past forgiveness and restoration of the land, the psalmist pleads for renewed life among God’s covenant people. The prayer rises from a community burdened by the effects of sin, divine displeasure, and national weakness, fitting the needs of Israel after exile or another season of chastening. God had repeatedly raised up and restored His people when they turned to Him, as seen throughout Judges and the history of the kings. Such renewal required more than improved circumstances; it called for hearts restored to faithful worship and obedience. Jesus Christ fulfills the hope of spiritual life, giving eternal life to all who believe in Him.

Isa 57:15 For thus says the One who is high and lifted up…

that Your people may rejoice in You?
Restored fellowship with the Lord is the purpose of revival. Israel’s joy was meant to arise from God Himself—His mercy, presence, salvation, and covenant faithfulness—and to find expression in worship among the gathered people. The request recognizes that lasting joy cannot be secured by political peace, material prosperity, or relief from trouble apart from reconciliation with God. The Lord ultimately gives His people fullness of joy through the salvation accomplished in Christ, who reconciles sinners to God and establishes their hope beyond every season of distress.

Psalm 85:7

Show us Your loving devotion,
Israel appeals to God’s covenant faithfulness, asking Him to make His mercy visible in renewed restoration. The prayer rises from a community that remembers the Lord’s past forgiveness and deliverance (Psalm 85:1–3) yet still feels the weight of present need. God’s steadfast love rests on His promises to Abraham, Moses, and David, and it sustains His people when outward circumstances seem unchanged. His mercies remain new every morning because His character does not change (Lamentations 3:22–23).

Psa 31:16 Make Your face shine on Your servant; save me…

O LORD,
The psalmist calls upon YHWH, Israel’s personal covenant God, who revealed Himself to Moses and redeemed His people from Egypt. The name recalls the Lord’s power, holiness, and faithfulness to keep every promise. Israel does not appeal to an unknown deity but to the God who has acted in history for the sake of His people.

and grant us Your salvation.
Salvation includes God’s deliverance from sin, enemies, oppression, and the consequences of His people’s disobedience. The immediate cry seeks restoration for the nation, likely amid conditions of weakness following judgment or exile. The Lord’s saving purpose reaches its fullness in Jesus Christ, through whom God grants forgiveness, reconciliation, and eternal life. In Him, God’s steadfast love and salvation are revealed together.

Psalm 85:8

I will listen to what God the LORD will say;
The psalmist waits for the covenant God to speak after recalling His past forgiveness and pleading for renewed mercy. Israel had received God’s word through Moses, the Law, priests, and prophets, and faithful response required reverent hearing followed by obedience. The LORD remains the God who binds Himself to His people in covenant, whose authority is joined to steadfast faithfulness.

for He will surely speak peace to His people and His saints;
God’s promised peace is shalom: restored fellowship with Him, security under His care, and the wholeness that sin had shattered. For Israel, such peace followed divine forgiveness and renewed faithfulness. The promise ultimately directs attention to Christ, the Prince of Peace, who reconciles sinners to God through His sacrifice and gives peace to all who belong to Him.

God’s mercy is not permission to resume rebellion; His restoration calls His people to walk in holiness. The Lord who forgives also preserves and sanctifies His own, completing the work He begins in those who trust Him.

Psalm 85:9

Surely His salvation is near to those who fear Him,
The psalmist assures God’s covenant people that deliverance is close at hand for all who honor Him with reverent obedience. Fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom (Proverbs 9:10) and marks those who trust His word rather than their own strength. Israel’s history testified that the Lord was near whenever His people called upon Him (Deuteronomy 4:7). This hope reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, whose name, Yeshua, means “salvation,” God’s promised salvation, who came as Immanuel, “God with us” (Matthew 1:23).

that His glory may dwell in our land.
God’s glory had filled the tabernacle in the wilderness and later Solomon’s temple, revealing His holy presence among Israel (Exodus 40:34–35; 1 Kings 8:10–11). The restoration sought in Psalm 85 was not merely national security or material prosperity, but the return of God’s favorable presence to the land He had given to Abraham’s descendants. Christ revealed that glory in human flesh, dwelling among His people (John 1:14), and the promise will be completed in the New Jerusalem, where God will dwell with His redeemed forever (Revelation 21:3, 23).

Psalm 85:10

Loving devotion and faithfulness have joined together;
God’s covenant love and His unwavering commitment to His promises meet in His restoration of His people. His mercy does not overlook His word, and His faithfulness does not withhold compassion. The salvation prayed for in Psalm 85 rests on the character of the God who remembered His covenant with Abraham, preserved Israel despite her sin, and promised lasting redemption through David’s greater Son. In Jesus Christ, God’s love and faithfulness are displayed fully as He fulfills His promises and brings salvation to those who trust Him.

Christ bore sin’s penalty and rose again, bringing believers into peace with God (Romans 5:1). The union also anticipates the Messiah’s righteous reign, when the Prince of Peace will establish justice, wholeness, and peace throughout His kingdom.

Psalm 85:11

Faithfulness sprouts from the earth,
Restored people answer the Lord’s steadfast love with covenant obedience. Like new growth after rain, faithfulness appears where God has renewed hearts and blessed the land. The image joins humanity, formed from the ground, to God’s saving work; His grace produces the allegiance He requires. Hosea likewise calls God’s people to sow righteousness in hope of reaping His steadfast love.

Hos 2:20-23 “And I will betroth you in faithfulness,…

and righteousness looks down from heaven.
Perfect righteousness belongs to the Lord and comes from His heavenly rule as blessing, judgment, and help. God does not merely observe His people’s renewal; He supplies what they lack and establishes them in His ways. Heaven’s righteousness and earth’s faithfulness meet ultimately in Christ, who came from heaven to fulfill God’s promises and bring His people into reconciliation with God.

Psalm 85:12

The LORD will indeed provide what is good,
The LORD is the faithful source of every true blessing, granting His people both temporal provision and spiritual renewal. Psalm 85 celebrates God’s restoration of Israel after judgment and pleads for renewed mercy, righteousness, and salvation. Material abundance belongs within God’s covenant care, yet the highest good is His presence and favor. Every good gift comes from God, and His goodness reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, the Good Shepherd who gives His life for His people.

and our land will yield its increase.
Israel’s agricultural prosperity depended upon the LORD’s blessing, for He gave rain, fertile seasons, and fruitful harvests in accordance with His covenant promises (Leviticus 26:4; Deuteronomy 28:1–14). A productive land testified that God had turned from judgment to favor and had restored His people. The promised increase also anticipates the spiritual fruit produced where God’s righteousness and peace rule the hearts of His people. God’s final restoration will bring enduring abundance and peace in the renewed creation.

The later ministry of John the Baptist, preparing the way for Christ in fulfillment of Isaiah 40:3, reflects this pattern of the Lord’s coming.
