The People Confess Their Sins

Nehemiah 9:1

The month was Tishri, following the Feast of Trumpets, the Day of Atonement, and the Feast of Tabernacles. Two days after the joyful conclusion of the feast, the people returned for a solemn assembly, showing that celebration before God must also lead to continued obedience, confession, and renewal.

Nehemiah 9:9

You saw the affliction of our fathers in Egypt;
Israel’s slavery involved forced labor, harsh taskmasters, and systematic oppression (Ex. 1:11–14). God had foretold Abraham that his descendants would be afflicted in a foreign land before He judged their oppressors and brought them out (Genesis 15:13–14). The Lord’s knowledge of their misery was compassionate and covenantal; He remembered His promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and acted to redeem their descendants (Ex. 2:23–25; 3:7–8). Israel’s deliverance from Egypt also portrays the greater deliverance Christ accomplishes, rescuing His people from bondage to sin.

You heard their cry at the Red Sea.
Trapped between Pharaoh’s army and the sea, the Israelites cried out in fear and distress (Ex. 14:10–12). The Lord answered by commanding Moses to stretch out his hand over the waters, dividing the sea and providing a path of escape for His people while destroying the pursuing Egyptian army (Ex. 14:21–28). The crossing revealed God as Israel’s Savior and Warrior, bringing His people from threatened death into freedom. Paul later identifies the passage through the sea as a pattern of baptism and union with God’s redeemed people (1 Cor. 10:1–2).
