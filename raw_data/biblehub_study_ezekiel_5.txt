Ezekiel’s hair represented the inhabitants of Jerusalem under judgment. After most of the divided hair was burned, struck, or scattered, the few strands pictured a small remnant whom God would preserve. Even in judgment, the Lord did not abandon His covenant purposes or allow Israel to be utterly destroyed. Isaiah likewise foretold that a remnant would return to the mighty God (Isaiah 10:20–22). Preservation did not remove the need for repentance, but it displayed God’s mercy and kept alive the line through which His promises would be fulfilled.

Ezekiel 5:16

When I shower you with the deadly arrows of famine and destruction
God portrays famine as arrows released against Jerusalem: deadly, directed, and impossible to evade. The image recalls the covenant warnings of Deuteronomy 32:23–24, where famine belongs among the judgments reserved for persistent rebellion. Jerusalem’s coming siege would prove that the Lord’s warnings were not empty threats.

The calamity would come through Babylon’s armies, yet God declares Himself sovereign over the judgment. Years of idolatry, injustice, and refusal to heed the prophets had brought the nation under the curses of the covenant. I will intensify the famine against you
Judgment would increase because earlier discipline had not produced repentance. Leviticus 26:18–20 warned that continued defiance would bring multiplied punishment and a land unable to yield its food. God’s escalating discipline revealed both the gravity of sin and the seriousness of His call to return to Him.

Food, security, and ordinary life would fail under the siege. The removal of bread fulfilled the covenant curse of Deuteronomy 28:48 and exposed Israel’s dependence upon the God they had forsaken. Physical famine also reflected a deeper spiritual barrenness: life and provision ultimately come from the Lord.

Ezekiel 5:17

I will send famine and wild beasts against you,
God’s covenant curses included famine and dangerous animals as judgments for persistent rebellion (Leviticus 26:22, 26; Deut. 28:48). Siege warfare would exhaust food supplies, while depopulated fields and villages allowed wild animals to threaten survivors. God’s judgment reached beyond Jerusalem’s walls into the ordinary sources of food, safety, and livelihood.

Deu 32:24 They will be wasted from hunger and ravaged by pestilence…

Lev 26:22 I will send wild animals against you…

and they will leave you childless.
The death of children brought profound grief and cut off family inheritance and future hope. Children were God’s gift and a sign of His blessing (Psalm 127:3–5), yet Judah’s continued idolatry and violence would bring the covenant curse of lost offspring (Deut. 28:18). The judgment exposed the ruin produced by abandoning the Lord who gives life and preserves generations.

Deu 32:25 Outside, the sword will take their children,…

Jer 15:7 I will scatter them with a winnowing fork…

Plague and bloodshed will sweep through you,
Disease, violence, and death would spread through the land alongside famine. The horrors of siege commonly produced pestilence through hunger, overcrowding, and unburied bodies, while war brought slaughter to those within and beyond the city. These calamities fulfilled the warnings God had given Israel through Moses and demonstrated that the Lord remained faithful to His word even when His people were faithless.

Ezk 7:15 The sword is outside; plague and famine are within.…

Ezk 7:23 Forge the chain, for the land is full of crimes…

and I will bring a sword against you.
The sword points to the Babylonian army, which God appointed as the instrument of His judgment against Judah. Babylon’s destruction of Jerusalem and the temple in 586 BC ended the kingdom’s outward security and sent many survivors into exile (2 Kings 25:8–21). God’s use of a foreign nation did not excuse Babylon’s later pride and cruelty, for He would also judge them.

Ezk 11:8 You fear the sword, so I will bring the sword against you…

The Lord’s declaration establishes the certainty of the coming judgment. Jerusalem’s fall would not result from chance, military weakness, or Babylonian power alone, but from the sovereign God carrying out the covenant consequences He had long announced.
