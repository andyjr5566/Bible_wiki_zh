Bildad: Man Cannot Be Righteous

Job 25:1

Then Bildad the Shuhite replied:
Bildad was one of Job’s three friends who came from distant regions to mourn with him and offer counsel in his suffering. His name, Bildad, may mean “confusing love” or “Bel has loved,” indicating a possible connection to ancient Semitic cultures. As a Shuhite, he was likely descended from Shuah, Abraham’s son by Keturah (Genesis 25:1–2), linking him to peoples living east of Canaan. Bildad’s final speech is brief and centers on God’s sovereign majesty, purity, and authority over all creation. Like Eliphaz and Zophar, he assumes that suffering must reveal personal sin, yet the Lord’s later rebuke shows that Job’s friends had not spoken rightly about Him (Job 42:7). Bildad’s emphasis on God’s greatness remains true, but his application fails to account for the reality of righteous suffering and God’s purposes beyond human understanding.

Bildad declares that God alone rules over all creation. His sovereignty reaches beyond human authority, suffering, and judgment. The Creator of heaven and earth is worthy of reverent fear, for no power can challenge His rule.

He establishes harmony in the heights of heaven.
God governs the heavenly realm with perfect order, including the hosts that serve before Him. Job’s suffering had not placed the universe beyond God’s control. The peace God maintains in heaven anticipates the full reconciliation accomplished through Christ, who possesses all authority and will restore all things under God’s righteous rule.

Job 25:3

Can His troops be numbered?
Bildad points to the immeasurable hosts under God’s command: angels, stars, and all the forces of heaven. The Lord knows and governs what no human being can count, calling the stars by name (Psalm 147:4) and commanding the heavenly army with perfect authority. Elisha’s servant glimpsed that unseen reality when God opened his eyes to behold horses and chariots of fire surrounding them (2 Kings 6:17). God’s resources are never depleted, and no earthly power can stand against His rule.

Psa 68:17 The chariots of God are tens of thousands…

Rev 5:11 Then I looked, and I heard the voices of many angels…

Dan 7:10 A river of fire was flowing, coming out from His presence.…

On whom does His light not rise?
God’s light reaches across the whole earth, sustaining life through the ordered course of creation and exposing all people to His righteous rule. “Nothing is hidden from its heat” (Psalm 19:6), and no heart or nation lies outside God’s knowledge. The universal light of creation also bears witness to the true Light, Jesus Christ, who gives light to every person (John 1:9). Bildad rightly magnifies God’s majesty, though he wrongly assumes that Job’s suffering proves personal guilt.

Job 25:4

How then can a man be just before God?
Bildad confronts Job with the absolute holiness and majesty of God, before whom no sinful human being can establish righteousness by personal merit. Humanity’s universal guilt leaves every person accountable to the Creator, as Romans 3:23 declares. Job’s longing to present his case before God therefore points beyond human self-defense to the need for a righteous mediator. God answers this need through Jesus Christ, whose atoning death and resurrection provide justification for all who believe in Him (Romans 5:1).

How can one born of woman be pure?
Every descendant of Adam enters a fallen world marked by sin and death. No ordinary human birth can produce the moral purity required to stand unashamed before a holy God. The sacrifices prescribed under the old covenant testified that cleansing required the shedding of blood, while also revealing that animal offerings could not finally remove sin. Jesus Christ was born of a woman, yet conceived by the Holy Spirit and wholly without sin. Through His sacrifice, God makes sinners clean and clothes them with Christ’s righteousness (2 Corinthians 5:21).

Job 25:5

If even the moon does not shine,
Bildad portrays the moon, appointed by God as the lesser light to rule the night (Genesis 1:16), as dim before the Creator’s holiness. Its borrowed and changing light illustrates the dependence of all creation upon God. What appears bright to human eyes cannot rival the glory of the One who made it.

Ezk 32:7 When I extinguish you, I will cover the heavens…

Isa 13:10 For the stars of heaven and their constellations…

and the stars are not pure in His sight,
The stars display God’s power and order in the heavens (Psalm 8:3; Isaiah 40:26), yet they remain creatures and cannot possess the Creator’s absolute purity. Bildad’s argument presses toward the question of how fallen humanity could claim righteousness before such a holy God. Job’s suffering does not prove that he is uniquely sinful, but the speech rightly affirms that no creature stands before God on the basis of its own perfection.

Job 25:6

how much less man,
Bildad concludes by setting human frailty beneath the majesty of the God who rules the heavens. Humanity cannot claim purity or self-sufficiency before the Creator. His words rightly humble mankind, though they do not establish that Job’s suffering resulted from a particular hidden sin.

who is but a maggot,
The image of decay confronts human mortality and corruption. Formed from dust and destined to return to dust, people cannot stand before God on the basis of their own righteousness. The fallen condition of mankind requires mercy rather than human boasting.

Job 7:5 My flesh is clothed with worms and encrusted with dirt;…

and the son of man,
“Son of man” identifies man as a member of the mortal human race. Every descendant of Adam shares weakness, dependence, and accountability before God. The title later used by Jesus also points to His true humanity, though He alone entered human weakness without sharing human sin.

who is but a worm!”
The lowly worm portrays humanity as helpless before divine holiness and power. Isaiah used similar language when God called Israel “worm Jacob,” yet also promised to help and redeem His people. Human humility is not despair, for the God before whom man is lowly is also the Redeemer who raises the needy by His grace.
