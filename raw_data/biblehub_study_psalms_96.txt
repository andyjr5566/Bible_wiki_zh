Sing to the LORD, All the Earth

Psalm 96:1

Sing to the LORD a new song;
Israel’s worship rose from God’s mighty acts of creation, deliverance, covenant faithfulness, and judgment. A new song answered fresh displays of His saving power, as when He delivered His people from Egypt or gave victory over their enemies. Psalm 96 calls God’s people to praise Him for His continuing reign and for the salvation He makes known among the nations. The new song ultimately points to the redemption accomplished through Christ, whose death and resurrection give His people an everlasting reason to worship (Revelation 5:9).

Psa 98:1 A Psalm. Sing to the LORD a new song, for He has done wonders…

Isa 42:10 Sing to the LORD a new song—His praise…

Psa 33:3 Sing to Him a new song; play skillfully with a shout of joy.

sing to the LORD, all the earth.
The Lord is not a tribal deity confined to Israel; He is the Creator and King of every nation. Israel’s praise was meant to proclaim His glory among the peoples and to summon the whole world to acknowledge Him. God’s promise to bless all families of the earth through Abraham finds its fulfillment in the gospel going to every nation through Jesus Christ. The final gathering of redeemed people from every tribe, language, people, and nation will join this universal song of worship before God’s throne (Revelation 7:9–10).

Psalm 96:3

Declare His glory among the nations,
God’s splendor, holiness, and saving power were never intended to remain known only within Israel. Israel’s worship testified to the living God before surrounding peoples, fulfilling God’s promise that all families of the earth would be blessed through Abraham’s offspring (Genesis 12:3). The glory that filled the tabernacle and temple revealed the Lord’s dwelling presence, while His redemptive acts displayed His character to the world. The command anticipates the gospel’s advance through Christ, whose disciples are sent to every nation.

The Lord’s mighty works in Egypt, at the Red Sea, in the wilderness, and throughout Israel’s history demonstrated that He alone rules over creation and nations. Egypt’s deliverance was designed to make God’s name known beyond Israel’s borders (Exodus 9:16). His greatest wonder is the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ, through whom salvation is offered to all peoples. The church continues this calling by proclaiming God’s works until the day when people from every nation worship before His throne.

Psalm 96:4

The LORD alone possesses unlimited power, perfect holiness, and sovereign authority over heaven and earth. Israel’s worship rested on the reality that the God who created all things, delivered His people from Egypt, and kept His covenant is greater than every ruler, nation, and spiritual power. His greatness cannot be measured or exhausted.

Psalm 96:5

For all the gods of the nations are idols,
The nations surrounding Israel trusted in images fashioned by human hands and served gods associated with fertility, storms, war, and kingship. Such worship could not deliver from sin, death, or judgment. Israel’s God alone hears, speaks, acts, and reigns over every people and ruler. The emptiness of idols exposes the folly of giving created things the worship owed to the Creator.

The LORD, Yahweh, stands above all false gods because He created the heavens and all they contain. The sun, moon, stars, and powers often worshiped by pagan peoples are themselves His handiwork. His creation of heaven declares His absolute sovereignty and His power to fulfill His covenant promises. The New Testament identifies the Son as the agent of creation, for all things were made through Him and for Him.

Psalm 96:6

Splendor and majesty are before Him;
God reigns in unveiled glory, exalted above every earthly ruler and false god. Isaiah saw the Lord enthroned in the temple, and the seraphim proclaimed His holiness as the foundations shook (Isaiah 6:1–4). John likewise beheld God’s heavenly throne surrounded by worship and radiant glory (Revelation 4:2–3). The Lord’s majesty is not borrowed or ceremonial; it belongs to Him eternally as Creator, King, and Judge.

Worship rightly responds to God with reverence, joy, and confidence, because the King who is glorious in heaven has made a way for His people to draw near.

Psalm 96:7

Ascribe to the LORD, O families of the nations,
The worship of the LORD belongs not to Israel alone but to every people descended from Noah. Nations that once followed idols are summoned to acknowledge the Creator and King over all the earth. God’s promise to Abraham anticipated this worldwide blessing, for through his offspring all families of the earth would be blessed (Genesis 12:3). The psalm looks forward to the gathering of the nations under the reign of the Messiah and finds further fulfillment in Christ’s command to make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19).

Idols have no life or power, but the LORD made the heavens and rules over every kingdom. Jesus Christ reveals the fullness of God’s glory, for He is the radiance of God’s glory and upholds all things by His powerful word (Hebrews 1:3).

Psalm 96:8

Ascribe to the LORD the glory due His name;
Israel’s worship begins with the acknowledgment that the LORD alone possesses supreme honor because of who He is and all He has done. He revealed His holy name and character to Moses as merciful, gracious, righteous, and faithful (Exodus 34:5–7). His glory is not bestowed by human praise but rightly recognized by it. The call rejects every rival claim to worship and fulfills the command to have no other gods before Him (Exodus 20:3). Jesus likewise taught His disciples to begin prayer with reverence for the Father’s name: “Hallowed be Your name” (Matthew 6:9).

bring an offering
Worship in Israel included tangible gifts presented before the LORD in thanksgiving, consecration, and acknowledgment of sin and forgiveness. The offerings prescribed in Leviticus 1–7 testified that all provision belonged to God and that access to Him required atonement. Sacrifices could not replace a faithful heart, but they gave God’s people an appointed way to approach Him. They ultimately pointed to Jesus Christ, whose sacrifice fulfilled the sacrificial system and secured forgiveness for all who trust in Him (John 1:29; Hebrews 10:11–14).

Israel’s worship was never meant to be confined to Israel. The LORD, the covenant God of His people, is King over every land, ruler, and people. Earthly kings exercised limited and temporary authority, but God’s reign is absolute and everlasting. The good news of His kingship brings peace and salvation, anticipating Isaiah’s proclamation that “Your God reigns” and Christ’s command to make disciples of all nations.

Unlike the false gods of surrounding nations, the Lord made the heavens and rules over them. Their gladness anticipates the day when all creation is freed from the curse and shares in the liberty brought by God’s final redemption (Romans 8:19–21).

The cultivated land, dependent on rain and God’s blessing, joins the summons for all creation to praise the Lord. Israel’s fields testified to His covenant provision through grain, vineyards, olive groves, and pasturelands. The sabbatical and Jubilee laws of Leviticus 25 also showed that the land belonged to God and rested under His care. Isaiah foretold a day when even wilderness places would rejoice and blossom (Isaiah 35:1–2).

Crops, living creatures, and every provision arising from the ground belong to the Creator. God declared His completed creation “very good” (Genesis 1:31), though the fall brought decay and frustration into the created order. Romans 8:19–22 looks ahead to creation’s liberation when the children of God are revealed in glory.
