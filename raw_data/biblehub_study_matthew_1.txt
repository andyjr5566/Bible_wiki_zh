The Genealogy of Jesus

Matthew 1:1

This is the record of the genealogy of Jesus Christ,
Matthew begins with Jesus’ documented human lineage, grounding the gospel in God’s acts within history. Jewish genealogies established family identity, inheritance, priestly or royal claims, and covenant membership. Jesus Christ is the promised Messiah, whose coming fulfills the purposes God revealed throughout Israel’s history.

the son of David,
David received God’s covenant promise that his kingdom and throne would endure forever through a descendant who would reign in righteousness (2 Samuel 7:12–16; Isaiah 9:6–7). Jesus possesses the rightful royal claim as David’s heir, fulfilling the expectation of Israel’s coming King. His kingdom surpasses David’s earthly reign, for He reigns eternally and brings salvation to His people.

the son of Abraham:
Abraham received God’s promise that through his offspring all the families of the earth would be blessed (Genesis 12:1–3; 22:18). Jesus is the fulfillment of that promise, bringing redemption first within Israel and then to every nation. Matthew’s opening line therefore joins the royal covenant with David and the worldwide blessing promised through Abraham, presenting Jesus as the long-awaited Savior in whom God’s covenant purposes reach their fulfillment.

Through Isaac, rather than Ishmael, God established the covenant line and promised to multiply Abraham’s descendants. Isaac’s preservation on Mount Moriah also revealed God’s provision of a substitute sacrifice, anticipating God’s greater provision in His Son.

Jacob, though marked by sin and conflict, was chosen by God before his birth and later received the covenant name Israel. God’s grace transformed Jacob and carried the promised line forward through him.

Judah later confessed that Tamar had acted more righteously than he had (Genesis 38:26). Her inclusion in the Messiah’s genealogy reveals God’s sovereign grace working through human sin, failure, and repentance. Perez became the chosen line, although Zerah first appeared at the birth.

Perez the father of Hezron,
Perez, whose name means “breach” or “bursting forth,” carried forward the family line of Judah, from whom Jacob had prophesied that royal authority would arise (Genesis 49:10). His descendants became prominent within Judah, and his genealogy is preserved in Ruth 4:18–22 and 1 Chronicles 2:5. God faithfully maintained the promised line through generations otherwise scarcely recorded in Scripture.

Amminadab’s family also became closely connected to Israel’s priesthood, for his daughter Elisheba married Aaron (Exodus 6:23).

Amminadab the father of Nahshon,
Nahshon was a chief of the tribe of Judah during Israel’s wilderness journey and represented his tribe in the census of the people (Numbers 1:7). He led Judah’s division when the tribes camped and marched, a fitting indication of Judah’s growing prominence among the tribes (Numbers 2:3). Through Nahshon, the royal line continued while his sister Elisheba’s marriage to Aaron linked his family to the high-priestly household.

Salmon was the father of Boaz by Rahab,
Rahab had been a Canaanite inhabitant of Jericho who sheltered Israel’s spies and confessed faith in the Lord as the God of heaven and earth (Joshua 2:9–11). Her household was spared when Jericho fell, and she was received among Israel (Joshua 6:22–25). By faith she turned from her former life to identify with God’s people, and Scripture commends her faith as active and obedient (Hebrews 11:31; James 2:25). Her place in the Messiah’s genealogy displays God’s grace toward those once outside the covenant people and anticipates the gathering of Gentiles through Christ.

Boaz the father of Obed by Ruth,
Ruth, though a Moabite widow, forsook her homeland and its gods to remain with Naomi and trust in the God of Israel (Ruth 1:16–17). Boaz, a righteous and generous relative of Naomi’s family, acted as kinsman-redeemer by marrying Ruth and preserving the family line and inheritance (Ruth 4:9–10). Their marriage came through God’s providence and brought redemption to a family marked by loss. Boaz’s willing redemption of Ruth points forward to Christ, who redeems His people at great cost and brings them into His covenant family.

Obed the father of Jesse,
Obed’s birth restored joy and hope to Naomi, whose emptiness was turned into blessing through the Lord’s faithful provision (Ruth 4:14–17). His name means “servant” or “worshiper.” Obed became the father of Jesse, the father of David, establishing the family line from which God promised an everlasting King. The genealogy moves from the redemption of one household in Bethlehem toward the fulfillment of God’s covenant with David and, ultimately, to Jesus Christ, the promised Son of David.

and Jesse the father of David the king.
Jesse of Bethlehem belonged to the tribe of Judah, the tribe appointed for royal rule in Jacob’s prophecy (Genesis 49:10). God chose Jesse’s youngest son, David, and Samuel anointed him as Israel’s king (1 Samuel 16:1–13). David established Jerusalem as Israel’s capital and received God’s covenant promise of an enduring dynasty (2 Samuel 7:12–16). Bethlehem, David’s hometown, would also be the birthplace of the greater King, Jesus Christ, the promised Son of David (Micah 5:2; Matthew 2:1).

Solomon the father of Rehoboam,

Abijah, also called Abijam, succeeded Rehoboam in Judah and continued the Davidic line during a period of conflict with the northern kingdom. His appeal to the Lord’s covenant with David during battle against Jeroboam showed that Judah’s rightful hope rested in God’s promise, not merely in political strength.
