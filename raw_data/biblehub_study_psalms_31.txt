For the choirmaster.
The psalm was entrusted to Israel’s chief musician for use in gathered worship. David’s cry for deliverance became the prayer of the covenant people, teaching worshipers to bring danger, sorrow, and confidence in God before Him together.

A Psalm of David.
David wrote from a life marked by conflict, betrayal, kingship, and dependence upon the LORD. His experience of fleeing enemies and receiving God’s preserving help gives concrete weight to the prayer, while the psalm also speaks for all who seek the Lord amid trouble.

In Israel’s covenant community, family, reputation, and belonging were vital to a person’s life and security; abandonment could feel like death itself. Job likewise mourned the loss of relatives and friends (Job 19:14). Human beings may forget the afflicted, but the Lord remembers His covenant and does not abandon those who trust Him. David’s rejection also anticipates Christ, who was despised, forsaken by men, and bore the horror of abandonment as He suffered for sinners (Isaiah 53:3; Matthew 27:46).

Christ was wounded and crushed for the sins of His people, yet His suffering was not defeat; through His death and resurrection, He brings restoration to those broken by sin and sorrow (Isaiah 53:5).

David knew that safety ultimately rested not in walls, armies, or escape from danger, but in nearness to God. Psalm 31:22

In my alarm I said, “I am cut off from Your sight!”
David recalls a moment when danger overwhelmed his confidence in God’s nearness. To be cut off from God’s sight meant, in Israel’s covenant understanding, to be deprived of His protecting favor and care. Yet David’s fear did not describe reality; the Lord had not abandoned him. Psalm 22 records a deeper cry of forsakenness that finds its fullest fulfillment in Christ, who bore judgment on the cross so that all who trust Him would never be cast away from God.

Psalm 31:23

Love the LORD, all His saints.
David calls God’s covenant people to wholehearted devotion. Love for the LORD includes trust, obedience, worship, and grateful allegiance to the God who has revealed His steadfast love. Israel was commanded to love the LORD with all the heart, soul, and strength (Deuteronomy 6:4–5), and Jesus affirmed this as the greatest commandment (Matthew 22:37–38). Those redeemed by God are set apart to love Him because He first loved them and has proven Himself faithful in deliverance and mercy.

Psa 97:10 Hate evil, O you who love the LORD! He delivers them…

Deu 6:5 And you shall love the LORD your God with all your heart…

1Jn 4:19 We love because He first loved us.

The LORD preserves the faithful,
The LORD watches over those who remain steadfast in reliance upon Him. David’s own life testified to that care: though pursued by Saul, threatened by enemies, and afflicted by distress, he was not abandoned. God’s preservation does not mean His people never suffer; it means their lives, faith, and final inheritance are secure in His hands. The same faithful God keeps believers and will complete His saving work in them (1 Thessalonians 5:23–24).

but fully repays the arrogant.
God’s protection of the faithful stands in sharp contrast to His judgment upon the proud. The arrogant exalt themselves, resist God’s authority, and trust in their own strength rather than in Him. Pharaoh’s defiance brought ruin upon Egypt, and Nebuchadnezzar was humbled until he acknowledged the sovereignty of the Most High. The LORD opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble (James 4:6), ensuring that rebellion against Him will not escape His righteous judgment.

Psalm 31:24

Be strong and courageous,
David closes a psalm of distress and deliverance by calling God’s people to steadfast confidence. Joshua received the same charge when he was appointed to lead Israel into Canaan, where courage was required to obey God amid real opposition. Strength for such obedience comes from the Lord’s presence, faithfulness, and saving power, not from human resolve alone.

God’s people may face trouble without surrendering to fear, because the Lord preserves the faithful and will finally fulfill His promises in Christ.
