Its completion testified that the Lord had given Israel rest from surrounding enemies and had established Solomon’s kingdom in peace.

The Temple was built on Mount Moriah, at the threshing floor David purchased after the plague upon Israel (2 Chronicles 3:1; 2 Samuel 24:18–25). Its splendor displayed the holiness and majesty of the LORD, yet its inner sanctuary also showed that sinful people could not approach God casually. The sacrifices and priesthood anticipated Christ, through whom God dwelt among His people and opened lasting access to Himself (John 1:14; Hebrews 10:19–22).
