The eastern tribes had built the altar beside the Jordan after returning to their inheritance east of the river. Although the half-tribe of Manasseh shared that territory, Reuben and Gad are named as the principal builders. The altar was not intended for sacrifices, which God had appointed to be offered at the tabernacle, but as a memorial visible to future generations. Its name preserved the purpose of the structure: to testify that the tribes on both sides of the Jordan belonged to the same covenant people.
