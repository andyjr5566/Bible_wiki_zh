This phrase refers to the women who had visited Jesus’ tomb early on the first day of the week. They were on their way to inform the disciples of the resurrection, as instructed by the angel. This highlights the role of women as the first witnesses to the resurrection, which is significant given the cultural context where women’s testimonies were often undervalued. This moment underscores the transformative nature of the Gospel, where societal norms are upended.

His presence confirmed that He had truly risen bodily, fulfilling His repeated promises that He would rise and meet them in Galilee. Jesus did not leave His followers to uncertainty; He revealed Himself, received their worship, and prepared them for their lifelong mission.

“All authority in heaven and on earth
Jesus possesses absolute dominion over every power, ruler, nation, angelic being, and created realm. Daniel foresaw the Son of Man receiving everlasting dominion from the Ancient of Days, and the resurrection openly vindicated Jesus as that promised King. Nothing in heaven or on earth lies outside His rule. The mission given to the disciples rests not on their strength, influence, or numbers, but on the reign of the crucified and risen Christ.

Matthew 28:20

and teaching them to obey all that I have commanded you.
Jesus calls His disciples to form obedient followers, not merely informed listeners. His instruction encompasses the whole of His teaching, including the righteousness of the Sermon on the Mount, the command to love God and neighbor, humility, forgiveness, prayer, holiness, and faith in Him. Like Israel, who was commanded to teach God’s words diligently to each generation (Deuteronomy 6:6–7), the church is charged to preserve and impart the Lord’s revealed will. Obedience flows from genuine discipleship and demonstrates love for Christ (John 14:15).

And surely I am with you always,
The risen Lord does not send His servants into the world without His sustaining presence. His promise recalls God’s assurance to Joshua as Israel entered the land: “The Lord your God is with you wherever you go” (Joshua 1:9). Jesus is Immanuel, “God with us” (Matthew 1:23), and His abiding presence confirms His divine authority and faithfulness. Through the Holy Spirit, He strengthens, guides, comforts, and preserves His people as they carry out His mission.

even to the end of the age.”
Jesus remains with His church throughout the present age until His visible return and the completion of God’s redemptive purposes. The mission continues until the Son of Man comes in glory to establish fully the everlasting kingdom foretold in Daniel 7:13–14. The promise gives believers confidence amid opposition and hardship: Christ’s authority will not fail, His gospel will accomplish His purpose, and His people will finally dwell with Him in the new creation (Revelation 21:1–4).
