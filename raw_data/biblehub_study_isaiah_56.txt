Salvation for Foreigners

Isaiah 56:1

This is what the LORD says:
The Lord speaks with covenant authority, calling His people to live in a manner consistent with His holy character. Isaiah places God’s command before the promise of deliverance, showing that faith in God’s coming salvation must produce obedience in daily life.

God required His people to uphold justice in courts, families, commerce, and care for the poor, the widow, the foreigner, and the oppressed. Such righteousness was never a means of earning salvation; it was the proper fruit of belonging to the God who judges evil and defends the helpless. Micah 6:8 and Amos 5:24 echo the same demand.

God promised to intervene for His people, including restoration after exile. The promised salvation ultimately centers in the Messiah, whose coming brought redemption from sin and whose return will complete God’s deliverance. God’s righteous character would be displayed openly as He saved His faithful people and judged rebellion. Christ revealed that righteousness through His sinless life, atoning death, and resurrection, and God’s righteousness will be fully seen when He returns to judge the world and establish the new heavens and new earth.

Isaiah 56:2

Blessed is the man who does this,
God pronounces His favor on everyone who practices the justice and righteousness commanded in Isaiah 56:1. Blessing rests not on heritage, status, or outward profession, but on faithful obedience flowing from reverence for the Lord. The promise anticipates the widening invitation of Isaiah 56, where foreigners and eunuchs who keep God’s covenant are welcomed among His people.

Every person is called to cling firmly to God’s revealed will with enduring faithfulness. Such perseverance reflects the righteous life pictured in Psalm 1, rooted in God’s instruction and bearing fruit through changing seasons. God seeks steadfast obedience that remains secure when pressure, opposition, or personal cost tempts His people to compromise.

who keeps the Sabbath without profaning it
Sabbath keeping marked Israel as a people belonging to the Lord, who created the world and delivered them from Egypt. By resting from ordinary labor and devoting the day to God, Israel confessed that its life, provision, and security came from Him. Neglect of the Sabbath revealed spiritual rebellion and contributed to the nation’s covenant unfaithfulness before the exile. Jesus affirmed that the Sabbath was made for humanity’s good and displayed His authority as Lord of the Sabbath. The weekly rest also directs believers toward the lasting rest secured through Christ.

God’s blessing accompanies a life that turns from evil in conduct as well as worship. Clean hands and a pure heart belong together before the holy God, as Psalm 24 teaches. True covenant faithfulness reaches into daily choices, restraining injustice, dishonesty, violence, and every act that contradicts God’s righteous character.

Isaiah 56:3

Let no foreigner who has joined himself to the LORD say,
God welcomes Gentiles who renounce idolatry and bind themselves to Him in faith and obedience. Israel remained God’s covenant nation, yet the Lord’s purpose always included blessing all nations through Abraham’s seed (Genesis 12:3). Foreigners such as Rahab and Ruth found a place among God’s people through faith, and the promise anticipates the gathering of Gentiles through Christ.

“The LORD will utterly exclude me from His people.”
No believing worshiper is rejected because of ancestry or former distance from Israel. The Lord calls His people to keep justice, do righteousness, and hold fast to His covenant. In Christ, Gentile believers are no longer strangers and aliens but fellow citizens with the saints and members of God’s household (Ephesians 2:19).

And let the eunuch not say,
Eunuchs bore a condition that often brought public shame and exclusion. Deuteronomy 23:1 restricted such men from entering the assembly of the LORD, preserving the ceremonial holiness of Israel under the Mosaic covenant. Isaiah announces God’s coming work of grace, in which faithful eunuchs would not be denied acceptance or honor before Him.

“I am but a dry tree.”
A dry tree pictures barrenness, the loss of descendants, and the fear of leaving no enduring remembrance. God promises faithful eunuchs a lasting name within His house, better than sons and daughters, because their inheritance rests in His eternal covenant rather than natural offspring. The Ethiopian eunuch’s conversion through Philip later displays this welcome extended through the gospel (Acts 8:26–39).

Though eunuchs had been restricted from Israel’s assembly under the Mosaic law, the Lord addresses them personally as people accountable to Him and capable of faithful worship. Sabbath observance marked trust in God’s provision, submission to His rule, and participation in the life of His covenant people.

Isaiah 56:6

And the foreigners who join themselves to the LORD
God welcomes Gentiles who turn from idols and bind themselves to Him in faithful allegiance. Israel remained the covenant nation, yet God’s saving purposes always included the nations through Abraham’s offspring. Ruth’s devotion to the God of Israel illustrates such wholehearted attachment, and the promise looks forward to Christ’s command to make disciples of all nations.

Zec 2:11 On that day many nations will join themselves to the LORD,…

Eph 2:19 Therefore you are no longer strangers and foreigners,…

to minister to Him,
Foreigners who came to the Lord were called to worship, serve, and honor Him within the covenant community. They did not receive the Levitical priesthood, which God assigned to Aaron’s line, but they were granted access to the worship of the true God. In Christ, believers from every nation are made a royal priesthood, offering spiritual sacrifices through faith and obedience.

to love the name of the LORD,
Love for the Lord’s name involves loving all that He has revealed Himself to be: holy, righteous, merciful, faithful, and sovereign. In Hebrew culture, a name represents one's character and essence. Such love is shown through reverence, trust, and obedience, fulfilling the command to love the Lord with all the heart, soul, and strength.

all who keep the Sabbath without profaning it
Sabbath observance marked Israel as a people set apart to the Lord and testified that their provision ultimately came from Him. Foreigners who honored the Sabbath demonstrated faith in Israel’s God and respect for His covenant order. The Sabbath also points toward the rest believers receive through Christ, who provides salvation apart from human striving.

The covenant hope announced by Isaiah finds its fulfillment in the new covenant established through Christ’s blood, by which Jews and Gentiles alike are brought near to God through faith.

God promised to gather faithful foreigners along with His covenant people to Mount Zion, the place of His appointed presence and worship. The promise anticipates the day when people from every nation will come to the Lord’s dwelling in Jerusalem, united in reverence for Him.

Israel’s Sinful Leaders

Isaiah 56:10

Israel’s rulers, priests, and false prophets had been entrusted with guarding the nation’s covenant life, yet they failed to recognize the spiritual danger created by idolatry, injustice, and rebellion. Their blindness left the people exposed to judgment.
