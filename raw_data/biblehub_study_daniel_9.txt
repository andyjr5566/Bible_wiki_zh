Daniel 9:4

And I prayed to the LORD my God and confessed,
Daniel approached the covenant God personally and reverently, yet he identified himself with the guilt of Judah. Though Daniel had lived faithfully in exile, he did not stand apart from his people as their accuser. His confession arose from Jeremiah’s prophecy concerning Jerusalem’s seventy-year desolation and from confidence that God alone could restore His people. Israel’s return would depend not on human strength or Persian policy, but on the mercy of the God against whom they had sinned.

Daniel confessed that Israel’s exile resulted from a long pattern of resisting God’s repeated warnings. The Lord had sent prophets such as Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Hosea, and Amos to expose sin, summon repentance, and promise restoration for those who returned to Him. Jeremiah had warned Judah for years that Babylonian judgment was coming, yet the nation refused to listen (Jeremiah 25:4–11). Daniel included himself among the guilty people, acknowledging corporate sin rather than assigning blame only to earlier generations.

Israel’s refusal therefore revealed not merely a failure of wisdom but rebellion against divine authority.
