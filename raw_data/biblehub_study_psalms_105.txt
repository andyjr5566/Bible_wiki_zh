Psalm 105:1

Give thanks to the LORD,
Israel’s worship began with remembering the LORD’s covenant mercy, mighty deliverance, and continual provision. Thank offerings accompanied sacrifices of peace offerings, declaring publicly that every good gift came from God. The psalm calls God’s people to respond to His faithfulness with grateful worship, especially for His preservation of Abraham’s descendants and His redemption of Israel.

The LORD had revealed Himself to the patriarchs and later made known His covenant name to Moses as the God who would deliver His people from Egypt. Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob called upon the LORD in dependence, building altars and trusting His promises. The Psalms served both public worship and private devotion, teaching God’s people to direct their hearts and voices toward Him. Believers likewise sing to the Lord with gratitude, allowing the word of Christ to dwell richly among them (Ephesians 5:19; Colossians 3:16).

Corporate praise unites God’s people around the truth of who He is and what He has done. Through Christ, His people continually offer “the sacrifice of praise to God” (Hebrews 13:15).

Psalm 105:10

He confirmed it to Jacob as a decree,
This phrase refers to God’s reaffirmation of His covenant promises to Jacob, the grandson of Abraham. The term “decree” indicates a formal and authoritative order, underscoring the seriousness and unchangeable nature of God’s promise. Jacob, later named Israel, is a central patriarch in the biblical narrative, and his life is marked by divine encounters and promises. The confirmation of the covenant to Jacob is recorded in Genesis 28:13-15, where God appears to him in a dream at Bethel, promising land, descendants, and blessings. This decree is part of the Abrahamic Covenant, initially given to Abraham in Genesis 12:1-3 and reiterated to Isaac in Genesis 26:3-4. The covenant’s confirmation to Jacob signifies the continuation of God’s plan through the patriarchal line, emphasizing the faithfulness of God across generations.

to Israel as an everlasting covenant:
The use of “Israel” here refers to both the individual Jacob, who was renamed Israel after wrestling with God (Genesis 32:28), and the nation that descended from him. The term “everlasting covenant” highlights the perpetual nature of God’s promises, which are not limited by time or human failure. This covenant includes the promise of land, a great nation, and blessings to all nations through his descendants, ultimately fulfilled in Jesus Christ, a descendant of Jacob. The everlasting nature of the covenant is echoed in other scriptures, such as Genesis 17:7, where God promises Abraham that the covenant will be everlasting. This covenant is foundational to the identity and theology of Israel, shaping their understanding of God’s faithfulness and their role in His redemptive plan. The concept of an everlasting covenant also points to the New Covenant established through Christ, as seen in Jeremiah 31:31-34 and Hebrews 8:6-13, where the promises to Israel find their ultimate fulfillment.

Psalm 105:11

“I will give you the land of Canaan
This phrase refers to God's promise to the descendants of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The land of Canaan, located in the ancient Near East, is a region that corresponds to modern-day Israel, Palestine, Lebanon, and parts of Jordan and Syria. This promise is first mentioned in Genesis 12:7 when God tells Abraham, "To your offspring I will give this land." The land of Canaan is significant as it represents the fulfillment of God's covenant with the patriarchs. Archaeological evidence supports the historical presence of various Canaanite cultures in this region, which the Israelites later inhabited. Theologically, this promise signifies God's faithfulness and sovereignty in fulfilling His promises.

The term "inheritance" in biblical context often refers to the land that God promised to His people, which they would receive as a divine gift. This concept is rooted in the idea of a familial inheritance, where land and blessings are passed down through generations. In Numbers 34, the boundaries of the land are detailed, emphasizing its importance as a tangible inheritance for the tribes of Israel. Spiritually, this inheritance is a type of the eternal inheritance believers receive through Christ, as mentioned in Ephesians 1:11. The land of Canaan as an inheritance also foreshadows the ultimate rest and inheritance believers will receive in the Kingdom of God, as seen in Hebrews 4:8-10.
