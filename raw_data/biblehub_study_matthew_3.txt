John’s coarse clothing identified him with Elijah, who was known by his garment of hair and leather belt (2 Kings 1:8). Malachi foretold Elijah’s coming before the day of the Lord (Malachi 4:5–6), and Jesus later identified John as the promised forerunner who came in Elijah’s prophetic role (Matthew 11:14; 17:10–13). Camel’s hair was rough and inexpensive, marking John’s separation from worldly comfort and status. His appearance matched his message: Israel needed repentance and readiness for the Messiah.

John did not seek the recognition, wealth, or religious prestige associated with Jerusalem’s leaders. He stood apart from the institutions that had grown spiritually complacent and called the people back to wholehearted obedience to God. His life testified that the coming King demanded humility, repentance, and faith.

Israel’s wilderness history had been a place of testing, dependence, and preparation; John’s wilderness ministry summoned the nation to such dependence again. His austere diet reinforced the urgency of his proclamation that the kingdom of heaven was near and that the Messiah was coming.

Matthew 3:6

Confessing their sins,
This phrase highlights the act of repentance, a central theme in the ministry of John the Baptist. Confession of sins was a public acknowledgment of wrongdoing and a desire for spiritual renewal. In the Jewish tradition, confession was often associated with the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:29-30) and other penitential practices. The act of confessing sins before baptism signifies a turning away from past transgressions and a commitment to a new way of life. This practice is echoed in 1 John 1:9, which emphasizes the importance of confession for forgiveness and cleansing.

1Jn 1:9 If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just…

Pro 28:13 He who conceals his sins will not prosper,…

they were baptized by him
John administered a baptism that outwardly marked repentance and readiness for the Messiah. Immersion in water portrayed cleansing and a decisive break with the former life, drawing upon Israel’s practices of ritual washing. John’s baptism did not replace the saving work Christ would accomplish through His death and resurrection, nor did it yet convey the baptism in the Holy Spirit promised through Jesus. It prepared repentant hearts to receive the One who would take away the sin of the world.

Though these groups differed sharply with one another, both stood among the nation’s religious authorities. The Holy Spirit came upon Jesus in a visible, gentle manner, marking Him as the Spirit-anointed Messiah. As the Spirit moved over the waters at creation, so the Spirit’s descent upon Jesus pointed to the beginning of God’s new creation through His Son.

The Spirit remained upon Jesus, equipping Him for His ministry of preaching, healing, confronting evil, and bearing sin. Isaiah had foretold that the Spirit of the Lord would rest upon the Messiah. The Father’s action, the Son’s obedience, and the Spirit’s descent reveal the united work of the triune God in redemption.

Matthew 3:17

And a voice from heaven said,
The Father spoke openly at Jesus’ baptism, giving divine testimony to the beginning of His public ministry. The Son stood in the Jordan, the Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father’s voice revealed the unity of the triune God. God’s public affirmation established Jesus’ authority before He preached, healed, confronted Satan, and called disciples.

Jhn 12:28-30 “Father, glorify Your name!” Then a voice came from heaven:……

“This is My beloved Son,
The Father identified Jesus as His unique and eternal Son, the promised King anticipated in Psalm 2:7. Jesus is greater than the prophets and kings who served God; He is the Son who shares the Father’s glory and carries the Father’s authority. His baptism did not confess sin, but marked His willing identification with the people He came to save.

The Father’s delight in Jesus fulfills the portrait of the Lord’s Servant in Isaiah 42:1. Jesus began His ministry under the Father’s full approval because He perfectly obeyed the Father’s will. The declaration assured those present that Jesus was the Messiah sent to accomplish God’s saving purpose through His righteous life, sacrificial death, and resurrection.
