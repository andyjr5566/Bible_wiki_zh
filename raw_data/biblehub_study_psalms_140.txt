Rescue Me from Evil Men

Psalm 140:1

For the choirmaster.
The psalm was prepared for use in Israel’s public worship under the direction of the appointed leader of sacred music. David’s plea for deliverance was therefore suitable for the whole congregation, teaching God’s people to bring threats, injustice, and fear before the Lord together.

A Psalm of David.
David wrote from a life marked by danger, including Saul’s pursuit, foreign enemies, rebellion, and betrayal. His position as king did not remove his need for God’s protection; it deepened his awareness that security rests in the Lord rather than human strength or power.

Rescue me, O LORD, from evil men.
David appeals to Yahweh, the covenant God who had chosen and sustained Israel. Evil men oppose God’s righteous purposes through deceit, oppression, and hostility toward those who seek to live faithfully. The request recognizes that deliverance ultimately comes from the Lord, who sees hidden wickedness and judges it rightly.

Protect me from men of violence,
Violent people threaten life and peace through force and deliberate cruelty. David asks not merely for escape but for God’s guarding care against those who intend harm. The Lord remains the defender of His people, and His protection finds its fullest assurance in Christ, who delivers believers from the dominion of evil and will finally judge all violence and injustice.

Psalm 140:2

who devise evil in their hearts
David identifies wickedness as deliberate rebellion formed within the inner person before it appears in words or deeds. Scripture presents the heart as the wellspring of human motives and conduct (Proverbs 4:23). Such inward corruption recalls the generation before the flood, when every inclination of human thought was continually evil (Genesis 6:5). God sees hidden plans as clearly as public actions, and He holds people accountable for both. Jesus likewise taught that evil acts proceed from the heart (Mark 7:21–23). Deliverance therefore requires more than restraint of outward behavior; it requires a heart renewed by God.

Pro 6:14 With deceit in his heart he devises evil;…

Mic 2:1 Woe to those who devise iniquity and plot evil on their beds…

and stir up war all day long.
The enemies described do not merely respond to conflict but actively promote it, pursuing violence without rest. David’s life included repeated threats from Saul, hostile nations, and treacherous men within Israel, making this plea a realistic cry for protection amid continual danger. Their restless hostility reflects the work of those who oppose God’s order and peace. Believers still face spiritual conflict against evil powers and human schemes, yet they are called to stand firm in God’s strength rather than return evil for evil (Ephesians 6:12; Romans 12:17–21).

Psalm 140:3

They sharpen their tongues like snakes;
David portrays his enemies as deliberately preparing deceitful words for harm. Like a blade honed for battle, their speech is calculated to wound, entrap, and destroy. The serpent in Eden used words to challenge God’s command and draw humanity into sin; likewise, wicked speech often works through subtle distortion, accusation, and lies. Paul applies similar language to humanity’s sinfulness in Romans 3:13, showing that corrupt speech reveals a corrupt heart.

Gen 3:1 Now the serpent was more crafty than any beast of the field…

the venom of vipers is on their lips.
Vipers strike suddenly, and their venom brings death. Malicious words may spread slander, divide families and communities, ruin reputations, and encourage evil. James likewise warns that the untamed tongue is “full of deadly poison” (James 3:8). David’s prayer recognizes that violence is not limited to weapons and physical attacks; words can carry the intent and effects of death. God sees the hidden malice behind flattering lips and false accusations.

Saul’s repeated attempts on David’s life illustrate such hostile plotting, yet David entrusted his defense to the Lord rather than taking vengeance into his own hands.

Psalm 140:6

I say to the LORD, “You are my God.”
David personally confesses allegiance to Yahweh, the covenant God of Israel. Amid nations devoted to many false gods, such a confession identified a worshiper as belonging to the Lord alone. David’s words unite personal trust with God’s sovereign rule, echoing Joshua’s resolve that his household would serve the LORD (Joshua 24:15). Every believer is likewise called to acknowledge God not merely as Creator and King, but as the One to whom he belongs. Thomas’s worship of the risen Christ, “My Lord and my God,” reveals the fullness of this confession in the New Testament (John 20:28).

Hear, O LORD, my cry for help.
David appeals to the God he knows as his own, trusting that Yahweh receives the prayers of His people. Surrounded by violent and deceitful enemies, David does not look first to human strength or political protection but to the Lord’s intervention. His confidence was formed through seasons of danger, including his flight from Saul, when God preserved him despite repeated threats (1 Samuel 23:14–15). Believers may bring every need before God with the same assurance, for Christ our great High Priest sympathizes with human weakness and gives mercy and grace in time of need (Hebrews 4:14–16).

Psalm 140:10

May burning coals fall on them;
Burning coals recall the fire and brimstone poured out upon Sodom and Gomorrah in God’s judgment against persistent wickedness (Genesis 19:24). David commits his enemies to the righteous Judge rather than taking vengeance into his own hands. Coals also belonged to the altar of incense, where prayer rose before God (Exodus 30:1–10); the petition places the cause of the afflicted before the Lord.

Psalm 140:12

I know that the LORD upholds justice for the poor
David rests in the settled knowledge that Yahweh, Israel’s covenant-keeping God, acts righteously on behalf of those oppressed by powerful and violent people. God’s concern for the poor runs through the Law and the Prophets, where He commands His people to protect widows, orphans, foreigners, and all who lack human defenders (Deuteronomy 10:18; Isaiah 1:17). Earthly courts may be corrupted, but the Lord’s judgment cannot be bribed or overturned.

Psalm 140:13

Surely the righteous will praise Your name;
David closes his plea for deliverance from violent and deceitful enemies with confidence that God will vindicate those who belong to Him. The righteous trust the Lord, submit to His ways, and respond to His saving acts with worship. God’s name reveals His holy character, covenant faithfulness, power, and mercy. Praise is therefore more than gratitude for rescue; it is the public acknowledgment that the Lord alone is righteous and worthy of honor. David’s confidence anticipates the continual praise of God’s people, fulfilled ultimately as all who are redeemed through Christ confess His lordship.

the upright will dwell in Your presence.
The upright are those whose lives are marked by integrity before God, not sinless perfection but sincere faith and obedience. David longed to live near the Lord’s presence, which Israel knew especially through the tabernacle and later the temple in Jerusalem. God’s presence brings safety, fellowship, and joy, in contrast to the ruin awaiting the wicked. Through Christ, believers are reconciled to God and receive access to Him now; the promise will reach its fullness when God dwells with His people forever in the new creation.
