The Beginning

John 1:1

In the beginning
John reaches behind the creation of heaven and earth to the eternal existence of the Son. Before anything was made, the Word already was. The opening recalls Genesis 1:1 and identifies the Word as present before all created things, sharing the eternity that belongs to God alone.

Gen 1:1 In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.

Psa 90:2 Before the mountains were born or You brought forth…

Heb 1:10 “In the beginning, O Lord, You laid the foundations…

was the Word,
The Word (Logos in Greek, meaning reason or the principle of order in the universe) is the eternal Son, later revealed as Jesus Christ in John 1:14. God created through Him, revealed His will through Him, and brings salvation through Him. He is not part of creation but the One through whom creation came into being.

Psa 33:6 By the word of the LORD the heavens were made…

2Pe 3:5 But they deliberately overlook the fact that long ago…

Heb 11:3 By faith we understand that the universe was formed…

and the Word was with God,
The Word existed in personal communion with God the Father from eternity. The Son is distinct from the Father, not merely another name or appearance of the Father, yet their fellowship is perfect and unbroken. The Father’s love for the Son precedes the foundation of the world.

Jhn 17:5 And now, Father, glorify Me in Your presence…

1Jn 1:2 And this is the life that was revealed; we have seen it…

Pro 8:30 Then I was a skilled craftsman at His side,…

and the Word was God.
The Word possesses the full nature and glory of God. Jesus Christ is neither a created messenger nor a lesser divine being; He is truly God and truly distinct from the Father. His incarnation did not begin His existence but revealed in human flesh the eternal Son through whom God is known.

Heb 1:8 But about the Son He says: “Your throne, O God,…

Col 2:9 For in Christ all the fullness of the Deity…

Tit 2:13 as we await the blessed hope and glorious appearance…

John 1:2

He was with God
The Word existed in eternal fellowship with the Father before creation. The Son was distinct in person yet fully shared the divine glory, holiness, and life of God. Jesus later spoke of the glory He possessed with the Father before the world existed (John 17:5). The eternal communion within the Godhead reveals that God did not become Father only when He created or when the Son entered the world.

1Jn 1:2 And this is the life that was revealed; we have seen it…

Jhn 17:5 And now, Father, glorify Me in Your presence…

Pro 8:30 Then I was a skilled craftsman at His side,…

in the beginning.
Before the heavens, the earth, time, and every created thing came into being, the Word already was. John deliberately recalls Genesis 1:1, showing that the One present at creation was not part of creation. All things were made through Him, and He sustains all things by His power (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16–17). The One who became flesh in Jesus Christ stands eternally above the world He came to redeem.

Gen 1:1 In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.

Pro 8:23 From everlasting I was established, from the beginning,…

Col 1:16 For in Him all things were created, things in heaven…

John 1:3

Through Him all things were made,
The eternal Word was active with the Father in the creation of all things. Genesis 1 reveals God creating by His spoken word, and John identifies the personal Word as the Son, Jesus Christ. Colossians 1:16 declares that all things were created through Him and for Him, while Hebrews 1:2 likewise names the Son as God’s agent in making the world. The Son is not part of creation but stands before it as its divine Maker. Proverbs 8 portrays wisdom present at creation, anticipating the perfect wisdom of God revealed in Christ.

Col 1:16 For in Him all things were created, things in heaven…

Heb 1:2 But in these last days He has spoken to us by His Son…

1Co 8:6 Yet for us there is but one God, the Father…

and without Him nothing was made
No created thing came into existence apart from the Son’s will and power. The material world is therefore not the work of an inferior being or an evil source, but the work of the holy and sovereign God. Visible creation, human life, angelic beings, and every realm of existence owe their origin to Christ. His authority over creation rests on His identity as its Creator.

Isa 44:24 Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer who formed you…

Psa 33:6 By the word of the LORD the heavens were made…

Heb 11:3 By faith we understand that the universe was formed…

that has been made.
Every created reality belongs within the scope of Christ’s work. Nothing exists independently of Him, and nothing lies beyond His rule. Hebrews 1:3 teaches that the Son not only made all things but also upholds them by His powerful word. The One through whom creation began remains actively present, sustaining the world and directing history toward His purposes.

Gen 1:1 In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.

Rev 4:11 Worthy are You, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor…

Eph 3:9 and to illuminate for everyone…

John 1:4

In Him was life,
The eternal Word possesses life in Himself and is the source of all created life. Through Him all things came into being, including humanity, to whom God gave the breath of life (Genesis 2:7). Jesus also gives eternal life to those who believe in Him, for He has authority to grant life and will raise the dead (John 5:21, 26; 11:25). All things hold together through Him (Colossians 1:16–17).

Jhn 5:26 For as the Father has life in Himself, so also…

1Jn 5:11-12 And this is that testimony: God has given us eternal life,…

Act 3:15 You killed the Author of life, but God raised Him…

and that life was the light of men.
The life Jesus gives reveals God’s truth and leads people out of sin and spiritual darkness. As the Light of the world, He makes known the Father, exposes evil, and provides the way to salvation (John 8:12; 14:6). Isaiah foretold that those dwelling in darkness would see a great light (Isaiah 9:2), fulfilled in Christ’s coming. In Him, the fountain of life becomes the light by which humanity sees God (Psalm 36:9).

Psa 36:9 For with You is the fountain of life; in Your light…

Isa 9:2 The people walking in darkness have seen a great light;…

2Co 4:6 For God, who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,”…

John 1:5

The Light shines in the darkness,
Jesus Christ enters a fallen world as the revelation of God’s holiness, truth, and life. John echoes Genesis 1:3, where God’s word brought light into the original darkness, showing that the eternal Son is the Creator and the source of new creation. Isaiah foretold that those dwelling in deep darkness would see a great light, fulfilled in the Messiah’s coming to save sinners. Christ’s light continues to expose sin, dispel spiritual blindness, and lead His people into the knowledge of God.

2Co 4:6 For God, who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,”…

Isa 9:2 The people walking in darkness have seen a great light;…

Isa 60:1 Arise, shine, for your light has come…

and the darkness has not overcome it.
The rebellion, unbelief, and powers of evil opposed Christ throughout His earthly ministry, culminating in His crucifixion. Yet death could not hold Him, and the resurrection declared His decisive victory over sin, Satan, and the world’s darkness. Evil neither extinguishes Christ’s life nor comprehends His divine glory. His light remains present and victorious, giving believers confidence amid suffering and spiritual conflict.

Jhn 12:35 Then Jesus told them, “For a little while longer…

1Jn 2:8 Then again, I am also writing to you a new commandment,…

1Th 5:5 For you are all sons of the light and sons of the day;…

The Witness of John

John 1:6

There came a man
John the Baptist entered history at the appointed time as the forerunner of the Messiah. Unlike the eternal Word introduced in the preceding verses, John was a man, born into the priestly family of Zechariah and Elizabeth. His birth fulfilled Gabriel’s promise and signaled God’s faithfulness to prepare Israel for the Lord’s coming (Luke 1:13–17).

Mat 3:1 In those days John the Baptist came, preaching…

Mrk 1:4 John the Baptist appeared in the wilderness,…

Luk 3:2 During the high priesthood of Annas and Caiaphas,…

who was sent from God.
John’s ministry rested on divine commission rather than human appointment. He fulfilled the prophetic promise of a voice preparing the way of the Lord (Isaiah 40:3; Malachi 3:1). His calling was to summon Israel to repentance and to identify Jesus as the promised Lamb of God.

Mal 3:1 “Behold, I will send My messenger, who will prepare the way”…

Mat 11:10 This is the one about whom it is written: …

Luk 1:17 And he will go on before the Lord in the spirit…

His name was John.
John means “Yahweh is gracious.” God Himself appointed his name before his birth, declaring the grace shown in sending a witness before Christ (Luke 1:13). John’s entire ministry directed attention away from himself and toward Jesus, whose coming brought the saving light of God into the world.

John 1:7

He came as a witness
John the Baptist was sent by God to give credible public testimony concerning the Messiah. His ministry fulfilled Isaiah 40:3 and Malachi 3:1 as he prepared Israel for the Lord’s arrival.

to testify about the Light,
The Light is Jesus Christ, the divine Son who reveals God, exposes darkness, and brings life to those who receive Him. John openly directed attention away from himself and toward Jesus, declaring Him to be the Lamb of God.

Mat 4:16 the people living in darkness have seen a great light;…

so that through him
God used John’s preaching, baptism, and testimony to call people to repentance and readiness for Christ. John was not the source of salvation; he was God’s appointed messenger pointing others to the Savior.

1Pe 1:21 Through Him you believe in God, who raised Him from the dead…

Eph 2:18 For through Him we both have access to the Father…

Rom 5:2 through whom we have gained access by faith into this grace…

everyone might believe.
The witness of John announced that faith in Jesus was God’s purpose for all people. Belief involves trusting Christ as the promised Messiah and receiving the life He alone gives.

Rom 1:16 I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power…

John 1:8

He himself was not the Light,
John the Baptist was not the Messiah or the source of divine revelation. Though many in Judea wondered whether he might be the promised Christ, John plainly denied it and directed attention away from himself to Jesus (John 1:20; 3:28). His humility preserved the essential distinction between the messenger and the One sent by the Father. Jesus alone is the true Light, revealing God’s truth, exposing sin, and bringing life to those who believe in Him (John 8:12).

Jhn 5:35 John was a lamp that burned and gave light,…

Luk 3:15-16 The people were waiting expectantly and were all wondering……

Act 13:24-25 Before the arrival of Jesus, John preached…

but he came to testify about the Light.
John’s God-given mission was to prepare the way for Christ, fulfilling the prophetic call to make ready the way of the Lord (Isaiah 40:3; Malachi 3:1). Through his preaching of repentance, his baptism, and his public identification of Jesus as the Lamb of God, John bore faithful witness to the Savior (John 1:29). His ministry shows that every true servant of God points beyond himself to Christ, so that others may believe in Him.

Jhn 3:28-30 You yourselves can testify that I said, ‘I am not the Christ…

John 1:9

The true Light,
Jesus Christ alone reveals God perfectly and exposes the darkness of sin. Light throughout Scripture belongs to God’s own character and saving presence (Psalm 27:1; Isaiah 60:19). Jesus fulfills the promised light for those dwelling in darkness, bringing truth, holiness, and life to a fallen world (Isaiah 9:2; Matt. 4:16).

Jhn 8:12 Once again, Jesus spoke to the people and said,…

Rev 21:23 And the city has no need of sun or moon to shine on it…

who gives light to everyone,
Christ’s coming makes God’s revelation known to humanity without distinction of nation, status, or background. He is the Servant appointed as a light to the nations (Isaiah 49:6). His light gives every person true knowledge of God and leaves unbelief without excuse, though only those who receive Him by faith enter the salvation and life He gives (John 1:12; 3:19–21).

Luk 2:32 a light for revelation to the Gentiles, and for glory…

Act 26:18 open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness…

was coming into the world.
The eternal Word entered human history through the incarnation, fulfilling God’s promises concerning the Messiah’s arrival. The One who made the world came to redeem people living in rebellion against their Creator. His coming begins the decisive revelation of God’s grace and truth, culminating in His death and resurrection for the salvation of all who believe.

John 1:10

He was in the world,
Jesus Christ entered the created order in true humanity without ceasing to be fully God. His birth fulfilled the promise of Immanuel, “God with us” (Isaiah 7:14), and brought God’s saving presence into human history. The eternal Son lived among the people He came to redeem, revealing the Father through His words, works, holiness, compassion, death, and resurrection.

Jhn 9:5 While I am in the world, I am the light of the world.”

Mat 1:23 “Behold, the virgin will be with child”…

The One born in Bethlehem is the divine Word through whom God created all things (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16). Genesis records God bringing creation into being by His spoken command; John reveals that the Son is eternally active in that divine work. Creation belongs to Christ and exists for His glory, yet its Maker entered it in humility as a servant.

Humanity generally failed to acknowledge its Creator and Messiah. Sin had darkened human understanding and hardened hearts against God’s revelation in creation, Scripture, and the person of His Son. Jesus was despised and rejected, as Isaiah foretold (Isaiah 53:3), and even many among His own covenant people refused Him. The rejection of Christ exposes the seriousness of unbelief while preparing the way for the saving sacrifice by which He would reconcile believers to God.

John 1:11

He came to His own,
Jesus entered the covenant people descended from Abraham, to whom God had given the Law, the prophets, the temple, and the messianic promises (Romans 9:4–5). Born in Bethlehem, raised in Nazareth, and ministering in Galilee and Judea, He came in fulfillment of God’s revealed purposes for Israel. The promised Messiah arrived among the very people prepared through Scripture to recognize Him.

Mat 15:24 He answered, “I was sent only to the lost sheep”…

Luk 19:44 They will level you to the ground—you and the children…

and His own did not receive Him.
Many rejected Jesus because He did not conform to expectations of a political deliverer and because His authority exposed unbelief and sin. Israel’s leaders opposed Him, fulfilling the pattern foretold in Isaiah 53:3 and echoed in Psalm 118:22. Yet the rejection was not universal: Jesus’ disciples, His mother, the apostles, and the earliest church were Jewish believers. Israel’s rejection of the Messiah also became the means by which the gospel went to the nations, while God’s purposes for Israel remain sure.

Isa 53:3 He was despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows…

Jesus came to His own people, yet many rejected Him (John 1:11). God’s saving invitation, however, extends to all who welcome the incarnate Word. Receiving Christ involves turning from unbelief to embrace Him personally as the One sent by the Father.

Jhn 13:20 Truly, truly, I tell you, whoever receives the one…

to those who believed in His name,
Faith rests in Jesus’ revealed identity and saving work. His name encompasses all that He is: the promised Messiah, the Son of God, and the Savior of the world. Such belief is more than agreement with facts; it is trust that yields a fruitful life, like seed received in good soil (Matthew 13:23). Salvation is found in no other name (Acts 4:12).

Christ grants believers a new standing within God’s family. They are no longer strangers to God but adopted children and heirs with Christ (Romans 8:15–17; Galatians 4:4–7). This privilege does not arise from ancestry, human effort, or religious heritage, but from God’s grace received through faith. John immediately explains that this family membership comes through new birth from God (John 1:13), fulfilling the promise that those once not God’s people would be called sons of the living God (Hosea 1:10).

John 1:13

children born not of blood,
Physical descent from Abraham, membership in Israel, and family heritage cannot make anyone a child of God. The promises given through Abraham find their fulfillment in Christ, through whom blessing reaches people from every nation.

Luk 3:8 Produce fruit, then, in keeping with repentance.…

Eph 1:5 He predestined us for adoption as His sons…

God grants a new heart and a new identity through the Holy Spirit. Jesus taught that entrance into God’s kingdom requires this birth from above, and all who receive Christ are made members of God’s family.

The Word Became Flesh

John 1:14

The Word became flesh
The eternal Son, through whom all things were created, truly entered human history by taking a complete human nature without ceasing to be God. Born of the virgin Mary in fulfillment of Isaiah 7:14, Jesus is Immanuel, “God with us.” He experienced human weakness, hunger, sorrow, temptation, and suffering, yet remained without sin. In Him, God made Himself known not merely through prophets or written revelation, but in the person of His Son.

Php 2:6-8 Who, existing in the form of God, did not consider…

Heb 2:14 Now since the children have flesh and blood,…

1Ti 3:16 By common confession, the mystery of godliness is great:…

and made His dwelling among us.
Jesus lived among ordinary people in the land of Israel, sharing their world and bearing their burdens. His presence fulfilled the pattern of the tabernacle, where the Lord dwelt among Israel in the wilderness, and of the temple in Jerusalem, where God’s glory was revealed. Jesus Himself became the true meeting place between God and man, the One through whom sinners may draw near to the Father.

Exo 25:8 And they are to make a sanctuary for Me,…

Ezk 37:27 My dwelling place will be with them; I will be their God…

Rev 21:3 And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying:…

We have seen His glory,
John speaks as an eyewitness, joined by the other apostles who saw Christ’s works, heard His words, and encountered Him risen from the dead. Peter, James, and John beheld His majesty on the mountain of Transfiguration, when His appearance shone with heavenly glory. Yet Christ’s glory was also displayed in His compassion, authority, obedience, sacrificial death, and victorious resurrection. The glory once associated with the tabernacle and temple was fully revealed in the incarnate Son.

Luk 9:32 Meanwhile Peter and his companions were overcome by sleep,…

Mat 17:2 There He was transfigured before them. His face shone…

2Pe 1:16-18 For we did not follow cleverly devised fables…

the glory of the one and only Son from the Father,
Jesus possesses a relationship with the Father unlike that of any created being. Believers become God’s children by adoption through faith, but Jesus is the eternal Son who shares the Father’s divine nature and glory. The Father sent Him into the world out of love, and the Son came willingly to accomplish redemption. His unique sonship establishes His authority to reveal the Father and to give eternal life.

Heb 1:3 The Son is the radiance of God’s glory…

1Jn 4:9 This is how God’s love was revealed among us: …

Mat 17:5 While Peter was still speaking, a bright cloud enveloped…

full of grace and truth.
Jesus perfectly reveals God’s mercy and faithfulness. He receives sinners with grace, forgives those who repent, heals the broken, and gives Himself as the sacrifice for sin. He also declares the truth about God, humanity, sin, judgment, and salvation without compromise. In Him, God’s covenant love reaches its fullest revelation, providing what the law could not accomplish: forgiveness, reconciliation, and new life for all who believe.

Jesus Christ possesses the complete fullness of deity, lacking nothing of the divine nature. The eternal Son became truly man without ceasing to be God, and in Him “the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily” (Colossians 2:9). Every saving gift flows from His inexhaustible sufficiency.

John 1:17

For the law was given through Moses;
God gave Israel the Mosaic Law through Moses at Mount Sinai after delivering the nation from Egypt. The Law established Israel’s covenant obligations, revealed God’s holiness, governed worship and community life, and exposed human sin. Its sacrifices and priesthood provided temporary, God-appointed means of atonement while pointing beyond themselves to the need for a final sacrifice. The Law was good and righteous, yet it could not remove sin or give new life. Jeremiah foretold a new covenant in which God would forgive iniquity and write His law upon the hearts of His people.

Jesus Christ brought the fullness toward which the Law and the prophets pointed. In Him, God’s undeserved favor is shown through His incarnation, sinless obedience, atoning death, and victorious resurrection. He fulfills the righteous demands of the Law and establishes the new covenant through His blood. Truth is revealed personally in the Son, who makes the Father known and exposes both sin and the way of salvation. Those who believe in Him are forgiven and justified by faith, not by works of the Law, and are called to live in the grace and holiness He provides.

John 1:18

No one has ever seen God,
God’s infinite holiness and glory exceed human ability to behold Him directly. Even Moses, who spoke with the LORD in a unique manner, was not permitted to see God’s face, lest he die (Exodus 33:20–23). God dwells in unapproachable light and is invisible to mortal eyes (1 Timothy 6:16). Old Testament appearances of God were gracious, accommodated revelations rather than a full unveiling of His divine essence.

Jesus possesses a unique and eternal relationship with the Father. Believers become God’s children by adoption through faith, but the Son belongs to the Father in a way that no created being can share. The Father sent His only Son into the world so that sinners might receive eternal life through Him (John 3:16).

1Jn 4:9 This is how God’s love was revealed among us: …

Jhn 3:16 For God so loved the world that He gave His one and only Son…

Jesus is fully divine, sharing the eternal nature, glory, and authority of the Father. John has already declared that the Word was God and that all things were made through Him (John 1:1–3). The Son is not a lesser deity or a created intermediary; He is the eternal God who took on human flesh.

Jesus identifies Himself as the true connection between heaven and earth, greater than Jacob’s ladder and the true dwelling place of God among His people. The title Son of Man joins His humble humanity with the royal authority promised in Daniel’s vision, where He receives everlasting dominion. Through Him God reveals Himself, fulfills His covenant promises, and brings sinners into fellowship with heaven.
