Uzziah, also called Azariah in Kings, succeeded his father Amaziah after a troubled period in Judah’s history. Though young, he was established by the people of Judah (2 Chronicles 26:1). His early years were marked by seeking the Lord under the instruction of Zechariah, and God granted him success as long as he depended on Him (2 Chronicles 26:5).

and he reigned in Jerusalem fifty-two years.
Uzziah’s unusually long reign brought Judah military strength, agricultural growth, fortified cities, and renewed influence over surrounding nations (2 Chronicles 26:6–15). His reign overlapped the ministries of prophets such as Isaiah, Amos, and Hosea. Yet prosperity became a testing ground for his heart. When pride led him to intrude into the priestly office by offering incense, the Lord struck him with leprosy, and his son Jotham governed in his place (2 Chronicles 26:16–21).

Uzziah, also called Azariah, ruled Judah for fifty-two years during a period of military strength and national prosperity. The Philistines had long threatened Israel and Judah from the coastal plain, and their conflict with God’s people stretched back to the days of the judges, Saul, and David. Uzziah did not merely defend Judah’s borders but took the initiative against a persistent enemy. His victories fulfilled the Lord’s purpose of preserving Judah in the land He had given to His people.

Gath, Jabneh, and Ashdod were important Philistine strongholds guarding the western approaches to Judah. Gath was known as the home of Goliath, while Ashdod was a prominent center of Philistine worship of Dagon. Destroying their walls removed their military protection and publicly displayed the weakening of Philistine power. The Lord gave Uzziah success against cities that had often stood in opposition to Israel, reminding Judah that security came not merely through fortifications or armies but through dependence on God.
