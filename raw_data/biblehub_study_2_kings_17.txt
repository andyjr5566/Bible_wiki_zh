Hoshea the Last King of Israel

2 Kings 17:1

In the twelfth year of the reign of Ahaz over Judah,
Ahaz ruled Judah during a period of deep spiritual compromise and growing Assyrian dominance. While Judah faced its own consequences for unfaithfulness, Israel’s political collapse was rapidly approaching. The reigns of the two kingdoms are placed together to show that God’s judgment was unfolding in history with precision.

Hoshea gained the throne after Pekah was assassinated, amid the instability that had weakened Israel for generations. He became Israel’s final king, ruling a nation already subject to Assyria. His accession did not reverse the nation’s long pattern of idolatry, covenant rebellion, and rejection of the prophets.

2 Kings 17:2

Hoshea’s reign remained under the searching judgment of the covenant God. His conduct did not restore Israel to faithful worship or lead the nation back from the sins that had become established since Jeroboam I. Israel’s coming exile was not merely the result of Assyrian power but of persistent rebellion against the Lord who had redeemed His people from Egypt. The judgment recorded later in the chapter traces the fall of Samaria to idolatry, rejection of God’s commandments, and refusal to heed His prophets.

Israel’s long history of disobedience had reached its appointed judgment, and during Hoshea’s reign Samaria fell to Assyria, bringing the kingdom to an end.
