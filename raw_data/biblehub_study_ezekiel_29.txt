Ezekiel dates the oracle from the exile of King Jehoiachin, placing it about 587 BC. Jerusalem was then under Babylonian siege, and Judah had looked to Egypt for military help despite the Lord’s warnings. Egypt’s brief movement against Babylon had raised false hopes in Jerusalem, but Babylon soon renewed the siege (Jeremiah 37:5–10). God’s judgment against Egypt exposed the futility of trusting human power instead of obeying Him.

In the twenty-seventh year,
The twenty-seventh year of Jehoiachin’s exile falls about 571 BC, making this the latest dated prophecy in Ezekiel. The long interval since Jerusalem’s fall shows that the Lord’s purposes continued to govern events beyond Judah’s immediate catastrophe.

on the first day of the first month,
The first month, Nisan, marked the beginning of Israel’s religious year and included Passover, when Israel remembered the Lord’s deliverance from Egypt. God’s judgment upon Egypt therefore stood in striking contrast to His former use of Egypt as the setting from which He redeemed His people.

“Son of man,
Ezekiel is repeatedly addressed as a mortal man standing before the eternal God. The designation humbles the prophet while affirming that God entrusts His revelation to a human messenger. Jesus later used “Son of Man” as a central title for Himself, identifying with humanity while revealing His authority as the promised ruler of Daniel 7.

Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon
Nebuchadnezzar II ruled Babylon from 605 to 562 BC and became the chief instrument of God’s judgment against Judah and surrounding nations. His military power was real, yet his campaigns remained under the sovereign direction of the Lord.

caused his army to labor strenuously against Tyre.
Nebuchadnezzar besieged Tyre for about thirteen years, from 585 to 572 BC. Tyre’s island stronghold, fortified defenses, and maritime resources made the campaign exceptionally difficult. Babylon’s army endured a costly siege against a city already condemned for pride, wealth, and rejoicing over Jerusalem’s fall.

The outcome shows that nations, armies, victories, and spoils remain under God’s authority.

I will give the land of Egypt to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon,
God appointed Nebuchadnezzar as the instrument of His judgment, even though the Babylonian king did not serve Him knowingly. Babylon’s advance into Egypt demonstrated that the Lord directs the rise and fall of empires. Babylonian records indicate that Nebuchadnezzar campaigned against Egypt late in his reign, fulfilling the announced humiliation of a nation that had repeatedly enticed Judah to trust in human power rather than in God.
