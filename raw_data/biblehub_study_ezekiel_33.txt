Ezekiel the Watchman for Israel

Ezekiel 33:1

Again the word of the LORD came to me, saying,
Ezekiel received another direct message from the LORD during Israel’s exile in Babylon. God had not abandoned His covenant people, though He was judging their rebellion; He continued to speak through His appointed prophet with warnings, instruction, and hope. The repeated arrival of God’s word establishes Ezekiel’s authority as a messenger who must faithfully deliver what he receives. Ezekiel 33 begins a renewed emphasis on personal responsibility and watchfulness as Jerusalem’s fall becomes certain. The LORD’s ongoing revelation through Ezekiel anticipates the fullness of God’s self-disclosure in Jesus Christ, the eternal Word who makes the Father known.

The title keeps Ezekiel humble while emphasizing the gravity of his prophetic office. A watchman stood on the walls or towers of a city, scanning the distance for approaching armies or other danger and sounding an alarm when danger came near. God appointed Ezekiel to a comparable spiritual duty among the exiles. He was responsible to proclaim God’s warning faithfully, regardless of whether Israel listened or refused. The watchman’s task was not to control the response of the people, but to deliver the warning entrusted to him.

Through Ezekiel, He continued to call individuals within the nation to turn from sin and live.

The Message of the Watchman

God again addresses Ezekiel as a human servant appointed to carry His word to Israel. Ezekiel stands before the exiles as a watchman, responsible to warn them faithfully while remaining dependent upon the Lord.

The message belongs to the covenant people living under Babylonian judgment for persistent idolatry, injustice, and rebellion. Though individuals are accountable before God, the nation shares a history of unfaithfulness that has brought devastation and exile.

Indeed, you are to them like a singer of love songs with a beautiful voice,
Ezekiel’s fellow exiles admired his compelling speech and vivid portrayals of God’s judgment and restoration. They treated the prophet’s ministry as an appealing performance rather than receiving him as the Lord’s messenger. Their response fulfilled the pattern of people who hear God’s word outwardly while remaining unwilling to submit their hearts to it (Isaiah 6:9–10).

Music was valued in Israel’s worship, public life, and royal courts, and skillful performance drew attentive listeners. Ezekiel’s God-given ability to communicate made his message memorable, yet the beauty of its delivery could not soften hearts devoted to selfish gain. Jesus likewise taught with unmatched authority, but many who heard Him refused to believe and obey Him (John 1:11; Matthew 7:28–29).

The exiles listened to Ezekiel while continuing in the sins that had brought Judah under God’s judgment. Hearing without obedience exposed a false response to divine revelation. God calls His people to receive His word with faith that produces action, as James teaches: “Be doers of the word, and not hearers only” (James 1:22).

God’s announced judgment would arrive exactly as He had declared, despite the people’s refusal to obey. Ezekiel’s hearers treated his words as pleasant speech while continuing in greed and sin (Ezekiel 33:30–32). The fall of Jerusalem and the sufferings of exile would expose the certainty of God’s warnings.
