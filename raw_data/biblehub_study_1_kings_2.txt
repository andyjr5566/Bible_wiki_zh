David Instructs Solomon

1 Kings 2:1

As the time drew near for David to die,
David’s approaching death closed a reign that had united Israel, established Jerusalem as its capital, and secured the kingdom from surrounding enemies. Though David had known both great faithfulness and grievous sin, the Lord’s covenant with him remained firm: his dynasty would endure, and one of his descendants would build the temple. David’s mortality also reminds Israel that its hope could not rest finally in any earthly king.

he charged his son Solomon,
David entrusted Solomon, whose name means peace, with the throne and with the responsibility to walk faithfully before the Lord. Solomon had been appointed to succeed David and to build the temple in Jerusalem, fulfilling God’s promise in 2 Samuel 7:12–13. The transfer of leadership carried spiritual weight: the stability of Solomon’s kingdom depended not merely on political strength, but on obedience to God’s covenant. Solomon’s reign would display Israel’s greatest earthly splendor, while the enduring promise to David’s house ultimately finds its fulfillment in Jesus Christ, the eternal Son of David.

David’s approaching death brings his reign to its close and makes clear that even Israel’s greatest king cannot escape mortality. Such knowledge calls God’s people to number their days wisely and to live in readiness before the Lord (Psalm 90:12). David’s final counsel also prepares Solomon to receive the throne and carry forward the covenant responsibilities of the house of David.

Rest with one’s fathers describes death and burial among one’s people, while also pointing beyond death to the enduring hope that God’s faithful purposes are not ended by the grave.
