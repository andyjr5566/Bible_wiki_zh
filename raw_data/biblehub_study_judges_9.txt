Abimelech’s Conspiracy

Judges 9:1

Now Abimelech son of Jerubbaal
Abimelech was the son of Gideon, who received the name Jerubbaal, meaning “Let Baal contend against him,” after tearing down Baal’s altar and challenging Baal to contend for himself. Gideon had delivered Israel from Midian by God’s power, yet Abimelech would seek dominion by manipulation and bloodshed. His name, “my father is king,” anticipates his attempt to seize authority even though Gideon had refused a hereditary kingship over Israel.

Abimelech’s mother was a concubine from Shechem, giving him family ties within the city. Shechem stood in the hill country of Ephraim and held deep covenant significance for Israel: Abraham built an altar there, Jacob buried foreign gods there, and Joshua renewed the covenant there. Abimelech turned a place associated with covenant loyalty into the base of a rebellion against God’s appointed order.

Judges 9:57

And God also brought all the wickedness of the men of Shechem back upon their own heads.
The leaders of Shechem had financed Abimelech’s rise with silver from the temple of Baal-berith and had joined in making him king after he murdered Gideon’s seventy sons, except Jotham. Their alliance with a violent usurper revealed contempt for God’s deliverance through Gideon and disregard for innocent blood. God allowed distrust and hostility to grow between Abimelech and Shechem, bringing their wicked partnership to ruin. Shechem, once associated with covenant renewal under Joshua, became a place where covenant unfaithfulness and bloodshed received judgment. God’s justice does not overlook public evil, political treachery, or violence against the innocent.

So the curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal came upon them.
From Mount Gerizim, Jotham had warned that Abimelech and the men of Shechem would destroy one another if they had acted falsely toward Gideon’s house. Jotham’s warning was fulfilled because it accorded with God’s righteous judgment. The account confirms that God’s word stands and that those who sow evil ultimately face its consequences. Yet God’s justice also points to the need for redemption, fulfilled in Christ, who bore judgment for sinners who turn to Him in faith.
