Manasseh Reigns in Judah

2 Kings 21:1

Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king,
Manasseh inherited the throne of Judah after the godly reign of Hezekiah, whose reforms had removed idols and called the nation back to the worship of the Lord. His youth would have made him dependent upon the guidance of royal officials and those closest to the court. Rather than continuing his father’s faithfulness, Manasseh soon led Judah into rebellion against God, reversing much of Hezekiah’s work. Joash had also ascended Judah’s throne as a child, showing that the preservation of the kingdom depended not upon a king’s age but upon the Lord and the king’s response to His word.

and he reigned in Jerusalem fifty-five years.
Manasseh’s reign was the longest recorded for any king of Judah or Israel. The long duration of his rule gave his idolatry deep influence throughout Judah and Jerusalem. Second Chronicles records that he erected pagan altars, practiced occultism, shed much innocent blood, and led the people into sins worse than those of the nations the Lord had driven from the land. Yet his later repentance, recorded in 2 Chronicles 33:10–13, reveals that God heard him when he humbled himself in captivity. His restoration did not remove all the consequences of the evil he had established, and Judah continued on a course toward the judgment later carried out through Babylon.

Manasseh later humbled himself in captivity and received mercy from God (2 Chronicles 33:10–13), yet the damage of his early rebellion deeply wounded Judah.

Manasseh died after ruling Judah for fifty-five years, the longest reign of any king in Judah. His reign began in deep rebellion against the Lord, marked by idolatry, occult practices, bloodshed, and the corruption of Jerusalem. His life bears witness both to the seriousness of sin and to God’s readiness to show mercy to the repentant.
