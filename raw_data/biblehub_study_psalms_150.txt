Let Everything That Has Breath Praise the LORD

Psalm 150:1

Hallelujah!
Hallelujah means “Praise the Lord” and summons God’s people to joyful, reverent worship. Psalm 150 opens and closes with this call, presenting praise as the fitting response to God’s character and works. The word also anticipates the worship of heaven, where redeemed multitudes proclaim “Hallelujah” before God’s throne (Revelation 19:1–6).

Praise God in His sanctuary.
Israel worshiped God at the tabernacle and later at the temple in Jerusalem, where His presence was uniquely manifested among His covenant people. The sanctuary testified that the holy God had made a way for sinners to draw near through sacrifice. Through Christ’s final sacrifice, believers now worship God as His dwelling place through the Holy Spirit, both individually and together as the church (1 Corinthians 3:16; 6:19; Hebrews 10:19–22).

Praise Him in His mighty heavens.
God’s praise belongs not only within earthly places of worship but throughout the heavens He created and rules. The sun, moon, stars, and angelic hosts bear witness to His power and glory (Psalm 19:1; Psalm 148:1–4). Earthly worship joins the unending praise offered before the throne of God, where heaven and earth will ultimately unite in honoring the Creator and Redeemer.

Psalm 150:2

Praise Him for His mighty acts;
God’s works in creation, the deliverance from Egypt, the conquest of Canaan, and His preservation of Israel reveal His sovereign power over nature, nations, and death itself. The resurrection of Jesus Christ stands as the supreme display of God’s saving power, securing victory over sin and the grave. God’s past acts call His people to trust His continuing faithfulness.

praise Him for His excellent greatness.
Worship rises not only from what God has done, but from who He is. His wisdom is unsearchable, His power is without limit, and His reign endures forever. Creation, providence, and redemption testify to a greatness no creature can equal. The praise offered on earth anticipates the everlasting worship of heaven, where God is honored as worthy of all glory.

Psalm 150:3

Praise Him with the sound of the horn;
The horn was the shofar, a ram’s horn sounded in Israel to summon the congregation, announce sacred assemblies, mark festivals, and signal decisive moments in the nation’s life. Its blast accompanied the revelation of God at Mount Sinai (Exodus 19:16) and the overthrow of Jericho (Joshua 6:4–5). The shofar’s penetrating sound called God’s people to attentive, united worship and proclaimed His holy authority.

Psa 98:6 With trumpets and the blast of the ram’s horn shout for joy…

1Ch 15:28 So all Israel brought up the ark of the covenant of the LORD…

praise Him with the harp and lyre.
Harps and lyres accompanied songs of praise in Israel’s worship, including the music organized by David for the sanctuary (1 Chronicles 15:16; 25:1). David himself used the harp in service before the Lord and in ministry to Saul (1 Samuel 16:23). Their ordered melodies joined the solemn blast of the horn, showing that worship rightly employs both joyful celebration and reverent skill to honor the God who reigns over all creation.

Psalm 150:4

Praise Him with tambourine and dancing;
Tambourines accompanied Israel’s celebrations of God’s saving acts, notably when Miriam led the women in song and dance after the Lord delivered Israel through the Red Sea (Exodus 15:20–21). David also danced before the Lord when the ark was brought to Jerusalem, rejoicing in God’s presence and covenant faithfulness (2 Samuel 6:14). Worship involves the whole person, joining inward reverence with outward joy before the God who redeems His people.

praise Him with strings and flute.
Harps, lyres, and other stringed instruments regularly accompanied the songs of Israel and were appointed for temple worship (1 Chronicles 15:16; Psalm 33:2). Flutes added their sound to public celebration and praise. The varied instruments call God’s people to offer their gifts in unity, directing every sound toward the glory of the Lord.

Psalm 150:5

Praise Him with clashing cymbals;
Cymbals were appointed for Israel’s public worship when David organized the Levites to celebrate before the ark with loud instruments (1 Chronicles 15:16). Their sharp, forceful sound suited occasions of holy rejoicing, including the bringing of the ark to Jerusalem, when David danced before the Lord with all his might (2 Samuel 6:14). Worship in God’s presence was not restrained or lifeless; it called for the wholehearted offering of every lawful gift and resource.

1Ch 15:16 David also told the leaders of the Levites to appoint…

Ezr 3:10 When the builders had laid the foundation of the temple…

praise Him with resounding cymbals.
The repeated call heightens the summons to vigorous praise. Cymbals also accompanied the temple musicians as they led the congregation in giving thanks to the Lord (1 Chronicles 16:5; 2 Chronicles 5:12–13). Their ringing sound proclaimed God’s greatness openly among His people. Such praise points beyond Israel’s gathered worship to the day when all nations will declare the Lord’s glory and sing to Him a new song (Isaiah 42:10–12).

Psalm 150:6

Let everything that has breath
Every living creature owes its life to God, who breathed life into Adam and sustains all creation by His power. Humanity bears a unique responsibility to lead creation in conscious, grateful worship, while the whole created order testifies to its Maker’s glory.

Job 12:10 The life of every living thing is in His hand…

praise the LORD!
Yahweh, the covenant God of Israel, is worthy of praise for His holiness, faithfulness, mercy, power, and saving works. Praise is the fitting response of those who recognize that life itself comes from His hand.

Hallelujah!
The Psalter closes with a joyful summons for all life to glorify the Lord. The final word of the book, “Hallelujah,” meaning “Praise the LORD,” points beyond Israel’s worship to the day when God’s reign is openly acknowledged by all creation.
