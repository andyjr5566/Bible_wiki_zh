A Plea to Return

Jeremiah 4:1

“If you will return, O Israel, return to Me,”
The Lord calls Judah and Israel to turn from rebellion and renew covenant fellowship with Him. Repentance requires more than relief from judgment; it is a return to the God whom they had forsaken. Hosea likewise summoned Israel to return to the LORD after acknowledging its sin (Hosea 14:1–2). God’s invitation reveals His readiness to receive a repentant people, fulfilled supremely in the Father’s welcome of the returning son (Luke 15:20).

Genuine return required the removal of idols, as Samuel commanded Israel to put away the Baals and Ashtoreths and serve the LORD alone (1 Samuel 7:3–4). No reconciliation with God can coexist with cherished rebellion.

Jeremiah delivered God’s own indictment and summons to the southern kingdom. Jerusalem, the seat of David’s throne and the temple, stood at the center of the nation’s spiritual responsibility. Hosea gave a similar call to “break up your fallow ground” and seek the LORD (Hosea 10:12).
