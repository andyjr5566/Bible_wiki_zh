Jerusalem filled with pilgrims for these sacred observances. The approaching feast provided the setting for Jesus’ death, for He would offer Himself as the true Passover Lamb, whose blood brings redemption from sin.

The third denial completed what Jesus had predicted, and the rooster’s next crow would bring Peter’s words back to his mind. Peter’s failure stands in sharp contrast to Jesus, who remained faithful under interrogation and suffering. Yet Peter’s later repentance and restoration show that grievous failure need not be the end for one who returns to the Lord.

The second crowing fulfilled Jesus’ precise prediction after Peter’s third denial. Jesus remained fully aware of Peter’s coming failure even as He faced His own trial and suffering.

“Before the rooster crows twice, you will deny Me three times.”
Jesus had foretold not merely that Peter would stumble, but the exact pattern of his denials. Peter’s threefold denial revealed the completeness of his failure, yet Jesus’ foreknowledge did not mean Peter was abandoned. The Lord had already prayed that Peter’s faith would not fail completely and that, after turning back, he would strengthen his brothers (Luke 22:31–32).

And he broke down and wept.
Peter’s tears showed genuine sorrow over denying the One he loved. His repentance became the beginning of restoration rather than the end of his service. After His resurrection, Jesus would graciously restore Peter through a threefold affirmation of love and entrust him with the care of His people (John 21:15–19).
