Jesus concluded the Olivet Discourse of Matthew 24–25, which revealed Jerusalem’s coming judgment, His return in glory, and the final separation of the righteous and the wicked. Matthew repeatedly marks the completion of Jesus’ major teaching sections in this way, drawing attention to His authority and preparing the reader for the Passion narrative. The teaching about watchfulness and judgment now gives way to the events through which the Son of Man will give His life.

Jesus Anointed at Bethany

Matthew 26:6

Bethany lay on the eastern slope of the Mount of Olives, about two miles from Jerusalem. Jesus often lodged there during His final days before the crucifixion, finding welcome among friends while continuing to teach in Jerusalem. Mary, Martha, and Lazarus lived nearby, and Lazarus’s resurrection had drawn many people to faith in Jesus and intensified the hostility of the religious leaders (John 11:45–53). Bethany became the setting for a devoted act that prepared Jesus for His burial and revealed the value of worship offered without reserve.

Simon is otherwise unknown in Scripture, but his designation indicates that he had suffered from leprosy. Because leprosy made a person ceremonially unclean and socially isolated under the Law, Simon’s ability to host a meal suggests that he had been healed. Jesus regularly showed compassion toward lepers, touching and cleansing those whom others avoided. A meal in Simon’s home displayed the restoring mercy of Christ and gathered around Him people whose lives had been marked by His power.
