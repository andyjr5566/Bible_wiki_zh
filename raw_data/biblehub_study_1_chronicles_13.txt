1 Chronicles 13:3

Then let us bring back the ark of our God,
The ark was the sacred chest associated with God’s covenant presence, containing the tablets of the Law and, according to Hebrews 9:4, Aaron’s rod and a pot of manna. After its return from Philistine territory, it had remained at Kiriath-jearim for many years (1 Samuel 7:1–2). David sought to restore the ark to a central place in Israel’s worship, recognizing that the nation’s strength depended upon the Lord rather than political or military success. The ark pointed to God dwelling among His covenant people and ultimately anticipates Christ, who came to dwell among humanity and brings believers into the presence of God.

1 Chronicles 13:6

David led a representative gathering from all Israel to recover the ark, displaying the nation’s renewed unity under the king God had chosen. Baalah, also called Kiriath-jearim, lay in the hill country of Judah, so the journey was literally an ascent. The ark had remained there since its return from Philistine territory in the days of Samuel, after the men of Kiriath-jearim placed it in Abinadab’s house (1 Samuel 6:21–7:2). David’s purpose was to restore the visible center of Israel’s worship to the life of the united kingdom.

The ark’s long stay there testified both to God’s patience with Israel and to the spiritual neglect that had marked much of Saul’s reign.
