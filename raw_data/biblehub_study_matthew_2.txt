Matthew 2:4

And when he had assembled all the chief priests and scribes of the people,
Herod summoned the leading authorities in Jerusalem, likely including members of the Sanhedrin. The chief priests included the high priest, former high priests, and prominent priestly families who oversaw temple worship and held considerable political influence under Rome. The scribes were trained interpreters of the Law, many of whom were associated with Pharisaic traditions. Their united presence shows that the location of the Messiah’s birth was a settled matter in Israel’s Scriptures, not hidden knowledge available only to a few. Herod’s urgency arose from the Magi’s report that a rightful “king of the Jews” had been born, a title that challenged his insecure and violently guarded rule.

Nazareth was a small agricultural village in lower Galilee, far from Jerusalem’s religious and political prominence. Joseph’s settlement there placed Jesus in the ordinary life of Israel’s common people, where He grew in wisdom and stature within a faithful Jewish family. Nathanael’s later question, “Can anything good come out of Nazareth?” reflects the town’s lack of status (John 1:46). The Messiah’s humble upbringing revealed that God’s promised King would not begin His earthly life among the celebrated and powerful.

So was fulfilled what was spoken through the prophets:
Matthew repeatedly shows that the events surrounding Jesus’ birth and childhood unfolded according to God’s long-declared purpose. No single Old Testament passage records the exact words that follow, but the prophets collectively portray the Messiah as humble, rejected, and despised. Isaiah foretold a servant “despised and rejected by men” (Isaiah 53:3), while Psalm 22 describes the scorn directed toward God’s righteous sufferer. Jesus’ identity and mission fulfilled not only individual predictions but also the united witness of the prophetic Scriptures.

The name Nazarene, possibly related to the Hebrew netzer, meaning branch, identified Jesus with an obscure town and carried the reproach associated with it. His lowly hometown anticipated the rejection He would endure throughout His ministry and at the cross. The title may also echo Isaiah’s promise of a shoot, or branch, from the line of Jesse (Isaiah 11:1), pointing to Jesus as David’s promised King. The One raised in overlooked Nazareth was the true Branch of David, the Savior who bore contempt in order to bring redemption.
