The Lord of the Sabbath

Luke 6:1

One Sabbath
The Sabbath was the seventh day, set apart by God for rest, worship, and remembrance of His creation and redemption. It stood as a covenant sign between the Lord and Israel, calling the nation to trust Him rather than live in unbroken labor. By Jesus’ day, Pharisaic traditions had added detailed restrictions that often treated ordinary acts of necessity as violations of Sabbath rest.

Woes to the Satisfied

Luke 6:24

But woe to you who are rich,
Jesus warns those whose wealth has become their security, identity, and source of hope. In a culture that commonly viewed prosperity as evidence of divine favor, He exposes the danger of possessing abundance without humble dependence on God. Wealth can harden the heart toward the poor, foster self-sufficiency, and hinder wholehearted obedience. The rich young ruler went away sorrowful because his possessions held first place in his life (Luke 18:18–23), and the rich fool trusted stored goods while remaining unprepared to meet God (Luke 12:16–21).

Earthly riches may provide ease, honor, and temporary relief, but they cannot secure forgiveness, eternal life, or fellowship with God. Those satisfied with present comforts while rejecting God have received all the consolation they seek. Jesus’ warning anticipates the account of the rich man and Lazarus, in which the wealthy man enjoyed luxury in life yet faced judgment, while Lazarus was comforted in God’s presence (Luke 16:19–31). Believers are called to seek treasure in heaven and to use material blessings as stewards who trust God rather than possessions.

Jesus forbids the self-righteous, censorious spirit that assumes God’s authority over another person’s heart and final standing. His disciples must exercise moral discernment and recognize sin, but they must not imitate the hypocrisy of religious leaders who scrutinized others while excusing themselves. Isa 30:13-14 This iniquity of yours is like a breach about to fail…

1Th 5:3 While people are saying, “Peace and security,”…

and great was its destruction!”
The loss is complete because the house was built without the foundation necessary for survival. Jesus’ warning calls every hearer to respond to His words with obedient faith, for eternal safety rests in Him alone.
