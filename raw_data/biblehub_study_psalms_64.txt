The Hurtful Tongue

Psalm 64:1

For the choirmaster.
The psalm was entrusted for use in Israel’s gathered worship under the direction of the leader responsible for sacred music. David’s private distress became a confession for the covenant community, teaching God’s people to bring fear, danger, and opposition before the Lord together.

A Psalm of David.
David knew the pressures of enemies, conspiracy, betrayal, and public slander throughout his life. Whether pursued by Saul, opposed by foreign powers, or wounded by those near him, he repeatedly found safety not in his position as king but in the God who had called and kept him.

Hear, O God, my voice of complaint;
David approaches God with confidence that the Lord receives the honest cry of His servants. His prayer does not deny the pain of present circumstances; it places that pain before the righteous Judge, who knows every hidden plan and every wounded heart. Such prayer is an act of faith, for it turns distress into dependence upon God.

Psa 61:1 For the choirmaster. With stringed instruments. Of David.…

preserve my life from dread of the enemy.
The danger David faced affected both his outward safety and his inward peace. He asks God not only to restrain those who threaten him but also to guard him from being ruled by fear. The Lord remains a refuge for His people when enemies are visible or concealed. David’s plea also anticipates the suffering of Christ, who endured the hostility of sinful men yet entrusted Himself to the Father, and who conquered death, the final enemy, through His resurrection.

Psalm 64:2

Hide me from the scheming of the wicked,
David asks God to conceal him from enemies who secretly devise harm. Having endured Saul’s pursuit, betrayal by trusted associates, and the rebellion of Absalom, David knew that danger often began in hidden counsel before it appeared in open attack. God is a refuge for His people, not merely by removing trouble but by preserving them through it. The prayer anticipates Christ, who was opposed by those who plotted privately against Him, yet remained secure in the Father’s sovereign purpose.

from the mob of workers of iniquity,
The danger comes from an organized company committed to evil rather than from a lone adversary. Their shared rebellion gives them boldness, but their numbers do not lessen God’s power to judge or deliver. Scripture repeatedly warns against joining those who plan violence and deceit, for sin often gathers strength through collective agreement. The religious leaders, false witnesses, and crowd involved in Christ’s condemnation reveal the same pattern of united opposition to God’s righteous King.

Psalm 64:3

who sharpen their tongues like swords
David portrays the wicked as preparing speech for injury with the same deliberation used to ready a weapon for battle. Their words are not careless outbursts but calculated attacks against the righteous. Scripture repeatedly warns that the tongue can bring death as well as life, and James describes its power to spread ruin like fire.

Psa 57:4 My soul is among the lions; I lie down with ravenous beasts…

Pro 12:18 Speaking rashly is like a piercing sword,…

and aim their bitter words like arrows,
Bitter speech is directed toward a chosen target, often from concealment and without warning. As arrows could wound an enemy from a distance, malicious words can damage reputations, relationships, and faith without open confrontation. The wise use speech to heal, but the wicked use it to pierce.

Psalm 64:4

ambushing the innocent in seclusion,
The wicked conceal their plans as they target those whose conduct exposes their sin. Secret violence reflects cowardice as well as deliberate rebellion against God’s justice. Joseph’s brothers acted similarly when they removed him from his father and plotted harm against him, yet God overruled their evil for His saving purposes.

The image portrays a calculated assault launched without warning, like an archer striking from concealment. Such attacks reveal the vulnerability of the righteous in a fallen world, but they do not escape the Lord’s sight. Believers must take up the shield of faith against the spiritual assaults described as the fiery darts of the wicked.

Hardened sinners may act as though no Judge sees and no judgment will come. Their confidence is not strength but blindness, for the Lord searches every heart and will bring hidden deeds into account. The fear of the Lord produces reverence, obedience, and refuge in the One who defends the innocent.

Psalm 64:5

They hold fast to their evil purpose;
Wickedness becomes entrenched when people deliberately persist in plans that defy God and harm others. Pharaoh’s repeated refusal to heed the Lord illustrates such hardened resolve. Apart from God’s grace, the human heart readily resists truth and commits itself to sin.

they speak of hiding their snares.
Their evil is calculated rather than accidental. Like hunters concealing traps for unsuspecting prey, they devise secret schemes against the innocent and righteous. Scripture repeatedly portrays such deceit as characteristic of those who oppose God’s ways and His people.

Psalm 64:6

They devise injustice and say,
David portrays enemies who do not merely fall into sin but deliberately plan harm against the innocent. Their actions resemble the wicked schemes condemned in Proverbs 6:16–19 and stand against God’s command to love justice, defend the vulnerable, and speak truth. Secret wrongdoing may appear carefully arranged, yet it remains fully exposed before the Lord.

Their confidence rests in concealment, strategy, and mutual agreement. Psalm 64 soon answers their boast: God Himself will strike them suddenly, turning their hidden violence back upon them.

Human motives lie beneath outward words and actions, often concealing pride, hatred, and deceit. Jeremiah declared that the heart is deceitful and known completely only by the Lord. God searches what people cannot see, judges righteous and wicked intentions, and provides the needed renewal of the heart through His grace.

Psalm 64:7

But God will shoot them with arrows;
God answers the concealed plots of the wicked with open and decisive judgment. Their weapons are words sharpened to wound the innocent, but the Lord’s arrows reach those who imagine themselves beyond accountability. Scripture repeatedly portrays God as the righteous Warrior who defends His people and judges rebellion (Deuteronomy 32:23; Psalm 7:13; Exodus 15:3). His judgment is neither random nor excessive; it is directed against evil and vindicates those who entrust themselves to Him. The final judgment committed to Christ will reveal the same perfect righteousness on a universal scale (Acts 17:31).

suddenly they will be wounded.
Those who plan harm in secret cannot predict the moment God brings their schemes to ruin. The swift reversal exposes the weakness of human pride and the certainty of divine justice. Their wounding includes the collapse of their evil purposes and the public consequences of their rebellion. God’s patience does not mean indifference; when His appointed time comes, judgment falls with certainty, warning the wicked and comforting the righteous.

Psalm 64:8

They will be made to stumble,
God does not overlook secret attacks against the innocent. David’s enemies planned violence and concealed their malice, but the Lord brings their course to ruin. Their downfall demonstrates that human schemes cannot prevail against God’s protection of His people or His purpose to judge evil.

Isa 8:15 Many will stumble over these; they will fall and be broken;…

Psa 35:6 May their path be dark and slick, as the angel of the LORD…

their own tongues turned against them.
The slander, deception, and threats formed against the righteous become the means of the wicked’s exposure and defeat. Scripture repeatedly warns that those who set traps for others often fall into them themselves (Psalm 7:15–16; Proverbs 12:13). God’s judgment is fitting: those who misuse speech to destroy others are condemned by the very evil they have spoken.

Job 15:6 Your own mouth, not mine, condemns you;…

All who see will shake their heads.
The public collapse of the wicked becomes a visible testimony to God’s justice. Their ruin evokes horror and contempt because it reveals the folly of resisting the Lord. Those who witness it are reminded that God sees hidden sin, defends the righteous, and will not allow rebellion to stand forever.

Psalm 64:9

Then all mankind will fear
God’s decisive judgment against hidden wickedness will make His power known beyond the immediate victims and enemies described in the psalm. Like the nations that trembled after the Lord delivered Israel from Egypt, people will recognize that the Judge of all the earth acts righteously. Such fear includes reverent awe before God’s holiness, justice, and sovereign rule.

Psalm 64:10

Let the righteous rejoice in the LORD
God’s judgment on the wicked and His preservation of His people give the righteous cause for joy. Their gladness rests in the LORD Himself, whose holiness, justice, and faithfulness never fail. Psalm 32:11 similarly calls the righteous to be glad in the LORD, and Philippians 4:4 directs believers to rejoice in the Lord always, even amid hardship.

and take refuge in Him;
The LORD is the secure shelter of those who trust Him. David knew danger from enemies, betrayal, and violence, yet he repeatedly found safety in God rather than in human strength. God remains “a refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble” (Psalm 46:1), guarding His people and sustaining them when evil appears powerful.

let all the upright in heart exult.
Uprightness begins within, where devotion to God produces integrity in life. The upright rejoice because God sees hidden motives, exposes secret evil, and vindicates those who walk before Him sincerely. Their joy anticipates the final triumph of God’s righteousness, when every wrong will be judged and those who belong to Him will dwell securely in His presence.
