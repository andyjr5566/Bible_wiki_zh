The Passover was appointed for the fourteenth day of the first month (Exodus 12:6; Leviticus 23:5). Under Ahaz, Judah’s worship had been deeply neglected, the temple had been closed, and its service interrupted. Hezekiah’s reforms began too late in the first month for the nation to prepare properly. Numbers 9:10–11 provided for a Passover in the second month when uncleanness or distance prevented observance at the appointed time. That provision displayed God’s mercy while preserving the holiness and central importance of the feast that remembered Israel’s redemption from Egypt.
