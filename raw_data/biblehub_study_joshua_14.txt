Land Division West of the Jordan

Joshua 14:1

Now these are the portions that the Israelites inherited in the land of Canaan,
Israel’s settlement in Canaan fulfilled the Lord’s covenant promise to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (Genesis 12:7; 15:18–21; 26:3; 28:13). The inheritance was not seized merely by military strength; it was received from God after He had driven out Israel’s enemies and established His people in the land. Each tribal allotment gave concrete form to God’s faithfulness and provided a lasting place for the families of Israel.

Eleazar, Aaron’s son and successor as high priest, represented the Lord’s authority in the division of the land. The inheritance was a sacred trust, governed by God’s command rather than political advantage. Through Eleazar’s ministry, Israel was reminded that the land belonged ultimately to the Lord and that His people were to live in it under His covenant.

Joshua 14:2

Their inheritance was assigned by lot
Israel distributed the land by lot before the LORD, recognizing that the final outcome rested under His sovereign direction (Proverbs 16:33). The lot prevented the strongest tribes or most influential leaders from determining their own portions and affirmed that Canaan was the LORD’s gift, not Israel’s conquest alone. The boundaries and general locations had been directed by God, while the size of each inheritance corresponded to the tribe’s population (Numbers 26:52–56).

These tribes received their inheritance west of the Jordan: Judah, Simeon, Benjamin, Dan, Ephraim, the half-tribe of Manasseh, Issachar, Zebulun, Asher, and Naphtali. Reuben, Gad, and the other half of Manasseh had already received land east of the Jordan under Moses’ leadership, after pledging to help their brothers conquer Canaan (Numbers 32; Joshua 13:8–33). Though divided by the river, all twelve tribes remained bound together as the covenant people of God.

Israel’s settlement in the land testified that God keeps His covenant, while their continued possession of it depended upon obedience to Him.

Joshua 14:3

For Moses had given the inheritance east of the Jordan to the other two and a half tribes.
Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh received Gilead and Bashan after requesting the pasturelands east of the Jordan for their abundant livestock (Numbers 32; Deuteronomy 3:12–20). Moses required them to cross the Jordan armed and help their brethren secure the land of Canaan before returning to their homes. Their settlement demonstrated both the certainty of God’s promised inheritance and the responsibility of every tribe to stand with the whole people of God.

But he granted no inheritance among them to the Levites.
The Levites received no tribal territory because the LORD Himself was their inheritance (Numbers 18:20–24; Deuteronomy 10:9). They served at the tabernacle, taught God’s law, and were supported through the tithes and offerings of Israel. Instead of a contiguous region, they received forty-eight cities scattered among the tribes, ensuring that spiritual instruction and priestly ministry remained throughout the land (Numbers 35:1–8). Their consecrated service anticipated the greater priesthood of Christ, the eternal High Priest through whom His people draw near to God (Hebrews 4:14–16).

Jacob adopted Joseph’s sons as his own in Genesis 48:5, granting Joseph the double portion ordinarily associated with the firstborn. Manasseh and Ephraim therefore received separate tribal inheritances, preserving twelve territorial tribes in Israel. Though Manasseh was the older son, Jacob gave Ephraim the greater blessing, and Ephraim later became prominent among the northern tribes.
