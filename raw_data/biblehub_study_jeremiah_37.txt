Jeremiah 37:1

Nebuchadnezzar II ruled Babylon from 605 to 562 BC and established Babylon as the dominant power of the ancient Near East. God used his military campaigns as judgment against Judah’s persistent rebellion, as Jeremiah had repeatedly warned. Babylon’s capture of Jerusalem and deportation of its leaders demonstrated that Judah’s covenant privileges did not excuse its sin.

Jeremiah 37:21

So King Zedekiah gave orders for Jeremiah to be placed in the courtyard of the guard
Zedekiah, Judah’s final king before Jerusalem’s fall, had been installed as Babylon’s vassal by Nebuchadnezzar. Though he feared Jeremiah’s message and at times sought his counsel, he lacked the courage to obey the Lord openly. Moving Jeremiah to the courtyard of the guard spared him from the harsh conditions of the dungeon and kept him within the palace complex under royal supervision. The arrangement also allowed Jeremiah to receive visitors and continue proclaiming God’s word during the siege.

Jeremiah’s preservation enabled him to remain a witness to the truth he had faithfully declared.
