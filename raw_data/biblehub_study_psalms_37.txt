Delight Yourself in the LORD

Psalm 37:1

Of David.
David wrote as one acquainted with danger, injustice, public opposition, and the deceptive security of power. Psalm 37 addresses the distress caused when those who reject God seem secure while the righteous suffer. Its wisdom calls God’s people to measure life by the Lord’s covenant faithfulness and final judgment rather than by present appearances.

Psa 3:1 A Psalm of David, when he fled from his son Absalom.…

Psa 18:1 For the choirmaster. Of David the servant of the LORD…

Psa 34:1 Of David, when he pretended to be insane before Abimelech,…

Do not fret over those who do evil;
God forbids the inward agitation that injustice can produce. Evildoers may gain influence, wealth, or protection for a season, but their apparent success does not escape the Lord’s rule. David directs the faithful away from anger and anxiety toward patient trust, because God sees every wrong and will establish righteousness in His time.

do not envy those who do wrong.
The prosperity of the wicked is not evidence of God’s approval or a lasting inheritance. Psalm 73 records a similar struggle and resolves it by looking to the final end of those who live without God. Believers are called to value the lasting blessings God gives His people rather than desiring temporary advantages gained through rebellion.

Psalm 37:2

For they wither quickly like grass
In Israel’s climate, grass could flourish after seasonal rains yet dry rapidly beneath the scorching sun. The prosperity of evildoers may appear vigorous and secure for a time, but it has no lasting root before God. Their apparent success does not overturn His justice or escape His appointed judgment. Scripture repeatedly compares human glory to grass because earthly strength, wealth, and influence quickly pass away (Isaiah 40:6–8; James 1:10–11).

and wilt like tender plants.
Young green vegetation is especially vulnerable to heat and drought, portraying the fragility of wicked power. The unrighteous may flourish briefly, but their course ends in decay because they stand apart from the Lord. By contrast, those who trust Him are like trees planted by streams of water, enduring and fruitful through every season (Psalm 1:3).

Psalm 37:3

Trust in the LORD
David directs the righteous away from anxiety over the apparent success of evildoers and toward confidence in the Lord’s righteous rule. Trust rests in God’s covenant faithfulness, His power to judge evil, and His care for those who belong to Him.

The land of Canaan was God’s promised inheritance for Abraham’s descendants and a visible testimony to His faithfulness. David calls God’s people to remain settled in obedient dependence rather than seeking security through sinful or fearful means. God’s provision for Israel also anticipates the lasting rest and inheritance secured for His people through Christ.

Faithfulness requires steady attention, like a farmer tending what has been entrusted to him. God’s people are to persevere in loyalty to Him, trusting His provision and reflecting His steadfast character in daily life. Such faithfulness is the fruit of the Spirit and finds its perfect pattern in Jesus Christ, the faithful and true King.

Psalm 37:4

Delight yourself in the LORD,
David calls God’s people to find their deepest joy, security, and satisfaction in the covenant Lord rather than in the temporary prosperity of the wicked. Psalm 37 addresses the troubling success of evildoers and directs the righteous to trust, commit their way to God, rest in Him, and wait patiently for His justice. Delight in the Lord grows through worship, obedience, meditation on His Word, and confidence in His faithful character. Such devotion turns the heart from self-rule toward God’s purposes, as seen in the blessed man who delights in the law of the Lord (Psalm 1:2).

God receives and fulfills desires shaped by fellowship with Him. The promise does not authorize selfish ambition; it assures believers that the Lord knows, purifies, and supplies what accords with His will. As a person abides in Christ and His words remain within them, prayer increasingly reflects God’s purposes (John 15:7). God’s answer may come in His appointed time and in forms wiser than expected, yet He will not withhold what is truly good from those who walk with Him.

Psalm 37:5

Commit your way to the LORD;
David calls God’s people to place the whole course of life before the Lord—its duties, decisions, burdens, and future. Abraham left his homeland at God’s command, trusting the Lord to lead him into the land He promised. Such commitment surrenders self-directed plans to God’s wise and sovereign purpose.

Faith rests in the Lord’s character rather than in visible circumstances. David knew seasons of danger, injustice, and waiting, yet he learned that God remains faithful to those who seek Him. Trust includes obedience, patience, and confidence that God’s promises will not fail.

The Lord acts for those who entrust themselves to Him, bringing His righteous purposes to fulfillment in His time. He delivered Israel, preserved David, and ultimately accomplished salvation through Jesus Christ. Believers may therefore place their needs and future in God’s hands, knowing that He cares for them and will fulfill all that accords with His will.

Psalm 37:6

He will bring forth your righteousness like the dawn,
David assures the faithful that the Lord will publicly vindicate those who entrust their cause to Him. Righteousness that may be obscured by slander, oppression, or the temporary success of evildoers will emerge with the certainty of sunrise. Dawn dispels darkness gradually yet irresistibly, portraying God’s appointed revelation of truth and His renewing mercy (Lamentations 3:22–23). Isaiah likewise promises that the righteousness of God’s people will “break forth like the dawn” as they walk in obedient faith (Isaiah 58:8). Christ, the Light of the World, ultimately reveals both God’s righteousness and the true standing of those justified through faith in Him (John 8:12).

The Lord will make the justice of His people as clear as the sun at its height, beyond concealment or successful contradiction. In Psalm 37, David addresses the distress caused when wicked people appear to prosper while the righteous suffer; God’s final judgment will correct every false appearance. The sun, a sign of God’s sustaining goodness and favor, reflects the certainty of His righteous rule (Psalm 84:11). God’s vindication points beyond present deliverance to the day when Christ judges the living and the dead in perfect justice (2 Timothy 4:1).

David calls God’s people to quiet submission before the Lord’s sovereign rule. Israel’s history repeatedly showed that deliverance belonged to God, as at the Red Sea when the Lord fought for His people while they stood firm. Stillness before God abandons anxious self-reliance and rests in His righteous care. The Hebrew word for “be still” can also mean to relax or let go, suggesting a release of anxiety and control.

Psalm 37:8

Refrain from anger
David calls God’s people to refuse the anger stirred by seeing evildoers prosper. Unchecked anger easily becomes sinful, producing rash words, retaliation, and bitterness. Proverbs 14:29 identifies patience with wisdom, while James 1:20 teaches that human anger does not accomplish God’s righteousness. Christ showed holy indignation toward sin and unbelief, yet He never yielded to personal vengeance or sinful passion (Mark 3:5; 1 Peter 2:23).

and abandon wrath;
Wrath must be laid aside rather than nurtured into revenge. Israel’s law entrusted judgment to God and restrained private vengeance, preserving justice within the covenant community. Believers likewise leave retribution in the Lord’s hands, trusting His righteous judgment (Romans 12:19). Jesus commanded His disciples to love enemies and pray for persecutors, overcoming evil through faithful obedience rather than retaliation (Matthew 5:44).

Anxiety over the apparent success of the wicked reveals a temptation to doubt God’s rule and timing. Psalm 37 repeatedly directs the righteous to trust the Lord, do good, and wait patiently for Him. God sees oppression, hears the afflicted, and will distinguish permanently between the righteous and the wicked. Jesus likewise forbade anxious preoccupation with earthly concerns and commanded His followers to seek God’s kingdom first (Matthew 6:33–34).

The Spirit produces peace, patience, and self-control in those who walk with God (Galatians 5:22–23). Trusting the Lord’s justice frees believers to pursue reconciliation, holiness, and the peace to which Christ has called them.

Under Israel’s covenant, being cut off could involve death, exile, or removal from the blessings and inheritance of the people. Jesus echoed this promise when He declared that the meek will inherit the earth (Matthew 5:5).

Psalm 37:11

God promises lasting inheritance to those who humbly trust Him rather than grasping for security through power, violence, or deceit. Psalm 37 contrasts the temporary success of the wicked with the enduring portion of the righteous. The land first recalls God’s covenant gift to Israel, yet it also points beyond Canaan to the final inheritance of all who belong to the Lord. Jesus applied this promise to His kingdom in Matthew 5:5, declaring that the meek will inherit the earth. Meekness is not weakness but yielded strength under God’s rule, displayed perfectly by Christ, who is gentle and humble in heart.

Such blessing follows the Lord’s faithful care and anticipates the fullness of His coming kingdom. Believers may endure present affliction, but God will establish everlasting peace in the new heaven and new earth, where His people will rejoice in His presence without end.

Cain’s murder of Abel, Saul’s pursuit of David, and Haman’s plot against the Jews reveal how righteousness exposes rebellion and provokes opposition. The conflict is spiritual as well as personal, for the lives of God’s people testify against evil. Yet the righteous are not abandoned to the schemes of men; the Lord sees every plan and will bring it to judgment.

Such anger cannot overturn God’s purpose or destroy His people’s final inheritance. Jesus warned that those who suffer for righteousness are blessed, while persistent enemies of God will face His righteous judgment. Believers are therefore called to endure without revenge, entrusting themselves to the Judge who acts justly.

Scripture repeatedly shows this pattern: Haman was hanged on the gallows he prepared for Mordecai, and those who dig a pit for others may fall into it themselves (Esther 7:10; Proverbs 26:27). Human rebellion cannot overthrow God’s righteous rule, and even hostile schemes ultimately serve His purposes.

Their security rests in the Lord’s faithfulness rather than favorable circumstances. In Christ, believers stand free from condemnation and are equipped to remain faithful in evil days.

Debt carried a moral obligation under God’s law, and refusal to repay exposed dishonesty, selfishness, and contempt for one’s neighbor. Israel’s laws protected the poor from exploitation while still upholding responsibility and justice (Exodus 22:25–27; Deuteronomy 15:7–11). The wicked use others for gain and break trust when repayment becomes costly. Their conduct reveals the insecurity and corruption of a life set against the Lord.

Psalm 37:22

Surely those He blesses will inherit the land,
God’s blessing rests on those who trust Him and walk in righteousness. The land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob stood as a tangible pledge of God’s covenant faithfulness to Israel (Genesis 12:7; 15:18–21). In Psalm 37, inheritance of the land assures the faithful that wickedness will not permanently dispossess those whom God has chosen to bless. Jesus draws on this promise when He declares that the meek will inherit the earth (Matthew 5:5), pointing toward the righteous rule of His kingdom. Through Christ, all who belong to Him share in God’s promised inheritance, while God’s covenant commitments to Israel remain secure (Galatians 3:29; Romans 11:28–29).

Human beings make plans, yet the Lord establishes their way (Proverbs 16:9).
