The Ninevites Repent

Jonah 3:1

Then the word of the LORD
God again addressed Jonah with a message carrying divine authority. The Lord’s word directs the course of His servants and accomplishes His purposes, even when human weakness and disobedience intervene. Like Jeremiah and Ezekiel, Jonah received revelation that he was responsible to proclaim faithfully.

Jonah, son of Amittai, came from Gath-hepher in Israel’s northern kingdom and had previously prophesied during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). His calling to Nineveh revealed God’s concern not only for Israel but also for the nations. Jonah’s experience showed that no servant can escape the presence or commission of the Lord.

After Jonah fled, endured the storm, and was delivered from the great fish, God restored him to his prophetic mission. The renewed command displayed God’s patience without diminishing His holy purpose. Jonah’s second opportunity anticipates the restoring grace seen in Peter’s recommissioning after his denial of Jesus (John 21:15–19).

Jonah 3:5

And the Ninevites believed God.
Jonah’s brief proclamation awakened faith throughout this great Assyrian city, notorious for violence, idolatry, and cruelty. Their belief recognized the authority of Israel’s God and accepted the reality of His announced judgment. It was not mere fear of disaster, but faith that bore visible fruit in repentance. God’s mercy toward Nineveh reveals His desire that the wicked turn from their evil ways and live (Ezekiel 18:23). Jesus later pointed to the Ninevites as a witness against those who heard a greater message yet refused to repent (Matthew 12:41).

Their united action accords with the call later given through Joel: to return to the Lord with fasting, weeping, and mourning, with hearts truly torn in repentance rather than outward garments alone (Joel 2:12–13).

Jonah’s warning penetrated from the streets of the city to its highest authority. The ruler of the Assyrian capital, accustomed to commanding armies and governing a feared empire, could not dismiss the announcement of God’s judgment.
