The House of David Strengthened

2 Samuel 3:1

Now the war between the house of Saul and the house of David was protracted.
After Saul’s death, Abner established Ish-bosheth, Saul’s son, as king over the northern tribes, while David reigned in Hebron over Judah. The divided kingdom endured civil conflict for years, revealing the instability left by Saul’s failed reign. David had already been anointed by Samuel, yet he did not seize the whole kingdom by force or impatience. He waited for the Lord to establish what He had promised. The struggle showed that God’s purposes advance in His time, even amid violence, divided loyalties, and human resistance.

David’s position steadily increased as the Lord gave him favor, capable leaders, and growing support among Israel. His rule over Judah became established during the seven and a half years he reigned from Hebron. The Lord’s presence with David, first evident when he was anointed, continued to distinguish him from Saul’s house. David’s rise anticipated the greater reign of Christ, the Son of David, whose kingdom advances despite opposition and will finally encompass all God’s people.

his third was Absalom, the son of Maacah daughter of King Talmai of Geshur;
Maacah was the daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur, an Aramean kingdom northeast of Israel. Her marriage to David likely strengthened relations with a neighboring ruler, yet the alliance also formed part of the troubled household from which Absalom arose. Absalom became renowned for his appearance and charisma, but he eventually used those gifts to steal the hearts of Israel and rebel against his father. After murdering his half-brother Amnon, Absalom fled to Geshur and found refuge with his maternal grandfather for three years (2 Sam. 13:37–38). His later revolt brought grief, division, and bloodshed to David’s kingdom, demonstrating the painful consequences of sin within David’s family and the enduring need for a righteous King.
