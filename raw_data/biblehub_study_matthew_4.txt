Jesus Begins His Ministry

Matthew 4:12

When Jesus heard that John had been imprisoned,
John the Baptist had been arrested by Herod Antipas for condemning Herod’s unlawful marriage to Herodias, his brother Philip’s wife (Matthew 14:3–4). John’s imprisonment brought his public ministry toward its close, though his witness continued even from prison. As the forerunner promised in Isaiah 40:3, John had prepared Israel for the Lord’s arrival; now Jesus stepped forward in open proclamation of the kingdom. Herod’s hostility revealed the cost of calling rulers and nations to repentance and foreshadowed the opposition Jesus would face from both political and religious authorities.

Mrk 1:14 After the arrest of John, Jesus went into Galilee…

Mat 14:3 Now Herod had arrested John and bound him…

He withdrew to Galilee.
Jesus departed from Judea at the appointed time, not in defeat or fear, but in faithful obedience to the Father’s plan. Galilee lay far from Jerusalem’s religious center and included communities shaped by both Jewish and Gentile influence. There Jesus fulfilled Isaiah’s promise that light would dawn in the region of Zebulun and Naphtali, “Galilee of the Gentiles” (Isaiah 9:1–2; Matthew 4:13–16). His ministry began among people often regarded with contempt by Judean leaders, demonstrating that God’s saving light reaches beyond the powerful and privileged. From Galilee Jesus proclaimed repentance, called His first disciples, and established the public ministry that would lead ultimately to the cross and resurrection.

From Capernaum, Jesus taught in synagogues, healed the sick, called disciples, and displayed the authority of God’s kingdom.
