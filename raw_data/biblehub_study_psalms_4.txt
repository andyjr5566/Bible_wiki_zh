Psalm 4:1

For the choirmaster.
The superscription assigns Psalm 4 to the leader responsible for directing Israel’s worship music. David’s prayer was therefore preserved for the gathered people of God, teaching the congregation to bring distress, opposition, and uncertainty before the Lord.

With stringed instruments.
Lyres, harps, and related instruments accompanied praise in Israel’s worship and gave voice to both lament and confidence. Music served the truth of God’s Word, helping the people remember His faithfulness and respond to Him together.

A Psalm of David.
David wrote from a life marked by royal responsibility, warfare, betrayal, repentance, and dependence upon God. His experience of opposition gives weight to the prayer, while his trust in the Lord points beyond himself to the greater King, Jesus Christ.

David appeals to God as the One who vindicates His servant and establishes what is right. He does not rest his case on personal merit but on the righteous character and covenant faithfulness of God. The believer’s final righteousness is likewise found in the Lord’s provision through Christ.

You have relieved my distress;
Past deliverance becomes the ground for present faith. God had made room for David when enemies and troubles pressed in upon him, and David remembers that mercy rather than surrendering to fear.

David seeks God’s undeserved favor and asks for a hearing from the One who has helped him before. Prayer joins humility with confidence: humility because help is needed, and confidence because the Lord receives the cries of those who belong to Him.

Psalm 4:2

How long, O men, will my honor be maligned?
David appeals to men of influence who had turned against the Lord’s anointed king. Their attacks on David’s honor also defied the God who had chosen and established him. The question exposes the injustice of persistent slander and rebellion against righteous authority. David’s suffering anticipates the greater rejection of Christ, the true Anointed One, whom men despised though the Father had appointed Him King.

Psa 69:19-20 You know my reproach, my shame and disgrace.…

Psa 44:13-14 You have made us a reproach to our neighbors…

Psa 31:18 May lying lips be silenced—lips that speak…

How long will you love vanity and seek after lies?
David’s opponents pursued empty ambitions and embraced false accusations rather than God’s truth. Their schemes could not endure because they were built on deception and opposition to the Lord’s purpose. Scripture repeatedly warns that idols, sinful desires, and human pride promise satisfaction but lead only to ruin. Those who reject God’s truth eventually discover the emptiness of the lies they have chosen.

Psa 12:2 They lie to one another; they speak with flattering lips…

Jer 2:5 This is what the LORD says: “What fault did your fathers…

Selah
The pause calls God’s people to weigh the seriousness of resisting His truth and dishonoring those whom He appoints. David’s question remains a summons to forsake deceit, turn from vain pursuits, and rest in the righteous rule of God.

Psalm 4:3

Know that the LORD has set apart the godly for Himself;
David calls his opponents to recognize that the LORD distinguishes those who belong to Him from those who pursue deceit and rebellion. God had chosen Israel as His treasured possession and called the nation to be holy before Him (Deuteronomy 7:6). Within Israel, the godly are those who trust the LORD, walk in covenant faithfulness, and seek His honor. Their security rests not in social standing, military strength, or personal innocence, but in God’s gracious claim upon them. The LORD’s setting apart of His people anticipates the calling of believers in Christ, chosen to belong to Him and to live in holiness (Ephesians 1:4; 1 Peter 2:9).

Lev 20:26 You are to be holy to Me because I, the LORD, am holy,…

Mal 3:17 They will be Mine,” says the LORD of Hosts…

2Ti 2:19 Nevertheless, God’s firm foundation stands…

the LORD hears when I call to Him.
David’s confidence arises from the LORD’s covenant faithfulness. Though surrounded by opposition, he knows that God receives the cry of those who belong to Him. The LORD is not distant from the righteous but watches over them and hears their prayers (Psalm 34:15). Such assurance does not promise that every request will be answered according to human desire; it affirms that God attentively responds according to His wisdom, righteousness, and purpose. Believers share this confidence through Christ, who grants access to the Father and assures His people that God hears petitions offered according to His will (1 John 5:14–15).

Psa 34:17 The righteous cry out, and the LORD hears;…

Jer 29:12 Then you will call upon Me and come and pray to Me,…

Psa 65:2 O You who listen to prayer, all people will come to You.

Psalm 4:4

Be angry, yet do not sin;
Anger may arise in the face of evil, injustice, or personal wrong, but it must never rule the heart or produce vengeance, bitterness, or reckless speech. David directs troubled and opposing people to fear God rather than continue in rebellion. Paul applies the same command to believers, warning that unresolved anger gives the devil an opportunity (Ephesians 4:26–27). Jesus displayed holy anger against the corruption of worship in the temple, yet His zeal remained wholly obedient to the Father (John 2:13–17).

Eph 4:26-27 “Be angry, yet do not sin.” Do not let the sun set…

Jas 1:19-20 My beloved brothers, understand this: Be quick to listen…

Pro 29:11 A fool vents all his anger, but a wise man holds it back.

on your bed,
Nighttime solitude removes many distractions and exposes the thoughts that govern the heart. Rather than plotting retaliation or nourishing resentment, God’s people are to bring their unrest before Him in private prayer and reflection. David himself often sought the Lord in the night watches (Psalm 63:6).

search your heart and be still.
Honest self-examination before God restrains sinful impulses and leads to repentance, faith, and obedience. Silence before the Lord recognizes His authority and allows His truth to correct restless desires. God searches every heart perfectly, and David later asks Him to reveal any grievous way within him (Psalm 139:23–24).

The pause calls for sober reflection. Anger must yield to the fear of God, inward examination, and quiet trust in His righteous judgment.

Psalm 4:5

Offer the sacrifices of the righteous
God required Israel’s sacrifices to be offered according to His commands and from hearts marked by repentance, faith, and obedience. Ritual without submission could not please Him, as Samuel told Saul: “To obey is better than sacrifice” (1 Samuel 15:22). David calls worshipers to bring offerings that correspond to righteous lives rather than attempting to cover rebellion with religious acts. The sacrificial system pointed beyond itself to the need for atonement ultimately fulfilled in Christ. Believers now offer themselves to God as living sacrifices through lives devoted to His will (Romans 12:1).

Deu 33:19 They will call the peoples to a mountain;…

Pro 21:3 To do righteousness and justice is more desirable…

Heb 13:15-16 Through Jesus, therefore, let us continually offer to God…

and trust in the LORD.
True worship rests on confidence in the Lord rather than in personal strength, circumstances, or religious performance. David directs those shaken by opposition and distress to rely on God’s faithfulness and righteous rule. Such trust includes obeying God when His purposes are not yet visible. Jesus perfectly entrusted Himself to the Father, even in suffering and death, and all who belong to Him are called to the same settled dependence on God’s promises.

Psalm 4:6

Many ask, “Who can show us the good?”
David recognizes the restless human search for security, prosperity, and a life that is truly good. In a time of opposition and uncertainty, many looked for visible solutions or favorable circumstances. Scripture directs that longing beyond earthly advantage to the wisdom and goodness of God, for He alone reveals the path of righteousness and gives lasting good to those who trust Him.

Psa 34:12-14 Who is the man who delights in life, …

Psa 27:13 Still I am certain to see the goodness of the LORD…

Lam 3:25 The LORD is good to those who wait for Him,…

Shine the light of Your face upon us, O LORD.
David appeals for the Lord’s favor, presence, and protection, echoing the priestly blessing of Numbers 6:24–26. God’s shining face brings peace because it assures His people that He sees them, receives them, and acts for their good. The request finds its fullest answer in Jesus Christ, the light of the world, through whom God’s saving favor has been made known.

Psalm 4:7

You have filled my heart with more joy
David recognizes that enduring joy comes from the Lord’s favor and presence. God gives a settled gladness that reaches the inner life and remains secure even when outward circumstances are uncertain. This joy anticipates the fullness of life found in Christ, who gives His people peace and satisfaction that the world cannot provide.

Psa 16:11 You have made known to me the path of life;…

than when grain and new wine abound.
Grain and new wine represented harvest abundance, security, and festive celebration in Israel. Though such provisions were covenant blessings from God, David valued the Giver above His gifts. Material prosperity may increase or disappear, but joy rooted in the Lord rests on His unchanging faithfulness.

Psalm 4:8

I will lie down and sleep in peace,
Sleep leaves a person exposed and unable to defend himself, yet David rests without fear because his security does not depend on favorable circumstances or human strength. The peace God gives reaches beyond the absence of conflict; it steadies the heart in the presence of danger. Jesus promised His disciples a peace unlike the fragile peace offered by the world (John 14:27).

Pro 3:24 When you lie down, you will not be afraid;…

Job 11:18-19 You will be secure, because there is hope…

Psa 3:5 I lie down and sleep; I wake again, for the LORD sustains…

for You alone, O LORD,
David places exclusive confidence in the covenant God of Israel rather than in allies, weapons, wealth, or personal power. The LORD remains faithful to His promises and is fully able to preserve those who entrust themselves to Him. Such settled trust accords with the promise that God keeps in perfect peace those whose minds are fixed on Him (Isaiah 26:3).

God’s protection includes every circumstance of life and extends beyond physical preservation to the safety of belonging to Him. David’s confidence anticipates the Shepherd’s care described in Psalm 23 and finds its fullest assurance in Christ, whose sheep cannot be taken from His hand or the Father’s hand (John 10:28–29).
