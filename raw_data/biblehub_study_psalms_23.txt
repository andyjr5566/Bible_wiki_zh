The LORD Is My Shepherd

Psalm 23:1

A Psalm of David.
David wrote from the experience of one who had guarded sheep in the fields of Bethlehem before he ruled Israel. His years as a shepherd taught him the dependence, vulnerability, and needs of a flock. As king, David also knew the burden of leading God’s people, whom Scripture often portrays as sheep in need of faithful care. The psalm belongs to Israel’s worship and testifies that the true security of God’s people rests not in earthly rulers, wealth, or strength, but in the Lord Himself.

The LORD is my shepherd;
Yahweh, the covenant God of Israel, personally watches over David and all who belong to Him. A shepherd provided pasture, water, direction, rescue, and defense against danger; David recognizes God as the One who performs these duties perfectly. Isaiah portrays the Lord gathering His lambs in His arms, and Ezekiel promises that God will seek His scattered flock and care for them. Jesus fulfills this divine shepherding work as the Good Shepherd who lays down His life for the sheep and leads them into eternal life.

I shall not want.
God’s shepherdly care supplies everything necessary for His people to live under His faithful hand. David does not deny hardship, danger, or seasons of need; he confesses that no true good will be withheld from those entrusted to the Lord. Contentment grows from confidence that God knows His people, leads them wisely, and provides according to His purposes. Christ, the bread of life, meets the deepest hunger of the soul and secures the inheritance of all who trust in Him.

Psalm 23:2

He makes me lie down in green pastures;
The Shepherd supplies more than survival; He provides abundant nourishment and secure rest. Sheep do not settle when threatened, hungry, or disturbed, so their lying down portrays the safety produced by the Shepherd’s presence. In a land where fertile grazing was precious, green pastures testified to careful guidance and faithful provision. The Lord fulfills His promise to feed His flock and give them rest (Ezek. 34:14–15), nourishing His people through His Word and sustaining them in fellowship with Himself.

He leads me beside quiet waters.
The Shepherd brings His sheep to life-giving water where they may drink without danger or fear. In Israel’s dry country, dependable streams were essential, and finding them required knowledge of the land and attentive care. The Lord leads His people into the peace and refreshment that flow from trusting Him. He does not abandon His flock in barren places but guides them according to His truth, ultimately fulfilling the promise that His redeemed people will neither hunger nor thirst (Isaiah 49:10; Revelation 7:16–17).

Psalm 23:3

He restores my soul;
The Shepherd renews the whole person when weariness, sin, sorrow, and fear have drained strength. David knew both the dangers of the wilderness and the restoring mercy of God. The Lord brings His people back from wandering and gives inward peace to those who depend on Him. Isaiah promised renewed strength to those who wait on the Lord, and Jesus welcomes the weary and burdened, giving rest to their souls.

He guides me in the paths of righteousness
The Shepherd does not merely revive His sheep; He leads them in the way that is right, safe, and pleasing to God. His guidance directs His people away from destructive paths and into obedient living shaped by His Word. Those who trust the Lord rather than their own understanding find that He makes their paths straight. Christ, the Good Shepherd, continues to lead His people through His Word and Spirit.

God’s restoring and guiding work displays His holy character, faithfulness, and covenant love. He acts consistently with the promises bound to His name, not because His people deserve His care, but because He is righteous and true. Israel’s history repeatedly showed that the Lord preserved and restored His people for the honor of His name among the nations.

Psalm 23:4

Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death,
David portrays the believer’s path through real danger, grief, suffering, and mortality. The steep ravines of Judea could conceal predators and robbers, yet a shepherd led his flock through such places toward needed pasture and water. The valley is not the shepherd’s destination; it is a passage under his care. Death casts a dark shadow over fallen human life, but the Lord leads His people beyond it. Christ entered suffering and death, rose in victory, and has secured the final deliverance of all who belong to Him.

I will fear no evil,
Confidence replaces terror because the sheep’s safety rests in the Shepherd rather than in their own ability. Evil remains real, but it does not stand outside God’s rule. The Lord’s people may face affliction without being abandoned or overcome, for He upholds them and will ultimately judge all evil.

for You are with me;
David turns from speaking about the Lord to speaking directly to Him. God’s nearness is the ground of peace in the darkest valley. The Lord promised His presence to Joshua, strengthened His servants throughout Israel’s history, and came near to His people in Jesus Christ, Emmanuel, “God with us.” The risen Christ continues with His disciples and will bring them safely into His eternal presence.

Your rod and Your staff, they comfort me.
The shepherd’s rod defended the flock from wild animals and corrected wandering sheep; the staff guided, rescued, and supported them on dangerous ground. God’s protection and discipline are not opposed to His love but reveal His faithful care. Jesus, the Good Shepherd, lays down His life for the sheep and preserves them by His powerful hand.

Psalm 23:5

You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies.
The Shepherd is also the gracious Host, receiving His servant into fellowship, honor, and safety. In the ancient Near East, a host accepted responsibility for the protection of those welcomed under his roof, and a shared meal marked peace and covenant fellowship. David pictures secure provision while hostile powers remain nearby. The Lord’s blessing cannot be overturned by those who oppose His people. The scene anticipates the kingdom feast and its final fulfillment in the marriage supper of the Lamb (Revelation 19:9).

You anoint my head with oil;
Oil refreshed and honored a welcomed guest, while also setting apart priests and kings for God’s service. God’s favor rests personally upon David, whom Samuel had anointed as king (1 Samuel 16:13). The image also recalls a shepherd’s care, since oil soothed wounds and protected sheep from pests. Such tender provision points to Christ, the Anointed One, who fulfills the offices of Prophet, Priest, and King.

Jesus promised life in abundance to those who follow Him (John 10:10), and the Holy Spirit continues to fill believers with the goodness of God.

Psalm 23:6

Surely goodness and mercy will follow me all the days of my life,
David rests in the certain care of the Shepherd who provides, protects, restores, and leads His sheep. God’s goodness supplies what His people truly need, while His mercy reflects His faithful covenant love that does not abandon them in sin, danger, or distress. Rather than merely trailing behind, God’s goodness and mercy pursue the believer throughout every season of life. The Lord revealed this same character to Moses as “merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth” (Exodus 34:6). His faithfulness remains constant even when circumstances are difficult, and His presence gives courage for every day (Deuteronomy 31:6).

Lam 3:22-23 Because of the loving devotion of the LORD…

and I will dwell in the house of the LORD forever.
David anticipates abiding in the presence of the God he worshiped. During his lifetime, the tabernacle stood as the appointed place of worship and sacrifice, bearing witness that the holy God had made a way to dwell among His people. The promise reaches beyond access to an earthly sanctuary, pointing to unbroken fellowship with the Lord that death cannot end. Jesus fulfills this hope by bringing His people into the Father’s house, where He prepares a place for them (John 14:2–3). The final fulfillment awaits the renewed creation, when God will dwell with His redeemed people forever and remove death, sorrow, and pain (Revelation 21:3–4).
