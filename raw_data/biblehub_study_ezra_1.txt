The Proclamation of Cyrus

Ezra 1:1

In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia,
Cyrus captured Babylon in 539 BC, and his first regnal year over Babylon began in 538 BC. As founder of the Persian Empire, he ruled lands stretching across much of the ancient Near East. His policy of permitting displaced peoples to return and restore their worship is reflected in records such as the Cyrus Cylinder, though Scripture identifies the Lord as the true source behind Cyrus’s actions.

Jeremiah had foretold that Judah would serve Babylon for seventy years before the Lord brought His people back to their land (Jeremiah 25:11–12; 29:10). The fall of Babylon and the return authorized by Cyrus showed that God had neither abandoned His covenant people nor forgotten His promise. The Lord governs the rise and fall of empires and brings His word to pass at the appointed time.

Long before Cyrus’s birth, Isaiah had named him as the ruler who would permit Jerusalem to be rebuilt and the temple foundations to be laid (Isaiah 44:28; 45:1–4). God’s sovereign rule extends over kings and nations, using even a Gentile emperor to advance His redemptive purposes.

Cyrus the Great founded the Persian Empire and conquered Babylon in 539 BC. In the first year of his rule over Babylon, he issued a decree permitting the Jewish exiles to return and rebuild the temple. Persian records, including the Cyrus Cylinder, reflect his broader policy of restoring displaced peoples and supporting local worship.

Israel’s God was not confined to a ruined city or a conquered land; He governed Persia, Babylon, and every nation. The title became especially prominent after the exile, strengthening the returned community’s confidence that the Lord still ruled over all powers.

Cyrus Restores the Holy Vessels

Ezra 1:7

King Cyrus also brought out the articles belonging to the house of the LORD
Cyrus publicly restored the sacred vessels that had been taken from Solomon’s temple. Their return prepared the way for the renewal of sacrificial worship in Jerusalem and demonstrated that the Lord had not abandoned His covenant people. Cyrus’s action fulfilled the Lord’s earlier word through Isaiah that Cyrus would order Jerusalem and the temple rebuilt (Isaiah 44:28; 45:1–13).

Sheshbazzar, called the prince of Judah and later identified as governor, received the sacred vessels from Cyrus and bore responsibility for returning them safely to Jerusalem (Ezra 1:8; 5:14). He served under Persian authority, yet his work fulfilled the purpose of Israel’s God. The return of the vessels was more than the transport of royal property; it marked the reestablishment of worship centered on sacrifice, holiness, and the presence of God. His leadership anticipates the greater deliverance accomplished by Christ, who brings His people out of bondage and restores them to fellowship with God.
