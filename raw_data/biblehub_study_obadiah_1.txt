Obadiah 1:1

Obadiah received a divine revelation concerning the future of Edom. His name, meaning “servant of the LORD,” suits a prophet whose message centers on God’s authority and justice. Though Obadiah is the shortest book in the Old Testament, its message reaches beyond one neighboring nation to reveal the certainty of God’s judgment upon pride, violence, and hostility toward His covenant people.

Edom descended from Esau, the brother of Jacob, and its long history was marked by rivalry and resentment toward Israel. Situated south and southeast of the Dead Sea, Edom occupied mountainous territory containing defensible rock fortresses and major trade routes. Edom’s security, wealth, and pride did not place it beyond the rule of the Lord GOD, who governs every people and nation. The Deliverance of Israel

Obadiah 1:15

For the Day of the LORD is near for all the nations.
God’s judgment upon Edom points beyond one hostile neighbor to the coming reckoning of every nation. Edom, descended from Esau, had long opposed Israel, the descendants of Jacob, and had rejoiced when Jerusalem fell to Babylon. Yet the Lord’s rule extends over all peoples, kingdoms, and rulers. Joel and Zephaniah likewise portray the Day of the LORD as a certain and approaching time when God intervenes to judge wickedness and vindicate His people. The final fulfillment of this worldwide judgment is seen in the judgment described in Revelation.

Edom had acted violently and treacherously toward Judah in the day of Jerusalem’s calamity. They stood aloof, celebrated Judah’s disaster, plundered the fugitives, and delivered survivors to their enemies. God’s judgment would answer their deeds with corresponding justice. The moral order of God’s kingdom does not permit cruelty, pride, betrayal, or violence to escape His notice. Jesus taught that the measure people use toward others will be measured back to them.

The warning reaches all nations and individuals: God holds every person accountable, and He will finally repay evil while delivering those who trust in Him.

While Edom faced irreversible judgment for its violence against Judah, God promised preservation and restoration for His covenant people.
