The sisters share one ancestry and covenant history, yet both turned from the Lord to idolatry and foreign alliances. Samaria established worship apart from the temple God had chosen, while Jerusalem possessed the temple yet repeatedly defiled its worship through spiritual adultery.

Samaria and Jerusalem had abandoned covenant faithfulness by pursuing the gods and practices of surrounding nations. God appointed foreign powers as instruments of judgment, bringing upon them the violence and shame they had embraced. Their punishment corresponded to their rebellion, confirming that no people can sow idolatry and injustice without reaping God’s righteous judgment.

Jer 25:14 For many nations and great kings will enslave them…

and you will bear the consequences of your sins of idolatry.
Israel’s idolatry was spiritual adultery against the Lord who had redeemed and covenanted with them. Samaria fell to Assyria in 722 BC, and Jerusalem later fell to Babylon, culminating in the destruction of the temple in 586 BC. Exile, death, and devastation revealed the terrible cost of worshiping created things rather than the Creator. God’s discipline exposed sin and preserved the truth that He alone is worthy of worship.

Then you will know that I am the Lord GOD.’”
The judgment of Samaria and Jerusalem would demonstrate the Lord’s holiness, faithfulness, and sovereign rule over nations. Those who had rejected His word would know Him through the fulfillment of His warnings. Judgment did not cancel God’s covenant purposes; He later promised cleansing, a new heart, and restored fellowship for His people. Such restoration finds its fulfillment in the new covenant, in which the Lord writes His law upon the hearts of those redeemed through Christ.
