I Stretch Out My Hands to You

Psalm 143:1

A Psalm of David.
King David knew the pressures of danger, betrayal, sin, and national responsibility. His life as shepherd, fugitive, warrior, and king taught him that safety and righteousness come from the Lord rather than human strength. The title connects Psalm 143 to David’s personal faith and to the covenant God made with his house, promising an enduring royal line (2 Samuel 7:12–16). That promise reaches its fulfillment in Jesus Christ, the Son of David and the eternal King.

O LORD, hear my prayer.
David addresses Yahweh, Israel’s covenant God, whose name recalls His self-revelation, faithfulness, and saving acts. Prayer rests on the assurance that the Lord sees the afflicted and hears those who call upon Him. David does not turn first to armies, advisers, or his own experience; he brings his need directly before God.

In Your faithfulness, give ear to my plea;
God’s faithfulness is the ground of David’s appeal. The Lord had bound Himself to His people by covenant and had repeatedly shown that He keeps His word despite human weakness and failure. David asks for God’s attentive intervention because the Lord’s steadfast character does not change. His plea reflects the confidence later expressed in Scripture: God remains faithful to all He has promised.

David seeks an answer according to God’s righteous character, not according to his own deserving. The Lord judges evil truly, defends the oppressed, and remains faithful to His covenant purposes. David’s dependence anticipates the fuller revelation of God’s righteousness in Christ, through whom sinners may stand accepted before God by faith rather than personal merit.

Psalm 143:2

Do not bring Your servant into judgment,
David appeals to the covenant Lord for mercy, knowing that God’s righteous judgment exposes every sin. As God’s servant, he does not claim innocence or demand favorable treatment. He entrusts himself to the Judge of all the earth, whose holiness and justice cannot overlook evil. Psalm 130:3 expresses the same confession: if the Lord kept account of sins, no one could stand.

for no one alive is righteous before You.
Every person stands guilty before God apart from His grace. David’s confession anticipates the biblical witness that “there is no one righteous, not even one” (Romans 3:10). Human righteousness cannot satisfy God’s perfect standard, making forgiveness and redemption necessary. God provides that righteousness through Jesus Christ, who bore sin so that those who believe in Him might become the righteousness of God in Him (2 Corinthians 5:21).

Psalm 143:3

For the enemy has pursued my soul,
David describes a relentless assault aimed not only at his safety but at his whole life before God. His enemies may have included Saul and other hostile rulers, yet their pursuit also reveals the spiritual conflict in which evil seeks to destroy faith, hope, and obedience. Believers likewise face an adversary who prowls like a roaring lion, seeking to devour those who trust in the Lord (1 Peter 5:8).

The psalmist feels beaten down, humiliated, and stripped of strength, as one trampled beneath a conqueror’s feet. Such suffering recalls Job’s sense that God had broken him down on every side (Job 19:10), while ultimately pointing to the Suffering Servant, who was wounded and crushed in bearing the sins of His people (Isaiah 53:5).

David’s darkness anticipates the need for Christ, the true Light of the world, who brings those in spiritual darkness into the light of life (John 8:12).

David’s anguish resembles the condition of those in the grave, cut off from ordinary life and unable to act. Though he speaks from profound despair, the God who hears him is able to restore life. The final hope beyond death rests in Christ’s victory over the grave, through which death will be swallowed up in victory (1 Corinthians 15:54–57).

Psalm 143:4

My spirit grows faint within me;
David describes inward collapse under prolonged affliction, danger, and isolation. His many years of flight from Saul, opposition from enemies, and later family sorrows taught him the limits of human strength. Similar lament appears in Psalm 42:6, where a downcast soul turns again toward God in hope. Jesus also entered profound sorrow in Gethsemane, declaring that His soul was overwhelmed with grief as He prepared to bear the sin of the world (Matthew 26:38). Weakness honestly brought before God becomes an appeal for His sustaining presence.

Fear and distress have reached the center of David’s thoughts, desires, and resolve. Threats from without have produced turmoil within, yet the psalm moves toward remembrance of God’s works, prayer for mercy, and trust in His guidance. David’s troubled heart anticipates the anguish of Christ, who said, “Now My soul is troubled,” as the cross drew near (John 12:27). God receives the believer’s unsettled heart and supplies the wisdom, peace, and strength that circumstances cannot provide.

Psalm 143:5

I remember the days of old;
David strengthens his present faith by recalling the Lord’s former acts of mercy, deliverance, and covenant faithfulness. The days of old include God’s dealings with the patriarchs, the Exodus, the wilderness provision, the conquest of Canaan, and His preservation of Israel under the judges and kings. Deuteronomy 32:7 likewise calls Israel to remember earlier generations in order to understand the faithfulness of God. Memory becomes a defense against despair, because the God who acted in history has not changed.

I meditate on all Your works;
David does not dwell merely on isolated blessings but considers the whole record of God’s works in creation, providence, judgment, and redemption. The heavens declare His glory, and Israel’s history reveals His power to save and sustain His people. Such meditation turns the heart from fear to reverent confidence, grounding prayer in what God has already shown Himself to be.

The Creator who formed the heavens and earth also governs the details of human life. David’s reflection on God’s handiwork leads him to acknowledge the Lord’s wisdom, power, and personal care. The same hands that delivered Israel and established David’s kingdom remained able to preserve him in his distress.

Psalm 143:6

I stretch out my hands to You;
David approaches God in helpless dependence, lifting empty hands in prayer and pleading for mercy. Moses likewise held up his hands as Israel prevailed over Amalek, showing that deliverance comes from the Lord rather than human strength (Exodus 17:11). Open hands also anticipate Christ, who willingly stretched out His hands on the cross to bear sin and secure redemption.

David desires God Himself, not merely relief from distress. As water sustains physical life, the Lord alone sustains and restores the inner life of His people. The longing echoes the deer panting for streams in Psalm 42:1 and finds its fulfillment in Christ, who gives the living water that becomes eternal life (John 4:14).

The image points to humanity’s deeper spiritual need, answered by Jesus’ invitation to all who thirst to come to Him and drink (John 7:37–38).

David begins the day seeking assurance of God’s covenant love and mercy. As Israel gathered fresh manna each morning, he depends upon the Lord for renewed provision, direction, and hope. Early prayer places the day beneath God’s faithfulness, as Jesus also rose early to pray in a solitary place.

Psalm 143:9

Deliver me from my enemies, O LORD;
David brings his danger directly before the covenant God, acknowledging that rescue lies beyond human strength. His enemies included violent opponents and unjust rulers, yet the psalm also recognizes the deeper threat of sin and human rebellion against God. Israel’s history testified that the LORD delivers His people, from the bondage of Egypt to the dangers they faced in the wilderness and the promised land. The request anticipates the greater salvation accomplished through Christ, who delivers His people from the dominion of sin, death, and the evil one.

Psa 144:2 He is my steadfast love and my fortress, my stronghold…

I flee to You for refuge.
God Himself is the safe place for those who trust Him. David does not merely ask for changed circumstances; he seeks the presence and protection of the LORD. The cities of refuge in Israel provided legal protection for the vulnerable, but they pointed beyond themselves to the security found in God. Psalm 46:1 likewise declares that God is “our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in trouble.” Christ receives all who come to Him in faith and grants the lasting refuge of forgiveness, peace with God, and eternal life.

Psalm 143:10

Teach me to do Your will,
David seeks more than deliverance from his enemies; he asks to be formed by God’s instruction. Obedience requires a heart willing to learn, because God’s will is not discovered by human wisdom alone. The request reflects the path of faith seen throughout Scripture: trusting the Lord rather than relying on personal understanding and asking Him to reveal the way of righteousness. God’s commands are not burdensome detours but the good path by which His people walk in truth.

David grounds his request in covenant devotion. The God who rescued Israel from Egypt and pledged Himself to His people is not distant or impersonal; He is David’s God. Personal allegiance to the Lord brings both confidence and accountability. David belongs to God, and therefore he desires to live under God’s rule. Believers likewise approach God as those received into His family through Christ, calling upon Him with trust and reverence.

David grounds his plea in the revealed character of the covenant God. The LORD had bound His name to His promises, mercy, holiness, and faithfulness, as proclaimed to Moses in Exodus 34:5–7. Deliverance would display God’s faithfulness before His people and among the nations.

Psalm 143:12

And in Your loving devotion, cut off my enemies.
God’s covenant love moves Him to defend those who belong to Him. David entrusts judgment to the Lord rather than taking vengeance into his own hands, asking God to remove those who oppose God’s purposes and threaten His servant. God’s faithful love is never separated from His righteousness; He delivers His people and judges persistent evil. The request anticipates Christ’s final victory over every enemy of God, including sin, death, and Satan.

Destroy all who afflict me,
David seeks the Lord’s decisive intervention against those who pursue him with violence and injustice. His enemies were not merely personal opponents but men who resisted the king God had chosen and thereby opposed God’s covenant purposes for Israel. Such prayers place vengeance in God’s hands, where justice is perfect and motives are fully known. Scripture looks forward to the day when the Lord will finally judge all evil and establish His righteous kingdom.
