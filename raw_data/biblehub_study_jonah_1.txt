and cast him into the sea,
The sea represented danger, death, and forces beyond human control. Jonah, who had tried to flee from the presence and mission of God, was surrendered to the waters he had hoped would carry him away from God’s call. Yet God’s judgment did not end His mercy. Jonah’s descent into the sea would lead to his preservation in the great fish and ultimately to his return to obedient service. His three days in the fish foreshadowed Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection, as Jesus Himself taught (Matthew 12:40).

Jonah 1:16

Then the men feared the LORD greatly,
The sailors had first cried out to their own gods in helplessness, but the LORD’s command over the sea revealed the impotence of every rival deity. When the storm ceased at Jonah’s descent into the sea, they recognized that Israel’s God rules the winds, waves, and human life. Their fear became reverent awe before the Creator, like Israel’s fear of the LORD after the Red Sea deliverance (Exodus 14:31). The pagan sailors responded to God’s revelation with greater humility than Jonah had shown in fleeing from His presence.

Jonah 1:17

Now the LORD had appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah,
God sovereignly prepared the creature as both judgment and rescue. Jonah’s flight from the Lord ended not in drowning but in a confinement that preserved his life and confronted his rebellion. The account centers on God’s command over creation, not on identifying the species of fish. As the Lord used the sea, wind, and lot to expose Jonah’s disobedience, He used the fish to keep His prophet alive for repentance and renewed service.

and Jonah spent three days and three nights in the belly of the fish.
Jonah’s confinement marked a profound descent from self-will into helpless dependence upon God. The full period underscores the reality and severity of his ordeal, as well as the miraculous preservation of his life. Jesus later identified Jonah’s three days and three nights in the fish as a sign pointing to His own death, burial, and resurrection (Matthew 12:40). Jonah emerged to obey God’s call; Christ rose in victory over sin and death.
