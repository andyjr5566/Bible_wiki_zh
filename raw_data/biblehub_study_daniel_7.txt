The setting anticipates Babylon’s fall in Daniel 5 and establishes that God rules over kingdoms before their rise, during their power, and at their end.

Daniel’s vision of the four beasts, the Ancient of Days, and the coming Son of Man left him deeply troubled. Although God revealed the certainty of His final kingdom, Daniel also saw the violence, arrogance, and oppression that would mark the rise of earthly empires. The revelation weighed upon him personally because it concerned the future of his people and the course of history under God’s sovereign rule. Like Ezekiel and John, Daniel encountered divine glory and judgment in a way that exposed the frailty of human understanding.

‘These four great beasts
The imagery of beasts in Daniel 7 is symbolic, representing kingdoms or empires. This vision parallels the dream in Daniel 2, where different metals represented successive empires. The beasts are described in detail earlier in the chapter, each with distinct characteristics, symbolizing the nature and power of the empires they represent. The use of beasts suggests ferocity and dominance, common traits of ancient empires.

In biblical prophecy, “kings” often represent kingdoms or empires rather than individual rulers. The four kings here are generally understood to symbolize four major empires that have significant roles in biblical history. These are traditionally identified as Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, and Rome. Each of these empires had a profound impact on the history of Israel and the broader ancient Near East.
