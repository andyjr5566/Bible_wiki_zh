Elihu calls Job and his friends to hear the storm as a testimony to God’s sovereign power. Thunder portrays the authority and majesty of the Lord, whose voice is glorious and powerful (Psalm 29:3–4). God’s creation does not operate independently of Him; it continually bears witness to His holiness, wisdom, and rule.

Job 37:3

God directs the storm with unrestricted authority, sending lightning across the heavens according to His will. At Sinai, lightning accompanied the Lord’s holy presence as He gave His law to Israel (Exodus 19:16). David likewise praised God for scattering His enemies with lightning (Psalm 18:14). What appears uncontrollable to man remains wholly governed by the Creator. Elihu’s description confronts Job with the greatness of God, whose power is active in the visible world and whose presence cannot be contained by any boundary.

Lightning also anticipates the majesty of divine judgment and revelation, imagery later associated with God’s heavenly throne (Revelation 4:5).

Elihu portrays the approach of God with the brilliance of a storm clearing from the north, where golden light breaks through the clouds. The image points beyond created beauty to the radiance of the Creator, whose holiness cannot be mastered or reduced to human understanding. Gold throughout Scripture often accompanies royal glory and sacred worship, as in the tabernacle and temple, reflecting the incomparable worth of God.

Job 26:7 He stretches out the north over empty space;…

Ezk 1:4 I looked and saw a whirlwind coming from the north,…

Ezk 1:27-28 From what seemed to be His waist up, I saw a gleam…

awesome majesty surrounds Him.
God’s majesty is not merely an attribute displayed at certain moments; it belongs to His very presence. The thunder, lightning, clouds, and winds described throughout Job 37 prepare for the Lord’s appearance from the whirlwind in Job 38. Like Israel at Sinai, Job is brought face to face with the holiness and power of the God who governs creation and rules over every human circumstance.

Job 37:23

The Almighty is beyond our reach;
God cannot be discovered, measured, or judged by human wisdom. Elihu’s words acknowledge the limits of human understanding before the Creator whose purposes encompass all creation and all history. God’s ways are higher than man’s ways, yet His hidden purposes never contradict His revealed character. Isaiah 55:8–9 and Romans 11:33 likewise call believers to humble trust before the unsearchable wisdom of God.

Job 11:7 Can you fathom the deep things of God…

Rom 11:33 O, the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge…

Isa 55:9 “For as the heavens are higher than the earth,…

He is exalted in power!
The Lord governs the storm, the stars, the animals, and the affairs of nations without weakness or limitation. Job has witnessed suffering he cannot explain, but suffering does not place God beyond control. The God who delivered Israel from Egypt and raised Jesus Christ from the dead possesses all power to accomplish His will.

Psa 21:13 Be exalted, O LORD, in Your strength; we will sing…

Psa 89:13 Mighty is Your arm; strong is Your hand.…

Psa 147:5 Great is our Lord, and mighty in power;…

In His justice and great righteousness
Divine power is inseparable from divine holiness. God never acts arbitrarily, corruptly, or cruelly. Though Job cannot yet see the reason for his affliction, the Judge of all the earth will always do what is right. God’s righteousness is displayed fully in Christ, who bore sin’s judgment so that God might remain just while justifying those who believe.

Psa 89:14 Righteousness and justice are the foundation of Your throne…

Deu 32:4 He is the Rock, His work is perfect; all His ways are just.…

Psa 97:2 Clouds and darkness surround Him; righteousness and justice…

He does not oppress.
Human rulers often use power to exploit the weak, but the Lord defends the oppressed and judges wickedness. His authority brings no injustice, and His discipline is never the abuse of a tyrant. Jesus revealed this righteous mercy when He welcomed the weary and burdened, offering rest to all who come to Him.

Job 34:12 Indeed, it is true that God does not act wickedly,…

Psa 92:15 “The LORD is upright; He is my Rock, and in Him”…

Job 37:24

Therefore, men fear Him,
Elihu closes his meditation on God’s power in the storm by calling all people to reverent awe. The Lord governs clouds, lightning, rain, ice, and wind with wisdom beyond human control or explanation. Job’s suffering had raised deep questions, but the created order testifies that God remains sovereign, righteous, and worthy of trust even when His purposes are hidden.

Ecc 3:14 I know that everything God does endures forever;…

for He is not partial to the wise in heart.”
God is not impressed by human intellect, status, or self-confidence. Those who consider themselves wise cannot place God under examination or demand that He justify His ways. Scripture repeatedly teaches that the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, because true understanding begins with humble submission to Him. God judges uprightly and without favoritism, and His wisdom exceeds the combined insight of every human heart.
