Tribute to Xerxes and Mordecai

Esther 10:1

Now King Xerxes
Xerxes I, called Ahasuerus in the Hebrew text, ruled Persia from 486 to 465 BC. His empire extended from India to Cush, encompassing many peoples, languages, and provinces. The king’s immense earthly authority forms the backdrop for God’s preservation of His covenant people through Esther and Mordecai.

imposed tribute
Royal tribute supplied the Persian court, supported its armies, and demonstrated the subjection of distant provinces to the throne. Xerxes’ power could command wealth from vast territories, yet the Book of Esther shows that even imperial decrees remain under God’s sovereign direction.

Esther 10:2

And all of Mordecai’s powerful and magnificent accomplishments,
Mordecai had exposed an assassination plot against King Xerxes, resisted Haman’s demand for homage, urged Esther to act for her people, and helped establish Purim as a lasting memorial of Israel’s deliverance. His courage and wisdom became instruments of God’s preserving care for the Jewish people during their exile.

together with the full account of the greatness to which the king had raised him,
King Xerxes elevated Mordecai from a despised target of Haman’s hatred to the highest office beneath the throne. The reversal displayed God’s sovereign ability to humble the proud and honor those who faithfully serve Him. Mordecai’s rise recalls Joseph’s promotion in Egypt, where God placed a faithful Israelite in authority within a foreign empire to preserve many lives.

are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Media and Persia?
Persian royal archives recorded significant events and honors within the empire, including Mordecai’s earlier service in uncovering the conspiracy against the king. Although those records have not survived, their mention confirms that Mordecai’s influence extended beyond the Jewish community. The Lord had brought him into the king’s court for such a time as this and caused his faithful service to bear enduring fruit.

Esther 10:3

For Mordecai the Jew was second only to King Xerxes,
Mordecai rose from sitting at the king’s gate to becoming the highest official in the Persian Empire under Xerxes. His elevation recalls Joseph’s authority under Pharaoh in Egypt (Genesis 41:40–41) and Daniel’s service in Babylon and Persia (Daniel 6:1–3). God placed Mordecai near the throne before Haman’s plot unfolded, demonstrating His providential care for His covenant people living in exile. With authority extending across a vast empire, Mordecai was able to secure the Jews’ preservation and advance their welfare.

preeminent among the Jews
Mordecai held honor not merely because of his royal office, but because he had faithfully identified himself with his people when their lives were threatened. He had refused to bow in compromise, mourned openly over the decree of destruction, urged Esther to act, and helped establish the observance of Purim after God delivered the Jews. His prominence reflected the esteem of a people who recognized courageous and faithful leadership.

and highly favored by his many kinsmen,
The Jewish community regarded Mordecai with favor because he used influence for their protection rather than personal advancement. Although many Jews remained dispersed throughout Persian territory, their shared deliverance strengthened their identity as God’s people. Mordecai’s standing among them illustrates the blessing of leadership marked by integrity, wisdom, and concern for those entrusted to its care.

seeking the good of his people
Mordecai did not treat his position as a means of self-exaltation. He sought the welfare of the Jewish people, fulfilling the kind of responsibility Jeremiah urged upon exiles who lived under foreign rule: to seek the peace and prosperity of the place where God had sent them (Jeremiah 29:7). His life shows that godly service pursues the good of others even within secular structures of power.

and speaking peace to all his countrymen.
Mordecai promoted peace, security, and well-being among his fellow Jews after a season of terror and conflict. His words helped unite a scattered people and establish stability following their deliverance. Such peace reflects more than the end of danger; it includes the justice, order, and communal flourishing that come when God preserves His people and raises up faithful servants on their behalf.
