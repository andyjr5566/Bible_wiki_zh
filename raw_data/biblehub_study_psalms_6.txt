David did not deny God’s right to correct him, but he appealed to the covenant God for mercy rather than judgment. Sin deserves rebuke, yet the repentant sinner may seek the LORD’s compassion.

Psalm 6:2

Be merciful to me, O LORD,
David appeals to YHWH, the covenant God of Israel, knowing that mercy is the only refuge for a sinner and sufferer. The Lord who revealed Himself as compassionate and gracious remains near to those who call upon Him in humble faith (Exodus 34:6; Psalm 51:1).

God’s healing restores what suffering has broken, whether in body, soul, or both. The Lord heard such cries throughout Israel’s history, as in Hezekiah’s illness, and Christ’s healing ministry revealed His authority over disease, suffering, and death (2 Kings 20:5; Matthew 8:16–17).

Psalm 6:3

My soul is deeply distressed.
David describes anguish that reaches his whole life before God, not merely a passing emotion. Similar grief appears in Hannah’s bitter weeping and in Jesus’ sorrow in Gethsemane, where suffering was brought honestly before the Father. Sin, enemies, illness, and the apparent absence of God can press heavily upon the faithful, yet such distress becomes a reason to seek the Lord rather than withdraw from Him.

1Sa 1:10 In her bitter distress, Hannah prayed to the LORD…

Job 7:11 Therefore I will not restrain my mouth;…

How long, O LORD, how long?
David’s repeated cry reveals faith under strain: he still addresses the covenant God even while he cannot understand the delay. Habakkuk raised the same question amid violence and injustice, and the martyrs in Revelation await God’s righteous judgment. God’s timing may test His people, but He does not forget them; the longing for relief ultimately points to His promised deliverance and final justice.

Psalm 6:4

Turn, O LORD,
David appeals for the Lord to turn from the severity of His discipline and to look upon him with restoring mercy. His suffering has made God’s help seem distant, yet he continues to address the covenant God by name and seeks His presence rather than relief apart from Him.

David asks for rescue of his whole life from the weakness, anguish, and danger surrounding him. The Lord who delivered Israel from bondage and repeatedly preserved David from his enemies is able to save His servant from every form of distress.

Psalm 6:5

For there is no mention of You in death;
Death removes the worshiper from the congregation of the living, where God’s works are remembered, confessed, and celebrated. David pleads for deliverance because his life is meant to bear witness to the Lord’s mercy and faithfulness. His words do not deny God’s power over death or the continued existence of the dead; they emphasize that death ends a person’s active testimony among God’s people on earth.

Isa 38:18 For Sheol cannot thank You; Death cannot praise You.…

Psa 30:9 What gain is there in my bloodshed, in my descent to the Pit?…

who can praise You from Sheol?
Sheol refers to the realm of the dead, pictured in the Old Testament as a place of silence, separation from earthly activity, and the end of public worship. David’s appeal rests on the desire that God’s saving work would be praised in the land of the living. Jesus Christ later conquered death through His resurrection, securing the hope that all who belong to Him will be raised to praise God forever.

Psalm 6:6

I am weary from groaning;
David’s lament rises from prolonged affliction that has drained both body and soul. His groaning is not empty despair but prayer offered from weakness, as God knows every unspoken burden and hears the cries of His people.

all night I flood my bed with weeping
Night, normally appointed for rest, has become a season of wrestling before God. David’s tears reveal the personal weight of his suffering and his refusal to hide his anguish from the Lord. The God who later kept David’s kingdom also attended him in the lonely hours of chastening and fear.

and drench my couch with tears.
The repeated picture of a bed soaked with tears emphasizes the depth of David’s sorrow. God does not overlook such grief; He records the tears of His servants and answers according to His mercy. David’s suffering also anticipates the Man of Sorrows, who entered human grief fully and wept with those who mourned.

Psalm 6:7

My eyes fail from grief;
David describes sorrow so severe that it drains his strength and shows itself in his body. Persistent weeping had weakened him, revealing that faith does not deny the reality of anguish. His grief arose under the weight of sin, divine discipline, and the distress brought by hostile men. Like Jeremiah, whose eyes were exhausted with tears over Jerusalem’s ruin, David brings unhidden pain before the Lord.

God’s answer does not depend on David’s remaining strength; He receives the prayer of the broken and acts against those who persist in evil.

Psalm 6:8

Depart from me, all you workers of iniquity,
David’s confidence replaces despair because the Lord has received his cry. Surrounded by enemies who oppose God’s righteous ways, he calls for separation from those committed to evil. The righteous must not join the counsel, conduct, or rebellion of the wicked, as Psalm 1 teaches. David does not claim sinlessness; he appeals to God as one who has humbled himself in repentance and faith. The words also anticipate Christ’s final judgment: “Depart from me, I never knew you” (Matthew 7:23). Persistent evildoers will ultimately be separated from the presence and favor of God.

for the LORD has heard my weeping.
David’s tears were not unnoticed or wasted before God. The Lord hears the distressed prayers of His people and responds according to His mercy and righteousness. Weeping reveals the depth of David’s sorrow under chastening and opposition, yet it also becomes the setting for renewed assurance. The God whose eyes are upon the righteous and whose ears are open to their cry (Psalm 34:15) receives the prayers of those who turn to Him.

Psalm 6:9

The LORD has heard my cry for mercy;
David’s confidence rises from anguish to assurance. The God who disciplines His people in righteousness does not abandon them in their weakness. Throughout Scripture, the LORD hears the distressed who call upon Him in faith, showing Himself merciful and gracious (Exodus 34:6; Psalm 34:17). Such mercy reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, whose intercession gives believers continuing access to God.

the LORD accepts my prayer.
God does more than observe human suffering; He receives the prayer of His servant. David rests in God’s covenant faithfulness before outward circumstances have changed. Believers may likewise approach God with confidence through Christ, trusting that He hears and answers according to His holy will (Hebrews 4:16; 1 John 5:14–15).

Psalm 6:10

All my enemies will be ashamed and dismayed;
David closes his prayer with confidence that the Lord will answer and reverse the hostility directed against him. Those who delight in the suffering of the righteous will face shame when God makes His justice known. God’s defense of His servants anticipates the final overthrow of all evil, when Satan and his works will be judged and removed forever (Revelation 20:10).

they will turn back in sudden disgrace.
The enemies’ assault will fail, and their intended triumph will become their humiliation. God has repeatedly brought sudden deliverance to His people, as when Pharaoh’s army was overwhelmed at the Red Sea (Exodus 14:26–28). David’s confidence rests not in his own strength but in the Lord, who hears the cry of the afflicted and acts decisively for those who trust Him.
