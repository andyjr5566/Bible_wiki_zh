The Root of Jesse

Isaiah 11:1

Then a shoot will spring up from the stump of Jesse,
Jesse was the father of David, from whose house God promised an enduring royal line. The stump pictures David’s dynasty cut down from its former glory through judgment and exile, yet not uprooted beyond recovery. God preserved His covenant promise and foretold a new King arising from David’s family. Jesus Christ came as the rightful heir of David, bringing hope where human kingdoms had failed.

1Sa 16:1-13 Now the LORD said to Samuel, How long are you going to mourn…

2Sa 7:12-16 And when your days are fulfilled and you rest…

Mat 1:5-6 Salmon was the father of Boaz by Rahab,…

and a Branch from his roots will bear fruit.
The Branch is the promised righteous ruler also announced in Jeremiah and Zechariah. His roots in Jesse’s household establish His true Davidic descent and His claim to David’s throne. Jesus bears lasting fruit through His righteous reign, His saving death and resurrection, and the gathering of a people redeemed from every nation. His kingdom will fulfill God’s promises with justice, peace, and life.

Isaiah 11:2

The Spirit of the LORD will rest on Him—
The promised King from Jesse’s line would be fully empowered by the Holy Spirit for His saving and righteous reign. Unlike many judges and kings in Israel, who received particular help from the Spirit for limited tasks, the Messiah would live and rule in the abiding fullness of God’s presence. Jesus was publicly revealed as this anointed One when the Spirit descended upon Him at His baptism, and He began His ministry in the power of the Spirit.

the Spirit of wisdom and understanding,
The Messiah would possess perfect discernment in judging people and governing God’s kingdom. He would not be deceived by appearances, political pressure, or human schemes. Jesus displayed this wisdom in His teaching, His responses to His opponents, and His knowledge of the human heart. In Him are found the wisdom and understanding needed for righteous judgment and true salvation.

the Spirit of counsel and strength,
God’s chosen King would perfectly know the Father’s purposes and possess the power to accomplish them. His counsel would not fail, and His strength would overcome every enemy of God’s people, including sin, death, and Satan. Jesus carried out the Father’s plan through His obedient life, atoning death, resurrection, and promised return as King.

Psa 28:7 The LORD is my strength and my shield; my heart trusts…

the Spirit of knowledge and fear of the LORD.
The Messiah would know the Father’s will completely and delight in reverent obedience to Him. Jesus consistently did what pleased the Father, submitting even to death on the cross. His holy fear of the LORD reveals the pattern of true righteousness: worshipful obedience flowing from love for God.

Isaiah 11:3

And He will delight in the fear of the LORD.
The promised Messiah will find joy in wholehearted reverence, obedience, and fellowship with the LORD. Jesus perfectly fulfilled this devotion, seeking the Father’s will in all things and honoring Him even unto death (John 4:34; Philippians 2:8). His rule will arise not from self-interest or human ambition but from perfect love for God and unwavering submission to God’s purposes. The fear of the LORD, the beginning of wisdom, will characterize the King whose kingdom rests on righteousness.

Pro 9:10 The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom…

He will not judge by what His eyes see,
The Messiah will not be governed by outward appearance, social standing, power, or visible success. Human rulers often judge according to what can be seen, but the LORD looks upon the heart (1 Samuel 16:7). Christ knows the hidden motives, sins, faith, and needs of every person. His judgments will therefore expose false appearances and vindicate those whom the world has overlooked or oppressed.

1Sa 16:7 But the LORD said to Samuel, “Do not consider his appearance…

and He will not decide by what His ears hear,
The Messiah will not be misled by rumor, accusation, partial testimony, or popular opinion. His knowledge is complete, and His verdicts are perfectly true. Jesus demonstrated this discernment during His earthly ministry, knowing what was in man and answering those who sought to test or deceive Him (John 2:24–25). When He reigns, justice will no longer be distorted by corruption or false witness, for every decision will proceed from divine wisdom and righteousness.

The Messiah will govern with the justice that human rulers so often deny to the vulnerable. The poor will not be dismissed because of their lack of wealth, status, or influence. His judgment will defend those afflicted by oppression, fulfilling the hope voiced in Psalm 72:4 that the righteous King would vindicate the needy and crush the oppressor.

No person will be overlooked or misrepresented before Him. God’s concern for the fatherless, widow, stranger, and poor runs throughout the Law and the Prophets (Deuteronomy 10:18), and the Messiah will administer that concern perfectly. His kingdom will expose every corrupt judgment and establish righteous order among all peoples.

Paul applies this picture to the Lord Jesus, who will destroy the lawless one “with the breath of His mouth” at His appearing (2 Thessalonians 2:8). His reign will not merely restrain wickedness; it will decisively remove it and vindicate all that is righteous.

Isaiah 11:5

The Messiah will be wholly governed by righteousness in His person and His rule. Like a soldier’s belt that secures his clothing and equips him for action, righteousness will mark every judgment He renders and every act of His kingdom. He will defend the poor, judge evil without partiality, and establish justice upon the earth (Isaiah 11:4). His perfect righteousness is fulfilled in Jesus Christ, whose obedience unto death provides righteousness for all who believe in Him.

Jesus Christ is the faithful and true King who will complete God’s plan of redemption and reign in righteousness (Revelation 19:11). His faithfulness gives lasting assurance to His people, for the One who saves them will also keep them.
