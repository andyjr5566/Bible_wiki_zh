Manasseh Reigns in Judah

2 Chronicles 33:1

Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king,
Manasseh succeeded Hezekiah about 697 B.C., inheriting a kingdom strengthened by his father’s faithfulness and reforms. Born after Hezekiah’s deliverance from illness, Manasseh himself was evidence of God’s mercy to Hezekiah. Yet his youth placed him under the influence of those who rejected Hezekiah’s devotion to the Lord. Unlike Josiah, who also began to reign as a child and sought God, Manasseh turned from the godly heritage entrusted to him.

and he reigned in Jerusalem fifty-five years.
Manasseh’s reign, approximately 697–642 B.C., was the longest of any king in Judah or Israel. His prolonged rule gave his idolatrous policies time to reshape Judah’s worship, corrupt its leaders, and fill Jerusalem with violence. Jerusalem, where the Lord had chosen to place His name, became the center of practices borrowed from the surrounding nations. The length of Manasseh’s reign also magnified the mercy later shown to him when God humbled him and he repented.

Evil was not measured by political success or popular approval, but by the holy judgment of the LORD, who sees both public actions and the heart. Manasseh’s reign demonstrates how quickly a nation can turn from spiritual reform when its leaders abandon God’s commands.

2 Chronicles 33:6

Rather than preserving the reforms of his father Hezekiah, Manasseh brought pagan worship into Judah and treated human life, made in God’s image, as an offering to idols.

2 Chronicles 33:9

So Manasseh led the people of Judah and Jerusalem astray,
Manasseh reigned for fifty-five years, the longest reign in Judah’s history, yet his rule reversed the reforms of his father Hezekiah. He rebuilt idolatrous shrines, erected altars to Baal and Asherah, worshiped the heavenly host, practiced divination, and caused his sons to pass through the fire. By royal example and public endorsement, he drew Judah and Jerusalem away from exclusive devotion to the LORD and into spiritual and moral corruption. God later humbled Manasseh through Assyrian captivity, and Manasseh repented and sought the LORD, but the evil he had established had deeply affected the nation.

Manasseh’s return to the LORD stands among Scripture’s clearest testimonies to God’s mercy toward a truly repentant sinner. After leading Judah into idolatry, violence, and covenant rebellion, he humbled himself in captivity and sought the God he had grievously offended. God heard him and restored him to Jerusalem, showing that forgiveness is not beyond reach for those who turn to Him with a contrite heart. His restoration accords with David’s confession in Psalm 51 and the promise that God forgives and cleanses those who confess their sins (1 John 1:9).
