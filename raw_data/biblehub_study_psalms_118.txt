Psalm 118:2

Let Israel say,
Israel, the covenant people redeemed from Egypt and established in the land by God’s promise, is summoned to bear public witness to the Lord’s faithfulness. The call invites the whole nation to respond in worship, likely through congregational or antiphonal praise. Israel’s history—from Abraham’s calling through the exodus, wilderness provision, conquest, exile, and restoration—testified that the Lord had not abandoned His covenant. Corporate confession also strengthened succeeding generations to remember the God who had acted for them.

Their duties included offering sacrifices, teaching God’s law, and leading the nation in holy worship. The priests’ public confession called Israel to recognize that even those appointed for sacred service stood wholly dependent on the covenant mercy of the Lord.

Yahweh, Israel’s covenant God, stands with His people in faithful help. The God who delivered Israel from Egypt, sustained them in the wilderness, and gave them the land does not abandon those who trust Him. His presence fulfills His promise: “He will not leave you or forsake you” (Deuteronomy 31:6). God’s support does not mean His servants escape every danger, but no enemy can overturn His purposes or separate His people from His care.

“The right hand of the LORD
The “right hand” is a biblical metaphor for strength and power. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the right hand was associated with authority and might. This imagery is used throughout scripture to describe God’s powerful acts of deliverance and judgment (Exodus 15:6, Isaiah 41:10). Theologically, it signifies God’s active involvement in the world and His ability to save His people. In the New Testament, Jesus is described as sitting at the right hand of God, indicating His divine authority and power (Mark 16:19).

In its fullest fulfillment, Jesus was rejected by Israel’s religious authorities, yet their rejection did not frustrate God’s purpose. Jesus applied these words to Himself after confronting the leaders who opposed Him (Matt. 21:42).

Psalm 118:24

This is the day that the LORD has made;
The day of deliverance celebrated in Psalm 118 came by the Lord’s sovereign work, not human strength. In its setting, Israel praised God for rescuing His people and establishing the rejected stone as the cornerstone (Psalm 118:22–23). Every day belongs to the Creator, yet this day especially marked a visible act of salvation. The New Testament applies the cornerstone passage to Christ, whom men rejected but whom God raised and exalted (Acts 4:10–11). His resurrection is the climactic day of God’s victory over sin and death.

Psalm 118:26

Blessed is he who comes in the name of the LORD.
Psalm 118 formed part of the Hallel sung at Israel’s great pilgrimage feasts, and worshipers approaching Jerusalem could receive this blessing from those serving at the temple. The welcome honors one sent under the LORD’s authority rather than one advancing a merely human cause. Jesus entered Jerusalem amid shouts drawn from this psalm, presenting Himself as Israel’s promised King and Messiah. Though many expected political deliverance, He came to accomplish the greater salvation of redemption through His death and resurrection.
