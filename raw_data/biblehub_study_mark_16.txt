Mark 16:16

Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved,
Faith in the risen Christ brings salvation, and baptism publicly marks the believer’s union with His death, burial, and resurrection (Romans 6:3–4). In the early church, those who received the gospel were baptized as an immediate act of obedience and identification with Christ (Acts 2:41; 8:12). Baptism does not replace faith or add human merit to Christ’s saving work; it testifies to the inward reality of trusting Him. Salvation rests on Christ alone, received through believing faith, while baptism stands as the God-given outward confession of that faith.

New tongues testified that the gospel was intended for every people and language, gathering humanity into Christ after the division of Babel. Paul instructed the churches to use such gifts in a way that honored God and strengthened His people (1 Corinthians 14:26–33).

Mark 16:18

they will pick up snakes with their hands,
God’s protection over His servants is seen in Paul’s escape from the viper’s bite on Malta after his shipwreck (Acts 28:3–5). Snakes represent danger and the work of evil throughout Scripture, beginning with the serpent in Eden. Such protection accompanies the advance of the gospel and displays Christ’s authority over the powers that oppose His mission. Believers are not called to seek danger or test God, but to obey Him confidently where He sends them.

and if they drink any deadly poison, it will not harm them;
The Lord is able to preserve His people from hidden attacks as well as open threats. Poisoning was a recognized means of murder in the ancient world, and God’s care extends even to dangers beyond human awareness. Jesus does not authorize deliberate acts of presumption; He assures His followers that no hostile power can thwart God’s purpose for their lives and witness.

The Ascension

Mark 16:19

After the Lord Jesus had spoken to them,
Jesus completed His post-resurrection instruction after appearing to His disciples over forty days. He had confirmed His bodily resurrection, opened their understanding of the Scriptures, commissioned them to proclaim the gospel, and promised the Holy Spirit’s power for their witness. His final words established the authority and mission by which the church would serve Him in the world.

He was taken up into heaven
Jesus visibly ascended from the earth, returning to the Father after completing His saving work. Acts 1:9–11 records that the disciples watched as He was lifted up and received by a cloud. His departure did not end His presence with His people; it prepared the way for the sending of the Holy Spirit and assured the disciples that the risen Christ reigns from heaven and will return in the same manner.

The exalted Christ occupies the place of supreme honor and authority promised in Psalm 110:1. His seated position declares that His atoning sacrifice is complete, unlike the repeated sacrifices of the old covenant priests. From the Father’s right hand, Jesus rules over all things, intercedes for His people, and awaits the final fulfillment of His kingdom when He returns as Judge and King.
