God Instructs Joshua

Joshua 1:1

Now after the death of His servant Moses,
Moses had completed the work God assigned him: delivering Israel from Egypt, receiving the law, and leading the nation through the wilderness. He died in Moab before entering Canaan, and the Lord buried him there (Deuteronomy 34:5–6). “His servant” honors Moses as God’s faithful covenant leader, prophet, and intercessor. Moses’ death closed a decisive era, yet God’s purposes for Israel did not end with the loss of its human leader.

the LORD spoke to Joshua son of Nun,
The Lord Himself appointed and directed Joshua, establishing that Israel’s future depended on God’s presence rather than Moses alone. Joshua belonged to the tribe of Ephraim and had stood with Caleb against the unbelief of the other spies, trusting God to give Israel the land (Numbers 13:8; 14:6–9). His name, meaning “The LORD is salvation,” pointed to the God who would save and establish His people.

Moses’ assistant,
Joshua had served beside Moses for many years, accompanying him on Sinai, remaining near the tent of meeting, and witnessing God’s dealings with Israel (Exodus 24:13; 33:11). Moses had publicly commissioned him before his death, and the Lord had already promised to be with him (Numbers 27:18–23; Deuteronomy 31:7–8). His years of humble service prepared him to lead Israel in obedience, courage, and dependence upon God.

God’s words would provide Joshua with the authority, command, and encouragement needed for the conquest of Canaan. The continuity of God’s revelation assured Israel that the covenant Lord remained present and faithful as a new generation entered the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

Israel’s conquest would demonstrate that the Lord judges wickedness, keeps His word, and preserves His people. Believers likewise face opposition with confidence that no power can finally overturn God’s purposes for those who belong to Him (Romans 8:31).

Joshua’s authority and success would be confirmed by the same divine presence that had marked Moses’ ministry. God’s faithfulness across generations gave Israel reason to follow Joshua with trust and obedience.

The Book of the Law is the Torah, the five books given through Moses that revealed God’s covenant, commands, promises, and ways. As Joshua assumed leadership after Moses’ death, Israel’s future depended not on military strength alone but on faithful submission to God’s revealed Word. God’s instruction was to remain on Joshua’s lips through reading, recitation, teaching, and conversation. Israel was likewise commanded to speak of God’s words in the home, on the road, in the morning, and at night (Deuteronomy 6:6–9). Public leadership and private faithfulness were to be governed by the same divine standard.

Joshua 1:17

Just as we obeyed Moses in all things, so we will obey you.
Israel recognized Joshua as Moses’ God-appointed successor. Moses had led the nation from Egypt, received God’s law, and guided Israel through the wilderness under the LORD’s direct direction. Joshua had served alongside Moses for many years and had already been commissioned before the whole nation (Deuteronomy 31:7–8). Their pledge of obedience was necessary as Israel prepared to cross the Jordan and take possession of the land God had promised. Loyalty to Joshua was ultimately loyalty to the LORD who had chosen and commanded him. Israel’s commitment would soon be tested, for obedience to Joshua’s instructions would require faith in God’s power at Jericho and throughout the conquest.

And may the LORD your God be with you, as He was with Moses.
God’s presence had distinguished Moses’ leadership through deliverance, provision, judgment, and guidance. The LORD parted the Red Sea, sustained Israel with manna, revealed His law, and led the people by the cloud and fire. Joshua did not need to become another Moses; he needed the same faithful God who had upheld Moses. The LORD had already promised Joshua, “As I was with Moses, so I will be with you” (Joshua 1:5). Israel’s blessing acknowledges that victory, courage, and faithful leadership would depend upon God’s abiding presence. God’s presence remains the strength of His people, fulfilled supremely in Christ, who promised to be with His disciples always (Matthew 28:20).
