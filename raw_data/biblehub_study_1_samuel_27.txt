1 Samuel 27:6

That day Achish gave him Ziklag,
Achish granted David a border town after David requested a place for his household and men to live apart from the royal city of Gath. Ziklag had been assigned within Judah’s inheritance and later associated with Simeon, but Philistine control of the area had displaced Israelite possession. Its location in the Negev gave David room to lead his six hundred men, protect their families, and conduct campaigns against Israel’s longstanding enemies. Achish regarded David as a dependable servant and useful ally against Saul, though David concealed from him the true targets of his raids. The Lord used David’s exile among the Philistines to preserve him from Saul and to prepare him for the responsibilities of kingship.

1 Samuel 27:8

Now David and his men went up and raided the Geshurites, the Girzites, and the Amalekites.
From Ziklag, David struck peoples occupying the southern frontier between Philistine territory and the wilderness leading toward Egypt. The Geshurites mentioned here were likely a southern desert people, distinct from the Aramean kingdom of Geshur northeast of Israel. The Girzites are otherwise unknown, but they belonged to the same region. The Amalekites had opposed Israel from the days of the exodus and stood under God’s judgment because of their persistent hostility toward His people (Exodus 17:8–16; Deuteronomy 25:17–19). David’s campaigns supplied his men while weakening forces that threatened Judah and Israel’s southern borders. His actions also continued the conflict Saul had failed to complete against Amalek (1 Samuel 15).
