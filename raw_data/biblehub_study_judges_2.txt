Israel Rebuked at Bochim

Judges 2:1

Now the angel of the LORD went up from Gilgal to Bochim
The Angel of the LORD speaks and acts with God’s own authority, and His appearance is commonly understood as a pre-incarnate manifestation of Christ. Gilgal had been Israel’s first camp in Canaan, marked by memorial stones, circumcision, Passover, and covenant renewal after the Lord brought them across the Jordan. Bochim, meaning “weepers,” became a place of grief over disobedience. The movement from Gilgal to Bochim exposed Israel’s decline from grateful obedience after victory to compromise with the inhabitants of the land.

Their idolatry was not a harmless addition to worship of the Lord; it denied His exclusive claim upon His people and drew them into practices marked by impurity and injustice. Hosea later used the same image to reveal the grief and seriousness of Israel’s unfaithfulness.

Whenever the LORD raised up a judge for the Israelites,
This phrase indicates the cyclical pattern of sin, oppression, repentance, and deliverance that characterizes the Book of Judges. The term “judge” (Hebrew: “shofet”) refers not only to a legal arbiter but also to a military leader or deliverer. The raising up of judges by the LORD underscores His sovereignty and mercy, as He intervenes in response to Israel’s distress. This divine intervention is a recurring theme throughout the Old Testament, highlighting God’s faithfulness to His covenant people despite their repeated unfaithfulness.
