Josiah Restores the Passover

2 Chronicles 35:1

Then Josiah kept the Passover to the LORD in Jerusalem,
Following the discovery of the Book of the Law and the renewal of Judah’s covenant with God, Josiah restored Passover worship according to the commands given through Moses. Jerusalem was the appointed place for this national observance, in keeping with the Lord’s instruction that sacrifice and worship be centered where He chose to place His name. The feast recalled Israel’s redemption from Egyptian bondage and called the nation to renewed faithfulness to the God who had redeemed them.

God’s provision through the lamb anticipated the perfect sacrifice of Jesus Christ, the Lamb of God, whose blood delivers believers from sin and eternal judgment. Josiah’s restoration of this sacrifice showed that worship and covenant fellowship required obedient trust in God’s appointed provision.

2 Chronicles 35:4

Prepare yourselves by families in your divisions,
Josiah called the Levites to serve in the Passover according to the established order of their ancestral houses and assigned duties. Moses had set apart the Levites for the Lord’s service, and David later arranged them into divisions for worship, gatekeeping, music, and ministry around the sanctuary (Numbers 3:6–10; 1 Chronicles 23–26). Order in worship guarded the holiness of the occasion and enabled the people to keep the Passover without confusion or neglect. God’s people were not gathered as isolated individuals, but as families and servants with distinct responsibilities before Him. The ordered service of Israel anticipates the church as Christ’s body, in which every believer is called to serve according to the grace given by God (1 Corinthians 12:12–27).

This gathering is significant as it reflects a return to covenant faithfulness, reminiscent of earlier times when all Israel gathered for worship, such as during the reigns of David and Solomon.

also observed the Passover at that time,
The Passover is a pivotal event in Israel’s history, commemorating their deliverance from slavery in Egypt. It is a time of remembrance and celebration of God’s salvation and faithfulness. Josiah’s observance of the Passover is notable because it was conducted according to the prescriptions found in the Book of the Law, which had been rediscovered during his reign. This observance is a return to the foundational practices of the faith, highlighting a revival of religious purity and devotion. The Passover also serves as a type of Christ, foreshadowing Jesus as the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world.
