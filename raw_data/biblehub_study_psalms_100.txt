Make a Joyful Noise

Psalm 100:1

A Psalm of thanksgiving.
Psalm 100 calls God’s people to approach Him with grateful worship. Thanksgiving belonged to Israel’s sacrificial worship and testified that every blessing came from the Lord’s faithful hand. The psalm directs worshipers to remember God’s covenant goodness and to answer His grace with praise.

Psa 50:14 Sacrifice a thank offering to God, and fulfill your vows…

Psa 69:30 I will praise God’s name in song and exalt Him…

Psa 107:1 Give thanks to the LORD, for He is good;…

Make a joyful noise to the LORD,
The LORD deserves open, wholehearted celebration from those who know His salvation and care. Israel rejoiced before Him when David brought the ark to Jerusalem, recognizing God’s presence among His people. Such praise acknowledges His kingship and declares that He alone is worthy of honor.

Psa 95:1 Come, let us sing for joy to the LORD; let us shout…

Psa 81:1 For the choirmaster. According to Gittith. Of Asaph.…

Psa 66:1 For the choirmaster. A song. A Psalm. Make a joyful noise…

all the earth.
The summons reaches beyond Israel to every nation, anticipating the day when all peoples will confess the Lord’s rule. God’s covenant work through Israel was never intended to conceal His glory from the nations but to display it. The gospel fulfills this worldwide call by gathering worshipers from every people to serve and praise the one true God.

Psalm 100:2

Serve the LORD with gladness;
Service to the LORD embraces every act of obedience offered in faith, whether in public worship or daily life. Israel’s worship was never intended to be empty ritual; the sacrifices, feasts, prayers, and ministry of the Levites were to arise from grateful devotion to the covenant God. Deuteronomy 28:47 warns that failure to serve the LORD with joy and a glad heart led to the bitter service of enemies. Gladness rests in God’s goodness, His faithful provision, and His redeeming mercy.

Deu 28:47 Because you did not serve the LORD your God with joy…

Psa 2:11 Serve the LORD with fear, and rejoice with trembling.

1Ch 28:9 As for you, Solomon my son, know the God of your father…

come into His presence with joyful songs.
God welcomed His people to draw near in worship, especially at the sanctuary where His name dwelt among Israel. Singing accompanied temple worship through appointed Levitical musicians and choirs, particularly under David’s organization of worship (1 Chronicles 15:16). Joyful song honors the LORD as King and declares His mighty works. The church continues this calling through psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, anticipating the day when the redeemed will sing before God’s throne (Ephesians 5:19; Revelation 5:9–14).

Psalm 100:3

Know that the LORD is God.
Israel was called to recognize Yahweh alone as the true and living God, supreme over creation and distinct from every idol. The confession stands with the Shema: “The LORD is our God, the LORD is one” (Deuteronomy 6:4). Worship begins with knowing the Lord as He has revealed Himself—Creator, covenant God, and righteous King.

Deu 4:35 You were shown these things so that you would know…

1Ki 18:39 When all the people saw this, they fell facedown…

It is He who made us,
Human life is not self-originating or accidental. The God who created the heavens and earth also formed mankind in His image (Genesis 1:26–27). His work of creation establishes His authority over every person and calls His creatures to humble gratitude and obedience.

and we are His;
God’s people belong to Him by His sovereign claim and covenant grace. Israel was His treasured possession among the nations (Exodus 19:5–6), not because of its greatness, but because of His love and faithfulness. Through Christ, those who receive Him are brought into God’s family as His children (John 1:12).

we are His people,
The Lord gathers a people for His name, setting them apart to display His holiness and proclaim His saving works. What began in God’s covenant purposes for Israel reaches the nations through the gospel. Believers in Christ are called “a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation” (1 Peter 2:9).

The Lord does not merely claim His people; He shepherds them. He provides, guides, guards, and restores those who depend upon Him, as Psalm 23 declares. Jesus fulfills this shepherd imagery as the Good Shepherd who lays down His life for the sheep and brings them safely into His flock (John 10:11, 27–28).

Psalm 100:4

Enter His gates with thanksgiving
The gates of the temple marked entry into the place God had chosen for His name to dwell among His covenant people. Israel did not approach the Lord casually or as though worship were a burden. Thanksgiving acknowledged that every blessing, deliverance, harvest, and provision came from His hand. Gratitude remains the fitting beginning of prayer and worship, even when believers bring burdens before God (Philippians 4:6).

Psa 118:19 Open to me the gates of righteousness,…

Psa 95:2 Let us enter His presence with thanksgiving;…

and His courts with praise;
The temple courts gathered worshipers from across Israel to honor the Lord through sacrifice, song, and public confession of His greatness. Praise centers on God’s character and mighty works, lifting attention beyond human need to His holiness, power, mercy, and faithfulness. Through Christ, believers now draw near to God with confidence and continually offer the sacrifice of praise to Him (Hebrews 13:15).

Psa 96:8 Ascribe to the LORD the glory due His name;…

Psa 84:2 My soul longs, even faints, for the courts of the LORD;…

give thanks to Him
Thanksgiving is a deliberate acknowledgment of God’s goodness in every circumstance. Israel’s worship included thank offerings and songs that remembered the Lord’s covenant faithfulness. Christians are likewise called to give thanks in all circumstances, trusting that God remains sovereign and good even amid hardship (1 Thessalonians 5:18).

and bless His name.
God’s name reveals His character, authority, and covenant presence. Blessing His name means honoring Him for who He is and declaring His worth before others. Such worship fulfills the desire that God’s name be hallowed among His people and throughout the earth (Matthew 6:9).

Psalm 100:5

For the LORD is good,
The LORD’s goodness fills creation and governs His dealings with His people. Israel knew His goodness in deliverance from Egypt, provision in the wilderness, the gift of the land, and the righteous law that revealed His holy ways. Every call to worship rests on God’s character rather than human circumstances. Jesus Christ displays the goodness of God perfectly through His compassionate ministry, atoning death, and victorious resurrection.

Psa 135:3 Hallelujah, for the LORD is good; sing praises…

Psa 34:8 Taste and see that the LORD is good; blessed is the man…

Nam 1:7 The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of distress;…

and His loving devotion endures forever;
God’s covenant love does not fail when His people fail. He remained faithful to His promises to Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and David, preserving Israel through judgment and exile and bringing them back according to His word. The cross gives the fullest revelation of that enduring love, for Christ bore sin’s judgment to secure forgiveness and eternal life for all who believe in Him.

His faithfulness continues to all generations.
God fulfills every promise in His appointed time. His faithfulness preserved the line of David until the coming of the Messiah and established an everlasting kingdom through Jesus Christ. Each generation may rest in the same dependable God, whose truth, mercy, and saving purpose never change.
