Psalm 11:2

For behold, the wicked bend their bows.
The wicked are portrayed as deliberate enemies of righteousness, preparing themselves to injure those who fear God. Bows were important weapons in ancient warfare, capable of striking from a distance before an opponent could respond. Psalm 37:14 similarly depicts the wicked drawing sword and bow against the poor, needy, and upright. Their hostility is not merely personal; it reflects rebellion against God and opposition to those who live under His rule.

They set their arrow on the string
The danger is immediate, not distant or imagined. The bow has been readied and the arrow positioned for release. God’s people are called to recognize spiritual opposition without being ruled by fear. Paul describes the assaults of the evil one as flaming arrows and directs believers to take up the shield of faith (Ephesians 6:16). Faith rests in God’s character and promises when threats appear prepared to strike.

Psalm 11:3

If the foundations are destroyed,
David speaks of the collapse of justice, truth, and moral order under the assault of the wicked. When rulers pervert judgment and evil appears to prosper, the structures that preserve community life seem to fail. Israel knew such seasons of ruin during the days of the Judges and in the decline that led to Jerusalem’s destruction.

what can the righteous do?”
The question voices the pressure felt by those who fear God when evil works in secret and public order is corrupted. David’s answer follows immediately: the Lord remains in His holy temple, and His throne is in heaven (Psalm 11:4). The righteous are not without refuge or purpose; they trust the Lord, reject compromise, continue in righteousness, and await His judgment. God Himself is the unshakable foundation, and He will ultimately vindicate those who seek Him.

Psalm 11:5

The LORD tests the righteous and the wicked;
God examines every life with perfect knowledge and justice. His testing does not uncover facts unknown to Him; it brings faith, motives, and conduct into the open. Abraham’s obedience on Mount Moriah showed the reality of his faith (Genesis 22:1–18), and trials continue to reveal whether God’s people will trust Him above comfort, fear, or self-interest. The righteous are not sinless, but they belong to the Lord and seek to walk in His ways. The wicked persist in rebellion against His rule. No social standing, outward religion, or hidden act escapes His judgment.

Jer 17:10 I, the LORD, search the heart; I examine the mind…

His soul hates the lover of violence.
God is not indifferent toward those who make violence their chosen way of life. He hates the shedding of innocent blood, oppression, cruelty, and every delight in harming others. Such conduct marked the earth before the flood, when it was filled with violence, bringing God’s judgment (Genesis 6:11–13). The Lord’s hatred of violence reflects His holy character and His care for those whom the violent exploit. His judgment warns sinners to repent, while His righteousness assures the afflicted that evil will not endure forever.

Psalm 11:6

On the wicked He will rain down fiery coals and sulfur;
The Lord’s judgment recalls the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah, when fire and sulfur fell from heaven upon cities hardened in sin (Genesis 19:24–25). Fire, sulfur, and consuming heat portray judgment that is holy, deliberate, and inescapable. God’s patience does not lessen His opposition to evil; those who reject His rule will ultimately answer to Him. Isaiah likewise pictures God’s righteous anger as burning sulfur prepared for the rebellious (Isaiah 30:33), and Revelation portrays final judgment in terms of fire and sulfur (Revelation 20:10–15).

Gen 19:24 Then the LORD rained down sulfur and fire…

Ezk 38:22 I will execute judgment upon him with plague and bloodshed.…

a scorching wind will be their portion.
A burning desert wind could wither vegetation, darken the sky, and bring devastation without relief. Such a wind depicts the severity of the judgment assigned to the wicked. Their portion stands in sharp contrast to the inheritance of the righteous, whose portion is the Lord Himself (Psalm 16:5). What sinners have chosen apart from God will become their appointed end, while those who trust Him find refuge under His righteous care.

Psalm 11:7

For the LORD is righteous;
The LORD’s character is morally perfect, utterly faithful, and unwavering in all His ways. He judges humanity according to truth and governs the world without corruption or partiality. Amid the violence and instability described in Psalm 11, David rests in the certainty that God remains on His throne and will deal rightly with evil.

He loves justice.
God actively upholds what is right and defends those harmed by wickedness. His law calls His people to reflect His character by practicing righteousness, protecting the vulnerable, and refusing oppression. Though injustice may appear to prosper for a time, the LORD will bring every work into judgment and establish His righteous order.

The upright will see His face.
Those made upright before God and walking in faithful obedience will dwell in His favor and presence. David’s hope reaches beyond present deliverance to the final blessing of God’s people: seeing Him face to face. This promise finds its fullest fulfillment when the redeemed behold God in the new creation (Revelation 22:4).
