Divine blessing was not sought for private prosperity alone, but so that God’s saving power would become known among the nations.

Psalm 67:2

that Your ways may be known on earth,
God called Israel to display His holiness, justice, and mercy before surrounding peoples. The nation’s obedience to His law was meant to reveal the character of the Lord who alone governs all the earth (Deuteronomy 4:6–8). Israel’s central location among ancient trade routes gave the nations repeated opportunity to hear of the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The prayer reaches beyond Israel’s welfare to the worldwide acknowledgment of God’s righteous rule.

Hab 2:14 For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory…

Your salvation among all nations.
God’s saving purpose has always included the nations, according to His promise that all families of the earth would be blessed through Abraham (Genesis 12:3). Deliverance granted to Israel pointed forward to the greater redemption accomplished by the Messiah. Jesus Christ brought salvation to Jew and Gentile alike, removing the dividing wall of hostility and forming one people through His cross (Ephesians 2:14–16). The psalm anticipates the command to make disciples of all nations and the future worship of a redeemed multitude from every people and language (Matthew 28:19–20; Revelation 7:9).

Psalm 67:3

Let the peoples praise You, O God;
God’s praise is not confined to Israel but summons every nation to acknowledge the Creator and King of all the earth. Israel’s worship was meant to display God’s saving power before the nations, fulfilling His promise that all families of the earth would be blessed through Abraham’s offspring (Genesis 12:3). The blessing of God’s salvation carries a missionary purpose: redeemed people proclaim His greatness so that others may know Him.

Through Christ, Gentiles who were once far from God are brought near and made fellow heirs of His promises (Ephesians 2:13–19). God will receive the universal worship that sin has resisted, and His house will stand as a house of prayer for all nations (Isaiah 56:7).

Psalm 67:6

The earth has yielded its harvest;
Israel’s harvests depended upon the Lord’s rain, seasons, and sustaining care. The ingathering of grain, wine, and oil brought thanksgiving before God, especially during the Feast of Tabernacles (Leviticus 23:39–43). Fruitful fields testified that the Creator remained faithful to His covenant order: “seedtime and harvest… shall not cease” (Genesis 8:22). The abundance also anticipates the psalm’s wider purpose, that God’s saving ways would be known among all nations. Material provision was never meant to terminate in Israel’s comfort but to lead the world to recognize and praise the Lord.

Lev 26:4 I will give you rains in their season, and the land will yield its produce, and the trees of the field will bear their fruit…

God, our God, blesses us.
The God who rules the whole earth had bound Himself personally to Israel as their covenant God. His blessing included fertile land and security, but it also called His people to grateful obedience and holy worship. Israel’s enjoyment of God’s goodness was to display His character before the nations. Every true blessing ultimately finds its fullness in Christ, through whom God grants redemption and every spiritual blessing to His people (Ephesians 1:3).

Psalm 67:7

God blesses us,
God’s favor rests on His people by covenant grace, supplying what they need and making His presence known among them. Israel’s harvest, peace, and security were tangible gifts from the Lord, yet they also pointed beyond material abundance to communion with the God who saves. The blessing promised to Abraham included the larger purpose that God would bless all families of the earth through his offspring.

Israel was never chosen merely for its own privilege.
