Hurry, O LORD, to Help Me!

Psalm 70:1

For the choirmaster.
The psalm was entrusted for public worship under the direction of Israel’s appointed musical leader. David’s urgent prayer became the prayer of the congregation, teaching God’s people to seek His help together in times of danger.

Psalm 70:2

May those who seek my life be ashamed and confounded;
David appeals to the Lord to overthrow those who pursue him with murderous intent. Saul’s repeated attempts on David’s life and the hostility of surrounding enemies showed that the king’s safety rested ultimately in God’s hand. Shame and confusion describe the collapse of evil plans under divine judgment, as those who oppose God’s anointed are exposed and defeated. The prayer does not arise from private revenge but from confidence that God judges wickedness and preserves His servant. Christ likewise endured the plots of hostile rulers, false witnesses, and betrayal, yet His resurrection openly triumphed over the powers arrayed against Him (Colossians 2:15).

may those who wish me harm be repelled and humiliated.
Those who delight in David’s downfall are asked to be driven back in disgrace. In Israel’s honor-based culture, public humiliation marked the failure of a cause and the loss of standing before the community. God’s protection reverses the designs of the wicked: the hunters become scattered, and those who scheme against the righteous bear the consequences of their own rebellion. The final victory belongs to Christ, whose death and resurrection defeated sin, death, and Satan, assuring all who seek the Lord that evil will not prevail.

Psalm 70:3

May those who say, “Aha, aha!”
David’s enemies rejoiced at the prospect of his downfall and openly mocked his distress. Their taunt echoes the scorn directed at the righteous throughout Scripture, including the derision endured by Christ at the cross (Matthew 27:39–44). Psalm 40:15 contains the same plea, showing that such opposition persisted in David’s life and foreshadowed the hostility of those who reject God’s Anointed.

retreat because of their shame.
David asks the Lord to reverse the triumph his enemies sought and to expose the evil of their schemes. Retreat in battle represented defeat and disgrace, fitting the fate of those who exalt themselves against God and His servants. The Lord ultimately humbles the proud while vindicating those who seek Him.

Psalm 70:4

May all who seek You rejoice and be glad in You;
Those who earnestly pursue the Lord find their joy in His presence, character, and saving work. David’s prayer reaches beyond his own distress to include all who seek God with a whole heart. The Lord promised Israel that those who searched for Him sincerely would find Him, and that promise finds its fullest assurance in Christ, who brings believers near to God. Such joy rests not on changing circumstances but on the faithfulness of the God who hears, delivers, and keeps His covenant.

may those who love Your salvation
God’s salvation includes His rescue of His people from danger, oppression, and sin. Israel remembered His mighty deliverance from Egypt, His preservation in the wilderness, and His restoration after exile. David knew personal deliverance from enemies, yet the salvation celebrated in the Psalms ultimately points to the redemption accomplished by Jesus Christ. Those who love God’s salvation cherish not merely its benefits but the Savior who has shown mercy and brought them into fellowship with Himself.

always say, “Let God be magnified!”
Continual praise is the fitting response to God’s saving grace. God is not made greater by human worship, but His greatness is publicly acknowledged when His people honor Him above their troubles, enemies, and achievements. David calls the redeemed to make God’s glory their constant testimony, joining the worship of all who recognize that salvation belongs to the Lord.

Psalm 70:5

But I am poor and needy;
David acknowledges his weakness before God while enemies seek his harm. His need includes distress, danger, and utter dependence upon the Lord. Such humility marks those who know that human strength cannot secure deliverance. Psalm 70 closely parallels Psalm 40:13–17, preserving David’s urgent cry for mercy.

hurry to me, O God.
David asks for immediate help because the danger is real and pressing. Urgent prayer does not deny God’s wisdom or timing; it lays desperate need before the One who alone can act. The cry recalls Psalm 22:19, where David calls upon the Lord to come quickly to his aid.

You are my help and my deliverer;
God is not merely a source of assistance but David’s personal rescuer. The Lord had delivered Israel from Egypt, preserved David through persecution, and repeatedly shown Himself faithful to His covenant people. Jesus Christ fulfills God’s saving purpose by delivering His people from sin, death, and the power of the enemy.
