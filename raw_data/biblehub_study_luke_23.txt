Preparation Day refers to the day before the Sabbath, which is Friday. In Jewish tradition, this day was used to prepare for the Sabbath, a day of rest, by completing tasks such as cooking and cleaning, as no work was permitted from sundown on Friday to sundown on Saturday. This context is crucial for understanding the urgency with which Jesus’ body was taken down from the cross and placed in the tomb. The Jewish leaders were eager to ensure that all was completed before the Sabbath began, as seen in John 19:31, where they requested the bodies not remain on the crosses. This highlights the importance of the Sabbath in Jewish culture and the adherence to the Law of Moses.

The women became eyewitnesses to the location where Jesus was buried. Joseph of Arimathea placed Jesus in his own new rock-hewn tomb, fulfilling the Scripture that the suffering Servant would be with a rich man in His death (Isaiah 53:9). Their knowledge of the tomb’s location would later confirm that the empty tomb was the very place where Jesus’ body had been laid.

They watched as Jesus’ body was carefully wrapped and laid in the tomb before the Sabbath began. The burial was necessarily hurried because evening approached, leaving the women unable to complete the customary preparation with spices and ointments. Luke 23:56

Then they returned to prepare spices and perfumes.
The women who had followed Jesus from Galilee had witnessed both His death and the location of His tomb. Their preparation of spices and perfumes showed devoted love and their intention to complete the customary honoring of His body after the Sabbath. Nicodemus had already brought myrrh and aloes for the burial (John 19:39–40), but the women prepared additional burial spices, expecting to return when the Sabbath restrictions had ended. Their grief did not turn them away from Christ; they remained faithful when His death appeared to have ended every hope.

And they rested on the Sabbath, according to the commandment.
Although they longed to serve Jesus’ body, the women obeyed the Fourth Commandment and ceased their work from Friday evening until Saturday evening (Exodus 20:8–11). Their obedience in sorrow reflected reverence for God’s law. Jesus’ body rested in the tomb throughout the Sabbath, completing His work of atonement, while His followers waited in darkness and grief. At dawn on the first day of the week, the women would return to the tomb and discover that death could not hold the crucified Messiah.
