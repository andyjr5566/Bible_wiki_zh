The Futility of Life

Ecclesiastes 6:1

There is another evil
Ecclesiastes identifies a grievous disorder in life rather than an act of moral wickedness. The fallen world often presents conditions that frustrate human hopes and expose the limits of earthly prosperity.

Ecc 5:13 There is a grievous evil I have seen under the sun:…

Ecc 5:16 This too is a grievous affliction: Exactly as a man is born,…

Gen 6:5 Then the LORD saw that the wickedness of man…

I have seen
Solomon records what his wisdom, wealth, and experience had taught him. His conclusions arise from close observation of life among people of every station.

Ecc 1:14 I have seen all the things that are done under the sun…

Ecc 8:9 All this I have seen, applying my mind to every deed…

Ecc 5:18 Here is what I have seen to be good and fitting:…

under the sun,
Life in the present world does not always reflect immediate justice or reward. Earthly circumstances must be understood in light of God’s sovereign rule and the final accountability that lies beyond this life.

Ecc 1:3 What does a man gain from all his labor,…

Ecc 1:9 What has been will be again, and what has been done…

Ecc 2:18 I hated all for which I had toiled under the sun,…

and it weighs heavily upon mankind:
The burden described in the following verse concerns wealth and honor without the ability to enjoy them. Possessions cannot satisfy the soul, secure life, or prevent another from receiving what a person has accumulated.

Ecc 3:10 I have seen the burden that God has laid…

Ecclesiastes 6:2

God gives a man riches, wealth, and honor,
The Lord is sovereign over material provision, status, and opportunity. Solomon received extraordinary riches and honor from God (1 Kings 3:13), and Job’s great possessions were likewise under God’s authority (Job 1:1–3). Human diligence has value, yet prosperity ultimately rests in God’s hand (Deuteronomy 8:18).

1Ch 29:12 Both riches and honor come from You, and You…

1Sa 2:7 The LORD sends poverty and wealth; He humbles and He exalts.

Pro 10:22 The blessing of the LORD enriches, and He…

so that he lacks nothing his heart desires;
Abundance can supply every outward longing without supplying inward peace. Possessions, influence, pleasure, and security cannot satisfy the soul apart from the God who gives them. The Lord is the Shepherd whose provision brings true contentment, not merely full storehouses (Psalm 23:1).

Psa 21:2 You have granted his heart’s desire and have not withheld…

Psa 37:4 Delight yourself in the LORD, and He will give…

Psa 145:16 You open Your hand and satisfy the desire…

but God does not allow him to enjoy them.
Enjoyment is itself a gift from God. Ecclesiastes repeatedly exposes the sorrow of possessing much while remaining unable to receive it with gratitude, peace, and faith (Ecclesiastes 2:26; 5:19). Wealth without the ability to enjoy God’s goodness becomes another burden rather than a blessing.

Ecc 5:19 Furthermore, God has given riches and wealth…

Ecc 2:24-25 Nothing is better for a man than to eat and drink…

1Ti 6:17 Instruct those who are rich in the present age…

Instead, a stranger will enjoy them.
Death, injustice, disaster, political change, or an undeserving heir may transfer a lifetime of labor to another. Solomon had already grieved over leaving the fruit of his work to one who had not labored for it (Ecclesiastes 2:18–19). Earthly treasures cannot be held permanently and should never become the foundation of hope (Matthew 6:19–21).

Deu 28:30-33 You will be pledged in marriage to a woman.…

Psa 49:10-11 For it is clear that wise men die, and the foolish…

Luk 12:20-21 But God said to him, ‘You fool! This very night…

This is futile and a grievous affliction.
A life centered on gaining and preserving wealth ends in deep sorrow when wealth cannot give joy or endure beyond its owner. The painful emptiness described here calls people to receive God’s gifts rightly, fear Him, and seek treasures that cannot be taken away.

Ecc 1:2 “Futility of futilities,” says the Teacher,…

Ecc 5:16 This too is a grievous affliction: Exactly as a man is born,…

Psa 39:5-6 You, indeed, have made my days as handbreadths,…

Ecclesiastes 6:3

A man may father a hundred children and live for many years;
Numerous children and long life were widely regarded as marks of God’s blessing, strength, and security. God promised Abraham and Jacob descendants beyond counting, and the patriarchs valued long lives as evidence of divine favor. Yet such outward blessings cannot give lasting meaning to a life alienated from God.

Psa 127:3-5 Children are indeed a heritage from the LORD…

Psa 128:3-6 Your wife will be like a fruitful vine, flourishing…

Job 42:16-17 After this, Job lived 140 years and saw his children…

yet no matter how long he lives, if he is unsatisfied with his prosperity
Wealth, family, and years cannot satisfy the soul when they become ends in themselves. Ecclesiastes repeatedly exposes the emptiness of seeking fulfillment in earthly gain. True wholeness comes from receiving God’s gifts with gratitude and fearing Him, rather than trusting possessions or achievements.

Pro 27:20 Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied; so the eyes of man…

1Ti 6:6-10 Of course, godliness with contentment is great gain.…

Luk 12:15-21 And he said to them, “Watch out! Guard yourselves…

and does not even receive a proper burial,
Burial customs in Israel honored the dead and preserved a person’s memory among family and community. To die without burial was a severe disgrace and could signify divine judgment, as seen in the warnings against wicked rulers. A life marked by abundance may still end in dishonor when its owner leaves no enduring testimony of faithfulness.

Jer 22:18-19 Therefore this is what the LORD says concerning Jehoiakim…

2Ch 21:19-20 This continued day after day until two full years…

Isa 14:19-20 But you are cast out of your grave like a rejected branch,…

I say that a stillborn child is better off than he.
The comparison is deliberately severe. A stillborn child does not experience the frustrations, emptiness, and final disgrace of a long life spent without satisfaction in God. The words echo Job’s grief and press the reader to recognize that mere existence, prosperity, and longevity are not the highest good; life finds its true purpose in the Lord.

Job 3:16 Or why was I not hidden like a stillborn child,…

Psa 58:8 Like a slug that dissolves in its slime,…

Job 10:18-19 Why then did You bring me from the womb?…

Ecclesiastes 6:4

For a stillborn child enters in futility
A child who dies before birth receives none of the earthly blessings commonly associated with life: labor, possessions, honor, family, or public remembrance. Ecclesiastes uses this painful image to expose the poverty of a long life filled with abundance but devoid of contentment and the ability to enjoy God’s gifts. The child’s brief existence appears futile from an earthly standpoint, yet the comparison chiefly emphasizes the tragedy of a person who lives many years without receiving life as a gift from God.

Job 3:16 Or why was I not hidden like a stillborn child,…

Psa 58:8 Like a slug that dissolves in its slime,…

Job 10:18-19 Why then did You bring me from the womb?…

and departs in darkness,
The stillborn child never sees the daylight or enters the visible world of human activity. Darkness portrays death’s hiddenness and the limits placed upon human understanding. Ecclesiastes repeatedly confronts the fact that wealth, longevity, and achievement cannot overcome death or secure lasting meaning. Life apart from reverent dependence on God remains unable to escape that darkness.

Job 10:21-22 before I go—never to return—to a land of darkness and gloom,…

Job 17:13 If I look for Sheol as my home, if I spread out my bed…

Psa 49:19 he will join the generation of his fathers…

and his name is shrouded in obscurity.
In Israel, a name represented personal identity, family continuity, and remembrance among later generations. A child who never receives public recognition leaves no earthly legacy. Yet even a celebrated name eventually fades from human memory, as Ecclesiastes observes elsewhere. Earthly remembrance cannot give permanent significance; only God’s knowledge and judgment endure beyond death.

Job 18:17 The memory of him perishes from the earth,…

Psa 109:13 May his descendants be cut off; may their name be blotted…

Psa 9:5-6 You have rebuked the nations; You have destroyed the wicked;…

Ecclesiastes 6:5

The child, though neither seeing the sun nor knowing anything,
This phrase refers to a stillborn child, a poignant image used by the author to convey the futility and vanity of life under the sun. In the ancient Near Eastern context, the sun often symbolized life, vitality, and the passage of time. The child, having never seen the sun, represents a life that never began in the earthly sense. This imagery is used to emphasize the idea that sometimes it is better not to have experienced the burdens and toils of life at all. The concept of “knowing” in biblical terms often implies experiential knowledge, which the child lacks, highlighting the innocence and absence of worldly suffering. This can be connected to Job 3:16, where Job laments his own birth, wishing he had been like a stillborn child who never saw the light of day.

Psa 58:8 Like a slug that dissolves in its slime,…

Job 3:16 Or why was I not hidden like a stillborn child,…

Job 10:18-19 Why then did You bring me from the womb?…

has more rest than that man,
The “man” in this context is someone who has lived a life full of toil and dissatisfaction, yet without finding true fulfillment or peace. The term “rest” here implies a state of peace and freedom from the struggles and vanities of life. In biblical literature, rest is often associated with the peace of God and the ultimate rest found in Him, as seen in Hebrews 4:9-10, which speaks of a Sabbath rest for the people of God. The comparison suggests that the stillborn child, despite never experiencing life, is in a state of rest that the man, burdened by life’s vanities, cannot achieve. This reflects the broader theme of Ecclesiastes, which questions the meaning of life and the pursuit of earthly achievements without a relationship with God. The passage challenges readers to consider the eternal perspective and the true source of rest and fulfillment, which is found in God alone.

Ecc 4:2-3 So I admired the dead, above the living,…

Job 3:17-19 There the wicked cease from raging, and there the weary…

Psa 49:19-20 he will join the generation of his fathers…

Ecclesiastes 6:6

even if he lives a thousand years twice over
A lifespan of two thousand years would surpass even Methuselah’s 969 years and represent the fullest imaginable measure of earthly longevity. Yet extended days cannot overcome the emptiness of a life deprived of God-given satisfaction. Time itself does not create meaning, and long life without gratitude, worship, and contentment only prolongs vanity.

Psa 90:4 For in Your sight a thousand years are but a day…

2Pe 3:8 Beloved, do not let this one thing escape your notice:…

Ecc 11:8 So if a man lives many years, let him rejoice in them all.…

but fails to enjoy his prosperity.
Wealth, honor, family, and accomplishment are gifts that cannot satisfy a person whom God has not enabled to receive them with joy. Ecclesiastes (Qoheleth, “the Preacher”) repeatedly exposes the sorrow of possessing much while remaining inwardly empty. Jesus likewise warned that life does not consist in the abundance of possessions (Luke 12:15). Prosperity becomes a burden rather than a blessing when it is separated from the fear of God.

Ecc 5:18-20 Here is what I have seen to be good and fitting:…

Ecc 2:24-25 Nothing is better for a man than to eat and drink…

1Ti 6:17 Instruct those who are rich in the present age…

Do not all go to the same place?
Death brings both the wealthy and the poor to the grave (Sheol, the abode of the dead), stripping away earthly distinctions and ending every attempt to secure lasting significance through possessions or years. The same reality confronts every generation: humanity returns to the dust, and each person stands before God. Ecclesiastes therefore directs the heart away from self-sufficient striving and toward reverent dependence on the Creator.

Ecc 3:19-20 For the fates of both men and beasts are the same:…

Gen 3:19 By the sweat of your brow you will eat your bread,…

Heb 9:27 Just as man is appointed to die once, and after that…

Ecclesiastes 6:7

All a man’s labor is for his mouth,
Human work is largely directed toward obtaining daily bread. In an agrarian society, families depended upon the produce of their fields, flocks, and trade for survival. The burden of labor recalls God’s judgment after the fall, when Adam would eat bread through painful toil (Genesis 3:17–19). Hunger drives people to work, as Proverbs 16:26 observes, yet even necessary labor cannot secure lasting gain or peace.

Pro 16:26 A worker’s appetite works for him because his hunger…

Gen 3:19 By the sweat of your brow you will eat your bread,…

2Th 3:10 For even while we were with you, we gave you this command:…

yet his appetite is never satisfied.
Food sustains the body, but human desire reaches beyond what material provision can satisfy. Ecclesiastes repeatedly exposes the limits of earthly pursuits: the eye is never satisfied with seeing, nor the ear with hearing (Ecclesiastes 1:8). The restless appetite of fallen humanity reveals a deeper spiritual need. Christ alone gives the enduring satisfaction that labor, wealth, pleasure, and possessions cannot provide; He is the bread of life, and whoever comes to Him will not hunger (John 6:35).

Ecc 1:8 All things are wearisome; the eye is not satisfied…

Pro 27:20 Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied; so the eyes of man…

Hab 2:5 and wealth indeed betrays him. He is an arrogant man…

Ecclesiastes 6:8

What advantage, then, has the wise man over the fool?
Wisdom is better than folly in many earthly matters, yet it cannot secure lasting satisfaction or escape death. Both the wise and the foolish share the frailty of life and stand before God (Ecclesiastes 2:14–16). Human wisdom reaches its proper end when it leads to the fear of God and obedience to His commandments (Ecclesiastes 12:13). Christ alone is the wisdom of God and the source of eternal life (1 Corinthians 1:30).

Ecc 2:13-16 And I saw that wisdom exceeds folly, just as…

Ecc 7:11-12 Wisdom, like an inheritance, is good…

Pro 14:8 The wisdom of the prudent is to discern his way…

What gain comes to the poor man who knows how to conduct himself before others?
Skill in dealing with people may improve a poor man’s circumstances, but it cannot satisfy the soul or guarantee lasting gain. Social ability, reputation, and outward success remain limited by the same restless desires that affect every person. God looks beyond appearance to the heart, calling His people to humility, righteousness, and trust in Him (1 Samuel 16:7; Matthew 6:19–21).

Ecc 4:13-15 Better is a poor but wise youth than an old but foolish king…

Pro 19:1 Better a poor man who walks with integrity…

Col 4:5-6 Act wisely toward outsiders, redeeming the time.…

Ecclesiastes 6:9

Better what the eye can see
Contentment receives God’s present gifts with gratitude rather than treating them as insufficient. In a world marked by uncertainty, the Preacher directs attention to what the Lord has actually provided: daily bread, work, relationships, and the ordinary mercies of life. Possessions cannot secure the future, but thankful enjoyment of God’s provision guards the heart from needless restlessness.

Ecc 4:6 Better one handful with tranquility than two handfuls…

Heb 13:5 Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content…

1Ti 6:6-8 Of course, godliness with contentment is great gain.…

than the wandering of desire.
Uncontrolled desire roams endlessly from one object to another, promising satisfaction but never arriving at it. Wealth, honor, pleasure, and power had been pursued by kings and nations throughout the ancient world, yet none could deliver lasting peace. Proverbs warns that human desire is never naturally filled, and James shows how sinful desire, when embraced, produces death. God calls His people to seek Him rather than to be ruled by ever-changing appetites.

1Jn 2:16 For all that is in the world—the desires of the flesh…

Pro 27:20 Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied; so the eyes of man…

Jas 1:14-15 But each one is tempted when by his own evil desires…

This too is futile
The pursuit of more cannot heal the deepest emptiness of the human heart. Earthly gain is temporary, and even abundant possessions cannot give a person control over life, death, or judgment. Only fellowship with God gives lasting meaning to the gifts He places in human hands.

Ecc 1:2 “Futility of futilities,” says the Teacher,…

Rom 8:20 For the creation was subjected to futility,…

and a pursuit of the wind.
Chasing fulfillment apart from God is like trying to grasp the wind: effort is expended, but nothing can be held. Jesus warned against laying up treasures that perish and directed His followers toward treasure in heaven. What is received from God with faith and contentment endures in significance; what is pursued as an ultimate end disappears.

Ecc 2:11 Yet when I considered the works my hands had accomplished…

Ecc 1:14 I have seen all the things that are done under the sun…

Ecc 2:17 So I hated life, because the work that is done…

Ecclesiastes 6:10

Whatever exists was named long ago,
God established the identity, place, and purpose of all created things before human beings could alter or control them. As Adam’s naming of the animals displayed delegated dominion under God, God’s prior naming displays His absolute rule over creation and history. Nothing falls outside His knowledge or His ordained purposes (Isaiah 46:9–10).

Psa 147:4 He determines the number of the stars;…

Isa 40:26 Lift up your eyes on high: Who created all these?…

Isa 46:10 I declare the end from the beginning, and from ancient times…

and it is known what man is;
Humanity is fully known by the Creator: formed from dust, fallen through sin, and unable to secure lasting satisfaction apart from God. The Lord knows the human heart, its weakness, and its need for mercy (Psalm 103:14; Jeremiah 17:9–10). Human limits are not discoveries that surprise God but realities present within His wise design and judgment.

Psa 144:3-4 O LORD, what is man, that You regard him…

Psa 8:4-5 what is man that You are mindful of him?…

Psa 103:14 For He knows our frame; He is mindful that we are dust.

but he cannot contend with one stronger than he.
No person can successfully dispute God’s wisdom, resist His judgment, or overturn His purposes. Job learned that God is mighty in strength and cannot be challenged without ruin (Job 9:4). Ecclesiastes calls the weary and dissatisfied heart away from striving with God and toward humble submission, trusting the Lord whose authority will finally be acknowledged by every creature (Philippians 2:10–11).

Job 40:2 Will the faultfinder contend with the Almighty?…

Isa 45:9 Woe to him who quarrels with his Maker—one clay pot…

Rom 9:20 But who are you, O man, to talk back to God?…

Ecclesiastes 6:11

For the more words, the more futility—
Human argument cannot overturn God’s ordering of life or remove the limits He has placed on mankind. Endless speculation, complaint, and self-justification only multiply emptiness when they seek to contend with the Creator. Wisdom is shown in humble submission to God, whose purposes remain sure even when human understanding is limited.

Pro 10:19 When words are many, sin is unavoidable,…

Pro 17:27 A man of knowledge restrains his words, and a man…

Ecc 5:2 Do not be quick to speak, and do not be hasty…

and how does that profit anyone?
No lasting gain comes from words that resist God’s truth or attempt to secure meaning apart from Him. Ecclesiastes repeatedly asks what advantage remains from human striving under the sun, directing attention beyond temporary possessions, achievements, and debates. Jesus likewise warned that gaining the world is no profit if a person loses his soul. True profit is found in fearing God, receiving His gifts with gratitude, and living in obedience before Him.

Pro 14:23 There is profit in all labor, but mere talk…

Ecc 1:3 What does a man gain from all his labor,…

Ecc 2:11 Yet when I considered the works my hands had accomplished…

Ecclesiastes 6:12

For who knows what is good for a man
Human judgment cannot finally determine what brings lasting profit, satisfaction, or righteousness. Ecclesiastes, meaning “Preacher,” exposes the limits of wealth, pleasure, achievement, and reputation, directing the reader to receive life as God’s gift and to fear Him.

Psa 34:12 Who is the man who delights in life, …

Psa 4:6 Many ask, “Who can show us the good?” Shine the light…

Deu 30:15 See, I have set before you today life and prosperity,…

during the few days
Earthly life is brief even when measured in decades. Moses similarly taught that human years quickly pass away, calling God’s people to number their days and gain a heart of wisdom (Psalm 90:10, 12).

Job 14:1 “Man, who is born of woman, is short of days…”…

Psa 39:5 You, indeed, have made my days as handbreadths,…

Psa 90:10 The length of our days is seventy years—or eighty…

in which he passes through his fleeting life like a shadow?
A shadow has no permanence and disappears with changing light. Job and the psalmists also describe humanity in such terms, contrasting man’s frailty with the enduring faithfulness of God (Job 8:9; Psalm 144:4). Such brevity calls for trust in the Lord rather than devotion to possessions and pleasures that cannot endure.

Psa 144:4 Man is like a breath; his days are like a passing shadow.

Job 8:9 For we were born yesterday and know nothing;…

1Ch 29:15 For we are foreigners and strangers in Your presence,…

Who can tell a man what will come after him
No one possesses independent knowledge of what lies beyond his life or of the final outcome of his labor. God alone declares the end from the beginning (Isaiah 46:9–10). The uncertainty of inheritance, family, legacy, and future events humbles human ambition and summons faith in God’s providence.

Jas 4:14-15 You do not even know what will happen tomorrow!…

Pro 27:1 Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know…

Psa 146:4 When his spirit departs, he returns to the ground;…

under the sun?
Life viewed only under the sun—the earthly, temporal realm apart from God—appears elusive and often frustrating. God’s revelation lifts the believer beyond what is seen, setting hope on eternal realities and on the judgment by which God will bring every deed into account (Ecclesiastes 12:13–14; Colossians 3:1–2).

2Co 4:18 So we fix our eyes not on what is seen but on what is unseen…

Col 3:2 Set your minds on things above, not on earthly things.
