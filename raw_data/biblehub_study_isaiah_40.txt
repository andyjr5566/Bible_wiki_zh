Prepare the Way for the LORD

Isaiah 40:1

“Comfort, comfort My people,”
Isaiah turns from the announced Babylonian captivity of Isaiah 39 to God’s promised restoration. The doubled command reveals God’s determined compassion toward Israel after the discipline required by their sin. Though exile would bring loss, humiliation, and longing for Jerusalem, it would not cancel the covenant God made with His people. The Lord summons His servants to proclaim consolation grounded in His own coming deliverance. The return from Babylon would demonstrate His faithfulness, while the larger hope of Isaiah’s message finds its fulfillment in Jesus Christ, who brings forgiveness, reconciliation, and rest to all who come to Him.

says your God.
God identifies Himself as the God who remains personally bound to Israel. Exile would not mean that He had abandoned the descendants of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob or surrendered His purposes for them. His authority guarantees the promise, because the comfort proclaimed rests on His character rather than on Israel’s strength or deserving. The same covenant-keeping God who judges sin also provides redemption, proving faithful to His word across generations.

Isaiah 40:12

The seas, rivers, depths, and springs that appear boundless to mankind are fully known and governed by the Creator. Waters often represent the untamed forces of creation, yet God set their boundaries and commands them. The Spirit of God moved over the waters before He brought light and order to the world (Genesis 1:2). His hand sustains what human strength cannot contain.

He stretched out the heavens and appointed the courses of the sun, moon, and stars. Their glory declares His handiwork (Psalm 19:1), but their greatness also exposes the limits of human knowledge. The God who formed them does not need counsel from any creature.

Isaiah 40:14

God’s knowledge is neither acquired nor dependent on any creature. Isaiah confronts every attempt to reduce the Lord to the level of human rulers, who require counselors, experts, and accumulated experience. The God who measured the waters, marked off the heavens, and called the nations into being possesses perfect wisdom within Himself. Job likewise asks, “Can anyone teach God knowledge?” (Job 21:22). Creation itself testifies that God acted alone, speaking all things into existence by His word.

Idols reveal humanity’s attempt to make deity manageable, but they are lifeless works of human hands. God later made Himself known perfectly in Jesus Christ, the image of the invisible God, who reveals the Father without reducing His glory to an idol (Colossians 1:15).
