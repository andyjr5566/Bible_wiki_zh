Psalm 88:1

A song. A Psalm of the sons of Korah.
The sons of Korah descended from the Levite who rebelled against Moses, yet Korah’s descendants did not perish with him (Numbers 26:11). They later served as temple gatekeepers and musicians, displaying God’s mercy across generations. Their worship ministry joined personal sorrow to the gathered praise of Israel.

Though Psalm 88 is marked by darkness and unanswered anguish, the sufferer still directs his plea to the God of his salvation.

The Pit evokes Sheol, the grave, where human power and earthly hope appear to end. Job also spoke of descending to the Pit in anguish (Job 17:16), and Hezekiah mourned that the dead no longer praise God among the living (Isaiah 38:18). Yet Christ truly entered death and was not abandoned to the grave; His resurrection establishes hope for all who trust in Him (Acts 2:27).

David voiced a similar cry in Psalm 22:1, words later taken upon Christ during His crucifixion (Matthew 27:46). Jesus bore the judgment sin deserved so that all who belong to Him would never be finally cast away.
