Freedom for Hebrew Slaves

Jeremiah 34:8

After King Zedekiah had made a covenant with all the people in Jerusalem to proclaim liberty,
Zedekiah required the people to free their Hebrew slaves in obedience to the law of Moses. Every Israelite servant was to be released after six years of service, remembering that the Lord had redeemed Israel from slavery in Egypt (Exodus 21:2; Deuteronomy 15:12–15). The proclamation of liberty also recalled the Jubilee pattern, in which God declared freedom and restoration among His covenant people (Leviticus 25:10). With Babylon’s army threatening Jerusalem, Judah publicly acknowledged an obligation it had long neglected. The covenant called the nation to practice the justice and mercy that belonged to a people redeemed by God.

Jeremiah 34:20

I will deliver into the hands of their enemies who seek their lives.
The Lord announced judgment upon Zedekiah, Judah’s leaders, and the people who had sworn before Him to free their Hebrew servants but then forced them back into slavery. Their deliberate reversal of a covenant oath showed contempt for God’s authority and for the mercy required by His law. Babylon, under Nebuchadnezzar, would become the instrument of that judgment when Jerusalem fell. The warning fulfilled the covenant curses of Deuteronomy 28:25, where rebellion against the Lord results in defeat before one’s enemies. God’s patience did not cancel His justice; those who treated their solemn promises lightly would face the consequences of covenant unfaithfulness.
