John 6:16

The phrase “when evening came” indicates the time of day, which is significant in the context of Jewish customs. Evening marks the beginning of a new day in Jewish tradition, as days are counted from sunset to sunset. This timing is also important in the narrative structure of the Gospel of John, as it often highlights transitions and significant events occurring at night or in the dark, symbolizing spiritual darkness or misunderstanding.

The sea is a place of both livelihood and testing for the disciples, as seen in various Gospel accounts. This setting also foreshadows the upcoming miracle of Jesus walking on water, demonstrating His authority over nature and His divine identity.

Judas’s coming betrayal fulfilled Scripture concerning the treachery of a close companion and revealed that outward association with Christ does not guarantee a believing heart.

For although Judas was one of the Twelve,
Judas had been chosen to accompany Jesus, witness His works, hear His teaching, and share in the ministry entrusted to the apostles. Outward association with Christ and participation in religious service did not produce true faith in Judas’s heart. His place among the Twelve made his betrayal a grievous act of treachery.

he was later to betray Jesus.
Judas’s betrayal fulfilled what Scripture had foretold concerning a close companion who would turn against the Lord (Psalm 41:9; John 13:18). God’s foreknowledge and prophetic purpose did not remove Judas’s responsibility for his deliberate sin. His betrayal led to Jesus’ arrest and crucifixion, through which Christ willingly gave Himself for the redemption of sinners.
