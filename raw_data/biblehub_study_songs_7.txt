Admiration by the Bridegroom

Song of Solomon 7:1

How beautiful are your sandaled feet, O daughter of the prince!
The bridegroom begins with admiration from her feet upward, honoring both her grace and her noble bearing. Sandals suited joyful procession, travel, and public dignity in ancient Israel. “Daughter of the prince” presents her as a woman of recognized honor, worthy of the king’s delight. Psalm 45 similarly portrays a royal bride whose beauty and splendor reflect the favor bestowed upon her.

The curves of your thighs are like jewels,
Her form is compared to precious ornaments, displaying beauty, value, and harmony. Within the safety of marriage, the bridegroom’s praise celebrates the goodness of the body God created and the exclusive delight of covenant love. The imagery also recalls the preciousness of God’s people, whom He calls His treasured possession.

The comparison points to the work of a skilled artisan shaping an exquisite jewel. Her beauty is neither trivial nor accidental, but bears witness to the wisdom of the Creator, whose works are marvelous. God fashions His people with purpose and continues His sanctifying work, preparing them for faithful love and holy fellowship.

Song of Solomon 7:5

Your head crowns you like Mount Carmel,
Mount Carmel rose prominently above the coastal plain of Israel, renowned for its fruitful slopes and commanding beauty. Elijah’s victory over the prophets of Baal on Carmel also marked the mountain as a place where the living God displayed His power and vindicated His name (1 Kings 18:19–39). The bride’s bearing possesses similar dignity, beauty, and prominence. Her loveliness is not merely physical; it reflects honor worthy of the king’s delight.

the hair of your head like purple threads;
Purple garments and fabrics belonged especially to kings, rulers, and the wealthy because purple dye was costly and difficult to obtain. The bride’s flowing hair is pictured with royal splendor, fitting the kingly setting of the song. Her beauty is precious rather than common, and her appearance corresponds to the honor bestowed on her by her beloved.

Solomon is so taken by the bride’s beauty that her locks hold his attention as though he were bound by them. Love gladly yields to the beloved’s beauty and devotion. The king’s captivated affection portrays the delight of covenant love, in which faithful love is neither cold nor distant but warmly expressed. The Lord’s redeeming love for His people likewise gives them dignity, beauty, and a place of cherished fellowship with Him.

Song of Solomon 7:6

How fair and pleasant you are,
The husband delights in the beauty and loveliness of his bride, praising her without shame or restraint. Their mutual love honors God’s design for marriage, in which husband and wife receive one another with gratitude and joy. Human beauty, rightly cherished within covenant faithfulness, reflects the goodness of the Creator, who declared His creation very good (Genesis 1:31).

Song of Solomon 7:7

Your stature is like a palm tree;
The bride’s bearing is likened to the palm’s upright beauty, strength, and fruitfulness. Palm trees flourished even in dry regions, providing shade, food, and visible evidence of life in barren places. Scripture also associates the palm with the flourishing righteous (Psalm 92:12). Solomon’s praise honors the beloved as a woman of dignity and vitality within the covenant joy of marriage.

The image continues the comparison to a fruitful palm, celebrating the bride’s beauty and capacity for nurture. Fruitfulness represented divine blessing and abundance in Israel’s agrarian world, recalling the Lord’s provision in the land flowing with milk and honey (Exodus 3:8). Marital love receives such physical delight as a good gift from God, joining beauty, faithfulness, and the hope of life-giving blessing.

His words express the delight and intimacy proper to the covenant bond of marriage. Scripture also uses the flourishing palm to portray the righteous life rooted in the Lord (Psalm 92:12), reminding God’s people that true fruitfulness comes from Him.

The bride’s physical beauty is honored without shame, presenting marital love as part of God’s good creation. The vine later becomes an important image for Israel and for Christ, the true vine whose people bear lasting fruit through abiding in Him (John 15:1–5).

Marital love is presented as honorable delight rather than shame, reflecting the goodness of God’s design for husband and wife.

Song of Solomon 7:10

I belong to my beloved,
The bride rejoices in the security of a faithful covenant bond. Her identity is not marked by possession without love, but by mutual devotion within marriage. Song of Solomon portrays the goodness of marital love as God intended it, marked by exclusivity, delight, and trust. The bride’s words echo Song of Solomon 6:3 and anticipate the covenant confidence of believers who belong to Christ.

The language stands in contrast to the disorder introduced by sin in Genesis 3:16, displaying love that seeks the good of the other rather than control or conflict. A husband’s devoted love reflects, in its proper measure, Christ’s sacrificial love for His Church, whom He has redeemed and desires to bring into His glory.
