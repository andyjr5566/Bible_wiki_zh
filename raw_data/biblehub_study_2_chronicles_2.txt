Preparations for the Temple

2 Chronicles 2:1

Now Solomon purposed to build a house for the Name of the LORD
Solomon began the work God had promised David that his son would accomplish (2 Samuel 7:12–13). David had gathered materials and received the pattern for the temple, but the LORD appointed Solomon, a man of peace, to build it (1 Chronicles 22:8–10). The temple would house the ark of the covenant and serve as Israel’s appointed center of sacrifice, prayer, and worship. God’s Name resting there declared His covenant presence with His people, though heaven itself could not contain Him (1 Kings 8:27–29). Solomon’s purpose revealed obedience to the charge entrusted to him and placed the worship of the LORD at the heart of Israel’s national life.

and a royal palace for himself.
Solomon also prepared to establish the royal household and administration needed to govern the kingdom. His palace complex, later described in 1 Kings 7, included halls for judgment, official business, and the king’s residence. The paired building projects showed that Israel’s king was to exercise real authority while remaining subject to the LORD whose house stood foremost. The palace displayed the stability and prosperity granted during Solomon’s reign, but the temple bore greater significance, reminding king and people alike that Israel belonged to God.

2 Chronicles 2:8

Send me also cedar, cypress, and algum logs from Lebanon,
Lebanon’s forests supplied timber prized throughout the ancient world for strength, beauty, and durability. Cedar and cypress were especially suited for the temple and royal buildings because they resisted decay and conveyed honor and permanence. Solomon sought the finest materials available for the house dedicated to the Lord, following the pattern of excellence established in David’s preparations (1 Chronicles 22:2–5). Algum wood, likely an imported luxury hardwood, was later used for supports, stairs, and musical instruments in connection with the temple (2 Chronicles 9:10–11).

2 Chronicles 2:17

Solomon numbered all the foreign men in the land of Israel
Solomon counted the resident non-Israelites living throughout the land, following David’s earlier preparation for the Temple. Many were descendants of the Canaanite peoples who remained in Israel and were assigned labor obligations under Israel’s kings. Solomon organized them for the demanding work of quarrying stone, transporting materials, and constructing the Temple and royal buildings. Israelite men were not reduced to forced labor but served as soldiers, officials, and overseers.
