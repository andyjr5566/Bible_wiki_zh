Hezekiah Shows His Treasures

Isaiah 39:1

At that time
The Babylonian embassy arrived during the period following Hezekiah’s illness and recovery, when Judah remained under the shadow of Assyrian power. God’s restoration of Hezekiah had become known beyond Judah and drew the attention of distant rulers.

2Ki 20:1 In those days Hezekiah became mortally ill.…

Isa 38:1 In those days Hezekiah became mortally ill.…

Merodach-baladan son of Baladan king of Babylon
Merodach-baladan was a Chaldean ruler of Babylon who repeatedly resisted Assyrian control. His name honored Marduk, Babylon’s chief god, and his rule reflected Babylon’s growing ambition to challenge Assyria. He sought allies wherever opposition to Assyria could be found.

2Ki 20:12 At that time Merodach-baladan sent letters and a gift…

2Ch 32:31 And so when ambassadors of the rulers of Babylon were sent…

sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah,
Royal letters and gifts commonly opened diplomatic negotiations and expressed a desire for friendship or alliance. Merodach-baladan’s embassy likely carried political purposes as well as goodwill, inviting Hezekiah to look toward Babylon for security against Assyria. The encounter exposed a temptation for Judah to trust foreign strength rather than the Lord who had delivered Jerusalem.

for he had heard about Hezekiah’s illness and recovery.
Hezekiah’s recovery had been granted by the Lord in response to prayer and confirmed by a miraculous sign. News of such an extraordinary event spread through the surrounding nations, making Hezekiah a ruler of unusual interest. What should have led Judah to magnify God before the nations would soon reveal Hezekiah’s vulnerability to pride.

Isaiah 39:2

And Hezekiah welcomed the envoys gladly
Fresh from deliverance from Assyria and recovery from mortal illness, Hezekiah received Babylon’s representatives with apparent pleasure. Merodach-Baladan of Babylon was seeking support against Assyria, and Hezekiah may have viewed the visit as an opportunity for political alliance. Yet 2 Chronicles 32:31 reveals that God allowed this encounter to test Hezekiah’s heart. The king who had formerly sought the Lord in crisis now acted from pride and self-confidence.

2Ki 20:12 At that time Merodach-baladan sent letters and a gift…

2Ch 32:31 And so when ambassadors of the rulers of Babylon were sent…

and showed them what was in his treasure house—the silver, the gold, the spices, and the precious oil,
Hezekiah displayed the resources God had granted Judah rather than giving God the glory for them. The royal treasures, including valuable metals, imported spices, and costly oils, testified to Judah’s prosperity and to the Lord’s blessing. By exposing them to Babylonian officials, Hezekiah gave a future enemy knowledge of the wealth it would later carry away.

2Ki 20:13 And Hezekiah received the envoys and showed them…

1Ki 10:10 Then she gave the king 120 talents of gold,…

as well as his entire armory—all that was found in his storehouses.
The king also revealed Judah’s military resources. Whether he hoped to impress Babylon or strengthen a proposed alliance, Hezekiah treated human wealth and military strength as grounds for security. Judah’s preservation had not come through its armory but through the Lord’s intervention against Sennacherib.

2Ch 32:27 Hezekiah had very great riches and honor…

2Ch 32:28 He also made storehouses for the harvest of grain…

There was nothing in his palace or in all his dominion that Hezekiah did not show them.
Hezekiah withheld nothing from the Babylonian envoys, displaying both personal and national resources without discernment. Isaiah would soon declare that all these treasures would be carried to Babylon, along with some of Hezekiah’s descendants. The event warns that pride can turn God-given blessings into occasions for spiritual failure when His glory is forgotten.

Isaiah confronted the king after Babylonian envoys had seen the treasures of Judah. Although Hezekiah had faithfully sought the Lord during his illness, his response to Babylon’s visit revealed pride and a dangerous openness to political dependence on a foreign power.

The envoys’ interest in Judah’s wealth and strength carried consequences far beyond a courteous diplomatic visit.

Isaiah 39:4

“What have they seen in your palace?” Isaiah asked.
Isaiah confronted Hezekiah with the implications of his hospitality toward the Babylonian envoys. The royal palace housed the wealth of Judah, including treasures dedicated to the Lord and resources essential to the nation’s security. By asking what the visitors had seen, Isaiah exposed the king’s failure to guard what God had entrusted to him. Babylon was still rising in power, but its interest in Judah’s wealth would eventually become conquest.

2Ki 20:15 “What have they seen in your palace?” Isaiah asked.…

2Ch 32:31 And so when ambassadors of the rulers of Babylon were sent…

“They have seen everything in my palace,” answered Hezekiah.
Hezekiah’s answer reveals neither caution nor repentance. Having been healed by God and delivered from Assyria, he had received an opportunity to testify to the Lord’s greatness before foreign representatives. Instead, he displayed his own riches and military resources. His confidence had shifted from humble dependence upon God to satisfaction in the visible prosperity of his kingdom.

2Ki 20:13 And Hezekiah received the envoys and showed them…

2Ki 20:14 Then the prophet Isaiah went to King Hezekiah…

“There is nothing among my treasures that I did not show them.”
Hezekiah withheld nothing from Babylon, exposing the full wealth and strength of Judah to a future enemy. The treasures included silver, gold, spices, weapons, and valuables connected with the royal and national life of Jerusalem. Isaiah would immediately announce that these very possessions, along with members of Hezekiah’s royal line, would one day be carried to Babylon. The event warns that God’s gifts are to be received with gratitude and guarded with wisdom, not used to exalt human achievement.

Isaiah 39:5

Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah,
Isaiah served Hezekiah as God’s prophet during critical years for Judah. After the king showed Babylonian envoys the treasures of his palace and kingdom, Isaiah confronted him with the coming consequences. Hezekiah’s earlier faith during the Assyrian threat did not remove the need for humility and obedience after God had granted him healing and deliverance.

2Ki 20:16 Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah, “Hear the word of the LORD:

“Hear the word of the LORD of Hosts:
The LORD of Hosts rules over heaven’s armies and every earthly kingdom, including Judah, Assyria, and Babylon. Isaiah’s announcement carried divine authority rather than personal counsel. God would use Babylon, then still rising in power, to judge Judah for its persistent sin and covenant unfaithfulness. The warning prepared the way for the later exile, while also confirming that Judah’s future remained under the sovereign rule of the God who judges and restores His people.

But Hezekiah said to Isaiah,
Hezekiah answered the prophet after hearing that Babylon would one day carry Judah’s treasures and royal descendants into exile. The prediction followed Hezekiah’s prideful display of Judah’s wealth to Babylonian envoys, revealing how a moment of self-exaltation could bear consequences beyond his own reign.

“The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good.”
Hezekiah recognized that Isaiah had spoken God’s true and righteous judgment. His response resembles Eli’s submission to God’s hard word in 1 Samuel 3:18. God’s judgments remain good because they are holy, even when they expose sin and bring painful consequences. Chronicles records that Hezekiah humbled himself after his pride, and God delayed His wrath during Hezekiah’s lifetime (2 Chronicles 32:25–26).
