Psalm 45:2

You are the most handsome of men;
The psalmist celebrates the king’s royal dignity, strength, and God-given splendor on the occasion of his wedding. In Israel, outward bearing could reflect fitness for leadership, as David’s appearance accompanied the Lord’s choice of him (1 Samuel 16:12). The king’s beauty points beyond physical features to the attractiveness of righteous rule, courage, wisdom, and devotion to God. The royal portrait finds its fullest fulfillment in Christ, the true King. Isaiah 53:2 shows that Jesus did not draw men through merely outward impressiveness, yet His holiness, mercy, and authority reveal a beauty greater than human appearance.

Divine favor marks the king’s speech, enabling him to rule with truth, justice, and wisdom. A king in Israel was responsible to speak according to God’s law and to defend the oppressed rather than flatter the powerful. Jesus perfectly fulfills this calling. Those who heard Him recognized the grace of His words (Luke 4:22), and He came full of grace and truth (John 1:14). His teaching reveals the Father’s will, exposes sin, comforts the humble, and summons all people to repentance and faith.

His victory is accomplished by divine truth and authority, and believers are equipped with the sword of the Spirit, the Word of God (Ephesians 6:17).

Psalm 45:8

All your garments are fragrant with myrrh and aloes and cassia;
Costly spices mark the king’s royal splendor and consecration. Myrrh belonged to the gifts presented to the child Jesus by the Magi and later appeared in connection with His burial, joining royal honor with the suffering through which He redeemed His people. Cassia was included in the holy anointing oil prescribed for the tabernacle, while aloes were used in preparations for burial, including the burial of Jesus. The fragrant garments portray a king surrounded by holiness, joy, and the favor of God.
