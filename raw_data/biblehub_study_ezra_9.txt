Israel’s return from exile had displayed God’s mercy, yet renewed compromise threatened to repeat the rebellion that had brought judgment. God had commanded His people to remain separate from the idolatry of Canaan, since covenant unfaithfulness would corrupt worship and destroy the nation’s witness (Exodus 34:12–16; Deuteronomy 7:1–4). Ezra recognizes that deliberate disobedience after such grace would be especially grievous.

Neh 9:29 You admonished them to turn back to Your law,…

Psa 78:10 They failed to keep God’s covenant and refused…

and intermarry with the peoples who commit these abominations?
The concern was not racial or national superiority, but the spiritual danger of binding Israel’s covenant households to peoples devoted to false gods and detestable practices. Israel’s history had already shown the danger when Solomon’s foreign wives turned his heart toward idols (1 Kings 11:1–8). The restored community needed to preserve faithful worship of the Lord so that the promised line, temple service, and knowledge of God would not again be swallowed by pagan religion.

Deu 7:3-4 Do not intermarry with them. Do not give your daughters…

Exo 34:15-16 Do not make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land,…

Would You not become so angry with us
Ezra appeals to God as the righteous covenant Judge. Divine anger is God’s holy response to evil, especially when His redeemed people, Israel (meaning “one who struggles with God”), knowingly reject His commands. The exile had proved that God’s warnings were not empty; persistent rebellion brings real discipline, even upon those who possess His law and have experienced His deliverance.

Deu 6:14-15 Do not follow other gods, the gods of the peoples…

Deu 29:24-28 So all the nations will ask, ‘Why has the LORD……

as to wipe us out, leaving no remnant or survivor?
The small community in Judah was itself a remnant preserved by God after the Babylonian captivity. Ezra fears that continued rebellion could bring complete ruin, leaving no surviving people through whom God’s covenant purposes might continue. His prayer rests on the sobering reality that Israel deserved judgment, while also acknowledging that every remaining hope depended upon the mercy of the Lord.

Ezra 9:15

O LORD, God of Israel, You are righteous!
Ezra begins with God’s character rather than Israel’s failure. The Lord had judged Judah for persistent rebellion, yet He had also preserved His covenant people and opened the way for their return from Babylon. His discipline was just, and His mercy was undeserved.

Psa 119:137 Righteous are You, O LORD, and upright are Your judgments.

Dan 9:14 Therefore the LORD has kept the calamity in store……

Deu 32:4 He is the Rock, His work is perfect; all His ways are just.…

For we remain this day as a remnant.
The returned exiles were a small surviving portion of Israel, preserved by God’s faithfulness after the exile. Their existence in Jerusalem, the rebuilt temple, and the renewed worship of the Lord testified that God had not abandoned His promises to Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and David. Yet their survival placed them under a renewed obligation to live as a holy people.

Isa 10:20-22 On that day the remnant of Israel and the survivors…

Neh 1:3 And they told me, “The remnant who survived the exile…

2Ki 19:30-31 And the surviving remnant of the house of Judah…

Here we are before You in our guilt,
Ezra does not distance himself from the sin of the people but confesses, “our guilt.” Marriages with the surrounding pagan peoples endangered Israel’s covenant identity and exposed families to the idolatry from which the exile had delivered them. Confession brought the nation honestly before the holy God whose commands they had violated.

Dan 9:8-9 O LORD, we are covered with shame—our kings, our leaders…

Lev 26:40-42 But if they will confess their iniquity…

Psa 51:3-4 For I know my transgressions, and my sin…

though because of it no one can stand before You.”
Israel had no defense before the Lord and no claim upon His favor. Sin leaves people unable to stand before God on the basis of their own righteousness. Ezra’s prayer rests wholly upon divine mercy, anticipating the grace by which God provides forgiveness and grants His people acceptance before Him.
