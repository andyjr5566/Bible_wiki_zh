Ezekiel received God’s authoritative revelation among the exiles in Babylon. Ezekiel 25 begins judgments against Ammon, Moab, Edom, and Philistia, neighboring peoples who rejoiced over Judah’s destruction and acted with hostility toward God’s covenant people. The Lord’s message shows that He governs every nation and holds all peoples accountable for pride, violence, malice, and contempt for His purposes. The divine word given through the prophets anticipates God’s fullest self-disclosure in Jesus Christ, the eternal Word made flesh.

‘Because Edom acted vengefully against the house of Judah,
Edom descended from Esau, Jacob’s brother, and its hostility toward Judah reflected a long history of conflict between the two related peoples. When Babylon destroyed Jerusalem in 586 BC, Edom rejoiced over Judah’s calamity, exploited fleeing survivors, and joined in violence against them (Psalm 137:7; Obadiah 10–14). God regarded Edom’s attack on Judah not merely as political opportunism but as treachery against a brother nation in its hour of devastation.

The Lord held Edom accountable for taking advantage of Judah’s judgment rather than fearing God and showing compassion. Amos likewise records God’s condemnation of Edom for pursuing his brother with the sword and maintaining anger without mercy (Amos 1:11–12).

The Lord’s judgment reached beyond the centers of power to every remaining refuge, demonstrating that rebellion against Him ultimately brings no safe haven.

God promised to judge the Philistines for their longstanding hostility toward Israel and their desire to destroy Judah. Their violence against God’s covenant people was also defiance of the God who had chosen and protected them. The judgment would come through historical instruments, yet the sentence belonged to the Lord Himself.
