Retribution for Israel’s Sin

Hosea 10:1

Israel was a luxuriant vine, yielding fruit for himself.
God had planted and nurtured Israel as His covenant people, expecting the fruit of faithfulness, justice, and holy worship. Instead, the nation used God’s gifts for its own desires. Abundance did not produce gratitude or obedience, but spiritual pride and independence from the Lord.

The more his fruit increased, the more he increased the altars.
Prosperity became an occasion for multiplying places of idolatrous worship. Rather than remembering the God who gave the increase, Israel credited and served the Baals, believing fertility and harvests came through pagan gods. The nation’s outward wealth exposed an inward rebellion against the Lord.

Hos 8:11 Though Ephraim multiplied the altars for sin,…

The better his land produced, the better he made the sacred pillars.
Israel adorned and strengthened the religious symbols forbidden by God, adopting practices associated with the Canaanite peoples. The land’s fruit, given through the Lord’s covenant mercy, was redirected toward false worship. Israel’s prosperity therefore became evidence against them, for they used the blessings of God to deepen their unfaithfulness to Him.

When Assyrian judgment approached, the people’s confidence in their idol collapsed. The calf they trusted for security, fertility, and national stability could not save them.
