in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.
Ophrah was Gideon’s hometown in the territory of Manasseh and the setting of his earliest acts of obedience. There he tore down Baal’s altar, received the name Jerubbaal, and began the work that led to Israel’s rescue. Gideon came from the relatively small clan of Abiezer, yet the Lord raised him from that humble setting to lead the nation. His burial in Ophrah returned him to the place where God first called him, reminding Israel that the Lord can accomplish His purposes through those who depend on Him.

Israel treated its covenant relationship with the LORD as disposable, pursuing the Canaanite gods instead. and they set up Baal-berith as their god.
At Shechem, Israel formally embraced Baal-berith, “lord of the covenant,” meaning “Lord of the Covenant,” replacing the true God of the covenant with a false god. The choice was especially grievous because Shechem had been associated with covenant renewal in Israel’s history, including Joshua’s charge to serve the LORD alone. Israel’s later alliance with Baal-berith reveals the depth of its rebellion and prepares for the violence and judgment that would unfold through Abimelech in Shechem.

Judges 8:35

They did not show kindness to the house of Jerubbaal
After Gideon’s death, Israel quickly forgot both the Lord who had delivered them and the judge through whom He had brought that deliverance. Their lack of loyalty toward Gideon’s household soon appeared in Abimelech’s murder of his seventy brothers, with only Jotham escaping. Gideon had torn down Baal’s altar and received the name Jerubbaal, “Let Baal contend against him,” yet the nation returned to Baal worship rather than honoring the family of the man God had used to rescue them.
