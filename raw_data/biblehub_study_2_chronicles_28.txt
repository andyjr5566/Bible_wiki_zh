Ahaz began ruling Judah at an age associated with adult responsibility and military service in Israel (Numbers 1:3). His accession placed the Davidic throne in the hands of a king who would lead the nation away from covenant faithfulness during a time of pressure from Syria, Israel, Edom, and Assyria.

2 Chronicles 28:2

Instead, he walked in the ways of the kings of Israel
Ahaz rejected the covenant pattern expected of Judah’s Davidic kings and adopted the rebellious practices long established in the northern kingdom. Israel’s rulers had led the nation into idolatry from the days of Jeroboam, who established false worship centers at Bethel and Daniel (1 Kings 12:28–30). Ahaz chose a path already marked by spiritual corruption, disregarding the reforms and faithful examples found among some of Judah’s earlier kings. His conduct showed contempt for the Lord’s authority and brought Judah into alliance with the sins that had invited God’s judgment upon Israel.

and even made cast images of the Baals.
Ahaz actively promoted Baal worship by producing idols for pagan devotion, directly violating God’s command against graven images and false gods (Exodus 20:3–4). The Baals were Canaanite fertility deities associated with rain, crops, and prosperity, but their worship involved moral corruption and dependence upon powerless idols. Rather than seeking the Lord who had preserved Judah and established David’s dynasty, Ahaz encouraged the worship of gods made by human hands. His apostasy reached beyond personal unfaithfulness and helped draw the people of Judah toward judgment.
