Twenty-Four Divisions of Priests

1 Chronicles 24:1

These were the divisions of the descendants of Aaron.
David organized Aaron’s descendants into twenty-four priestly courses to provide an orderly rotation for ministry before the Lord. First Chronicles 24 records the assignment of these courses by lot, showing that priestly service was governed by God’s order rather than personal preference. The arrangement continued into the postexilic period and remained in use in the days of the New Testament, when Zechariah served in the course of Abijah (Luke 1:5). The regularity of this service testified that worship of the holy God required faithful, consecrated servants.

The sons of Aaron were Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.
Nadab and Abihu died without sons after offering unauthorized fire before the Lord, a judgment that revealed the danger of approaching God contrary to His command (Leviticus 10:1–2). Eleazar and Ithamar therefore became the continuing priestly lines. Eleazar’s descendants supplied most of the priestly leaders and later included Zadok, whose line served prominently in the temple. Aaron’s priesthood, with its sacrifices and mediating service, anticipated the greater and eternal priesthood of Jesus Christ, who perfectly brings His people to God (Hebrews 7:23–25).
