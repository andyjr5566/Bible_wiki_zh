Judges 18:1

In those days there was no king in Israel,
Israel lacked a centralized human ruler during the period of the judges. God remained Israel’s true King, yet the tribes repeatedly neglected His covenant and acted according to their own judgment. The recurring notice in Judges 17:6; 19:1; and 21:25 exposes the disorder produced when God’s authority was ignored. The events involving Daniel reveal not merely a political weakness but spiritual instability, as a tribe entrusted with an inheritance pursued security apart from faithful obedience.

The tribe of Daniel was one of the twelve tribes of Israel, descended from Dan, the fifth son of Jacob and Bilhah. In the context of Judges, the Danites were seeking a new territory because they had not yet secured their inheritance in the Promised Land. This group of six hundred men represents a significant military force, indicating the seriousness of their mission. Historically, the tribe of Daniel struggled to conquer the land allotted to them, which led to their migration northward. This event is part of the broader narrative of the tribal confederation in Israel before the establishment of the monarchy.

Some manuscripts read “Manasseh,” likely reflecting an ancient effort to avoid linking Moses’ name directly with this idolatry; the received Hebrew text identifies Jonathan as Moses’ descendant.

and his sons were priests for the tribe of Dan
Jonathan and his descendants maintained this unauthorized priesthood for generations. Though Jonathan was a Levite, God had assigned the priesthood specifically to Aaron’s line. Dan’s private sanctuary, images, and self-appointed priesthood displayed the disorder of an age when Israel rejected God’s revealed order and followed what seemed right in its own eyes.

The Danite shrine endured until a time of national judgment, when the land experienced captivity. The wording may point to the devastation connected with the Philistine oppression and the loss of the ark, though it also anticipates the later exile brought by Israel’s persistent idolatry. What Daniel treated as a lasting religious institution could not preserve them from the consequences of covenant unfaithfulness.

The Danites installed the image taken from Micah’s house as the center of their own unauthorized worship in Laish, renamed Daniel Their actions openly violated God’s command against making and serving carved images (Exodus 20:4–5). Although they retained religious language and employed a Levite priest, their worship had departed from the Lord’s revealed order.
