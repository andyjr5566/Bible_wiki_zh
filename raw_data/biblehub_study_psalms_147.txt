It Is Good to Sing Praises

Psalm 147:1

Hallelujah!
Hallelujah means “Praise the LORD” and calls God’s people to joyful, united worship. Psalms 146–150 each begin and end with this summons, directing all praise to the covenant God of Israel. Revelation 19 records the same cry in heaven as the redeemed worship God for His righteous reign and final victory.

Rev 19:1 After this I heard a sound like the roar…

Psa 113:1 Hallelujah! Give praise, O servants of the LORD…

How good it is to sing praises to our God,
Singing joins truth, memory, and affection in worship. David appointed Levites for musical ministry before the Lord, and Israel sang in response to God’s deliverance, holiness, and faithfulness. The Lord who restores Jerusalem, gathers His scattered people, and heals the brokenhearted is worthy of praise from both individuals and the assembled congregation.

Psa 47:6 Sing praises to God, sing praises; sing praises…

Psa 92:1 A Psalm. A song for the Sabbath day. It is good to praise…

how pleasant and lovely to praise Him!
Praise is fitting because God is altogether good, beautiful, and sovereign over creation and history. Worship delights the believer because it turns the heart from fear and self-concern to the Lord’s mercy and power. Shared praise also strengthens the unity of God’s people as they confess together the greatness of the One who has redeemed them.

Psalm 147:2

The LORD builds up Jerusalem;
Jerusalem’s restoration after Babylonian devastation displayed God’s faithfulness to His covenant promises. Through Zerubbabel, Ezra, and Nehemiah, God rebuilt the city, restored temple worship, and renewed His people’s identity around His Word. Jerusalem also anticipates the future city where God will dwell with His redeemed people.

He gathers the exiles of Israel.
The return from exile fulfilled promises proclaimed by Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and other prophets: God would bring His scattered people back to their land and restore them to fellowship with Him. Israel’s gathering reveals both God’s judgment against sin and His mercy toward the repentant. It also points forward to Christ, the Shepherd who gathers His people from every nation into one redeemed family.

Psalm 147:3

He heals the brokenhearted
God draws near to those crushed by grief, sin, loss, and oppression. He restores the inner life—the affections, will, and hope—that suffering has wounded. Isaiah foretold that the Messiah would bind up the brokenhearted (Isaiah 61:1), and Jesus applied that promise to Himself in Nazareth (Luke 4:18). Through Christ, forgiveness, reconciliation with God, and enduring hope reach those human strength cannot restore.

Psa 34:18 The LORD is near to the brokenhearted; He saves the contrite…

and binds up their wounds.
God tends His people with the care of a physician dressing an injury, giving both protection and restoration. His healing addresses the real damage caused by sin and sorrow rather than merely concealing it. Jeremiah proclaimed God’s promise to restore health and heal wounds (Jeremiah 30:17), a promise ultimately fulfilled in Christ’s redemptive work.

Psalm 147:4

He determines the number of the stars;
The stars that appear beyond human counting stand under God’s complete knowledge and rule. In contrast to surrounding nations that treated heavenly bodies as gods or signs controlling human destiny, Scripture presents them as creatures made by the Lord (Genesis 1:16). Their vast number magnifies His power rather than diminishing His nearness. Isaiah likewise calls Israel to look upward and recognize that the Creator brings out the host of heaven and sustains every one of them (Isaiah 40:26).

Jer 33:22 As the hosts of heaven cannot be counted…

Gen 22:17 I will surely bless you, and I will multiply…

He calls them each by name.
God’s naming of every star reveals personal knowledge joined to sovereign authority. The One who governs the innumerable lights of heaven also gathers the outcasts of Israel and heals the brokenhearted in the surrounding lines of the psalm (Psalm 147:2–3). His care is neither abstract nor limited by the scale of creation. Jesus applies this same pattern of personal knowledge to His people, declaring that the Good Shepherd calls His own sheep by name (John 10:3, 14).

Psalm 147:5

Great is our Lord,
The God of Israel stands above every ruler, nation, and false god. Psalm 147 praises Him as the One who rebuilds Jerusalem, gathers the exiles, and heals the brokenhearted. His greatness assures His people that no disaster, opposition, or exile can overturn His covenant purposes.

and mighty in power;
The Lord’s power formed the heavens, delivered Israel from Egypt, preserved His people in the wilderness, and governs all creation. The same power that numbers and names the stars sustains those who depend on Him. Jesus Christ displayed this divine power in His authority over sickness, demons, nature, sin, and death.

His understanding has no limit.
God knows every detail of His creation and every need of His people. His wisdom is not weakened by the complexity of human life or the vastness of the universe. He judges righteously, guides perfectly, and fulfills His purposes without error.

Psalm 147:6

The LORD sustains the humble,
God upholds those who recognize their need of Him, including the afflicted, oppressed, and lowly who entrust themselves to His care. His sustaining grace does not always remove hardship immediately, but preserves faith and gives strength within it. Jesus embodied such humility, submitting Himself to the Father even unto death, and God highly exalted Him (Philippians 2:5–11). The Lord’s care for the humble reveals that His kingdom does not operate by worldly power or self-exaltation.

but casts the wicked to the ground.
God opposes those who persist in rebellion, pride, and oppression of others. Their apparent strength cannot overturn His justice, for He brings down the arrogant and vindicates those who fear Him. Psalm 147 joins praise for God’s restoring mercy with confidence in His righteous judgment. The final defeat of wickedness will be fully displayed when Christ judges the world in righteousness.

Psalm 147:7

Sing to the LORD with thanksgiving;
Israel’s worship answered the LORD’s covenant faithfulness, His provision, His healing of the brokenhearted, and His gathering of His people. Thanksgiving acknowledges that every good gift comes from God and guards worship from becoming empty ritual. Believers likewise are called to give thanks in every circumstance, trusting God’s wise and faithful care.

make music on the harp to our God,
Harps and lyres accompanied the praise offered by Levites in Israel’s worship, and David himself used such instruments in devotion to the LORD. Music was not entertainment before a human audience but an offering directed to God. The words “our God” confess the covenant bond by which He claims His people and receives their joyful, reverent praise.

Psalm 147:8

who covers the sky with clouds,
The Lord rules the heavens and the earth with perfect wisdom. Clouds display His power, at times concealing the heavens and at times bringing the moisture on which life depends. God’s presence was associated with the pillar of cloud that led Israel through the wilderness (Exodus 13:21), yet His works in the clouds also remind mankind that His ways exceed human understanding (Job 37:11–16).

Rain does not come by chance or by the power of false gods, but by the provision of the Creator. In Israel’s agricultural life, seasonal rain meant crops, food, and survival; drought brought severe hardship. The Lord promised rain in its season as a covenant blessing for obedient Israel (Deuteronomy 11:14). His goodness also extends beyond His covenant people, for He sends rain on the righteous and the unrighteous alike (Matthew 5:45).

God supplies vegetation even on elevated and seemingly less fertile ground, providing pasture for animals and sustaining the whole order of creation. Psalm 104:14 likewise praises Him for causing grass to grow for livestock and plants for human cultivation. The God who rebuilds Jerusalem and gathers His scattered people also attends to the needs of the earth, showing that His care reaches from the affairs of nations to the grass covering the hills.

Psalm 147:9

He provides food for the animals,
God sustains the creatures He made, supplying their food according to the order established in creation (Genesis 1:30). His care reaches beyond mankind to every living thing, confirming His continuing rule over the world He formed. After the flood, God’s covenant included the animals as well as Noah and his descendants (Genesis 9:9–10), revealing His concern for all life under His sovereign care.

Though ravens were unclean birds under Israel’s ceremonial law (Leviticus 11:15), they remain dependent upon and cared for by their Creator. Jesus drew on God’s provision for birds to assure His disciples that the Father’s care for His people is even greater (Matthew 6:26).

Psalm 147:10

He does not delight in the strength of the horse;
Horses represented speed, cavalry, chariots, and the military power of nations such as Egypt and Assyria. Israel’s kings were forbidden to multiply horses or return to Egypt for them, lest the nation place its confidence in armed strength rather than the Lord (Deuteronomy 17:16). God is not impressed by the resources that make armies formidable, for victory belongs to Him (Psalm 20:7).

He takes no pleasure in the legs of a man.
Strong legs pictured the endurance, speed, and physical power of warriors and runners. Human ability, discipline, and courage have their proper place, but none can secure life, deliver from sin, or establish peace apart from God. The Lord looks beyond outward strength to hearts that fear Him and hope in His mercy, as the following verse declares (Psalm 147:11).

Psalm 147:11

The LORD is pleased with those who fear Him,
God delights in people who recognize His holiness, submit to His authority, and order their lives in obedience to Him. Such fear is not dread that drives sinners away from God, but reverent awe that turns them from sin and leads them to worship. Israel’s covenant life was built upon fearing the LORD, loving Him, and keeping His commandments (Deuteronomy 10:12–13). Human strength, military power, and outward accomplishment do not win God’s favor; He regards the humble heart that honors His name. The fear of the LORD remains the beginning of wisdom because it places God in His rightful place and man in his dependent position before the Creator and Judge.

Psa 25:14 The LORD confides in those who fear Him,…

who hope in His loving devotion.
Reverence toward God is joined with confident trust in His mercy. Those who fear Him do not rely on their own righteousness but wait upon His steadfast covenant love. The LORD’s faithfulness sustained Israel through judgment, exile, restoration, and every generation of need. His loving devotion reached its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, whose death and resurrection secure forgiveness and eternal life for all who believe. Hope in God rests on His unchanging character, not on favorable circumstances, and His people may trust that He will keep them, strengthen them, and fulfill His promises.

Psalm 147:12

Exalt the LORD, O Jerusalem;
Jerusalem, the city chosen for the Lord’s name and the site of Solomon’s temple, had known judgment, exile, and restoration. God’s preservation of the city and return of His people from Babylon displayed His covenant faithfulness. Jerusalem was therefore summoned to honor the Lord publicly for His mighty works and steadfast care.

Zion identifies the place of God’s dwelling among His people and points forward to His kingdom. The Lord was not merely Israel’s national God but “your God,” bound to His people by covenant mercy. Worship rises from grateful recognition that He gathers, protects, restores, and rules. The earthly Zion also anticipates the heavenly Jerusalem, where God’s redeemed people will praise Him without end.

Psalm 147:13

For He strengthens the bars of your gates
God secures Jerusalem as the defender of His covenant people. Strong gate bars represented protection from hostile armies and the stability needed for life within the city. Following the exile, the rebuilding of Jerusalem’s walls and gates under Nehemiah visibly testified to God’s restoring mercy and protection (Nehemiah 3; 6:15–16). Human watchmen and walls have their place, yet the Lord alone gives lasting safety to His people. His care ultimately finds its fullest revelation in Christ, the Good Shepherd, who lays down His life for His sheep and keeps them in His hand (John 10:11–15, 28).

and blesses the children within you.
God’s protection extends beyond the city’s walls to the families living inside them. Children are a heritage from the Lord, and their flourishing displays His covenant kindness toward present and future generations (Psalm 127:3–5). The blessing includes the peace, provision, and spiritual continuity needed for God’s people to raise their children in the knowledge of His works. Such prosperity reflects the covenant promise that the fruit of the womb would be blessed among a people walking under God’s favor (Deuteronomy 28:4). Jesus likewise welcomed children, showing the value of those whom the world often overlooks and calling His people to receive the kingdom with humble dependence (Mark 10:13–16).

Psalm 147:14

He makes peace at your borders;
The Lord grants security to His restored people, guarding the vulnerable boundaries where invading armies and territorial conflict often threatened Israel. Psalm 147 celebrates God’s rebuilding of Jerusalem and gathering of the exiles, so peace at the borders reflects His protection after judgment and dispersion. Such security recalls the covenant promise of Leviticus 26:6, in which God would give peace in the land and remove the threat of enemies. Christ, the Prince of Peace, reconciles believers to God through His sacrifice and will establish enduring peace throughout His kingdom.

The finest wheat reveals abundance rather than bare survival, showing that God supplies His people with what is good. Earthly bread points beyond itself to Jesus Christ, the Bread of Life, who gives eternal nourishment to all who come to Him in faith.

Psalm 147:15

The sovereign Creator rules all creation by His decree. The God who spoke light, land, and living creatures into being continues to govern the world He made. Israel knew His authority in the deliverance at the Red Sea, in the provision of manna, and in the ordered seasons that sustained the land. Nature is not independent of God but remains subject to His providence.

God accomplishes His purposes without resistance or delay. His decree brings rain, snow, frost, and thaw in the verses that follow, displaying His immediate rule over the natural world. His word also accomplishes redemption and judgment according to His appointed time. Isaiah declared that God’s word fulfills everything for which He sends it, and Christ, the eternal Word made flesh, perfectly carried out the Father’s will.

Snow covers the earth as wool covers and warms the body. Its whiteness recalls the cleansing promised in Isaiah 1:18, where the Lord makes scarlet sins white as snow. God’s command over snow, frost, and ice displays His sovereign care for creation (Job 37:6–10), providing moisture and sheltering the ground through winter.

Frost settles lightly across the land like ashes scattered from the hand. Ashes were associated with mourning, humility, repentance, and purification (Job 42:6; Numbers 19:9). The delicate covering soon disappears before the sun, reminding mankind of life’s frailty while declaring that every season remains under the Lord’s authority.

He casts forth His hail like pebbles.
The Lord sends hail with effortless authority, though its impact can devastate crops, homes, and armies. Hail fell upon Egypt as a judgment against Pharaoh’s hardened rebellion (Exodus 9:18–26), and God used it to strike Israel’s enemies during Joshua’s battle at Gibeon (Joshua 10:11). Creation is not independent of its Maker; the storehouses of snow and hail remain under His command (Job 38:22–23).

Yet the God whose power humbles all people also provides refuge for those who trust Him, granting peace with Himself through Jesus Christ (Romans 5:1).

Psalm 147:19

He declares His word to Jacob,
God revealed Himself to the patriarch and to the covenant family that descended from him. Jacob, renamed Israel, received God’s promises concerning the land, the nation, and the coming blessing for all families of the earth (Genesis 28:13–15). Israel’s existence rested on God’s gracious initiative, not human achievement.

His statutes and judgments to Israel.
Through Moses at Mount Sinai, the Lord gave Israel His commandments, ordinances, and judicial requirements, establishing them as His covenant people (Exodus 20; Deuteronomy 4:5–8). God’s law displayed His holiness and directed Israel toward righteousness, worship, mercy, and justice. The revelation entrusted to Israel ultimately leads to Christ, who fulfilled the Law and established the new covenant through His death and resurrection (Matthew 5:17; Hebrews 8:6–13).

Psalm 147:20

God entrusted Israel with His revealed word, covenant, statutes, and ordinances. At Sinai He established Israel as His treasured possession, a kingdom of priests, and a holy nation (Exodus 19:5–6). Circumcision, Sabbath observance, sacrifices, and dietary distinctions preserved their identity among surrounding peoples and called them to reflect the holiness of the Lord.

Through Christ, the promised Messiah of Israel, God’s saving truth has now gone to every nation.
