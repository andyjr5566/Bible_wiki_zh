Ezekiel 11:1

The eastern gate stood in the direction associated with the Lord’s glory, which had departed from the temple in Ezekiel’s earlier vision and would later return from the east in Ezekiel 43:1–4. At this gate, where worshipers and officials gathered, God exposed the corruption present among Judah’s leaders.

Ezekiel was carried in the Spirit to Jerusalem’s east gate, where he saw twenty-five leaders devising evil and giving wicked counsel to the city. Their confidence that Jerusalem was secure ignored the bloodshed, idolatry, and injustice that had provoked God’s judgment. God called His prophet to confront those who influenced the nation toward rebellion. Their position did not shield them from accountability; the shepherds of God’s people would answer for leading the flock astray (Jeremiah 23:1–2).

Though Ezekiel lived among the exiles in Babylon, God made him a witness against Jerusalem’s rulers and announced the judgment soon to come through Babylon. The repeated command underscores that silence was not an option when God’s holiness and the people’s spiritual welfare were at stake. Ezekiel’s ministry anticipates the greater Son of Man, Jesus Christ, who came not only to expose sin but to give His life as a ransom for many (Mark 10:45).
