1 Samuel 22:2

And all who were distressed or indebted or discontented rallied around him,
Men oppressed by hardship, debt, and bitter circumstances found refuge with David at Adullam. Saul’s unstable reign had brought fear and injustice, leaving many Israelites alienated and without hope. David did not reject these wounded and troubled men; he received them and formed them into a faithful band. His refuge became a picture of God’s care for those cast down by the world. Jesus likewise welcomed the weary, burdened, and socially overlooked, calling them to find rest in Him (Matt. 11:28).

He united men from troubled backgrounds, disciplined them, and led them in dependence upon the Lord. The cave of Adullam became an early training ground for the kingdom God had promised David. In David’s leadership, the Lord displayed the kind of shepherding that would find its fullness in Christ, the true King who gathers, guides, and transforms His people.

Moab was connected to David’s family through Ruth, his great-grandmother, and that ancestral relationship may have opened a door for him in a foreign land. Though Israel and Moab were often hostile, David wisely used every lawful means of preserving those entrusted to him.
