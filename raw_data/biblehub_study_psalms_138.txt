A Thankful Heart

Psalm 138:1

Of David.
David’s authorship joins Psalm 138 to the worshiping king whom God established over a united Israel and through whom He made Jerusalem the center of national worship. Having known danger, deliverance, sin, discipline, and restoration, David praises God from lived experience rather than mere ceremony.

I give You thanks with all my heart;
David offers God undivided gratitude from the whole inner person—mind, will, and affections. Such worship fulfills Israel’s calling to love the LORD wholly (Deuteronomy 6:5) and anticipates Jesus’ command that God be loved with all the heart, soul, and mind (Matthew 22:37). Thanksgiving recognizes that every victory, preservation, and covenant mercy comes from God.

before the gods I sing Your praises.
David publicly exalts the LORD in the presence of every rival claim to power, whether pagan idols, spiritual beings, or earthly rulers. The gods of the nations could not compare with the living God who made heaven and earth and who rules kings and kingdoms. David’s bold praise points forward to Christ, who disarmed every hostile spiritual power and reigns above all authority (Colossians 2:15).

Psalm 138:2

I bow down toward Your holy temple
David worships the Lord with humble submission, directing his prayer toward the sanctuary where God had placed His name. The tabernacle, and later the temple in Jerusalem, testified to God’s covenant presence among His people without containing His infinite being. Daniel continued this pattern by praying toward Jerusalem while exiled in Babylon (Daniel 6:10).

Thanksgiving rises from reverence for all God has revealed Himself to be. God’s name declares His holiness, power, mercy, justice, and covenant faithfulness. David gives thanks not merely for blessings received, but for the Lord Himself.

David rests in the certainty that God remains faithful even when human strength and circumstances fail.

Psalm 138:3

On the day I called, You answered me;
David testifies that the Lord heard his cry in distress and acted with personal faithfulness. Israel’s worship remembered the God who delivered His people from Egypt, preserved David from his enemies, and invited the afflicted to seek Him. Psalm 18:6 similarly recounts that the Lord heard David’s voice from His temple. God’s answer may include deliverance, guidance, correction, or sustaining grace, but prayer is never directed toward a distant or indifferent God. Jeremiah 33:3 later reaffirmed that the Lord calls His people to seek Him with confidence in His sovereign care.

Psa 34:4 I sought the LORD, and He answered me; He delivered me…

You emboldened me
God did not merely alter David’s circumstances; He gave him courage within. Kings, soldiers, and ordinary Israelites faced dangers beyond human strength, yet the Lord repeatedly commanded His servants not to fear because He was with them. Joshua received such strength before leading Israel into Canaan, and David knew it in conflicts with Saul, foreign armies, and internal trouble. The risen Christ gives His people the same courage through the Holy Spirit, enabling faithful witness and perseverance.

and strengthened my soul.
The Lord fortified David’s inner being—his mind, will, and affections—so that fear did not rule him. Such strength sustains faith when relief is delayed and obedience is costly. Isaiah proclaimed that the Lord gives power to the weary, and Paul prayed that believers would be strengthened with power through the Spirit in the inner person. God’s sustaining work preserves His servants and equips them to trust Him through every trial.

Psalm 138:4

All the kings of the earth
Earthly rulers represent the highest levels of human power, yet they remain subject to the King of kings. David looks beyond Israel to the day when rulers from every nation acknowledge the Lord’s sovereignty. Isaiah foretold that kings and princes would bow before the Servant of the Lord (Isaiah 49:7), and Revelation portrays the kings of the earth bringing their glory into the New Jerusalem (Revelation 21:24).

will give You thanks, O LORD,
Their praise will arise from recognition that God alone rules with perfect wisdom, justice, and mercy. Thanksgiving is fitting worship for those who receive His revelation and see His faithfulness. The Lord’s purpose is not merely to humble earthly authority but to bring the nations into willing honor of His name.

Earthly rulers who hear God’s words will acknowledge His righteous works, judgments, and saving acts. Singing joins public confession to worship, declaring that the Lord’s rule is wise and good. David’s vision reaches beyond Israel to the nations, anticipating the day when kings and peoples honor the God of Israel.

Psalm 138:6

Though the LORD is on high,
The Lord reigns in unapproachable majesty over heaven and earth, yet His exalted position never separates Him from His creation. Isaiah saw Him high and lifted up, while Isaiah 57:15 joins His high and holy dwelling with His presence among the contrite and humble.

He attends to the lowly;
God gives gracious attention to those who depend on Him rather than their own strength. He defended Israel in her affliction, heard the cries of the oppressed, and repeatedly lifted the poor and needy. Jesus revealed the same divine compassion by receiving the humble, healing the afflicted, and teaching that the poor in spirit belong to the kingdom of heaven.

God’s servants have often passed through severe trials, as Joseph endured betrayal and imprisonment and Daniel faced the lions’ den. Trouble surrounds the believer, yet it is not the final destination; the Lord leads His people through it.

Hostile men and spiritual enemies cannot act beyond God’s sovereign restraint. David had known the rage of Saul, foreign armies, and conspirators within his own household, but the Lord repeatedly kept His covenant servant. God remains a refuge for His people when opposition rises against them.

Psalm 138:8

The LORD will fulfill His purpose for me.
David rests in the certainty that the covenant God will bring His gracious design to completion. His own life had shown that God could preserve and establish His servant through danger, opposition, and failure. Joseph’s suffering and exaltation likewise revealed that human evil cannot overthrow God’s intended good (Genesis 50:20). God’s purposes include the salvation, sanctification, and final preservation of His people. Believers therefore trust the God who began a good work in them to complete it at the day of Christ Jesus (Philippians 1:6).

O LORD, Your loving devotion endures forever—
The LORD’s enduring covenant love is the foundation of David’s confidence. God’s mercy is not exhausted by the weakness of His people, nor is His faithfulness limited by changing circumstances. Israel’s history repeatedly displayed His steadfast love in deliverance, discipline, restoration, and forgiveness. God’s eternal love is supremely revealed in Jesus Christ, whose death and resurrection secure lasting redemption for all who believe.

do not abandon the works of Your hands.
David appeals to God as Creator and Redeemer, asking Him to continue sustaining what He has made and claimed. Humanity bears the marks of God’s creative work, and His redeemed people belong to Him in a special covenant relationship. The prayer recognizes complete dependence upon God’s preserving grace. The Lord who neither slumbers nor sleeps will not forsake His people, but will keep them and finish His work in them.
