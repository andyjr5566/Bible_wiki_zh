The Perils of the Pilgrim

Psalm 10:1

Why, O LORD, do You stand far off?
The psalmist brings anguish directly to the covenant God, trusting that the Lord reigns even while evil appears unchecked. God is never absent from His creation, yet He may delay visible deliverance so that faith is tested, wickedness is exposed, and His righteous judgment is revealed. Similar cries rise in Psalm 22:1 and find their deepest fulfillment in Christ, who bore abandonment and suffering at the cross.

Psa 71:12 Be not far from me, O God. Hurry, O my God, to help me.

Why do You hide in times of trouble?
The apparent concealment of God reflects the experience of the oppressed when arrogant and violent people prosper. Psalm 10 does not end in despair: the Lord sees trouble and grief, hears the afflicted, and judges the wicked (Psalm 10:14–18). Believers may therefore pray honestly in seasons of darkness while resting in God’s certain justice and care.

Psalm 10:2

In pride the wicked pursue the needy;
The wicked use power, wealth, and influence to exploit those least able to defend themselves. Such conduct violated Israel’s covenant calling to protect the poor, widows, orphans, and strangers (Deuteronomy 15:7–11; 24:17–22). Pride rejects the fear of the Lord and treats vulnerable people as objects for personal gain. God sees oppression as rebellion against His righteous rule.

God’s judgment demonstrates that no scheme against the innocent escapes His notice, and that those who sow injustice will ultimately reap its bitter fruit.

Psalm 10:3

For the wicked man boasts in the cravings of his heart;
The wicked openly celebrates desires that God condemns rather than seeking to restrain them. His heart becomes the measure of right and wrong, and he takes pride in pursuing pleasure, power, or revenge without regard for the Lord. Such boasting reveals the deceitfulness of the human heart and stands opposed to the humility that submits every desire to God’s will.

His admiration for the greedy exposes a society in which possessions and personal advantage replace love for God and concern for neighbor.

Psalm 10 exposes the spiritual root of oppression: a heart that exalts self, applauds greed, and refuses the fear of the Lord, which is the beginning of wisdom.

Psalm 10:4

In his pride the wicked man does not seek Him;
Pride persuades the wicked that they are sufficient in themselves and have no need to call upon the Lord. Such self-exaltation rejects the humility of faith and blinds the heart to God’s authority, mercy, and judgment. Proverbs 16:18 warns that pride precedes destruction, and Babel illustrates how human ambition seeks independence from God only to meet His intervention (Genesis 11:1–9).

The wicked arrange their plans as though God neither sees nor acts. Their thoughts leave no place for His commands, providence, or coming judgment, resembling the fool who says in his heart, “There is no God” (Psalm 14:1). Though such people may prosper for a time, their confidence rests on a false security. Romans 1:21–22 shows that refusing to honor God darkens the mind, while the one who seeks the Lord receives wisdom and a sure foundation.

Psalm 10:7

His mouth is full of cursing, deceit, and violence;
The wicked man’s speech reveals a heart set against God and neighbor. His curses seek another’s ruin, his deceit entraps the vulnerable, and his violent words become instruments of oppression. Jesus taught that the mouth speaks from the abundance of the heart (Matthew 12:34). Paul later cited this portrayal of sinful speech in his indictment of humanity’s universal guilt before God (Romans 3:14).

Hidden beneath outward speech lie plans that bring misery and injury to others. The psalmist exposes evil not merely as careless language but as deliberate hostility prepared in the heart. God calls His people to reject corrupt speech and to use their words for truth, grace, and edification (Ephesians 4:29).

Psalm 10:8

He lies in wait near the villages;
The wicked man preys where protection is weakest, like a predator stalking isolated victims beyond the safety of fortified towns. Amalek similarly attacked Israel’s weary and vulnerable stragglers in the wilderness (Deuteronomy 25:17–18). Such calculated cruelty reveals contempt for God, who commands His people to defend the weak rather than exploit them.

His violence is marked by deceit and cowardice, for he attacks those who have given no cause for harm and cannot prepare a defense. Cain’s murder of Abel first displayed this murderous rebellion against God, and Scripture repeatedly condemns the shedding of innocent blood. The Lord does not overlook such injustice, even when earthly courts fail.

Psalm 10:9

The wicked man hides his intentions as a predator conceals itself before attacking. Lions were feared throughout the ancient Near East for their strength and sudden violence, making the image a fitting portrait of ruthless enemies who use power against the defenseless. Scripture also portrays Satan as a roaring lion seeking victims to devour, revealing the spiritual malice behind oppression and deceit (1 Peter 5:8).

Human cruelty may be concealed for a time, but the Lord sees both the victim’s suffering and the attacker’s intent.

Psalm 10:12

The psalmist calls upon the covenant God to move openly against violent and arrogant evildoers. God’s apparent delay is not indifference; He sees every act of oppression and will judge in His appointed time. The prayer reflects confidence that the Lord is not a distant observer but the righteous King who defends His people.

Wickedness is not merely moral failure; it is rebellion against the Creator who sees, judges, and sustains every life. The apparent security and success of evildoers troubled other servants of God, including Asaph in Psalm 73, yet Scripture consistently affirms that God’s patience does not cancel His judgment.

Psalm 10:16

The LORD is King forever and ever;
God’s reign is eternal, unchallenged, and unlike the passing rule of earthly monarchs. Israel’s kings could fail, die, or lead the nation into sin, but the LORD remains the righteous King over all creation. Psalm 93:1 declares that the world is firmly established under His rule, and Psalm 145:13 celebrates His kingdom as everlasting. God’s kingship provides the foundation for the psalmist’s confidence that oppression and violence will not endure. Jesus Christ fulfills this royal hope as the King of kings whose kingdom will never end (Revelation 19:16).

the nations perish from His land.
The land ultimately belongs to the LORD, who gave Canaan to Israel and governs every nation that rises within or around it. Nations that exalt themselves against God, oppress the weak, and attack His people may appear powerful for a time, yet they remain accountable to His judgment. Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, and other kingdoms became instruments within God’s purposes but were themselves judged for pride, cruelty, and idolatry. Earthly powers pass away, while God’s kingdom remains, vindicating His justice and preserving His people.

Psalm 10:18

to vindicate the fatherless and oppressed,
God hears the cry of those whom society overlooks or exploits. In Israel, orphans and widows often lacked the family protection, inheritance, and legal standing needed to resist injustice. The Law commanded Israel not to afflict them, warning that the Lord Himself would hear their cry and judge their oppressors (Exodus 22:22–24). As Father of the fatherless and Judge of the oppressed, He calls His people to reflect His righteousness through mercy, honesty, and impartial justice (Deuteronomy 10:18; Psalm 68:5; James 1:27).

The psalmist looks to God to restrain evil and end the fear imposed by the wicked. Christ will ultimately establish the justice for which the oppressed wait, judging the world in righteousness and bringing an end to sorrow, violence, and tyranny (Isaiah 11:4–9; Revelation 21:4).
