In My Distress I Cried to the LORD

Psalm 120:1

A song of ascents.
Psalm 120 opens the collection of Psalms 120–134, associated with Israel’s worship as pilgrims traveled up to Jerusalem for the appointed feasts. Jerusalem stood on elevated ground, but the ascent also pictured drawing near to the Lord in worship. The psalm begins the collection with the reality of life in a hostile world, directing weary pilgrims to seek help from God.

Psa 121:1 A song of ascents. I lift up my eyes to the hills.…

Psa 122:1 I was glad when they said to me, “Let us go…

Psa 123:1 A song of ascents. I lift up my eyes to You…

In my distress
The singer speaks from real pressure and affliction, surrounded by deceitful and violent people. Israel’s history repeatedly showed that trouble drives God’s people to remember their dependence on Him. Distress does not place the believer beyond God’s care; it becomes an occasion to seek Him with greater confidence.

Psa 18:6 In my distress I called upon the LORD; I cried…

Psa 31:9 Be merciful to me, O LORD, for I am in distress;…

Jon 2:2 “In my distress I called to the LORD, and He answered me.”…

I cried to the LORD,
Earnest prayer is the faithful response to suffering. The covenant God who heard Israel’s groaning in Egypt remains attentive to the cries of His people. The psalmist does not rely on retaliation or human strength but brings the injustice directly before the Lord.

Psa 107:13 Then they cried out to the LORD in their trouble,…

Psa 130:1 A song of ascents. Out of the depths I cry to You, O LORD!

Psa 34:6 This poor man called out, and the LORD heard him; …

and He answered me.
God’s answer establishes the foundation for the prayer that follows. The Lord hears His servants and acts according to His righteousness, even when deliverance unfolds through hardship or waiting. His readiness to hear points forward to the access believers have through Jesus Christ, the perfect mediator who brings His people before God.

Psa 34:4 I sought the LORD, and He answered me; He delivered me…

Psa 118:5 In my distress I called to the LORD, and He answered…

Psa 138:3 On the day I called, You answered me; You emboldened me…

Psalm 120:2

Deliver my soul, O LORD,
The psalmist appeals to Yahweh as the faithful covenant God and only true Deliverer. “Soul” encompasses the whole person, including life, security, and honor. God hears the cries of His people in distress and rescues them from enemies beyond their own power.

Psa 6:4 Turn, O LORD, and deliver my soul; save me…

Psa 116:4 Then I called on the name of the LORD: “O LORD, deliver…

Psa 25:20 Guard my soul and deliver me; let me not be put to shame…

from lying lips
False accusations could destroy a person’s reputation, standing in the community, and even legal security. The Lord detests false witness and upholds those oppressed by slander and injustice.

Psa 31:18 May lying lips be silenced—lips that speak…

Pro 12:22 Lying lips are detestable to the LORD…

Psa 12:2 They lie to one another; they speak with flattering lips…

and a deceitful tongue.
Deceptive speech spreads harm with calculated intent, dividing communities and perverting justice. David’s dependence on God anticipates Christ, who endured false testimony yet committed Himself to the righteous Judge.

Psa 52:4 You love every word that devours, O deceitful tongue.

Zep 3:13 The remnant of Israel will no longer do wrong or speak lies,…

Jer 9:8 Their tongues are deadly arrows; they speak deception.…

Psalm 120:3

What will He do to you,
God hears the victim of slander and will judge those who deliberately use speech to destroy others. The question anticipates the punishment named in the next verse: sharp warrior’s arrows and burning coals, images of unavoidable and fitting judgment. God’s justice answers the lies that human courts and communities may fail to expose.

Psa 52:5 Surely God will bring you down to everlasting ruin;…

Psa 5:6 You destroy those who tell lies; the LORD abhors…

Pro 19:5 A false witness will not go unpunished…

and what will be added to you,
Persistent deceit brings no lasting advantage but adds guilt before the righteous Judge. The language points to recompense beyond the immediate harm done, echoing the biblical certainty that whatever a person sows, he will also reap. God’s patience does not cancel His judgment against unrepentant sin.

Rev 22:18 I testify to everyone who hears the words of prophecy…

Deu 28:61 The LORD will also bring upon you every sickness and plague…

Luk 12:47-48 That servant who knows his master’s will…

O deceitful tongue?
The tongue represents the person who knowingly speaks falsehood, slander, and betrayal. Such speech violates God’s command against bearing false witness and opposes the character of the Lord, who is truth. Though the tongue may appear small, its lies can wound families, divide God’s people, and conceal evil; therefore God holds its speaker accountable.

Psa 52:4 You love every word that devours, O deceitful tongue.

Jer 9:3-6 They bend their tongues like bows; lies prevail over truth.…

Pro 6:16-19 There are six things that the LORD hates,…

Psalm 120:4

Sharp arrows will come from the warrior,
God will answer deceitful speech with certain and penetrating judgment. The warrior’s arrows picture punishment that reaches those who wound others through lies, slander, and treachery. The Lord is the righteous Judge who defends those oppressed by false tongues.

Psa 45:5 Your arrows pierce the hearts of the king’s foes;…

Psa 7:12-13 If one does not repent, God will sharpen His sword;…

Psa 64:7 But God will shoot them with arrows; …

with burning coals of the broom tree!
Broom-tree coals were known for producing fierce, enduring heat in the wilderness. The image adds consuming judgment to the piercing arrows: evil words will not escape God’s reckoning. The punishment fits the offense, for lips that kindle conflict will face the holy fire of divine justice.

Psa 11:6 On the wicked He will rain down fiery coals and sulfur;…

Psa 140:10 May burning coals fall on them; may they be thrown…

Job 30:4 They plucked mallow among the shrubs, and the roots…

Psalm 120:5

Woe to me
The psalmist mourns the burden of living amid deceit, hostility, and disregard for the God of peace. The cry recalls Isaiah’s confession of uncleanness among a people of unclean lips (Isaiah 6:5), revealing sorrow over a world shaped by sin and longing for God’s deliverance.

Isa 6:5 Then I said: “Woe is me, for I am ruined,…

Isa 24:16 From the ends of the earth we hear singing…

Jer 15:10 Woe to me, my mother, that you have borne me,…

that I dwell in Meshech,
Meshech was associated with peoples north of Israel, likely in Asia Minor near the region of modern Turkey. Its distance from Jerusalem underscores the psalmist’s alienation from the worshiping community and from the peace of God’s dwelling place.

Ezk 38:2-3 “Son of man, set your face against Gog of the land of Magog”…

Ezk 39:1 As for you, O son of man, prophesy against Gog…

Gen 10:2 The sons of Japheth: Gomer, Magog, Madai…

that I live among the tents of Kedar!
Kedar, descended from Ishmael (Genesis 25:13), was a nomadic people of the Arabian desert known for their black tents (Song of Solomon 1:5). Meshech and Kedar lay in widely separated regions, so the language portrays not necessarily a literal residence in both places but the distress of being surrounded wherever one turns by those who oppose peace. God’s people remain pilgrims in such a world, seeking the city and kingdom God has prepared (Hebrews 11:13–16).

Sng 1:5 I am dark, yet lovely, O daughters of Jerusalem,…

Jer 49:28-29 Concerning Kedar and the kingdoms of Hazor,…

Isa 42:11 Let the desert and its cities raise their voices;…

Psalm 120:6

Too long
The psalmist’s suffering has not been brief. His lament reflects the weary endurance of God’s people when surrounded by hostility and awaiting the Lord’s deliverance.

Psa 13:1 For the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. How long, O LORD?…

Psa 74:10 How long, O God, will the enemy taunt You?…

Rev 6:10 And they cried out in a loud voice, “How long, O Lord…

have I dwelt
Life among the ungodly had become an ongoing burden rather than a passing encounter. The references to Meshech and Kedar in the preceding verse portray residence among distant, hostile peoples who neither honored the Lord nor shared His ways.

Psa 39:12 Hear my prayer, O LORD, and give ear to my cry for help;…

2Pe 2:7-8 if He rescued Lot, a righteous man distressed…

Ezk 2:6 But you, son of man, do not be afraid of them…

among those who hate peace.
Those around the psalmist oppose the wholeness, truth, and reconciliation that peace with God produces. His longing anticipates the reign of Christ, the Prince of Peace, who was rejected by those who preferred conflict and darkness yet will establish lasting peace in His kingdom.

Psa 35:20 For they do not speak peace, but they devise deceitful schemes……

Psa 55:21 His speech is smooth as butter, but war is in his heart.…

Jer 9:8 Their tongues are deadly arrows; they speak deception.…

Psalm 120:7

I am in favor of peace;
The psalmist stands for the wholeness, safety, and reconciliation that accord with God’s purposes for His people. His desire reflects the peace God established in creation and that He will finally secure under the reign of the Prince of Peace, Jesus Christ (Isaiah 9:6).

Psa 34:14 Turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it.

Heb 12:14 Pursue peace with everyone, as well as holiness,…

Rom 12:18 If it is possible on your part, live at peace with everyone.

but when I speak,
Rather than pursuing retaliation, the psalmist seeks resolution through truthful speech. His conduct anticipates the call for God’s people to pursue peace, speak wisely, and overcome evil with good (Romans 12:18, 21).

Pro 15:1 A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word…

Jer 9:8 Their tongues are deadly arrows; they speak deception.…

Pro 12:18 Speaking rashly is like a piercing sword,…

they want war.
Those around him answer peaceful words with hostility, revealing hearts set against God’s order. The conflict reminds believers that faithful pursuit of peace does not always produce peace with others, yet Christ will ultimately judge evil and establish lasting peace in His kingdom.
