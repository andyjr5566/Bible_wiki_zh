Jeremiah 1:3

and through the days of Jehoiakim son of Josiah king of Judah,
Jehoiakim, originally named Eliakim, was placed on Judah’s throne by Pharaoh Neco after his brother Jehoahaz was taken to Egypt. His eleven-year reign reversed much of Josiah’s reform and brought Judah under heavy tribute, first to Egypt and then to Babylon. Jeremiah confronted the king’s injustice, violence, and idolatry, warning that covenant rebellion would bring Babylonian judgment. Jehoiakim’s burning of Jeremiah’s written prophecies revealed his hardened rejection of God’s word, yet the message was rewritten and its judgment remained certain.

Zedekiah, originally named Mattaniah, was Josiah’s last surviving son to reign in Jerusalem. Nebuchadnezzar appointed him king after deporting Jehoiachin, but Zedekiah refused Jeremiah’s repeated counsel to submit to Babylon under God’s discipline. His rebellion led to the Babylonian siege of Jerusalem, famine within the city, the breaching of its walls, and the destruction of the temple. The fifth month of Zedekiah’s eleventh year marks the burning of Jerusalem and the temple in 586 BC.

Judah’s guilt included idolatry, injustice, violence, and persistent refusal to heed God’s word. Their rebellion had corrupted both public worship and daily life, revealing hearts turned from the Lord.

Hos 9:15 All their evil appears at Gilgal, for there I hated them.…

Pro 11:5 The righteousness of the blameless directs their path,…

Jer 16:18 And I will first repay them double their iniquity…

because they have forsaken Me,
The deepest sin was abandoning the God who had redeemed Israel and bound Himself to them in covenant. Judah sought security through foreign alliances and pagan worship rather than trusting the Lord alone.

2Ch 12:5 Then Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam…

Jdg 10:13 But you have forsaken Me and served other gods,…

Deu 31:16 And the LORD said to Moses, “You will soon rest…

and they have burned incense to other gods
Incense, meant to accompany devotion to the Lord, was offered to false gods instead. Such worship directly rejected the first commandment and transferred honor due to the Creator to powerless idols.

2Ki 22:17 because they have forsaken Me and burned incense…

1Ki 11:8 He did the same for all his foreign wives,…

Jer 11:13 Your gods are indeed as numerous as your cities…

and worshiped the works of their own hands.
Judah bowed before images fashioned by human skill, exchanging the living God for objects they had made. Their idolatry displayed spiritual blindness and stood in sharp contrast to worshiping the Lord, who made heaven, earth, and His people.

Isa 2:8 Their land is full of idols; they bow down…

Psa 115:4 Their idols are silver and gold, made by the hands of men.

Jeremiah 1:17

Get yourself ready.
God called Jeremiah to enter his ministry with deliberate readiness, knowing that his assignment would bring conflict as well as faithfulness. The prophet’s life and mind had to be ordered under God’s authority before he could confront Judah’s rebellion.

Job 38:3 Now brace yourself like a man; I will question you…

Luk 12:35 Be dressed for service and keep your lamps burning.

1Pe 1:13 Therefore prepare your minds for action. Be sober-minded.…

Stand up and tell them everything that I command you.
Jeremiah was commissioned to speak publicly and without withholding any part of God’s message. His ministry would include warnings of judgment, calls to repentance, and promises of restoration, fulfilling the Lord’s purpose to uproot and tear down, then to build and plant.

Ezk 2:1-7 “Son of man,” He said to me, “stand up on your feet”…

Deu 18:18-19 I will raise up for them a prophet like you…

Act 26:16-18 But get up and stand on your feet. For I have appeared…

Do not be intimidated by them,
Jeremiah would face resistance from Judah’s kings, officials, priests, and people throughout the final years before Jerusalem’s fall. Human opposition could not determine the content or courage of God’s messenger. The Lord required Jeremiah to fear God more than the powerful people before him.

Ezk 3:9 I will make your forehead like a diamond, harder than flint.…

Deu 31:6 Be strong and courageous; do not be afraid or terrified…

Jos 1:9 Have I not commanded you to be strong and courageous?…

or I will terrify you before them.
Retreating in fear would bring humiliation, because Jeremiah’s security rested in obedience to the One who sent him. God’s warning reinforced the seriousness of prophetic responsibility: the servant of God must not soften His word in order to gain acceptance or avoid suffering.

Jer 20:4 For this is what the LORD says: ‘I will make you a terror…

Jer 49:5 Behold, I am about to bring terror upon you,…

Deu 28:65-67 Among those nations you will find no repose,…

Jeremiah 1:18

Now behold, this day I have made you like a fortified city,
God appointed Jeremiah to stand secure amid the spiritual collapse of Judah. A fortified city possessed walls and gates designed to withstand siege; Jeremiah would endure not by personal courage alone, but by the Lord’s sustaining presence.

Isa 26:1 In that day this song will be sung in the land of Judah:…

Psa 48:12-13 March around Zion, encircle her, count her towers,…

Isa 60:18 No longer will violence be heard in your land,…

an iron pillar,
Jeremiah would be established with strength that could not be bent by intimidation, rejection, or persecution. His calling required him to proclaim God’s judgment faithfully before rulers, priests, and neighbors who resisted the truth.

Isa 48:4 For I knew that you are stubborn; your neck is iron…

Ezk 3:8-9 Behold, I will make your face as hard as their faces…

Rev 3:12 The one who overcomes I will make a pillar…

and bronze walls
The Lord would make His servant resilient against repeated attacks. Jeremiah’s ministry would bring suffering and loneliness, yet neither threats nor imprisonment could silence the message God placed in his mouth.

Jer 15:20 Then I will make you a wall to this people,…

Isa 45:2 I will go before you and level the mountains;…

Deu 33:25 May the bolts of your gate be iron and bronze,…

against the whole land—against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land.
Opposition would arise from every level of Judah’s society, including the monarchy, civil leaders, religious authorities, and common people. Judah had abandoned covenant faithfulness, and Jeremiah’s warnings of coming Babylonian judgment exposed sins that many refused to forsake. God’s protection did not mean Jeremiah would avoid hardship; it meant that his enemies would not defeat God’s purpose or prevent the fulfillment of His word.

Psa 2:2 The kings of the earth take their stand…

Ezk 22:25-29 The conspiracy of the princes in her midst…

Jer 26:8-11 and as soon as he had finished telling all the people…

Jeremiah 1:19

They will fight against you
Judah’s kings, officials, priests, prophets, and many citizens would oppose Jeremiah because his warnings exposed their idolatry, injustice, and false security. His ministry brought beatings, imprisonment, threats, and public rejection, yet he was appointed to speak God’s word without compromise.

Isa 54:15 If anyone attacks you, it is not from Me;…

2Ch 20:15 And he said, “Listen, all you people of Judah and Jerusalem!…

Deu 20:4 For the LORD your God goes with you to fight for you…

but will never overcome you,
Jeremiah would suffer deeply, but his enemies could not silence God’s message or frustrate God’s purpose for him. The Lord preserved him through plots, confinement, and Jerusalem’s fall, proving that human resistance cannot overturn divine commission.

Isa 54:17 No weapon formed against you shall prosper,…

Mat 16:18 And I tell you that you are Peter, and on this rock…

Rom 8:37 No, in all these things we are more than conquerors…

since I am with you to deliver you,”
God’s presence supplied the courage Jeremiah lacked in himself. As the Lord strengthened Moses and Joshua for difficult assignments, He promised to sustain His servant amid danger and to deliver him according to His sovereign purpose. God’s saving presence finds its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, Immanuel, meaning “God with us” (Matthew 1:23).

Exo 3:12 “I will surely be with you,” God said, “and this will be…”…

Isa 41:10 Do not fear, for I am with you; do not be afraid…

Psa 91:15 When he calls out to Me, I will answer him;…

declares the LORD.
The promise rests on the authority of the covenant God, not on Jeremiah’s ability or Judah’s response. What the Lord speaks stands firm, and His word accomplishes His purposes even when His messenger is resisted.

Zec 1:3 So tell the people that this is what the LORD of Hosts says:…

1Ki 8:56 Blessed be the LORD, who has given rest to His people Israel…
