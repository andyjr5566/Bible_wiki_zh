Moab’s Destruction

Isaiah 16:1

Send the tribute lambs to the ruler of the land,
Moab had once rendered a vast annual payment of lambs and rams to Israel’s king (2 Kings 3:4). Isaiah calls the humbled nation to renew submission to Judah’s ruler, seeking mercy rather than trusting its fortified cities or former strength. The tribute acknowledges that God had established David’s house in Jerusalem and that human rulers remain accountable to His sovereign rule.

Sela, meaning “rock” in Hebrew, was a rocky stronghold east or southeast of the Dead Sea, associated with the rugged territory near Edom and later Petra. Moab’s people are pictured as helpless and scattered, driven from the safety of their homes by invading armies. The image conveys terror, confusion, and the loss of every familiar protection. Those who had trusted in fortified cities, wealth, and national strength now wander without security.

Moab, descended from Lot through his son Moab, had long stood near Israel both geographically and historically. In judgment, the nation’s future generations and settled communities are exposed to the consequences of its pride and idolatry.

Isaiah 16:9

Isaiah joins the mourning of Jazer over Sibmah’s ruined vineyards. These places lay in the fertile highlands east of the Jordan, where vineyards had brought Moab wealth, commerce, and celebration. The prophet’s grief reveals that God’s judgment upon Moab is not cold or careless. The destruction of fruitful land displays the cost of pride and rebellion, yet the lament also reflects the Lord’s compassion toward those under judgment. Vine imagery elsewhere portrays both blessing and accountability, for the Lord expects the fruit of righteousness from those who enjoy His gifts.
