2 Kings 20:1

Hezekiah’s illness came during the years when Judah faced the overwhelming power of Assyria. After leading reforms that restored temple worship and called the nation back to covenant faithfulness, the king himself confronted the limits of human strength. The account in Isaiah 38 and 2 Chronicles 32 shows that the crisis affected not only Hezekiah personally but also the future of David’s royal line and Judah’s stability.

and how he constructed the pool and the tunnel to bring water into the city,
Hezekiah secured Jerusalem’s water supply by directing water from the Gihon Spring through a tunnel into the city, probably terminating at the Pool of Siloam. The tunnel, about 1,750 feet long, enabled Jerusalem to endure siege conditions without depending on water sources outside its walls. The Siloam Inscription, discovered within the tunnel, preserves an ancient account of the workers meeting as they excavated from opposite directions. Hezekiah’s preparation displayed wise stewardship, while his prayer during the Assyrian crisis showed that human diligence must remain subject to trust in God.
