The territories assigned to Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh formed Israel’s inheritance east of the Jordan River. Their possession of this land demonstrated that God’s promise to Abraham was being fulfilled in actual places, boundaries, cities, and pasturelands.

Moses distributed these inheritances before his death, carrying out the Lord’s command and preparing Israel for the next stage of conquest under Joshua. Although Moses would not enter Canaan, he faithfully secured the inheritance of the eastern tribes and established their place within the covenant people.

The eastern side of the Jordan lay outside the principal territory of Canaan, yet it was granted by God as a true inheritance to these tribes. Reuben, Gad, and Manasseh remained responsible to stand with their brothers in the conquest of the land west of the river.

Joshua 13:33

To the tribe of Levi, however, Moses had given no inheritance.
Levi received no tribal territory because the Levites were appointed for the service of the tabernacle and the instruction of Israel in God’s law. They were given forty-eight cities with surrounding pasturelands throughout the other tribes, and they were sustained by tithes and portions of the sacrifices. Their scattered dwelling placed spiritual servants among all Israel.

The LORD, the God of Israel, is their inheritance,
God Himself supplied what land could not provide: covenant fellowship, priestly privilege, and His faithful provision. The Levites ministered before the Lord, guarded the holy things, assisted the priests, and taught Israel to distinguish what was holy. Their calling showed that communion with God is a greater possession than earthly abundance.

God fulfilled His word given through Moses in Numbers 18:20–24 and Deuteronomy 10:9. Israel’s worship depended upon the Levites’ faithful service, while the Levites depended upon the Lord and the obedience of Israel in giving tithes. Their inheritance anticipates the lasting portion of all who belong to God through Christ.
