1 Chronicles 5:3

The sons of Reuben, the firstborn of Israel:
Reuben’s position as Jacob’s firstborn remained historically important, even though his sin with Bilhah brought the loss of the privileges normally attached to the birthright. Joseph received the double inheritance through Ephraim and Manasseh, while Judah received preeminence and the royal line that ultimately led to David and Christ (Genesis 49:3–4, 8–10; 1 Chron. 5:1–2). Reuben’s descendants nevertheless remained a recognized tribe within Israel. Their territory east of the Jordan, extending through fertile pasturelands, reflected God’s faithfulness to preserve the family of Jacob despite Reuben’s failure.

These four sons formed the principal Reubenite clans that entered Egypt with Jacob’s household and later emerged as part of Israel’s nation (Genesis 46:9; Ex. 6:14; Numbers 26:5–7). The genealogy shows that personal sin carries lasting consequences, yet God does not abandon His covenant purposes or erase those who belong to His people.
