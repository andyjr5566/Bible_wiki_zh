The Remainder Divided

Joshua 18:1

Then the whole congregation of Israel assembled at Shiloh
Israel gathered as one people after the major campaigns in Canaan, demonstrating that the tribes remained united under Joshua’s leadership and under the covenant of the Lord. Shiloh, in Ephraim’s territory, became Israel’s central gathering place for worship, national decisions, and the distribution of the remaining inheritance. The assembly marked an important transition from Israel’s wilderness journey to life established in the land God had promised. By gathering at the place chosen for the sanctuary, Israel began to fulfill the command to seek the place where the Lord would cause His name to dwell (Deuteronomy 12:5–14). Shiloh remained the principal religious center through the period of the judges and into the days of Samuel.

Sacrifices, priestly ministry, and national inquiries before the Lord would now be centered there. The settled location of the Tabernacle anticipated the later Temple in Jerusalem, though the Lord’s presence was never confined by any earthly structure.

Benjamin’s Inheritance

Joshua 18:11

The first lot came up for the clans of the tribe of Benjamin.
The casting of lots placed the distribution of the land under the Lord’s sovereign direction, not human preference or tribal strength (Leviticus 16:8; Acts 1:26). Benjamin descended from Jacob’s youngest son, the only son born in Canaan. Though one of the smaller tribes, Benjamin became known for courageous and skilled warriors, including left-handed slingers who could strike with remarkable accuracy (Judges 20:16). The inheritance given to Benjamin fulfilled God’s covenant promise to Abraham that his descendants would possess the land of Canaan (Genesis 15:18–21).

Their allotted territory lay between the tribes of Judah and Joseph:
Benjamin occupied a strategic central region between Judah in the south and the tribes descended from Joseph, especially Ephraim, in the north. Major roads passed through this territory, making it important for travel, commerce, and military defense. Cities associated with Benjamin included Jericho, Bethel, Gibeon, Mizpah, and Jerusalem, the city of peace; Jerusalem itself stood on the border between Benjamin and Judah and later became the center of Israel’s worship and kingship. Saul, Israel’s first king, came from Benjamin (1 Samuel 9:1–2). Benjamin’s position between Judah and Joseph also anticipated its recurring role in Israel’s national life, preserving a connection between the southern kingdom of Judah and the northern tribes during periods of division and conflict.
