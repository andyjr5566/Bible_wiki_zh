Ezekiel beheld the return of God’s manifest presence, traditionally called the Shekinah glory. God’s glory had departed from the temple because of Judah’s idolatry and rebellion, moving eastward from the sanctuary and then to the mountain east of Jerusalem (Ezekiel 10:18–19; 11:22–23). Its return by the same direction reveals that judgment had not canceled God’s covenant purposes for Israel. The glory would enter the temple through the east gate and fill the house again (Ezekiel 43:4–5), establishing the Lord’s dwelling among His restored people.

Revelation also describes the glorified Christ’s voice as the sound of many waters (Revelation 1:15). The long cubit measured about twenty-one inches, adding a handbreadth to the ordinary cubit of approximately eighteen inches. God specified the standard so that the altar would be built according to the revealed pattern. Such precision recalls the instructions for the tabernacle and Solomon’s temple, where every sacred structure testified that worship must approach God on His terms.
