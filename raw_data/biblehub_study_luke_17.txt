The Ten Lepers

He traveled with full knowledge that Jerusalem was the appointed place where His saving work would reach its climax. Having set His face toward the city (Luke 9:51), He moved in obedience to the Father’s will, toward His rejection, crucifixion, resurrection, and ascension. Jerusalem, the center of Israel’s worship and the site of the temple, would witness the fulfillment of the Servant’s suffering foretold in Isaiah 53 and the arrival of the humble King anticipated in Zechariah 9:9.

The route placed Jesus near the boundary between Jewish Galilee and Samaritan territory, where generations of religious and ethnic hostility had divided the people. Samaritans accepted the books of Moses but worshiped at Mount Gerizim rather than at Jerusalem, and many Jews avoided association with them. Jesus did not share that hostility. His ministry had already brought grace to Samaritans, including the woman at Jacob’s well (John 4), and His path toward Jerusalem continued to show that God’s mercy reaches beyond inherited barriers.
