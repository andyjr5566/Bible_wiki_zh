The Coming Deliverance of Jerusalem

Zechariah 12:1

This is the burden of the word of the LORD concerning Israel.
Zechariah delivers a solemn divine oracle concerning Israel and Jerusalem, introducing the events developed through chapters 12–14. The message includes severe conflict, national mourning, cleansing, and the LORD’s final deliverance of His covenant people. Israel remains central to God’s redemptive purposes, and the coming judgment of hostile nations demonstrates that God has not abandoned His promises to Abraham, David, and the prophets.

Zechariah 12:2

“Behold, I will make Jerusalem a cup of drunkenness to all the surrounding peoples.
God declares that the nations gathering against Jerusalem will be overwhelmed by His judgment and thrown into confusion. Scripture often portrays divine wrath as a cup given to rebellious peoples to drink (Isaiah 51:17; Jeremiah 25:15–16). Those who seek to destroy the city God has chosen will find that their assault brings judgment upon themselves. Jerusalem, though appearing vulnerable, becomes the appointed instrument through which God humbles hostile nations.

Judah will be besieged, as well as Jerusalem.
The attack will extend beyond Jerusalem to encompass Judah and its people. Earlier sieges of Jerusalem, including the Babylonian destruction in 586 BC, reveal the grievous reality of such warfare, yet this prophecy looks beyond those events to a climactic conflict involving the nations. God’s preservation of Judah and Jerusalem will display His faithfulness to His covenant promises. The surrounding chapters lead to Israel’s mourning over the One they pierced and the Lord’s decisive intervention for His people (Zechariah 12:10; 14:3–4).

Mourning the One They Pierced

Zechariah 12:10

Then I will pour out on the house of David and on the people of Jerusalem
The Lord Himself will initiate this work of renewal among Israel’s royal house and the inhabitants of its covenant city. The house of David recalls God’s promise that the Messiah would come through David’s line and reign forever. Jerusalem represents the nation gathered around its spiritual and political center. God’s outpouring will accomplish what human strength and religious reform cannot produce: a united turning of the people toward their Messiah.

a spirit of grace and prayer,
God will grant grace that awakens repentance and mercy that draws sinners into His presence. Prayer will arise from hearts made conscious of sin and dependent on God’s forgiveness. This promised work accords with the New Covenant, in which God gives His people a new heart and places His Spirit within them, enabling obedience and fellowship with Him. Genuine mourning and prayer flow from grace; they are not efforts by which people earn God’s favor.

The Lord identifies Himself with the One who was pierced, revealing the divine identity and suffering mission of the Messiah. Jesus fulfilled these words when He was crucified and His side was pierced by a Roman soldier. John explicitly connects that event with Zechariah’s prophecy. Israel’s future recognition of Christ will involve more than seeing historical facts; the nation will acknowledge that the rejected and crucified Jesus is the promised King. The cross also exposes the sin of all humanity, for Christ bore the judgment deserved by sinners.

The people’s response will be marked by profound personal and national grief. The loss of an only child meant the loss of a family’s dearest hope and future inheritance. Israel will grieve because the Messiah whom God sent was rejected, despised, and put to death. Such sorrow belongs to true repentance, where sin is seen not merely for its consequences but as rebellion against the holy and gracious God.

Bitter grief will give way to restoration, because the One who was pierced rose from the dead and will return in glory. God’s grace will bring Israel from recognition of guilt to cleansing, forgiveness, and renewed fellowship with her King.
