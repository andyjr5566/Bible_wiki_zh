Jeremiah’s Scroll Read in the Temple

Jeremiah 36:1

In the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah king of Judah,
The year was about 605 BC, when Babylon defeated Egypt at Carchemish and became the dominant power over Judah. Jehoiakim, unlike his godly father Josiah, resisted the Lord’s call to repentance and led Judah further into rebellion. The political upheaval of the period formed the setting for God’s warning that Babylonian judgment and exile were drawing near.

The message originated with the covenant God of Israel, not with Jeremiah’s own insight or political judgment. The Lord continued to speak to Judah despite its persistent disobedience, calling His people to hear, repent, and return. Jeremiah’s ministry bore divine authority because he faithfully delivered what the Lord commanded, including warnings that would soon be confirmed by history.

His end stood as a warning that neither position, wealth, nor royal lineage can shield those who despise the word of the Lord.

Jeremiah 36:31

I will punish him and his descendants and servants for their iniquity.
Jehoiakim had contemptuously burned Jeremiah’s scroll, rejecting the word God had sent to call Judah back to covenant faithfulness. His guilt involved more than a private act of defiance; as king, he led the nation in rebellion and encouraged officials who opposed God’s prophet. Judgment upon his descendants and servants shows that leadership carries far-reaching consequences. His household and court shared in the sins of idolatry, injustice, and resistance to God’s warnings. Jehoiakim’s line would lose the security and honor he sought to preserve through political maneuvering.

I will bring on them, on the residents of Jerusalem, and on the men of Judah,
Judgment would reach beyond the palace because the rebellion had spread throughout Jerusalem and Judah. The people had tolerated false worship, trusted foreign alliances, and refused the Lord’s commands. Jerusalem possessed the temple and Davidic throne, yet neither religious privilege nor national heritage could shield a people who persistently rejected God. The coming disaster would expose the emptiness of their confidence in human power and outward religion.

Jer 11:11-12 Therefore this is what the LORD says: ‘I am about to bring…

2Ki 21:12-15 this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says:…

all the calamity about which I warned them but they did not listen.”
The calamity included Babylon’s campaigns against Judah, the siege and destruction of Jerusalem, and the exile of many survivors. God had repeatedly warned the nation through Jeremiah and other prophets, giving opportunity for repentance before judgment fell. Their refusal to listen was willful disobedience, not lack of knowledge. God’s warnings proved true, demonstrating both His patience and His faithfulness to His word. The judgment upon Judah also anticipates the universal call to heed God’s final word in His Son, who summons sinners to repentance and faith before the day of judgment.

Jeremiah 36:32

God’s message did not perish when Jehoiakim destroyed the first scroll. Jeremiah’s obedience demonstrated that human rebellion cannot nullify divine revelation or prevent the fulfillment of God’s word.
