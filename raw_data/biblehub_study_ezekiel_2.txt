Ezekiel’s Call

Ezekiel 2:1

“Son of man,”
God addresses Ezekiel as a mortal man before the overwhelming glory he has seen. The title appears more than ninety times in Ezekiel and continually reminds the prophet that he is human, dependent, and called to serve the sovereign Lord. Ezekiel stands in sharp contrast to the divine glory revealed by the Chebar Canal. Jesus also called Himself the Son of Man, identifying with humanity while fulfilling Daniel’s vision of the heavenly ruler who receives everlasting dominion.

Ezk 3:1 “Son of man,” He said to me, “eat what you find here.”…

Dan 8:17 As he came near to where I stood, I was terrified…

He said to me,
The God whose glory Ezekiel beheld now speaks personally to him. Ezekiel’s prophetic ministry begins with divine revelation, not personal ambition or human insight. The Lord establishes His servant’s authority by giving him His own word.

Jer 1:7 But the LORD told me: “Do not say, ‘I am only a child.’…

Jer 1:9 Then the LORD reached out His hand, touched my mouth……

“stand up on your feet
Ezekiel had fallen facedown before the appearance of God’s glory. God calls him to rise, not because the vision was ordinary, but because he is being commissioned for active obedience. A prophet must humbly fear the Lord while standing ready to deliver His message.

Ezk 3:24 Then the Spirit entered me and set me on my feet.…

Dan 10:11 He said to me, “Daniel, you are a man who is highly precious…

and I will speak to you.”
The Lord promises to instruct Ezekiel for the difficult ministry ahead among Israel’s rebellious people. God does not send His servants without His word, and Ezekiel’s message will carry divine authority because it comes from the One who speaks.

Ezk 3:27 But when I speak with you, I will open your mouth…

Num 12:8 I speak with him face to face, clearly and not in riddles;…

Ezekiel 2:2

And as He spoke to me,
God addressed Ezekiel personally, calling him from the overwhelming vision of divine glory into the responsibility of prophetic service. The message Ezekiel would bear came from the Lord, not from human insight or priestly tradition.

Jer 1:9 Then the LORD reached out His hand, touched my mouth……

Exo 33:11 Thus the LORD would speak to Moses face to face, …

Num 12:6 He said, “Hear now My words: If there is a prophet among you…

the Spirit entered me
God’s Spirit strengthened Ezekiel inwardly for a task beyond natural ability. The Lord equips those He calls, enabling Ezekiel to receive and proclaim words of judgment and hope to Israel in exile.

Gal 4:6 And because you are sons, God sent the Spirit of His Son…

Jhn 14:17 The world cannot receive Him, because it neither sees Him…

Jhn 20:22 When He had said this, He breathed on them…

and set me on my feet,
Ezekiel had fallen facedown before the glory of God, but the Spirit raised him to stand as the Lord’s servant. God’s power restored him to readiness, giving him strength to remain before the holy God and carry His message faithfully.

Act 26:16 But get up and stand on your feet. For I have appeared…

Dan 10:11 He said to me, “Daniel, you are a man who is highly precious…

Dan 8:18 While he was speaking with me, I fell into a deep sleep,…

and I heard Him speaking to me.
Ezekiel received God’s words with clarity and authority. His ministry would rest upon obedient hearing, as he became a watchman and spokesman for the Lord among a rebellious people.

Ezekiel 2:3

“Son of man,” He said to me,
God addresses Ezekiel as a mortal man standing before the glory of the sovereign Lord. The title occurs more than ninety times in Ezekiel and continually reminds the prophet of his human weakness and dependence upon God. Jesus later used “Son of Man” of Himself, identifying with humanity while also fulfilling Daniel’s vision of the heavenly ruler who receives everlasting dominion (Daniel 7:13–14).

Dan 8:17 As he came near to where I stood, I was terrified…

Dan 10:11 He said to me, “Daniel, you are a man who is highly precious…

Dan 10:19 “Do not be afraid, you who are highly precious,” he said.…

“I am sending you to the Israelites,
Ezekiel’s calling was directed to the covenant people living under Babylonian exile. Though Jerusalem had not yet fallen completely, many Judeans had already been deported, including Ezekiel himself. Exile did not mean that God had abandoned Israel; He continued to speak, warn, judge, and preserve a remnant according to His covenant promises.

Amo 7:15 But the LORD took me from following the flock…

Mat 15:24 He answered, “I was sent only to the lost sheep”…

Mat 10:6 Go rather to the lost sheep of Israel.

to a rebellious nation that has rebelled against Me.
Israel’s deepest crisis was not Babylon’s power but persistent rebellion against the Lord. Idolatry, injustice, false worship, and refusal to heed God’s prophets had violated the covenant. The people descended from Jacob had been called to belong to the Lord among the nations, yet they had adopted the sins of the nations around them.

Ezk 12:2 “Son of man, you are living in a rebellious house…

Isa 1:2 Listen, O heavens, and give ear, O earth…

Deu 9:7 Remember this, and never forget how you provoked the LORD…

To this very day they and their fathers have rebelled against Me.
The rebellion of Ezekiel’s generation continued a long history reaching back through the wilderness, the period of the judges, and the kingdoms of Israel and Judah. Their fathers’ sins did not remove personal responsibility from those living in exile; each generation had continued the same resistance to God. Ezekiel’s ministry would expose that enduring unfaithfulness while also preparing the way for the Lord’s promised cleansing, new heart, and restored people.

Jer 7:25-26 From the day your fathers came out of Egypt…

2Ki 17:14 But they would not listen, and they stiffened their necks…

Neh 9:16-17 But they and our fathers became arrogant and stiff-necked…

Ezekiel 2:4

They are obstinate and stubborn children.
Israel’s rebellion had become deeply settled, continuing the pattern seen in the wilderness when the Lord called the nation stiff-necked after the sin of the golden calf (Exodus 32:9). Though they bore the privileges of God’s covenant and had received His law, many refused His authority. Their condition called for correction, as children who had rejected the wise rule and care of their Father.

Deu 9:6 Understand, then, that it is not because of your…

Deu 31:27 For I know how rebellious and stiff-necked you are.…

Isa 48:4 For I knew that you are stubborn; your neck is iron…

I am sending you to them,
The Lord did not abandon His covenant people in exile. He raised up Ezekiel among the captives in Babylon to confront sin, announce judgment, and preserve hope for those who would hear. Like Moses, Isaiah, and Jeremiah, Ezekiel received a commission that rested on God’s authority rather than the willingness of the people.

Ezk 3:4 Then He said to me, “Son of man, go now…

Jer 1:7 But the LORD told me: “Do not say, ‘I am only a child.’…

Isa 6:8-9 Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying…

and you are to say to them,
Ezekiel was responsible to deliver God’s words faithfully, not to soften them or measure success by public acceptance. Israel’s resistance could not excuse silence or alter the message. The prophet stood before the exiles as God’s watchman, calling them to recognize their guilt and the righteousness of the Lord’s judgments.

Deu 18:18 I will raise up for them a prophet like you…

Jer 1:9 Then the LORD reached out His hand, touched my mouth……

Exo 4:12 “Now go! I will help you as you speak, and I will teach”…

‘This is what the Lord GOD says.’
Ezekiel spoke under the authority of the covenant Lord, the God who had redeemed Israel, given them the land, and disciplined them for persistent unfaithfulness. The message came from no human source. God’s word exposed rebellion, yet His continued sending of a prophet showed that judgment was not the end of His dealings with His people.

Ezk 3:27 But when I speak with you, I will open your mouth…

2Sa 12:7 Then Nathan said to David, “You are that man!…

Amo 1:3 For three transgressions of Damascus, even four,…

Ezekiel 2:5

And whether they listen or refuse to listen—
Ezekiel was sent to proclaim God’s word without controlling the response of his hearers. Israel remained responsible for receiving or rejecting the warning, while Ezekiel remained responsible for faithfully delivering it. Like Isaiah, who was commissioned to speak to a hardened people (Isaiah 6:9–10), Ezekiel’s ministry would expose the condition of the nation and leave no excuse for unbelief.

Ezk 3:11 Go to your people, the exiles; speak to them…

Jer 7:27 When you tell them all these things, they will not listen…

Isa 6:9-10 And He replied: “Go and tell this people, ‘Be ever hearing,…

for they are a rebellious house—
The exiles belonged to a nation marked by longstanding resistance to the Lord, from the wilderness rebellion to the idolatry and covenant-breaking that brought Judah under judgment. Their exile in Babylon did not automatically produce repentance. God therefore sent Ezekiel among them to confront sin, call for repentance, and declare that the Lord had not abandoned His covenant purposes.

Ezk 12:2 “Son of man, you are living in a rebellious house…

Jer 5:23 But these people have stubborn and rebellious hearts.…

Isa 30:9 These are rebellious people, deceitful children,…

they will know that a prophet has been among them.
Whether the people believed Ezekiel immediately or not, the fulfillment of God’s warnings would confirm that he had spoken by divine authority. Jerusalem’s fall, the destruction of the temple, and the continued exile would demonstrate that the Lord had sent His messenger. Ezekiel’s presence among the captives testified that God still spoke to His people even in judgment. His ministry also anticipates Christ, the greater Prophet promised through Moses, whose words demand a response and whose authority will ultimately be acknowledged by all.

Ezk 33:33 So when it comes to pass—and surely it will come…

Deu 18:21-22 You may ask in your heart, “How can we recognize a message…

1Sa 3:20 So all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew…

Ezekiel 2:6

But you, son of man, do not be afraid of them or their words.
God repeatedly calls Ezekiel “son of man,” reminding him that he is a mortal man sent to speak for the Lord among his own people. The Israelites in exile had rebelled against God and would resist the message entrusted to the prophet. Ezekiel’s authority would rest not in public approval but in the God who commissioned him. God’s servants must not allow threats, ridicule, or rejection to silence faithful proclamation.

Deu 1:17 Show no partiality in judging; hear both small and great…

Mat 10:19-20 But when they hand you over, do not worry…

Luk 21:15 For I will give you speech and wisdom…

Do not be afraid, though briers and thorns surround you, and you dwell among scorpions.
Briers, thorns, and scorpions portray the painful hostility of a rebellious people. Thorns recall the curse brought by sin, and scorpions picture the danger Ezekiel would face as he lived among those who opposed God’s word. The Lord did not remove Ezekiel from this hard setting; He commanded him to stand firm within it. Christ likewise endured rejection from those He came to save, and His disciples were warned to expect opposition as they bore witness to Him.

Num 33:55 But if you do not drive out the inhabitants of the land…

Jos 23:13 know for sure that the LORD your God will no longer…

Deu 8:15 He led you through the vast and terrifying wilderness…

Do not be afraid of their words or dismayed by their presence, though they are a rebellious house.
Mockery, threats, and intimidating opposition would confront Ezekiel, but God forbade fear and discouragement. Israel’s rebellion was not merely against the prophet but against the Lord whose message Ezekiel delivered. Ezekiel was responsible to speak God’s words whether the people listened or refused, leaving the outcome in God’s hands. Israel’s rejection anticipates the rejection of Jesus by many of His own people, yet God’s purposes of judgment, repentance, and restoration would not fail.

Deu 31:8 The LORD Himself goes before you; He will be with you.…

Jer 1:8 Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you…

Isa 30:9 These are rebellious people, deceitful children,…

Ezekiel 2:7

But speak My words to them,
God sent Ezekiel to the exiles as His authorized messenger, not to offer his own opinions or soften the divine indictment. Ezekiel’s responsibility was faithful proclamation, as Jeremiah had also been commanded to speak all that God sent him to say (Jeremiah 1:7). God’s words carried authority over a people who had lost their land, temple, and king because they had disregarded those words.

Jer 26:2 This is what the LORD says: Stand in the courtyard…

Jer 1:9 Then the LORD reached out His hand, touched my mouth……

Deu 18:18 I will raise up for them a prophet like you…

whether they listen or refuse to listen,
The prophet’s faithfulness was measured by obedience to God, not by the people’s response. Ezekiel was to declare the message fully even when rejection was certain. God would use that witness to make Israel accountable and to show that a prophet had been among them (Ezekiel 2:5; 3:17–19). Like the sower who scatters seed across differing soils, God’s truth is proclaimed without compromise, while the hearer bears responsibility for receiving or rejecting it.

Ezk 3:11 Go to your people, the exiles; speak to them…

Jer 7:27 When you tell them all these things, they will not listen…

Isa 6:9-10 And He replied: “Go and tell this people, ‘Be ever hearing,…

for they are rebellious.
Israel’s resistance was directed ultimately against the Lord who had redeemed and covenanted with them. Their history included rebellion in the wilderness, idolatry in the land, and persistent refusal to heed the prophets. The Babylonian exile testified to the seriousness of covenant disobedience, yet God’s continued sending of Ezekiel also revealed His purpose to call His people back to Himself. Jesus Christ perfectly obeyed the Father where Israel and all humanity had failed, providing redemption for rebels who turn to Him.

Ezk 3:27 But when I speak with you, I will open your mouth…

Isa 30:9 These are rebellious people, deceitful children,…

Deu 9:6 Understand, then, that it is not because of your…

Ezekiel 2:8

And you, son of man,
God repeatedly addresses Ezekiel by this title, reminding him that he is a mortal man standing before the holy God. As one taken from among the exiles, Ezekiel bears God’s word to a people who share his humanity and need. Jesus later used the title Son of Man in fulfillment of Daniel 7:13–14, revealing Himself as the true human representative and eternal King.

Dan 8:17 As he came near to where I stood, I was terrified…

Ezk 3:17 Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house…

Ezk 40:4 “Son of man,” he said to me, “look with your eyes,…

listen to what I tell you.
Ezekiel’s ministry depended upon receiving God’s word before proclaiming it. Faithful service begins with submission to the Lord’s command, not with the approval of the people. God’s servants must hear with the intention of obeying.

Deu 18:19 And I will hold accountable anyone who does not listen…

Mat 17:5 While Peter was still speaking, a bright cloud enveloped…

Jhn 10:27 My sheep listen to My voice; I know them…

Do not be rebellious like that rebellious house.
Israel’s persistent refusal to heed God had brought covenant judgment and exile. Ezekiel was not to adopt the hardness, fear, or resistance of those to whom he was sent. Though surrounded by rebellion, he was called to remain loyal to the God who had spoken.

Deu 9:24 You have been rebelling against the LORD…

Isa 30:9 These are rebellious people, deceitful children,…

Neh 9:17 They refused to listen and failed to remember…

Open your mouth and eat what I give you.”
God would soon give Ezekiel a scroll containing His message of lamentation, mourning, and woe. Eating the scroll pictured the prophet’s inward reception of God’s word; he could not faithfully speak what he had not first received and embraced. John experienced a similar commission when he ate the little scroll in Revelation 10:9–10. God’s word sustains His servants even when the message they must deliver announces judgment.

Ezk 3:2-3 So I opened my mouth, and He fed me the scroll.…

Jer 15:16 Your words were found, and I ate them. Your words became…

Rev 10:9-10 And I went to the angel and said, “Give me the small scroll.”…

Ezekiel 2:9

Then I looked
Ezekiel turned his attention as the Lord continued to reveal His message. His prophetic ministry depended upon receiving what God showed him and faithfully declaring it to a rebellious people.

Ezk 1:4 I looked and saw a whirlwind coming from the north,…

Dan 7:2 “In my vision in the night I looked, and suddenly”…

and saw a hand reaching out to me,
A hand extended toward Ezekiel, presenting the revelation he was called to receive. God’s hand signifies His power, authority, and personal involvement in commissioning His servant. The message did not arise from Ezekiel’s own thoughts or observations; it came to him by divine initiative.

Ezk 8:3 He stretched out what looked like a hand…

Dan 10:10 Suddenly, a hand touched me and set me trembling…

and in it was a scroll,
The scroll contained God’s written message for Judah and Jerusalem. Scrolls preserved official decrees, records, and sacred instruction, marking the contents as authoritative and binding. Ezekiel would soon learn that it contained words of lamentation, mourning, and woe, revealing the certainty of God’s judgment against persistent covenant unfaithfulness.

Ezk 3:1-3 “Son of man,” He said to me, “eat what you find here.”…

Rev 10:2 He held in his hand a small scroll, which lay open.…

Rev 5:1 Then I saw a scroll in the right hand of the One…

Ezekiel 2:10

which He unrolled before me.
God placed the written revelation before Ezekiel, making clear that the prophet’s message originated with the Lord rather than with human insight. The opened scroll prepared Ezekiel to receive, internalize, and proclaim God’s decree to the exiles. Ezekiel would soon be commanded to eat the scroll, showing that God’s servant must receive His word before speaking it to others.

Luk 4:17 The scroll of the prophet Isaiah was handed to Him.…

Rev 10:8-10 Then the voice that I had heard from heaven…

Neh 8:5 Ezra opened the book in full view of all the people…

And written on the front and back of it
The writing on both sides showed the fullness of God’s message of judgment. Ancient scrolls were ordinarily written on one side, but no space remained unused on this scroll. Israel’s rebellion had produced a complete record of guilt and a comprehensive sentence from the righteous Judge. The two-sided tablets given through Moses likewise displayed the completeness of God’s covenant law.

Rev 5:1 Then I saw a scroll in the right hand of the One…

Exo 32:15-16 Then Moses turned and went down the mountain…

were words of lamentation, mourning, and woe.
The Lord’s message announced sorrow, bereavement, and impending calamity for Jerusalem and Judah. The coming destruction by Babylon would bring the consequences of persistent idolatry, violence, and covenant unfaithfulness. Yet Ezekiel’s proclamation of judgment also served God’s merciful purpose: the people would know that the Lord had spoken and would be called to repentance.
