Tola son of Puah, the son of Dodo,
Tola’s recorded lineage—son of Puah and grandson of Dodo—establishes him as a real Israelite leader within the covenant community. Though Scripture preserves few details of his acts, God’s work through faithful servants is not measured only by public achievements. The names Puah and Dodo are not widely referenced elsewhere, but their inclusion signifies the importance of family lineage in Israelite society and establishes Tola’s legitimacy and heritage. Tola’s name means “worm” or “scarlet,” possibly symbolizing humility or redemption.

As a Gileadite, he came from the region east of the Jordan, associated chiefly with Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh. His leadership demonstrates that God raised deliverers from throughout Israel, including the tribes beyond the Jordan, and preserved unity among His people despite regional divisions.

Israel’s misery had come under the oppression of the Ammonites as God’s discipline for idolatry, yet discipline did not cancel His covenant mercy. The Ammonites, descendants of Lot, had long opposed Israel’s possession of the land east of the Jordan. Their invasion followed eighteen years of oppression during Israel’s idolatry and demonstrated the severity of God’s discipline. Ammon’s claim against Israel would later be answered by Jephthah with a review of God’s earlier victories and Israel’s rightful inheritance (Judges 11:12–28).
