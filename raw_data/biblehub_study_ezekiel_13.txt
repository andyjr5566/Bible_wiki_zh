Reproof of False Prophets

Ezekiel 13:1

Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying,
Ezekiel received God’s message while living among the Jewish exiles in Babylon following the deportation of 597 BC. Though Jerusalem still stood at this point, the nation faced coming judgment because of persistent idolatry and rebellion. God continued to speak through His appointed prophet, exposing the lies of false prophets who promised peace and security despite the people’s sin. Ezekiel’s authority rested not on personal opinion or popular expectation, but on the word God gave him to proclaim. God’s revelation through the prophets ultimately points to His fullest self-disclosure in Jesus Christ, the Word made flesh.

Ezekiel 13:2

“Son of man,
God addresses Ezekiel by a title that underscores his human frailty before the sovereign Lord. Ezekiel stands as a member of the exiled people and as God’s appointed watchman, called to speak divine truth in the midst of national ruin. The title occurs repeatedly throughout the book, keeping attention on the contrast between the mortal messenger and the glory of the God who sends him. Jesus later used “Son of Man” as a principal title for Himself, identifying with humanity while also fulfilling Daniel’s vision of the heavenly ruler who receives everlasting dominion.

God commands Ezekiel to confront men within Israel who claimed prophetic authority without having received His message. Their deception was especially destructive because it carried the appearance of religious assurance. While Jeremiah had warned Jerusalem before its fall, these false prophets contradicted God’s announced judgment and encouraged resistance to His discipline.

False prophecy was an immediate danger among the exiles and those remaining in Judah. Many people longed for promises of swift deliverance from Babylon, and the false prophets supplied words that appealed to fear, pride, and national hopes. Their activity did not lessen God’s judgment; it hardened people against repentance and obscured the path of faithful submission to Him.

God had forbidden anyone to speak presumptuously in His name, and the failure of a prophet’s word exposed such a claim as false. Human invention can imitate the language of faith, yet it cannot replace revelation or overturn what God has declared.

Ezekiel 13:19

You have profaned Me among My people for handfuls of barley and scraps of bread.
The women addressed by Ezekiel claimed prophetic authority while using God’s name to advance their own interests. Handfuls of barley and scraps of bread were meager payments, yet they treated divine truth as something to be sold. In a time of hardship and impending Babylonian judgment, even small provisions could become powerful temptations. Their willingness to exchange God’s message for personal gain dishonored Him before the covenant people. Micah likewise condemned prophets who proclaimed peace when fed but war when unpaid (Micah 3:5).

Mic 3:11 Her leaders judge for a bribe, her priests teach…

Pro 28:21 To show partiality is not good, yet a man will do wrong…

Isa 56:11 Like ravenous dogs, they are never satisfied.…

By lying to My people who would listen,
The people were not merely victims of deception; many were willing listeners because the false messages offered safety, peace, and escape from judgment. God had warned Judah through Jeremiah and Ezekiel that Jerusalem would fall because of persistent rebellion, but false prophets promised the opposite. Such lies hardened the people against repentance and made them less willing to heed God’s true servants. Isaiah described a similar desire for teachers who would speak smooth things rather than confront sin (Isaiah 30:10).

False assurances could lead the righteous, the vulnerable, or those inclined to obey God into ruin. By declaring judgment or doom where God had not spoken, the false prophets crushed lives and led people away from the path of obedience and preservation. Their ministry brought death rather than life because it replaced God’s word with manipulation. Those who speak falsely in God’s name bear serious responsibility for the harm their teaching causes.

God alone determines life and death, and He will judge those who misuse His name to reverse His righteous purposes.
