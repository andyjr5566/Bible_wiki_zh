Solomon’s reign had entered a season of established peace, wealth, and national strength, enabling Israel to devote its resources to worship, government, and security.

and to Eloth
Eloth, also called Elath, lay near Ezion-geber and served as another important harbor on the gulf. Together these cities connected Israel’s inland kingdom with international maritime trade. Solomon’s building and use of ports showed the breadth of the peace, order, and prosperity God had granted his kingdom. Eloth remained strategically important in later generations, when Amaziah restored it to Judah (2 Kings 14:22).

Deu 2:8 So we passed by our brothers, the descendants of Esau,…

on the coast of Edom.
Edom descended from Esau and often stood in hostility toward Israel, yet during Solomon’s reign its territory was subject to Israelite authority. Control of Edom’s coast enabled Solomon to benefit from sea trade without depending on foreign powers for access to the Red Sea. The Lord’s promises to David included dominion over surrounding enemies, and Solomon’s reign displayed that blessing in a season of stability and abundance.

2 Chronicles 8:18

So Hiram sent him ships captained by his servants,
Hiram king of Tyre strengthened the alliance formed during the Temple’s construction by supplying the maritime resources Israel lacked. Tyre’s sailors and shipbuilders were widely respected throughout the Mediterranean world. Solomon’s kingdom possessed access to the Red Sea port of Ezion-geber, while Hiram provided the experienced personnel needed to conduct long-distance sea trade.

They went with Solomon’s servants to Ophir
Ophir was famed for its gold and other precious resources. Its precise location is unknown, though it was reached through routes connected with the Red Sea and likely lay in Arabia, eastern Africa, or beyond. The expedition reflects the wide reach of Solomon’s trade network and the prosperity granted during the period when he walked in obedience to the Lord.

and acquired from there 450 talents of gold,
Four hundred fifty talents represented an extraordinary amount of wealth. The gold helped supply the royal treasury and supported the splendor associated with Solomon’s kingdom, including the Temple and its service. Yet Solomon’s riches also demonstrated that material abundance, however great, could not preserve a king from spiritual compromise.
