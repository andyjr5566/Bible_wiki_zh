The LORD Exalts the Humble

Psalm 113:1

Hallelujah!
“Hallelujah,” from the Hebrew phrase Hallelu Yah, meaning “Praise the LORD,” calls God’s people to united, joyful worship. Psalm 113 opens the Egyptian Hallel (Psalms 113–118), sung in Israel’s worship and associated with the Passover celebration. Jesus and His disciples likely sang from these psalms after the Last Supper before going to the Mount of Olives (Matthew 26:30). Revelation 19 carries the same cry into heaven, where redeemed multitudes praise God for His righteous judgment and salvation.

Give praise, O servants of the LORD;
The Lord summons His servants to worship Him. Israel, including the priests and Levites who led temple praise, belonged to God by covenant and redemption. All who trust the Lord are likewise called to serve Him with reverence and gratitude. The call to praise arises not merely from duty but from the privilege of belonging to the living God.

praise the name of the LORD.
The name of the LORD reveals His holy character, covenant faithfulness, mercy, power, and saving works. Israel had seen that name displayed in the exodus, wilderness provision, and the Lord’s continuing care for His people. Believers praise God supremely through Jesus Christ, whose name is exalted above every name and before whom every knee will bow (Philippians 2:9–11).

Psalm 113:2

Blessed be the name of the LORD
The covenant name of the LORD reveals the holy, self-existent God who redeemed Israel and remains faithful to His promises. Blessing His name honors all that He is—righteous, merciful, sovereign, and good. Job voiced such worship amid devastating loss, declaring, “Blessed be the name of the LORD” because God’s worthiness does not change with human circumstances.

both now and forevermore.
Praise belongs to the LORD in every generation and beyond the limits of earthly life. Psalm 113 begins the Hallel, sung by Israel during major feasts, joining God’s people across time in adoration. The continual worship of heaven reflects the unchanging reality that the LORD reigns forever and deserves unending praise.

Psalm 113:3

From where the sun rises
The eastern horizon marks the dawning of each day and recalls God’s unfailing care in renewing life and sustaining His creation. His mercies are new every morning because His faithfulness does not fail (Lamentations 3:22–23). Scripture also uses the eastward dawn to portray the unmistakable appearing of the Son of Man, whose coming will be visible to all (Matthew 24:27).

to where it sets,
The western horizon completes the daily course of the sun. Together, east and west encompass the whole inhabited world, declaring that no place lies beyond the reach of God’s rule. The Lord who governs the beginning and end of every day is worthy of worship among every people and nation.

the name of the LORD is praised.
The name of the LORD reveals His holy character, covenant faithfulness, authority, and saving power. Israel knew Him as the great I AM, the eternal God who keeps His promises (Exodus 3:14). Praise belongs to Him continually, not merely in one land or at one hour, because His glory fills the earth. The universal worship of the Lord anticipates the day when every knee will bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord (Philippians 2:9–11).

Psalm 113:4

The LORD is exalted over all the nations,
Every kingdom, ruler, and people remains under God’s sovereign rule. Israel’s worship acknowledged that Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, and every other earthly power were accountable to the LORD, however formidable they appeared. God reigns over the nations from His holy throne (Psalm 47:8), directing history toward His purposes and judging pride, injustice, and idolatry. Jesus Christ fulfills this royal authority as King of kings and Lord of lords, before whom every nation will ultimately stand (Revelation 19:16).

His glory above the heavens.
The created heavens declare God’s greatness, yet they cannot contain the fullness of His majesty. His holiness, power, and wisdom surpass all that humanity can see or measure. Though exalted above creation, the LORD graciously reveals Himself and acts within history for the good of His people. Christ, through whom all things were created and hold together, displays the divine glory that stands above all creation (Colossians 1:16–17).

Psalm 113:5

Who is like the LORD our God,
Israel’s God stands alone in holiness, power, wisdom, and covenant faithfulness. The gods of the surrounding nations were portrayed as limited and dependent, but the LORD created heaven and earth and rules over all He has made. Moses celebrated His incomparability after the Red Sea deliverance: “Who among the gods is like You, O LORD?” (Exodus 15:11). No ruler, idol, angel, or earthly power can rival Him.

Exo 15:11 Who among the gods is like You, O LORD? Who is like You…

Psa 89:6 For who in the skies can compare with the LORD?…

the One enthroned on high?
God reigns in supreme majesty above every earthly and heavenly authority. His throne rests on righteousness and justice (Psalm 89:14), and His exalted position does not make Him distant from His people. Psalm 113 goes on to declare that He stoops to raise the poor and needy, revealing both His transcendence and His merciful care.

Psalm 113:6

He humbles Himself
The Lord, exalted above every nation and reigning in glory beyond the heavens, stoops in mercy to concern Himself with what He has made. His supreme greatness does not remove Him from His creatures. Jesus Christ supremely reveals this divine condescension by taking human flesh and humbling Himself unto death on the cross (Philippians 2:7–8).

God gives deliberate attention to all that occurs within His creation. Nothing escapes His knowledge, including the hidden thoughts, needs, sins, and suffering of mankind. His watchfulness assures both righteous judgment and faithful care.

Psalm 113:7

He raises the poor from the dust
God sovereignly reverses human helplessness and humiliation. Dust recalls mortality, grief, and the low condition of those stripped of honor and resources (Genesis 3:19; 1 Samuel 2:8). His care is not distant sympathy but effective intervention, raising the afflicted into dignity and usefulness. Hannah celebrated this same work of God, who humbles the proud and exalts the lowly according to His righteous purpose.

and lifts the needy from the dump
The ash heap was a place of refuse, sickness, and social rejection, portraying the deepest material and personal desperation. God does not overlook those cast aside by society; He restores them and gives them a place among His people. Mary echoed this truth when she praised the Lord for exalting the humble (Luke 1:52). The Lord’s mercy ultimately points to Christ, who brings those dead in sin into new life and grants them an inheritance among His redeemed (Ephesians 2:4–6).

Psalm 113:8

to seat them with nobles,
God raises those the world overlooks and grants them honor according to His purpose. Joseph rose from slavery and imprisonment to govern Egypt, David was taken from tending sheep to rule Israel, and Esther was placed in Persia’s royal court for the preservation of her people. Such reversals reveal that human rank does not limit God’s sovereign choice.

with the princes of His people.
The Lord gives the humble a place among the leaders and honored members of His covenant people. His grace not only rescues from lowliness but also grants dignity, service, and inheritance. Believers share already in Christ’s exalted life through union with Him and await the day when they will reign with Him in His kingdom.

Psalm 113:9

He settles the barren woman in her home
God graciously reverses the sorrow of women such as Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, Hannah, and Elizabeth, whose infertility brought deep personal grief in a culture where children were treasured as God’s inheritance. Their accounts show that barrenness was never beyond the Lord’s power or outside His compassionate care.

as a joyful mother to her children.
God gives not merely a child but a restored place of joy, belonging, and fruitfulness within the household. Hannah’s praise after Samuel’s birth celebrates the Lord who humbles and exalts, bringing life where hope had seemed absent (1 Samuel 2:1–10). Such deliverance also points to God’s power to make His people spiritually fruitful through His saving work.
