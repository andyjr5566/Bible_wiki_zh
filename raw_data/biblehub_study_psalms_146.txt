Praise the LORD, O My Soul

Psalm 146:1

Hallelujah!
Hallelu Yah means “Praise the LORD” and opens Psalm 146 with a summons to worship. The psalmist joins Israel’s long-standing pattern of responding to God’s saving power, covenant faithfulness, and righteous rule with praise. Hallelujah also appears in the worship of heaven in Revelation 19:1–6, where the redeemed celebrate God’s final victory and judgment.

Psa 111:1 Hallelujah! I will give thanks to the LORD with all my heart…

Psa 113:1 Hallelujah! Give praise, O servants of the LORD…

Praise the LORD,
The LORD is the covenant God of Israel, whose name declares His eternal faithfulness to His people. Praise recognizes that He alone is worthy of trust, unlike earthly rulers whose power and life are temporary. Psalm 146 develops that contrast by calling God’s people to rest their hope in the Creator, Defender of the oppressed, Provider for the needy, and King forever.

Psa 150:6 Let everything that has breath praise the LORD! Hallelujah!

O my soul.
The psalmist addresses his own inner being, calling every part of himself to bless the Lord. Worship is not merely public speech or religious habit; it rises from a heart that remembers who God is and what He has done. Like Psalm 103:1, this personal summons shows that faithful praise involves deliberate devotion, especially when the soul must be redirected from fear, self-reliance, or misplaced trust.

Psalm 146:2

I will praise the LORD all my life;
The psalmist pledges unceasing worship to Yahweh, Israel’s covenant God, whose faithfulness endures beyond every changing circumstance. Praise is not reserved for prosperity or public worship but belongs to the whole course of life. David expressed the same resolve when he wrote, “I will bless the LORD at all times” (Psalm 34:1). Daily prayer, songs, sacrifices, and gathered worship formed part of Israel’s life with God, yet true praise also arose from personal trust and gratitude. God’s eternal reign gives lasting reason for such devotion.

I will sing praises to my God while I have my being.
Singing joins the heart, mind, and body in joyful acknowledgment of God’s goodness. “My God” reveals the confidence of one who knows the Lord personally and rests in His care. Human life is brief and dependent upon God’s sustaining power; therefore every remaining day is an opportunity to honor Him. The commitment anticipates the life of Christ, who perfectly obeyed the Father and offered Himself in faithful worship. It also directs believers to use their earthly lives for God’s glory, until praise is made complete in His presence.

Psalm 146:3

Put not your trust in princes,
Earthly rulers may exercise real authority, yet their power is given, limited, and accountable to God. Israel’s kings repeatedly showed the weakness of human leadership: Saul disobeyed the Lord, and Solomon’s later compromise drew the nation toward idolatry. God permits civil authority, but no ruler can bear the weight of ultimate confidence or replace the Lord’s rule.

in mortal man,
Every human being is frail, temporary, and subject to death. Psalm 146:4 immediately recalls that a man’s breath departs and his plans perish. Political strength, military power, wealth, and wisdom cannot overcome the limits God has placed upon mankind. Blessedness belongs instead to those whose help is the God of Jacob (Psalm 146:5).

Human leaders may provide temporary aid, but they cannot deliver from sin, death, judgment, or the deepest needs of the soul. The Lord brought Israel out of Egypt, preserved His people, and alone proved Himself able to save. Jesus Christ fulfills that saving work perfectly, providing the redemption no human ruler or earthly system can give.

Psalm 146:5

Blessed is he
True happiness rests on God’s favor rather than human strength, wealth, or political power. Psalm 146 contrasts the frailty of earthly rulers with the security of those who depend upon the Lord.

The God who preserved Jacob through exile, danger, family conflict, and return remains faithful to His covenant people. Jacob’s life testified that God’s help is not merely occasional rescue but sustaining presence and fulfilled promise.

Psalm 146:6

the Maker of heaven and earth, the sea, and everything in them.
The Lord created every realm of existence and rules over all He has made. Heaven, earth, and sea belong to Him, including every creature and every human life. The sea, often associated with danger and disorder in Scripture, lies under His command. Because God made all things, no earthly ruler, human power, or false god can rival His authority. The Creator also sustains His creation, giving life and breath to all people.

He remains faithful forever.
God’s faithfulness does not fade with time or fail under changing circumstances. He keeps His covenant promises, judges with righteousness, and preserves His people. Unlike princes, whose help is limited and whose lives quickly pass away, the Lord remains trustworthy in every generation. His enduring faithfulness finds its fullest assurance in Jesus Christ, through whom all things were created and in whom God’s promises are fulfilled.

Psalm 146:7

He executes justice for the oppressed
The LORD defends those who are crushed by human power, refusing to overlook oppression or corruption. His law required Israel to protect widows, orphans, the poor, and foreigners because He Himself champions their cause (Deuteronomy 10:18; 24:17–22). The prophets condemned leaders who used authority for gain while neglecting the vulnerable (Isaiah 1:17; Micah 6:8). God’s justice may be delayed in human eyes, but He sees every wrong and will judge righteously. Jesus revealed this concern in His ministry to the poor, the afflicted, and those cast aside by society (Luke 4:18–19).

and gives food to the hungry.
The God who judges evil also supplies the needs of those who depend on Him. He gave Israel manna throughout their wilderness journey, teaching them that life is sustained by His provision (Exodus 16:4, 35). Jesus fed hungry crowds in the wilderness, displaying divine compassion and pointing to Himself as the bread of life (Matthew 14:13–21; John 6:35). God often provides through the willing service and generosity of His people, who are called to care materially for those in need (Acts 2:44–45; James 2:15–16).

The LORD sets the prisoners free,
The LORD delivered Israel from literal slavery in Egypt and later promised release to captives exiled in Babylon (Exodus 6:6; Isaiah 49:9). His power reaches beyond earthly chains, for sin enslaves the human heart and brings death. Through His death and resurrection, Jesus frees believers from sin’s dominion and gives them new life in righteousness (Romans 6:18, 22). His ministry also showed His authority over demonic oppression, restoring those held captive by evil to soundness and peace (Mark 5:1–20).

Psalm 146:8

the LORD opens the eyes of the blind,
God’s power over blindness reveals His compassion and His authority over human weakness. Jesus healed blind men repeatedly, including the man born blind in John 9:1–7, displaying works that identified Him as the promised Messiah (Isaiah 35:5). Physical healing also points to humanity’s deeper need, for sin darkens the mind and heart until God grants understanding. Paul prayed that believers would have “the eyes of your hearts enlightened” so that they might know the hope and riches of God’s calling (Ephesians 1:18).

the LORD lifts those who are weighed down,
The Lord sees those bowed beneath suffering, poverty, oppression, grief, guilt, and the consequences of living in a fallen world. Israel’s history testified that He heard the cries of slaves in Egypt and delivered them from bondage. Jesus extended the same divine care when He called the weary and burdened to come to Him for rest (Matt. 11:28–30). God does not merely observe the afflicted; He sustains them and gives strength to endure.

No sinner possesses such righteousness independently, yet Christ is perfectly righteous and grants His righteousness to all who believe in Him (2 Cor. 5:21). God’s favor toward the righteous therefore assures His people that faithful obedience is neither unseen nor forgotten.

Psalm 146:9

The LORD protects foreigners;
Foreigners in Israel often lacked land, clan protection, and legal standing. The Law commanded Israel to treat them with justice and love, remembering Israel’s own bondage and dependence on God in Egypt (Exodus 22:21; Leviticus 19:34; Deuteronomy 10:18–19). God’s care for the sojourner reveals His righteous concern for those without earthly security and anticipates the gathering of people from every nation into Christ.

Deu 10:18-19 He executes justice for the fatherless and widow,…

Lev 19:33-34 When a foreigner resides with you in your land,…

He sustains the fatherless and the widow,
Orphans and widows were especially exposed to poverty, exploitation, and loss because they lacked a husband or father to provide and defend them. God repeatedly commanded His people to protect them, making such care an evidence of faithful worship (Deuteronomy 24:17–22; James 1:27). The Lord Himself supplies what human strength and social structures cannot provide. Jesus Christ fulfills this pattern of divine compassion, receiving the helpless and giving eternal life to all who depend upon Him.

God does not merely observe oppression; He overturns the plans of those who pursue evil and prey upon the vulnerable. Though the wicked may appear to prosper for a time, their path ends under God’s judgment (Psalm 37:12–13; Proverbs 16:9). Christ’s death and resurrection secured the final defeat of sin, death, and every hostile power, assuring God’s people that wickedness will not prevail.

Psalm 146:10

The LORD reigns forever,
Earthly rulers rise and fall, but the Lord’s dominion has no beginning or end. Nebuchadnezzar confessed that God’s kingdom is “an everlasting kingdom” and His rule endures through every generation (Daniel 4:34). His sovereign authority over creation, nations, history, and salvation gives lasting security to those who trust Him.

your God, O Zion,
Zion, Jerusalem, was the place where the temple stood and where the Lord made His dwelling known among His covenant people. The God who rules all things is not distant from His people; He binds Himself to them in faithful covenant love. Zion also anticipates the New Jerusalem, where God will dwell with His redeemed people forever (Revelation 21:2–3).

for all generations.
The Lord’s faithfulness does not expire with one age or depend on the strength of one people. His promise to be God to Abraham and his descendants finds its fullest fulfillment in Christ, through whom all who belong to Him receive the promised inheritance (Genesis 17:7; Galatians 3:29). Every generation is called to remember His works and proclaim His reign.

Hallelujah!
Praise is the fitting conclusion to the knowledge that the Lord reigns eternally and keeps His covenant. The heavenly multitude will cry “Hallelujah” when God openly judges evil and establishes His righteous kingdom (Revelation 19:1–6). His everlasting kingship calls His people to worship with confidence, joy, and reverence.
