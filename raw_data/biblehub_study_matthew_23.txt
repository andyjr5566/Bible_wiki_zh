Woes to Scribes and Pharisees

Matthew 23:1

Then Jesus spoke to the crowds and to His disciples:
Jesus addressed the people gathered in the temple courts during His final week in Jerusalem, shortly before His crucifixion. The scribes and Pharisees held great religious influence, occupying Moses’ seat as teachers and interpreters of the Law. Jesus spoke publicly so that ordinary people and His disciples would discern the difference between honoring God’s Word and imitating leaders whose outward devotion concealed pride and unbelief.

Matthew 23:2

“The scribes and Pharisees
The scribes were trained interpreters and teachers of the Law of Moses, while the Pharisees were a prominent religious movement devoted to applying the Law and the traditions of the elders to daily life. Many people regarded them as guides in matters of righteousness. Jesus exposed the danger of religious authority separated from humble obedience, especially when outward devotion concealed pride, injustice, and unbelief.

Moses’ seat represented the recognized responsibility to teach God’s Law to Israel. Synagogues sometimes contained a special seat for the teacher, reflecting the role of explaining and applying the Scriptures to the congregation. Jesus acknowledged that the scribes and Pharisees occupied an office connected with Moses’ teaching, yet He soon condemned their failure to practice what they taught. True spiritual authority remains accountable to God’s Word and must be joined to faithful obedience.

The scribes and Pharisees occupied “Moses’ seat,” serving as recognized teachers of God’s Law in Israel. Jesus upheld the authority of the Law given through Moses and called His hearers to obey God’s commands carefully, as Deuteronomy 5:32 commands. Their office did not make every tradition or interpretation infallible; Jesus had already condemned traditions that set aside God’s Word (Mark 7:8–13). Yet wherever they faithfully taught the Law, the people were responsible to obey it.

Jesus exposed leaders whose public reputation for devotion concealed hearts marked by pride, self-interest, and neglect of justice, mercy, and faithfulness (Matthew 23:23). Isaiah had condemned similar hypocrisy among those who honored God outwardly while remaining far from Him in heart (Isaiah 29:13).

The leaders imposed heavy demands upon others while failing to bear those demands themselves (Matthew 23:4). Jesus calls His followers to a faith in which doctrine and conduct agree. Hearing God’s Word without obeying it brings self-deception, but those who do the Word demonstrate genuine devotion to the Lord (James 1:22). Jesus Himself perfectly fulfilled the Law and embodied the righteousness He taught.

Matthew 23:5

Jesus exposed leaders whose religious activity sought public admiration rather than God’s approval. Their outward devotion concealed hearts that often resisted the very righteousness they taught. Isaiah had condemned worship offered by lips while the heart remained far from God (Isaiah 29:13). Jesus likewise warned against giving, praying, and fasting for human recognition, since such display forfeits the Father’s reward (Matthew 6:1–6). God desires obedience flowing from faith, humility, and love.

They broaden their phylacteries
Phylacteries, or tefillin, were small leather boxes containing passages of Scripture and worn during prayer in remembrance of Deuteronomy 6:8 and 11:18. The command called Israel to keep God’s Word continually before mind and action. Enlarging these visible symbols turned a reminder of submission to God into an advertisement of personal piety. The law was never intended to produce spiritual status; it was meant to direct the heart toward wholehearted love for the Lord.

Jesus wore such tassels in faithful obedience to the law (Matthew 9:20), yet He did not use them to attract honor. The leaders’ exaggerated tassels revealed a concern to appear more devout than others. Religious signs have value only when joined to a life that truly fears God and practices mercy, justice, and faithfulness (Matthew 23:23).

Jesus exposed religious leaders who sought such recognition as evidence of personal importance rather than serving others. He taught His disciples to choose humility, trusting God rather than social acclaim to grant honor (Luke 14:7–11).

The Pharisees desired positions that advertised authority, yet their concern for appearance concealed hearts often unwilling to practice the mercy, justice, and faithfulness they taught. God’s people are not to show favoritism according to visible status, but to honor those who humbly obey Him (James 2:1–4; Matthew 23:11–12).

Christ forbids the desire for titles that feed pride or establish spiritual domination. His servants are called to humility, remembering that God gives grace to the humble (James 4:6).
