Jesus and Zacchaeus

Luke 19:1

Then Jesus entered Jericho
Jericho stood in the Jordan Valley as one of the world’s oldest inhabited cities and a major crossroads for trade and travel. Israel first captured Jericho under Joshua’s leadership, when the Lord brought down its walls and opened the way into the promised land (Joshua 6). Its later rebuilding under Hiel fulfilled Joshua’s warning concerning the city (1 Kings 16:34). Jesus entered this historically significant city on His final journey toward Jerusalem, where He would die and rise again. In Jericho, the Lord brought salvation to Zacchaeus, a man widely despised because of his role as a tax collector.

His movement through Jericho led directly to the salvation of Zacchaeus and displayed His mission to seek and save the lost (Luke 19:10). The road through Jericho became a place of mercy, repentance, and transformed life before Jesus continued toward the cross.

Luke alone records his encounter with Jesus, showing that no person stood beyond the reach of Christ’s mercy.

Jesus came to His Father’s house as the promised Lord who would suddenly come to His temple, fulfilling Malachi 3:1.
