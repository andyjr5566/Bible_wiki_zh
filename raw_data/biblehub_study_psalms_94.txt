The LORD Will Not Forget His People

Psalm 94:1

Psalm 94:2

Rise up, O Judge of the earth;
God is the Judge of all the earth, perfectly righteous in every verdict and sovereign over every ruler, nation, and hidden deed. The psalmist appeals to the Lord to act openly against evil, confident that injustice does not escape His sight. Abraham likewise rested in God’s righteous character when he asked, “Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?” God’s judgment reveals His holiness and vindicates those who fear Him.

Isa 33:22 For the LORD is our Judge, the LORD is our lawgiver,…

render a reward to the proud.
The proud exalt themselves above God, trusting their own strength and resisting His authority. Their apparent security is temporary, for the Lord repays every person according to his deeds. Scripture repeatedly warns that pride leads to destruction, while God gives grace to the humble. The plea anticipates the final judgment, when Christ will judge the living and the dead and no arrogance, oppression, or rebellion will remain unanswered.

Psalm 94:3

How long will the wicked, O LORD,
The psalmist appeals to the covenant God who sees oppression and promises to judge evil. Israel’s faith did not deny the reality of injustice; it brought its anguish directly before the Lord. Habakkuk raised the same question when violence, strife, and perverted justice filled Judah (Habakkuk 1:2–4). God’s delay is not indifference, for He acts according to His righteous purpose and appointed time.

how long will the wicked exult?
The wicked not only prosper for a season but often boast as though they will never answer to God. Their apparent triumph tests the faith of the righteous, especially when power and wealth seem to be signs of divine favor. Job also wrestled with the prosperity of those who defy God (Job 21:7–15). Psalm 94 answers the lament by affirming that the Lord knows human thoughts and will not abandon His people (Psalm 94:11, 14). Final justice will be fully revealed when Christ returns to repay affliction to those who afflict His people and to establish His righteous kingdom (2 Thessalonians 1:6–10).

Psalm 94:4

They pour out arrogant words;
The wicked speak as though they are answerable to no one, displaying contempt for God and disregard for those harmed by their actions. Their proud speech reveals hearts that reject the fear of the Lord, which Scripture identifies as the beginning of wisdom. Such defiance may appear unchecked for a time, but God hears every boast and will vindicate those who trust Him.

all workers of iniquity boast.
Those who practice evil openly celebrate their power, success, and apparent freedom from judgment. The psalmist places their confidence against the certainty of God’s rule: human pride cannot overturn His justice or escape His knowledge. Pride precedes destruction, and the Lord ultimately humbles those who exalt themselves against Him.

Psalm 94:5

They crush Your people, O LORD;
The psalmist appeals to the God of justice while wicked rulers and oppressors afflict those who belong to Him. Israel had known such cruelty in Egypt, under hostile nations during the judges, and through the Assyrian and Babylonian invasions. Their violence could not cancel God’s covenant or escape His judgment. Jesus likewise warned His disciples that tribulation would mark life in a fallen world, yet He promised peace and final victory in Himself.

they oppress Your heritage.
Israel was the LORD’s treasured possession, chosen by grace and set apart for His purposes. Those who harm God’s covenant people set themselves against the God who claims them as His own. The psalm looks beyond present suffering to the certainty that the Judge of all the earth will defend His people, expose evil, and establish righteousness. Believers in Christ share that assurance, awaiting the day when God removes suffering and oppression forever.

Psalm 94:6

God commanded Israel to protect widows and resident foreigners, who often lacked family power, land, and legal advocates (Ex. 22:21–22; Deut. 10:18). Their murder openly defies the Lord’s justice and reveals a society ruled by cruelty rather than covenant faithfulness.

they murder the fatherless.
Fatherless children were especially exposed to oppression after losing their provider and defender. The Lord identifies Himself as the protector of orphans (Psalm 68:5) and holds their attackers accountable. The psalmist’s appeal anticipates God’s righteous intervention against those who believe their violence escapes His notice.

The psalmist confronts those who oppress God’s people while imagining that the LORD neither sees nor judges. Their blindness is moral and spiritual: they disregard the God who formed the eye and planted the ear. Psalm 94:9

The psalmist confronts those who imagine that the Lord ignores violence, injustice, and human rebellion. The Creator who designed the ear hears every word, cry, and secret counsel. God’s knowledge reaches beyond outward actions to the thoughts and intentions of the heart. He hears the prayers of His people and remains attentive to the oppression they endure.

The God who fashioned human sight is never blind to evil or indifferent to righteousness. His eyes are upon all humanity, and no deed escapes His holy judgment. The Lord sees the affliction of His people, the pride of the wicked, and the hidden purposes of every heart. His perfect knowledge assures believers that He will judge evil rightly and sustain those who trust Him.

Psalm 94:11

The LORD knows the thoughts of man,
God sees beyond outward conduct into motives, plans, fears, and secret reasoning. Psalm 94 comforts the oppressed by declaring that unjust rulers and evildoers cannot hide their designs from the Judge of all the earth. The Lord who formed the ear and the eye also knows the human heart completely. His knowledge exposes hypocrisy, vindicates the righteous, and assures believers that no wicked scheme will finally escape His justice. Scripture repeatedly joins God’s searching knowledge with His righteous judgment (1 Samuel 16:7; Jeremiah 17:10; Hebrews 4:12–13).

that they are futile.
Human wisdom, when set against God or pursued apart from Him, cannot accomplish lasting good or overturn His purposes. The proud calculations of the wicked are empty because God will bring their injustice to account. Paul cites Psalm 94:11 to show that the wisdom of the world is foolish before God (1 Corinthians 3:19–20). Human beings need the wisdom God provides, ultimately revealed in Christ, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge (Colossians 2:3).

Psalm 94:12

Blessed is the man You discipline, O LORD,
Divine discipline reveals the LORD’s fatherly care rather than His rejection. He corrects His people to turn them from destructive paths, deepen their trust, and shape them in holiness. Israel experienced such correction in the wilderness when unbelief and disobedience brought consequences, yet God continued to preserve His covenant people and lead them toward the promised land. Hebrews 12:6 confirms that the Lord disciplines those He loves, treating them as true sons and daughters. The covenant name LORD declares that the God who corrects His people is personally faithful to them.

Heb 12:5-6 And you have forgotten the exhortation…

and teach from Your law,
Correction is joined to instruction. God does not merely expose sin; He teaches His people the way of wisdom, righteousness, and life through His revealed Word. The law given through Moses showed Israel how to worship the LORD, live justly, and remain distinct from the nations. Psalm 119 celebrates that same Word as a lamp for the believer’s path. God’s discipline brings repentance, and His instruction establishes obedience. Jesus Christ fulfilled the law and taught its true righteousness, calling His disciples to hear His words and build their lives upon them.

God remained faithful through Israel’s afflictions, including exile and restoration, and He continues to be a refuge for His own (Psalm 46:1). Christ gives the deepest fulfillment of this promise, granting rest to weary sinners who come to Him in faith (Matthew 11:28–30).

Scripture repeatedly portrays the pit as the downfall prepared for those who plot destruction against others (Psalm 7:15–16; Proverbs 26:27). The Lord will vindicate His people and repay evil with perfect righteousness (Romans 12:19).

Psalm 94:14

For the LORD will not forsake His people;
Israel’s security rests in the LORD’s covenant faithfulness, not in her own strength or unbroken obedience. In a psalm lamenting wicked rulers and oppression, God’s people are assured that injustice does not mean God has withdrawn His care. He preserved Israel through bondage, wilderness testing, invasion, exile, and restoration, fulfilling His promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The LORD’s discipline corrects His people but does not cancel His covenant purpose. Christ’s promise to remain with His disciples to the end of the age reveals the same faithful presence for all who belong to Him.

He will never abandon His heritage.
Israel was the LORD’s inheritance and treasured possession among the nations, chosen to bear witness to His holiness and to receive His promises. Though many in Israel rebelled, God preserved a remnant and continued His redemptive plan through the line of promise until the coming of the Messiah. God’s gifts and calling are irrevocable, and His purposes for Israel will reach their appointed fulfillment. Through Christ, believers receive an eternal inheritance, secured by His death and resurrection and guaranteed by the Holy Spirit.

Psalm 94:18

If I say, “My foot is slipping,”
This phrase reflects a moment of vulnerability and instability, both physically and spiritually. In biblical times, the metaphor of a “slipping foot” often symbolized a loss of stability or security, akin to falling into sin or facing overwhelming trials. The psalmist acknowledges human frailty and the constant threat of stumbling in life’s journey. This imagery is echoed in other scriptures, such as Psalm 73:2, where the psalmist confesses, “But as for me, my feet had almost slipped; I had nearly lost my foothold.” It highlights the need for divine assistance in maintaining spiritual steadfastness.

Psalm 94:19

When anxiety overwhelms me,
This phrase captures the human experience of deep distress and worry. In the biblical context, anxiety often arises from external threats or internal struggles. The Psalms frequently address the theme of anxiety, reflecting the psalmist’s reliance on God during times of trouble. Historically, the Israelites faced numerous challenges, including invasions, exile, and personal trials, which would have contributed to feelings of anxiety. This phrase resonates with other scriptures, such as Philippians 4:6-7, which encourages believers to present their requests to God in times of anxiety. The overwhelming nature of anxiety is acknowledged, yet it is also a call to seek divine intervention.

God’s comfort is not merely relief from trouble but the sustaining assurance of His covenant faithfulness, presence, and promised justice. He consoles His people as a shepherd cares for his flock and as a father upholds his children. The comfort promised in Isaiah 40 finds its fullest fulfillment in Jesus Christ, who gives peace to troubled hearts and rest to the weary. God’s consolation produces deep joy even when outward circumstances remain difficult.

Earthly rulers may wield real authority, yet no government founded on injustice can share fellowship with the holy Judge. Israel’s history included kings and foreign empires that oppressed the innocent, but the Lord never approved their evil or surrendered His sovereign rule.

Pharaoh’s command against Hebrew infants and Haman’s decree against the Jews reveal how injustice can be legalized without becoming righteous. God sees every oppressive statute, hears the cry of His people, and will judge rulers who use authority against His commandments. Psalm 94:21

They band together against the righteous
The wicked do not merely resist godly people as individuals; they often unite their influence, schemes, and authority against those committed to God’s ways. Daniel’s opponents conspired to exploit the law and cast him into the lions’ den because his faithful conduct gave them no legitimate accusation (Daniel 6:4–9). Jesus likewise warned that the world’s hatred of His disciples would reflect its prior hatred of Him (John 15:18–20). Such hostility reveals the continuing conflict between rebellion against God and those who trust and obey Him.

Psa 37:12-14 The wicked scheme against the righteous…

Psa 83:3-4 With cunning they scheme against Your people…

Psa 56:6 They conspire, they lurk, they watch my steps…

and condemn the innocent to death.
Corrupt rulers and false witnesses can manipulate courts and public opinion to destroy those who have done no wrong. Naboth was murdered through fabricated charges when Ahab and Jezebel coveted his vineyard (1 Kings 21:1–16). The supreme example came in the condemnation of Jesus Christ, whose judges acknowledged His innocence yet delivered Him to crucifixion under political pressure (Matthew 27:24–26). God sees every perversion of justice, and the innocent suffering of Christ became the appointed means of redemption while also assuring believers that no injustice escapes God’s final judgment.

Surrounded by arrogant evildoers and oppressed by injustice, the psalmist rests in the covenant faithfulness of the LORD. A stronghold was a fortified height or secure city where enemies could not easily reach those within. Rocky heights and caves in Israel offered shelter from pursuit, as David experienced while fleeing Saul. The psalmist calls the LORD “my God,” expressing personal trust in the unchanging God who judges wickedness and preserves His people. God remains the sure refuge for all who take shelter in Him, ultimately fulfilled in Christ, the rock upon whom believers stand securely (1 Corinthians 10:4).

Psalm 94:23

He will bring upon them their own iniquity
God’s judgment answers evil with its fitting consequence. Those who devise injustice, oppress the vulnerable, and reject the Lord eventually encounter the ruin their sin has produced. Scripture repeatedly affirms this moral order: “whatever a man sows, this he will also reap” (Galatians 6:7). Israel’s history demonstrates the same truth, as persistent idolatry and covenant rebellion led to the judgments of exile announced by the prophets.

Pro 5:22 The iniquities of a wicked man entrap him;…

Jer 2:19 Your own evil will discipline you; your own apostasies…

Psa 7:16 His trouble recoils on himself, and his violence falls…

and destroy them for their wickedness.
Wickedness is not a harmless alternative to righteousness; it provokes the holy opposition of God and leads toward destruction. The Lord’s judgment upon Sodom and Gomorrah illustrates His response to entrenched evil (Genesis 19). The psalmist has especially in view arrogant evildoers who crush God’s people while imagining that the Lord does not see or act (Psalm 94:4–7). Their apparent security is temporary, for God’s justice reaches every hidden work.

The LORD our God will destroy them.
The covenant God of Israel is not distant from the suffering of His people. He sees oppression, hears the cry of the afflicted, and acts in faithfulness to His righteous character. Vengeance belongs to the Lord alone (Deuteronomy 32:35), freeing believers from personal retaliation and calling them to trust His judgment. God’s final justice will be fully revealed through Jesus Christ, whom He appointed as Judge of the living and the dead (Acts 10:42).
