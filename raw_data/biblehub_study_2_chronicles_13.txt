Abijah, called Abijam in 1 Kings, was the son of Rehoboam and grandson of Solomon. His political separation from Judah became a deliberate religious rebellion against God’s ordained order.

Bethel,
Bethel was a place of deep patriarchal significance, where Jacob had worshiped the Lord after his vision of the stairway to heaven (Genesis 28:10–22). Jeroboam later made it a center of false worship by placing one of his golden calves there (1 Kings 12:28–29). Its capture brought Judah’s influence into a city that had become identified with Israel’s spiritual compromise.

The fuller record included Abijah’s conduct as king, his administration of Judah, and the public address he delivered before the battle at Mount Zemaraim.
