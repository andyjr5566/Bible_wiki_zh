Isaiah 43:2

When you pass through the waters, I will be with you;
The Lord speaks to Israel, His redeemed and covenant people, assuring them that coming judgments and hardships will not cancel His presence. Israel had passed through the Red Sea when God delivered them from Egypt (Exodus 14:21–22), and later crossed the Jordan as He brought them into the land He promised (Joshua 3:14–17). Waters often portray danger, disorder, and forces beyond human control, yet the God who rules the sea remains with His people within every threatening circumstance.

Israel would endure exile and affliction, but neither foreign powers nor divine discipline would destroy the people God had chosen for His redemptive purposes.

Israel had endured judgment and exile because of sin, yet the Lord had not abandoned the people He called by name. God’s regard for Israel reveals His faithfulness to His covenant and His intention to display His glory through them among the nations.

Israel’s restoration would not rest on military strength or favorable circumstances, but on the faithfulness of the God who redeemed and called her by name. The command recalls God’s charge to Joshua (Joshua 1:9) and His deliverance of Israel at the Red Sea (Exodus 14:13–14). God’s abiding presence reaches its fullest revelation in Jesus Christ, Immanuel, “God with us” (Matthew 1:23).

The return from exile also anticipates God’s gathering of believers from every nation into Christ’s kingdom.
