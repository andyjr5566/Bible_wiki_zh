The LORD Loves the Gates of Zion

Psalm 87:1

A Psalm of the sons of Korah.
Korah’s descendants served as Levitical musicians and temple singers despite their ancestor’s rebellion against Moses. Their continued place in Israel’s worship displays God’s mercy across generations. The Korahite psalms repeatedly exalt God’s reign, His dwelling place, and the blessedness of those who draw near to Him.

Psalm 87 was written for corporate worship, joining praise with instruction. Israel sang of Jerusalem not merely as a national capital but as the city chosen by God for His name and presence.

Psalm 87:2

The LORD loves the gates of Zion
Zion, the city of David and site of the temple, held a unique place in God’s covenant purposes. Its gates represented the life of the holy city, where worshipers entered, elders judged, and the people gathered. God’s love for Zion centered on His chosen dwelling among His people and anticipated the heavenly Jerusalem, where He will dwell with the redeemed forever.

more than all the dwellings of Jacob.
God’s special love for Zion did not diminish His covenant care for all Israel, the descendants of Jacob. Jerusalem became the center of Israel’s worship after David established his throne there and Solomon built the temple. The earthly Zion pointed forward to Christ, the true meeting place between God and humanity, and to the church and the New Jerusalem as the people and dwelling of God.

Psalm 87:3

Glorious things are ascribed to you,
God has spoken of Jerusalem with honor because He chose it as the place where His name would dwell. There David established his throne, Solomon built the temple, and the sacrifices pointed forward to the atoning work of Christ. Jerusalem stands at the center of God’s covenant dealings with Israel and of many prophetic promises concerning the coming King and His kingdom.

O city of God.
Jerusalem’s greatness rests not in human power or earthly beauty, but in the presence and purpose of God. The prophets look beyond the city’s troubled history to the day when the Messiah will reign from Zion and the nations will come to worship the Lord. Jesus entered Jerusalem as King, died and rose again nearby, and will return in glory according to God’s promise. The earthly city also directs hope toward the heavenly Jerusalem, where God will dwell forever with His redeemed people.

Psalm 87:4

“I will mention Rahab and Babylon among those who know Me—
Rahab is a poetic name for Egypt, recalling its pride, power, and historic oppression of Israel. Babylon would later become the empire that destroyed Jerusalem and carried Judah into exile. God’s purpose reaches beyond Israel’s enemies and oppressors: He will bring people from nations marked by rebellion into the knowledge of Himself. The Lord alone determines the boundaries of His people, and His saving mercy is not limited by national history or human hostility.

Isa 19:21 The LORD will make Himself known to Egypt…

Isa 51:9 Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the LORD.…

Jer 51:7 Babylon was a gold cup in the hand of the LORD,…

along with Philistia, Tyre, and Cush—
Philistia represented a longstanding military threat on Israel’s western border, while Tyre was a wealthy Phoenician city celebrated for commerce and often entangled in idolatry and pride. Cush lay far to the south, representing distant peoples beyond Israel’s immediate world. The gathering of these lands anticipates the day when peoples from every direction will acknowledge the God of Zion. Israel’s God is not a tribal deity but the sovereign Lord of all nations.

when I say, ‘This one was born in Zion.’”
Zion is Jerusalem as the chosen dwelling place of God and the center of His kingdom. God counts men and women from formerly hostile nations as natives of Zion because He gives them a new identity among His redeemed people. Physical ancestry does not secure citizenship in God’s kingdom; faith in the Lord brings people into His covenant family. The promise points forward to the inclusion of Gentiles through Christ, who has made believing Jews and Gentiles one people in Him.

Psalm 87:5

And it will be said of Zion:
Zion, Jerusalem, stands as the city God chose for His name and the earthly center of His redemptive purposes. The proclamation anticipates the day when her God-given dignity and glory will be openly acknowledged. Her future exaltation accords with Isaiah 2:2–3, when the nations will seek the Lord’s instruction from Zion, and with the New Jerusalem, where God dwells eternally with His redeemed people.

“This one and that one were born in her,
People once identified with distant nations will be counted as Zion’s own citizens. Psalm 87:4 names Egypt, Babylon, Philistia, Tyre, and Cush, showing that God’s grace reaches beyond Israel to gather worshipers from every people. Through faith in Christ, those formerly far off are brought into God’s household and share in the blessings promised through Abraham (Genesis 12:3; Ephesians 2:11–22).

and the Most High Himself will establish her.”
Zion’s security rests upon the sovereign work of the Most High, not upon human strength or political power. God established Jerusalem as the place of His dwelling, preserved His covenant purposes through its history, and will fulfill His promises concerning His kingdom. Christ, the cornerstone, establishes His people as God’s spiritual house while assuring the final triumph of God’s dwelling among humanity (Ephesians 2:19–22; Revelation 21:3–4).

Psalm 87:6

The LORD will record in the register of the peoples:
The Lord Himself will enroll men and women from the nations as belonging to Zion. His record is complete, personal, and righteous, identifying those received into His covenant people. The nations once known for opposition to Jerusalem—including Egypt, Babylon, Philistia, Tyre, and Cush—will not remain outside God’s saving purpose. Scripture later reveals the final judgment books and the book of life, where God’s perfect knowledge confirms both judgment and belonging (Exodus 32:32–33; Revelation 20:12).

“This one was born in Zion.”
The immediate picture is of people from foreign lands being counted as native-born citizens of Zion. God grants them a standing they could never obtain through natural descent or earthly heritage. The promise looks forward to the gathering of the nations under the reign of the Messiah, when believers from every people become fellow citizens in God’s household. Through Christ, those born again are joined to the heavenly Jerusalem and numbered among God’s redeemed people (John 3:3–7; Hebrews 12:22–23).

Psalm 87:7

Singers and pipers will proclaim,
Music marked Israel’s public worship, especially in connection with the sanctuary, festivals, and the celebration of God’s saving acts. The singers and musicians join the nations named earlier in the psalm in honoring Zion, the city God chose for His dwelling. Their praise anticipates the day when people from every land will be counted among God’s people and rejoice in His presence.

“All my springs of joy are in You.”
Zion is addressed as the place from which blessing flows because the Lord established His name there. Springs supplied life and refreshment in a dry land, and the image portrays God as the true source of spiritual life, joy, and renewal. The hope embodied in Zion reaches its fulfillment in Christ, who gives living water to all who come to Him in faith.
