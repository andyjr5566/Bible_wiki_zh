John 14:13

And I will do whatever you ask in My name,
Jesus promises to answer the prayers of His disciples as they ask under His authority and in harmony with His character, teaching, and redeeming purpose. Prayer in Jesus’ name is not a formula for obtaining personal desires; it is the approach of those who belong to Him and seek the accomplishment of His will. The promise follows Jesus’ assurance that His followers will continue His works as He returns to the Father (John 14:12). Through prayer, believers depend on the risen Christ to carry forward His mission in the world. Matthew 7:7–8 calls disciples to ask, seek, and knock, while 1 John 5:14–15 clarifies that God hears requests made according to His will.

so that the Father may be glorified in the Son.
Answered prayer ultimately displays the Father’s glory through the Son’s power, faithfulness, and saving work. Jesus does not act independently from the Father, for the Father and the Son are united in purpose and nature. As Christ supplies what His people need to obey Him, bear witness, and serve others, God’s character is made known. Jesus’ coming death, resurrection, and exaltation would bring the fullest glory to the Father, fulfilling the redemptive plan announced throughout Scripture. Every work accomplished through the Son leads to the honor of the Father.

John 14:15

Jesus grounds obedience in personal devotion to Himself. In the Upper Room, facing His arrest and departure, He calls the disciples to remain bound to Him by love rather than fear or mere duty. God promised under the new covenant to write His law upon the hearts of His people (Jeremiah 31:33), enabling them to desire what He commands. Obedience guards Christ’s teaching as precious truth and puts it into practice.

Jesus possesses divine authority to command His followers. His commands include faith in Him, love for one another, humility, purity, forgiveness, prayer, and faithful witness. Above all, He commands His disciples to love one another as He has loved them (John [Yahweh is gracious] 13:34). Such obedience reflects Christ’s character and demonstrates that His disciples belong to Him.

Jesus rises to meet the suffering appointed for Him. He does not flee the events ahead but proceeds willingly toward His arrest, trial, and crucifixion. The disciples are called to leave the upper room with Him, moving toward the Mount of Olives and Gethsemane, where the path of obedience will lead Jesus to the cross.
