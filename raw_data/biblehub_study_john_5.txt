John the Baptist faithfully identified Jesus as the Lamb of God and bore public witness that He was the promised Messiah. Yet John’s witness, though divinely appointed, pointed beyond himself. Jesus possessed a greater testimony because the evidence of His identity came directly from the Father through the works entrusted to Him.

For the works that the Father has given Me to accomplish—
Every miracle, act of mercy, and authoritative word belonged to the mission the Father gave the Son. Jesus did not act independently or merely display supernatural power; He carried out the Father’s saving purpose in the world. Isaiah had foretold that the coming of God’s salvation would be marked by opened blind eyes, healed limbs, and restored speech (Isaiah 35:5–6).

Jhn 17:4 I have glorified You on earth by accomplishing…

Jhn 4:34 “My food is to do the will of Him who sent Me”…

Jhn 9:4 While it is daytime, we must do the works…

the very works I am doing—
Jesus’ ministry openly demonstrated the arrival of God’s kingdom. He healed the sick, cast out demons, gave sight to the blind, fed the hungry, and raised the dead. These signs revealed His authority over sin, disease, nature, and death, confirming that He is the eternal Son who had taken on flesh.

testify about Me that the Father has sent Me.
The works themselves bore witness that Jesus came from the Father as the Messiah and Savior. Those who received His works rightly were called to see not only miraculous acts but the Father’s confirmation of the Son. Jesus’ mission proceeded from the Father’s love, leading ultimately to the cross, resurrection, and the gift of eternal life.

John 5:37

And the Father who sent Me has Himself testified about Me.
The Father’s witness confirms that Jesus came by divine commission and speaks with God’s own authority. He testified through the works entrusted to the Son, through the Scriptures that foretold the Messiah, and audibly at Jesus’ baptism and transfiguration, declaring Him to be the beloved Son. The Father’s testimony establishes Jesus as the promised Christ, the One to whom Moses, David, Isaiah, and the prophets pointed.

Jhn 8:18 I am One who testifies about Myself, and the Father, who sent Me…

Mat 3:17 And a voice from heaven said, “This is My beloved…

Mat 17:5 While Peter was still speaking, a bright cloud enveloped…

You have never heard His voice nor seen His form,
Jesus exposes the spiritual estrangement of those who searched the Scriptures yet refused the One whom the Scriptures revealed. God had spoken to Moses and the prophets, and Israel had heard His voice at Sinai, though they saw no visible form of Him. The religious leaders before Jesus possessed the record of God’s revelation but did not know the Father because they rejected His Son. Jesus alone makes the invisible God known, for He is the perfect image of the Father and the mediator through whom God is revealed to humanity.

John 5:38

nor does His word abide in you,
God’s word was entrusted to Israel through the Law, the Prophets, and the Writings, and Scripture was read publicly and taught diligently in Jewish life. Yet knowledge of the Scriptures had not produced obedience or faith in the leaders confronting Jesus. God’s word abides in a person when it is received inwardly, shapes the heart, and bears fruit in faithful response to God. Psalm 119:11 joins God’s word in the heart with victory over sin.

1Jn 2:14 I have written to you, fathers, because you know Him…

Jhn 8:31 So He said to the Jews who had believed Him,…

Col 3:16 Let the word of Christ richly dwell within you…

because you do not believe
Their unbelief was revealed by their refusal to accept the testimony of John the Baptist, the works of Jesus, the Father, and the Scriptures. Faith in John’s Gospel involves more than acknowledging facts; it means trusting Jesus, receiving Him as God’s Son, and submitting to the salvation He provides. Their study of Scripture became fruitless because they would not believe the One to whom Scripture pointed.

Jhn 16:9 in regard to sin, because they do not believe in Me;

Mrk 16:16 Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved…

the One He sent.
Jesus came from the Father with divine authority to reveal God and accomplish redemption. The prophets had been sent by God, but Jesus is the eternal Son sent into the world, the fulfillment of the Law and the Prophets. Isaiah’s promise of the Spirit-anointed Servant finds its fulfillment in Him. To reject Jesus is therefore to reject the Father who sent Him.

The Witness of Scripture

John 5:39

You pore over the Scriptures
Israel’s religious leaders devoted careful attention to the Law, the Prophets, and the Writings. Their study preserved reverence for God’s revelation, yet diligent examination could not substitute for faith in the God who gave it.

Act 17:11 Now the Bereans were more noble-minded…

Deu 17:18-19 When he is seated on his royal throne, he must write…

Psa 1:2 But his delight is in the Law of the LORD, and on His law…

because you presume that by them you possess eternal life.
Knowledge of the written Word and outward obedience to religious traditions were treated as sufficient grounds for life before God. Eternal life, however, is received through knowing the Father and the Son whom He sent, not through mastery of Scripture apart from its fulfillment in Christ.

2Ti 3:15 From infancy you have known the Holy Scriptures,…

Gal 3:21 Is the law, then, opposed to the promises of God?…

Rom 3:20 Therefore no one will be justified in His sight…

These are the very words that testify about Me,
The whole canon of Scripture bears witness to the promised Messiah. The sacrifices of the Law, the royal promises to David, the suffering servant of Isaiah, and the hope announced by the prophets find their fulfillment in Jesus. Scripture is not an end in itself; it leads God’s people to the Son, in whom its promises are accomplished.

Luk 24:27 And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets,…

Luk 24:44 Jesus said to them, “These are the words I spoke to you…

Act 10:43 All the prophets testify about Him…

John 5:40

yet you refuse
Jesus confronts those who diligently searched the Scriptures yet resisted the One to whom the Scriptures pointed. His miracles, teaching, and fulfillment of God’s promises provided abundant testimony, but religious knowledge without faith left their hearts closed. Jerusalem’s leaders would later reject and crucify the Messiah, fulfilling Jesus’ lament that the city was unwilling to receive the One sent by God (Matthew 23:37).

Pro 1:24-25 Because you refused my call, and no one took…

Mat 23:37 O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets…

Luk 13:34 O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets…

to come to Me
Jesus Himself is the appointed source of salvation, not merely a teacher who directs people elsewhere. Coming to Him requires repentance, faith, and trust in His person and work. He welcomes the weary and burdened, giving rest to those who believe (Matthew 11:28). The Shepherd promised in Ezekiel 34 gathers His sheep, and His sheep hear His voice.

Jhn 6:35 Jesus answered, “I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to Me…

Mat 11:28 Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened,…

Jhn 7:37 On the last and greatest day of the feast, Jesus stood up…

to have life.
The life Jesus gives is eternal life: reconciliation with God, forgiveness of sins, and participation in the life of His kingdom. Sin brings death, but God gives eternal life through Jesus Christ (Romans 6:23). Those who receive Him possess abundant life now and await its fullness in the resurrection.

Jhn 20:31 But these are written so that you may believe…

John 5:41

I do not accept glory from men,
Jesus sought the Father’s approval and fulfilled the mission entrusted to Him, never depending on human praise to establish His identity or authority. His refusal of earthly honor exposed the spiritual emptiness of leaders who prized recognition from one another above the praise of God (John 12:43). The Son shares the Father’s glory and came in humility as the Servant who would bear sin and give His life as a ransom for many (Isaiah 53; Mark 10:45). God will not yield His glory to idols or self-exalting humanity (Isaiah 42:8), and Christ calls all who follow Him to seek honor that comes from God alone.

1Th 2:6 Nor did we seek praise from you or from anyone else…

Jhn 8:50 I do not seek My own glory. There is One who seeks it……

Jhn 7:18 He who speaks on his own authority seeks his own glory…

John 5:42

but I know you,
Jesus saw beyond the Jewish leaders’ public devotion and recognized the condition of their hearts. His knowledge of humanity’s inner thoughts and motives revealed His divine authority (John 2:24–25). Their opposition to His Sabbath healing and His claims of equality with the Father exposed a deeper refusal to receive the One sent by God.

Jhn 2:24-25 But Jesus did not entrust Himself to them…

Luk 16:15 So He said to them, “You are the ones who justify…

1Co 8:3 But the one who loves God is known by God.

that you do not have the love of God
Israel’s law called God’s people to love Him with their whole being (Deuteronomy 6:4–5). Yet these leaders honored religious tradition while rejecting God’s Son, whom the Father had sent. Their desire for human praise rather than God’s glory demonstrated that genuine love for God was absent (John 5:43–44).

1Jn 5:3 For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments.…

1Jn 4:20 If anyone says, “I love God,” but hates his brother,…

1Jn 4:8 Whoever does not love does not know God…

within you.
Outward obedience cannot replace an inward relationship with God. The prophets had warned against worship that retained ceremonies while the heart remained far from the Lord (Isaiah 29:13; Hosea 6:6). God’s New Covenant work writes His law upon the heart, producing love, faith, and obedience from within (Jeremiah 31:33).

John 5:43

I have come in My Father’s name,
Jesus came bearing the Father’s authority, revealing the Father’s character, and accomplishing the work the Father sent Him to do. His words, works, signs, and sacrificial mission were not self-appointed. The Father’s testimony through Scripture, John the Baptist, and Jesus’ miracles confirmed His identity. Israel’s promised Messiah was not merely a political deliverer but the eternal Son, the divine King foretold in Isaiah 9:6. Jesus’ unity with the Father, later stated plainly in John 10:30, makes rejection of the Son a rejection of the Father who sent Him.

Jhn 10:25 “I already told you,” Jesus replied “but you did not believe…

Jhn 8:42 Jesus said to them, “If God were your Father,…

Jhn 12:49-50 I have not spoken on My own, but the Father who sent Me…

and you have not received Me;
Many religious leaders examined the evidence while refusing its conclusion. They saw works that no mere man could perform, yet their devotion to status, tradition, and human approval blinded them to the One to whom the Scriptures pointed. Isaiah had foretold that the Servant of the Lord would be “despised and rejected by men” (Isaiah 53:3). Their expectation of a Messiah who would immediately overthrow Rome kept them from receiving the Messiah who first came to deal with sin and reconcile sinners to God.

Jhn 1:11 He came to His own, and His own did not receive Him.

Luk 19:14 But his subjects hated him and sent a delegation after him…

Mat 21:42-43 The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.…

but if someone else comes in his own name,
A leader acting in his own name seeks personal authority and glory rather than submission to God. Jesus warned that false christs and false prophets would arise and deceive many (Matthew 24:24). In the generations following Jesus’ ministry, revolutionary figures drew followers by promising national restoration through force; Simon bar Kokhba, who led a failed revolt against Rome in A.D. 132–135, became a notable later example. Such leaders offered the kind of deliverance many desired, but they could not provide forgiveness, eternal life, or peace with God.

2Th 2:3-4 Let no one deceive you in any way, for it will not come…

The readiness to embrace self-promoting leaders exposed the spiritual blindness behind the rejection of Jesus. Those who do not love the truth become vulnerable to convincing lies, signs, and promises that appeal to earthly hopes (2 Thessalonians 2:9–10). Faith must be governed by God’s revealed Word and centered on Christ, whose authority rests not in human ambition but in His relationship with the Father.

John 5:44

How can you believe
Jesus confronts the Jewish leaders with the moral obstacle that kept them from receiving Him. Their unbelief was not caused by insufficient evidence, since the Father’s testimony, John the Baptist’s witness, the Scriptures, and Jesus’ works all pointed to His identity. Their hearts were fixed on preserving status rather than submitting to God’s revelation.

Jhn 12:43 For they loved praise from men more than praise from God.

Gal 1:10 Am I now seeking the approval of men, or of God?…

1Th 2:4 Instead, we speak as those approved by God…

if you accept glory from one another,
Religious honor within their community had become more important than obedience to God. Leaders who depended on one another’s approval were unwilling to confess the One whom the Father had sent, especially when doing so threatened their influence and standing. Jesus later exposed the same desire for public recognition among those who loved prominent seats and human praise.

1Th 2:6 Nor did we seek praise from you or from anyone else…

Luk 6:26 Woe to you when all men speak well of you,…

Mat 23:7 the greetings in the marketplaces, and the title…

yet do not seek the glory that comes from the only God?
God alone gives the approval that endures, and He gives it to those who honor His Son. Israel’s confession that the Lord is one should have led these leaders to seek His judgment above every human opinion. Faith turns from self-exaltation and the praise of others to receive Christ, whom the Father has glorified.

John 5:45

Do not think that I will accuse you before the Father.
Jesus came into the world to save rather than to condemn, and He stands before the Father as the advocate for those who believe in Him. The leaders’ rejection of Him nevertheless leaves them accountable to God, for they have refused the One whom the Father sent. Their condemnation does not arise from any failure in Christ’s saving mission but from their unbelief in the light they have received.

Rom 8:33-34 Who will bring any charge against God’s elect?…

Your accuser is Moses,
Moses, whom these leaders honored as Israel’s great lawgiver, would testify against them through the Law they claimed to uphold. The Law exposed sin and required wholehearted love for God, yet their resistance to God’s Son revealed that they had not received the God who gave the Law. Moses had warned Israel that the covenant document would stand as a witness against disobedience, and his writings pointed beyond themselves to God’s promised redemption.

Deu 31:26-27 Take this Book of the Law and place it beside the ark…

Heb 10:28 Anyone who rejected the law of Moses died without mercy…

Rom 2:12 All who sin apart from the law will also perish…

in whom you have put your hope.
Their confidence rested in their connection to Moses and in possession of the Law, but the Law was never given as an alternative to faith in Christ. Moses wrote of the coming Prophet and of God’s saving purpose, preparing Israel to recognize the Messiah. Hope placed in Moses while rejecting the One of whom Moses wrote was misplaced; Christ fulfills the Law and grants the righteousness and life that the Law could not produce.

Rom 2:17-20 Now you, if you call yourself a Jew; if you rely on the law…

Php 3:4-9 though I myself could have such confidence.…

John 5:46

If you had believed Moses,
Moses was Israel’s lawgiver, prophet, and covenant mediator, whose writings formed the foundation of Jewish faith and worship. The leaders who opposed Jesus claimed loyalty to Moses, yet they missed the One to whom Moses pointed. Moses foretold that God would raise up a prophet like himself whom Israel must hear (Deut. 18:15). His books also portray Christ through the Passover lamb, the sacrificial system, the manna from heaven, and the bronze serpent lifted up for Israel’s healing.

Luk 16:29-31 But Abraham replied, ‘They have Moses and the Prophets;…

Deu 18:15-19 The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me…

Act 3:22-23 For Moses said, ‘The Lord your God will raise up…

you would believe Me,
Genuine faith in God’s revelation through Moses would lead to faith in Jesus. Jesus did not stand against the Law; He fulfilled its promises, requirements, and anticipations. The Law exposed sin and showed the need for a righteous Deliverer, while Jesus came as the Savior and final sacrifice to whom the Law’s sacrifices and ceremonies bore witness.

Jhn 14:11 Believe Me that I am in the Father and the Father…

Jhn 14:1 Do not let your hearts be troubled. You believe in God;…

Jhn 12:44-45 Then Jesus cried out, “Whoever believes in Me…

because he wrote about Me.
Moses wrote of the coming Messiah both by prophecy and by the events God ordained in Israel’s history. The promised offspring who would defeat the serpent, the prophet like Moses, the lamb whose blood delivered from judgment, and the provision of atonement all find their fulfillment in Christ. Rejecting Jesus therefore revealed not a deeper devotion to Moses, but a failure to receive the testimony God had given through him.

John 5:47

But since you do not believe what he wrote,
Moses wrote with divine authority in the Law, and Jesus had just declared that Moses wrote about Him (John 5:46). The Jewish leaders honored Moses publicly, yet they failed to receive the testimony embedded in the Torah concerning God’s promised Deliverer. The sacrifices, priesthood, Passover, covenant promises, and prophetic hope all pointed beyond themselves to Christ. Their rejection of Jesus revealed that they had not truly believed the Scriptures they claimed to defend.

Jhn 1:45 Philip found Nathanael and told him, “We have found the One…

Luk 16:31 Then Abraham said to him, ‘If they do not listen to Moses…

Luk 24:27 And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets,…

how will you believe what I say?”
Jesus’ words are inseparable from the revelation already given through Moses. The Law exposes sin, establishes humanity’s need for atonement, and directs faith toward the Messiah whom God would send. Rejecting Moses’ witness made the leaders unwilling to receive Christ’s own testimony, though He fulfilled the Scriptures in His life, ministry, death, and resurrection. Genuine faith receives both the written Word of God and the incarnate Word to whom it bears witness.

Jhn 10:25-26 “I already told you,” Jesus replied “but you did not believe…
