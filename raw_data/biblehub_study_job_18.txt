Bildad: God Punishes the Wicked

Job 18:1

Then Bildad the Shuhite replied:
Then Bildad’s second speech continues the debate between Job and his friends. Rather than answering Job’s anguish with compassion, Bildad returns to the conviction that God’s justice is always evident in a person’s circumstances. Bildad, whose name may mean “son of contention” or “Bel has loved,” defends the traditional belief that suffering proves wrongdoing. He sees Job’s refusal to confess hidden sin as a challenge to God’s moral order. the Shuhite Bildad belonged to the Shuhites, a people likely descended from Shuah, Abraham’s son by Keturah. His presence reflects the wider ancient world in which reverence for God and concern for justice were known beyond Israel. replied Bildad answers Job’s previous defense with increasing severity. His words expose the limits of human wisdom when it judges suffering without knowing God’s purposes.

Bildad’s words express a true biblical principle of divine judgment, though Job’s suffering must not be treated as proof that he belongs among the wicked.
