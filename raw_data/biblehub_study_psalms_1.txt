Psalm 1:1

Blessed is the man
God’s favor rests on the person who orders life according to His will. The singular man represents every believer who trusts the Lord and seeks to live in faithful obedience. Psalm 1 opens the Psalter by presenting two roads: the way of the righteous, which leads to life, and the way of the wicked, which ends in judgment.

Psa 40:4 Blessed is the man who has made the LORD his trust…

Psa 112:1 Hallelujah! Blessed is the man who fears the LORD,…

Pro 3:13 Blessed is the man who finds wisdom, the man…

who does not walk in the counsel of the wicked,
The righteous refuse advice shaped by rebellion against God. Wicked counsel may appear practical or appealing, yet it directs the heart away from God’s truth and treats sin as acceptable. God’s people must discern the influences that form their convictions, since friendship, culture, and worldly wisdom can gradually weaken obedience.

Pro 12:5 The plans of the righteous are just, but…

Pro 1:10 My son, if sinners entice you, do not yield to them.

Eph 5:11 Have no fellowship with the fruitless deeds…

or set foot on the path of sinners,
Refusing wicked counsel must lead to refusing sinful conduct. Sinners here are those whose settled course of life defies God’s commands. Proverbs repeatedly warns against joining those who invite others into evil, because shared actions soon form shared loyalties and habits.

Pro 1:15 my son, do not walk the road with them…

Pro 4:14-15 Do not set foot on the path of the wicked…

Pro 2:12-15 to deliver you from the way of evil, from the man…

or sit in the seat of mockers.
Mockers openly scorn God, His holiness, and those who fear Him. Sitting among them portrays settled identification with their contempt and a hardened comfort in rebellion. The movement from walking, to standing on a path, to sitting warns that compromise can deepen into participation and finally into defiant rejection of righteousness.

Pro 22:10 Drive out the mocker, and conflict will depart…

Pro 21:24 Mocker is the name of the proud and arrogant man…

2Pe 3:3 Most importantly, you must understand that in the last days…

Psalm 1:2

But his delight is in the Law of the LORD,
The righteous man treasures God’s revealed will as the covenant instruction given to Israel through Moses. The Law disclosed the holiness of the LORD, exposed sin, directed worship, and taught His people how to live faithfully before Him. Psalm 119 echoes such love for God’s commandments, not as a burden but as wisdom, light, and life. Jesus fulfilled the Law perfectly and revealed in Himself the righteousness toward which it pointed (Matthew 5:17).

Jer 15:16 Your words were found, and I ate them. Your words became…

Psa 119:47 I delight in Your commandments because I love them.

Psa 40:8 I delight to do Your will, O my God; …

and on His law he meditates day and night.
God’s Word occupies the righteous person’s mind and shapes his desires, choices, and conduct. Like Joshua, who was commanded to keep the Book of the Law continually before him (Joshua 1:8), he returns to it with steady attention and obedient faith. Such meditation is more than study; it is the inward reception of God’s truth until life is governed by His will.

Jos 1:8 This Book of the Law must not depart from your mouth;…

Psa 119:97 Oh, how I love Your law! All day long it is my meditation.

Psa 63:6 When I remember You on my bed, I think of You…

Psalm 1:3

He is like a tree planted by streams of water,
In the arid land of Israel, a tree beside an irrigation channel stood in striking contrast to the dryness around it. Its life was sustained by a dependable source beyond itself. The righteous are established through continual dependence on the Lord and His revealed Word, not on changing circumstances. Jeremiah 17:7–8 uses the same picture for the one who trusts in the Lord, whose roots reach toward the water even in seasons of heat and drought. Jesus likewise taught that abiding in Him is the source of enduring spiritual life and fruitfulness (John 15:4–5).

Jer 17:7-8 But blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD,…

Isa 58:11 The LORD will always guide you; He will satisfy you…

Ezk 31:4-5 The waters made it grow; the deep springs made it tall,…

yielding its fruit in season,
A healthy tree bears the fruit proper to its nature and at the appointed time. A life shaped by God’s instruction produces obedience, wisdom, love, holiness, and works that serve others. Such fruit does not arise from self-effort alone but from a heart nourished by fellowship with God. The fruit of the Spirit described in Galatians 5:22–23 reveals the character formed in those who walk by the Spirit. God’s work is neither hurried nor barren; He brings fitting fruit according to His purposes and timing.

Gal 6:9 Let us not grow weary in well-doing, for in due time……

Jhn 15:5 I am the vine and you are the branches…

Col 1:10 so that you may walk in a manner worthy of the Lord…

whose leaf does not wither,
The tree remains visibly alive because its roots have access to unfailing water. In the same way, the righteous receive strength to endure seasons of trial without spiritual collapse. Suffering, opposition, and outward loss may come, but the life God sustains is not exhausted by them. Ezekiel’s vision of trees fed by the river flowing from God’s sanctuary portrays this continuing vitality, with leaves that do not fade and bring healing (Ezekiel 47:12).

Ezk 47:12 Along both banks of the river, fruit trees…

Psa 92:12-14 The righteous will flourish like a palm tree…

Pro 11:28 He who trusts in his riches will fall,…

and who prospers in all he does.
Prosperity in the psalm is the flourishing of a life that fulfills God’s will, rather than a guarantee of uninterrupted wealth or ease. Joshua was commanded to meditate on God’s law so that he might wisely carry out the task God had given him (Joshua 1:8). The righteous person’s labor is fruitful because it is ordered by the Lord, and even adversity cannot finally frustrate God’s purpose. Psalm 1 will contrast this lasting blessing with the wicked, whose way ends in judgment.

Gen 39:2-3 And the LORD was with Joseph, and he became a successful man…

Jos 1:8 This Book of the Law must not depart from your mouth;…

2Ch 26:5 He sought God throughout the days of Zechariah…

Psalm 1:4

Not so the wicked!
The wicked stand in sharp contrast to the righteous, whose lives are nourished by delight in God’s law and sustained by His favor. Wickedness describes a settled resistance to God’s rule, whether expressed through open rebellion, injustice, or self-directed living. Proverbs 4:14–19 portrays such a path as darkness, leading those who walk in it toward ruin.

Psa 37:20 But the wicked and enemies of the LORD will perish…

Psa 92:7 that though the wicked sprout like grass,…

Pro 10:28 The hope of the righteous is joy, but…

For they are like chaff
Chaff was the light, worthless husk separated from grain during threshing. After the grain was tossed into the air on a threshing floor, the useful kernels fell back to the ground while the husks were carried away. The image exposes the emptiness of a life without God. Unlike the fruitful tree of Psalm 1:3, the wicked possess no lasting spiritual substance and bear no fruit that endures before the Lord. Job 21:18 and Isaiah 17:13 likewise portray the enemies of God as chaff before the wind.

Isa 29:5 But your many foes will be like fine dust…

Hos 13:3 Therefore they will be like the morning mist,…

Job 21:18 Are they like straw before the wind, like chaff…

driven off by the wind.
The wind pictures the instability of those who reject God’s truth and the certainty of His judgment. Their apparent success cannot secure them, because they have no root in the Lord and no place among His people. John the Baptist drew on the same harvest imagery when he warned that Christ would gather His wheat but burn the chaff with unquenchable fire (Matthew 3:12).

Isa 17:13 The nations rage like the rush of many waters.…

Isa 41:16 You will winnow them, and a wind will carry them away;…

Job 27:21 The east wind carries him away, and he is gone;…

Psalm 1:5

Therefore the wicked will not stand in the judgment,
God’s judgment will expose every life according to His perfect righteousness. The wicked may prosper for a season, as Psalm 1 has acknowledged, but they possess no lasting defense before the Judge of all the earth. Their rejection of God’s rule leaves them unable to endure His verdict. Historic judgments, including the flood, Sodom and Gomorrah, and Jerusalem’s fall, reveal God’s holiness and foreshadow the final judgment before His throne (Revelation 20:11–15).

Rom 14:10-12 Why, then, do you judge your brother? Or why…

2Co 5:10 For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ,…

Jhn 5:28-29 Do not be amazed at this, for the hour is coming…

nor sinners in the assembly of the righteous.
God will finally separate those who belong to Him from those who persist in rebellion. The righteous are not accepted because of personal merit but because they trust the Lord and walk in His ways. Israel’s worshiping congregation anticipated the gathered people of God, fulfilled in the Church through faith in Christ. Those cleansed and justified by Him will dwell in God’s presence, while unrepentant sinners will have no place among His holy people (Matthew 25:31–46).

Mat 13:41-43 The Son of Man will send out His angels…

Mal 3:18 So you will again distinguish between the righteous…

Heb 12:22-24 Instead, you have come to Mount Zion, to the city…

Psalm 1:6

For the LORD guards the path of the righteous,
The Lord watches over, sustains, and directs those who fear Him. Righteousness in Israel meant walking in faithful obedience to God’s revealed will, not merely outward conformity. Psalm 23 portrays the same care: the Shepherd leads His people in paths of righteousness and preserves them through danger. Those who trust the Lord are ultimately secure because He knows them personally and keeps them in His covenant love. Jesus Christ perfectly walked the righteous path, and through Him believers are counted righteous and enabled to live in obedience.

Pro 2:8 to guard the paths of justice and protect the way…

Psa 91:11 For He will command His angels concerning you…

Psa 121:7-8 The LORD will guard you from all evil; He will preserve…

but the way of the wicked will perish.
A life ordered around rebellion against God ends in ruin. The wicked may appear prosperous for a time, but their course cannot endure under God’s judgment. Scripture repeatedly displays this end in the destruction of Sodom, the downfall of Pharaoh, and the judgment of nations that defied the Lord. Jesus likewise warned that the broad road leads to destruction. The contrast is final: God preserves His people, but every path that rejects Him ends in separation from His blessing and life.
