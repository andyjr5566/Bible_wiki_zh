1 Chronicles 22:2

David organized the resident foreigners living under Israel’s rule for service in preparing materials for the temple. Many were likely descendants of earlier Canaanite populations who remained in the land, along with others who had settled among Israel. Though not Israelites by birth, they lived under Israel’s administration and contributed to the work surrounding the worship of the God of Israel. Their inclusion anticipates the day when people from every nation will join themselves to the Lord and worship in His house (Isaiah 56:6–7).

The work required discipline, strength, and exact craftsmanship, since the temple would be built as a permanent and holy dwelling associated with the Lord’s name. The house of God in Jerusalem pointed beyond itself to Christ, in whom God dwelt among His people, and to the spiritual house formed from believers as living stones (John 2:19–21; 1 Peter 2:5).

God was not confined within the temple, yet He graciously made His presence known there among His people (2 Chronicles 6:18–21). The temple anticipated Jesus Christ, the true dwelling place of God with humanity, who spoke of the temple of His body (John 2:19–21).

Gold and silver would adorn the sanctuary and its furnishings, reflecting the holiness and glory of the God worshiped there. Bronze and iron supplied the strength needed for gates, tools, structural elements, and vessels. Israel also possessed an abundant body of skilled workers, continuing the pattern established when Bezalel, Oholiab, and other gifted artisans prepared the tabernacle. The temple would be built through human labor, yet every resource and ability ultimately came from the Lord.
