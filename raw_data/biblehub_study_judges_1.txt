The city had not yet become Israel’s political or spiritual center, but its position in the hill country made it strategically significant.

Judges 1:33

Naphtali failed to drive out the inhabitants of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath.
Naphtali, descended from Jacob’s son by Bilhah, received its inheritance in the northern part of Canaan. Yet the tribe did not complete the conquest God had commanded when Israel entered the land (Deuteronomy 7:1–2). Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath were Canaanite towns within Naphtali’s allotted territory, and their continued occupation testified to Israel’s incomplete obedience. The Lord had warned that remaining Canaanite populations would become snares, drawing His people toward their gods and practices (Exodus 23:32–33).

So the Naphtalites also lived among the Canaanite inhabitants of the land,
Instead of possessing the land in the fullness God had given, Naphtali settled alongside those whom Israel had been commanded to remove. Such coexistence exposed Israel continually to Canaanite idolatry, fertility worship, and social customs opposed to the holiness required of God’s covenant people (Leviticus 20:24–26). The pattern seen in Naphtali would recur throughout Judges: accommodation with the nations led to spiritual compromise, and compromise eventually brought oppression.

but the inhabitants of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath served them as forced laborers.
Naphtali gained economic advantage and control over these cities without carrying out God’s command completely. Forced labor may have appeared a practical substitute for conquest, but it preserved a Canaanite presence and its corrupting influence within Israel’s inheritance. Israel’s later struggles were not caused by any failure in God’s promise, but by the nation’s unwillingness to trust and obey Him fully.

The Amorites forced the Danites into the hill country
Dan’s inheritance included valuable lowland territory, but Amorite resistance confined the tribe to the less fertile uplands. Israel had been commanded to dispossess the Canaanite peoples, yet Daniel did not secure the land assigned to it. The continuing Amorite presence exposed the cost of incomplete obedience and left Israel vulnerable to its enemies and their idolatrous influence.

Judges 1:35

And the Amorites were determined to dwell in Mount Heres, Aijalon, and Shaalbim.
The Amorites maintained control of key towns in the territory allotted to Dan, despite Israel’s earlier victories in the land. Aijalon lay along an important route through the western foothills toward the coastal plain, while Shaalbim and Mount Heres strengthened Amorite control over the region. Dan’s inability to dislodge these inhabitants exposed Israel’s incomplete obedience to the Lord’s command to possess the land and remove its idolatrous peoples. The surviving Canaanite presence would become a continuing source of military pressure and spiritual temptation.

But when the house of Joseph grew in strength,
The house of Joseph, consisting chiefly of Ephraim and Manasseh, became strong enough to exert authority over the Amorite-held region. Their strength reflected the Lord’s faithfulness to preserve and establish the tribes of Israel in the inheritance He had promised. Yet military strength brought responsibility: Israel was to use the Lord’s gifts in obedient faith rather than adopt expedient arrangements with the nations remaining in the land.
