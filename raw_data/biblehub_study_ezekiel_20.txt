The Lord redeemed Israel from cruel bondage by His mighty hand, fulfilling His covenant promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Egypt had become a place of oppression and idolatrous influence, yet God set His people apart as His own possession. The Exodus displayed His authority over Pharaoh, Egypt’s gods, and every power that enslaves. It also anticipates the greater redemption accomplished by Jesus Christ, who delivers His people from sin and death.

There the Lord gave His law at Sinai, provided manna and water, and taught Israel to depend upon Him daily. Their journey exposed both God’s faithfulness and Israel’s unbelief, preparing a new generation to enter the promised land. Jesus’ forty days in the wilderness likewise revealed perfect obedience where Israel had repeatedly failed.

A Prophecy against the South

Ezekiel 20:45

Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying,
God again entrusted Ezekiel with a message for the exiles, confirming that the warning to follow came from the Lord rather than from the prophet’s own judgment. After recounting Israel’s persistent rebellion throughout her history, the revelation turns toward the coming judgment on Jerusalem and the land of Israel. The Lord’s continuing speech through Ezekiel showed that exile had not silenced God’s covenant claims upon His people.

Ezekiel 20:46

“Son of man,
God addressed Ezekiel by a title that kept the prophet mindful of his mortality before the holy God. Ezekiel stood among the exiles as one of them, yet he bore the solemn responsibility of delivering the Lord’s word to the covenant people.

Ezekiel was to turn his attention toward Judah and Jerusalem, the land from which many exiles had been taken. The determined posture signaled that God’s coming judgment was fixed because Judah had persisted in rebellion despite repeated warnings.

The forest pictured the inhabitants and leaders of Judah, who appeared strong and established but would be consumed by the fire of divine judgment. The prophecy anticipated Babylon’s invasion and Jerusalem’s destruction, events that demonstrated that neither privilege, temple worship, nor national identity could shield an unrepentant people from the Lord.

Ezekiel addresses Judah under the image of a forest in the southern land. Though the Negev was largely arid, the forest pictures a land filled with people, homes, leaders, and resources soon to be consumed. The judgment announced against the south would extend throughout Judah and reach Jerusalem.

‘Is he not just telling parables?’”
Ezekiel’s imagery of a consuming forest fire conveyed the certainty and universality of God’s judgment, but his hearers treated it as obscure speech that could be ignored. Their response exposed spiritual hardness: they preferred to debate the form of the message rather than submit to its meaning. God would soon command Ezekiel to speak even more plainly about Babylon’s sword against Jerusalem.
