Temple Construction Begins

1 Kings 6:1

In the four hundred and eightieth year after the Israelites had come out of the land of Egypt,
This phrase situates the construction of the temple within a historical timeline, marking 480 years since the Exodus. This period is significant as it represents a time of fulfillment of God’s promises to Israel, transitioning from a nomadic existence to a settled nation with a central place of worship. The Exodus is a foundational event in Israel’s history, symbolizing deliverance and covenant relationship with God. This timeline also reflects the long-awaited establishment of a permanent dwelling place for God among His people, as previously, the Ark of the Covenant had been housed in a tent or tabernacle.

The use of cedar wood in the construction of the temple is significant. Cedar was highly valued in the ancient Near East for its durability, resistance to decay, and pleasant aroma. It was imported from Lebanon, indicating the wealth and resources available to Solomon. The use of cedar also reflects the grandeur and majesty of the temple, as it was a material often used in royal and sacred buildings. This choice of material underscores the temple’s purpose as a dwelling place for God, emphasizing its sanctity and the reverence due to it.

1 Kings 6:19

Solomon also prepared the inner sanctuary
The inner sanctuary, or Holy of Holies, formed the most sacred chamber of the temple. It measured twenty cubits in length, width, and height, making it a perfect cube (1 Kings 6:20). Its restricted access emphasized that sinful humanity could not casually approach the holy God. The cubic form anticipates the New Jerusalem, whose length, width, and height are equal (Revelation 21:16), portraying God’s final dwelling with His redeemed people. Solomon’s work fulfilled the Lord’s promise to David that his son would build a house for His name (2 Samuel 7:12–13).

Cypress provided durable, beautiful timber for the entrance to the temple’s Holy Place. Its strength suited a structure built to honor the enduring presence and covenant faithfulness of God. The doors were later carved with cherubim, palm trees, and open flowers and overlaid with gold, joining natural beauty with sacred symbolism.

1 Kings 6:35

He carved into them cherubim, palm trees, and open flowers,
The temple doors bore images that echoed Eden and the tabernacle. Cherubim guarded the way to the tree of life after humanity’s fall and overshadowed the mercy seat above the ark, marking the holiness of God’s dwelling. Palm trees and flowers portrayed the beauty, fruitfulness, and peace of creation under God’s rule. Together, the carvings presented the temple as a sacred garden where the holy God dwelt among His covenant people.

and he overlaid them with gold
Gold covered the doors as it covered the inner sanctuary and many of the temple furnishings. Its splendor testified to the unmatched worth of the Lord who was worshiped there. Solomon gave costly materials for God’s house because Israel’s worship was not to be casual or common. The gold also foreshadowed the radiant glory of God’s final dwelling with His people in the new creation.

The Courtyard

1 Kings 6:36

Solomon built the inner courtyard
Solomon completed the sacred enclosure surrounding the temple proper, setting apart an ordered place for priestly ministry, sacrifice, and worship. The work fulfilled the promise that David’s son would build a house for the Lord’s name (2 Samuel 7:12–13). Its careful design taught Israel that approaching God required reverence and obedience.

Strong, carefully prepared stone gave the court a lasting boundary and reflected the excellence appropriate to the Lord’s house. The same pattern of stone and cedar appeared in Solomon’s royal court (1 Kings 7:12), linking the kingdom’s rule with its responsibility to honor God.

Materials gathered from beyond Israel’s borders served the worship of the God of Israel, whose glory was not confined by the temple yet graciously dwelt among His covenant people.

1 Kings 6:37

The foundation of the house of the LORD
This phrase refers to the beginning of the construction of the temple, a significant event in Israel’s history. The temple was to be the central place of worship for the Israelites, symbolizing God’s presence among His people. The foundation signifies the establishment of a permanent dwelling place for God, replacing the portable tabernacle used during the wilderness wanderings. The temple’s construction fulfills God’s promise to David that his son would build a house for His name (2 Samuel 7:12-13).

2Ch 3:1 Then Solomon began to build the house of the LORD…

was laid in the fourth year of Solomon’s reign,
Solomon’s reign is marked by peace and prosperity, allowing him to undertake such a monumental project. The fourth year indicates a period of preparation and planning before the actual construction began. This timing also reflects Solomon’s commitment to fulfilling his father David’s vision for the temple. The number four in biblical numerology often signifies completeness and universality, suggesting the temple’s role in unifying the nation under God’s covenant.

1 Kings 6:38

In the eleventh year,
Solomon completed the temple about 959 BC, seven years after construction began in the fourth year of his reign. David had prepared materials and received the pattern for the sanctuary, but God appointed Solomon to carry the work to completion. The finished house fulfilled the Lord’s promise that David’s son would build a house for His name.
