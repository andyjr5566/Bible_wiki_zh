Mark 7:36

Jesus ordered them not to tell anyone.
After healing the deaf man who also struggled to speak, Jesus commanded the witnesses to keep the miracle private. In Mark, such commands often guard against a false understanding of His identity. Many hoped for a Messiah who would bring immediate political deliverance, but Jesus came first to suffer, die, and rise again for sinners. His works revealed His divine authority and compassion, yet their full meaning could not be understood apart from the cross and resurrection. Jesus carried out the Father’s mission according to God’s appointed time rather than public pressure or popular expectation.

Mrk 5:43 Then Jesus gave strict orders that no one should know…

But the more He ordered them,
Jesus’ repeated command shows that He was not indifferent to obedience, even when people were stirred by joy and amazement. The miracle had taken place in the Decapolis, a largely Gentile region, where Jesus’ compassion was already reaching beyond Israel’s borders. Those present had seen a man’s hearing restored and his speech made clear by Christ’s direct power. Such a work made silence difficult, but amazement did not remove their responsibility to obey the Lord’s word.

Mat 12:16 warning them not to make Him known.

the more widely they proclaimed it.
News of Jesus spread because His works could not be dismissed as ordinary acts of kindness or skill. He did what only God could do, fulfilling the hope that the deaf would hear and the mute would sing for joy. Their proclamation increased public attention and contributed to the growing response to His ministry, while also drawing the notice of those who opposed Him. After His death and resurrection, the command would change: His disciples would be sent openly to proclaim the gospel to all nations.

Mark 7:37

The people were utterly astonished
The crowd in the Decapolis had witnessed Jesus restore hearing and speech to a man who had been unable to communicate normally. Their amazement arose from a visible, undeniable work of divine power. Jesus’ miracles were not displays of spectacle but signs revealing His authority over the brokenness brought by sin into the world.
